## SECTION 1 — PURPOSE AND SCOPE

### 1.1 Purpose

This Master SOP defines the standard operating procedures for all governance
work conducted under the PHAROS AI Governance Practice. It establishes:

- The procedural infrastructure through which PHAROS converts client AI
  production environments into auditable, trustworthy governed systems.
- The controls PHAROS applies to its own use of AI tools in client work.
- The regulatory alignment framework that keeps PHAROS's methods current
  with evolving AI law and international standards.

This SOP is the single authoritative source for PHAROS procedure. All
engagement-specific SOPs, sub-procedures, templates, and checklists are
subordinate to it. Conflicts are resolved in favor of this document.

### 1.2 Scope

This SOP applies to:
- All client-facing engagements conducted by PHAROS or under its direction.
- All internal PHAROS workflows where AI tools are used to generate,
  analyze, or validate work product.
- All PHAROS personnel, contractors, and AI systems operating on PHAROS
  engagements.

This SOP does not apply to:
- Personal research activities outside PHAROS engagements.
- Third-party systems not operating under PHAROS oversight.

### 1.3 Foundational Architecture

PHAROS operates on the Recursive AI Governance (RAG) Architecture documented
in the PHAROS Method Papers. The architecture treats governance not as a
policy overlay but as executable method infrastructure embedded in every
phase of production. The core failure condition this architecture addresses:
recursive AI-assisted production can generate evidence drift, authority
inflation, and workflow instability unless governance controls are built
into the recursion itself.

All procedures in this SOP derive from that architecture.

## SECTION 2 — DEFINITIONS

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Source Artifact** | A document, dataset, or record whose authority derives from its origin (regulatory text, primary research, client data). Not generated by PHAROS. |
| **Generated Artifact** | Any output produced by PHAROS or an AI tool operating under PHAROS direction. Subject to admissibility assessment before use as evidence. |
| **Control Artifact** | A document whose function is to govern, constrain, or validate other artifacts (this SOP, a checklist, a protocol). |
| **Recursive Production** | A workflow where outputs become inputs for further AI-assisted generation. Requires explicit governance to prevent evidence drift. |
| **Evidence Drift** | The process by which generated artifacts are re-used as if they were source artifacts, degrading the reliability of subsequent conclusions. |
| **Admissibility** | The determination that an artifact meets the standard required to support a specific claim or recommendation in a PHAROS deliverable. |
| **Control** | A procedural mechanism that binds a governance obligation to a named owner, a trigger condition, a required evidence form, and a review interval. |
| **Promotion Threshold** | The standard an artifact must meet before it can move from draft/internal to client-facing or regulatory-facing use. |
| **Method Lock** | A failure condition in which a governance procedure becomes self-referential and stops adapting to new evidence or regulatory change. |
| **PHAROS Principal** | The senior practitioner with final authority over PHAROS engagements, method, and this SOP. |

## SECTION 5 — CORE GOVERNANCE PROCEDURES

---

### 5.1 — CLIENT ENGAGEMENT INTAKE

**Purpose:** Establish the governance baseline for each engagement before
any analytical work begins.

**Trigger:** Client contract signed or Letter of Engagement issued.

**Steps:**

1. Complete the PHAROS Intake Form (Form PH-INT-001), capturing:
   - Client name and sector
   - AI systems in scope (names, functions, deployment status)
   - Claimed risk classification by client
   - Applicable regulatory instruments
   - Existing governance artifacts at client (policies, SOPs, checklists)
   - Requested PHAROS output type (audit / advisory / framework build)

2. Assign roles per Section 3. Confirm Engagement Lead.

3. Create the Engagement Archive with the following initial structure:
   ```
   /[ClientName]-[EngagementID]/
     /source-artifacts/        ← client-supplied documents
     /generated-artifacts/     ← PHAROS outputs
     /control-artifacts/       ← protocols, checklists, this SOP
     /regulatory-refs/         ← applicable regulatory texts
     /deliverables/            ← final client-facing outputs
   ```

4. Run initial artifact triage: classify every document received from
   the client as source, generated, or control.

5. Document the Governing Object: state explicitly whether this engagement
   is primarily a source-tree audit, recursive workflow assessment,
   governance failure investigation, disclosure/authorship problem, or
   control design build.

**Owner:** Engagement Lead
**Evidence required:** Completed PH-INT-001; Engagement Archive created;
Governing Object statement on file.
**Review interval:** Before engagement kickoff with client.

---

### 5.2 — AI SYSTEM CLASSIFICATION AND RISK TRIAGE

**Purpose:** Establish the regulatory risk profile of each AI system
in scope before analysis begins.

**Trigger:** Completion of 5.1.

**Steps:**

1. Apply the EU AI Act risk hierarchy to each system in scope:
   - Prohibited (Article 5 practices — must flag immediately)
   - High-Risk (Annex III categories — full documentation required)
   - GPAI (general-purpose AI model — GPAI obligations apply)
   - Limited/Minimal Risk (transparency obligations only)

2. Cross-reference against NIST AI RMF risk categorization.

3. For each system, document:
   - Intended purpose vs. actual deployment
   - Human oversight mechanisms in place
   - Existing audit logging capability
   - Training data provenance (where disclosable)

4. Produce the Risk Classification Register (Form PH-RCR-002).
   Flag any system where client classification differs from PHAROS
   classification. Escalate discrepancies to PHAROS Principal before
   proceeding.

5. Identify applicable conformity assessment routes if EU AI Act applies.

**Owner:** Governance Analyst with Engagement Lead sign-off
**Evidence required:** Completed PH-RCR-002; discrepancy memo (if any)
**Review interval:** Updated upon any material change to system scope.

---

### 5.3 — RECURSIVE GOVERNANCE AUDIT PROTOCOL

**Purpose:** Audit client AI production workflows for recursive governance
failure conditions consistent with the PHAROS Method Architecture.

**Trigger:** After 5.2 is complete.

**Steps:**

1. Map the client's AI production recursion: identify each point where
   AI output re-enters the workflow as input.

2. At each recursion point, ask:
   - What is the first-order source material here?
   - What is already a derived (generated) artifact?
   - Does derived material re-enter as if it were source material?
   - Where do admissibility rules change between recursion passes?
   - Where does fluent repetition harden into apparent authority?
   - Where is human interruption missing?

3. Document each failure condition found using the standard failure
   taxonomy:
   - **Evidence Drift** — generated artifact treated as source
   - **Authority Inflation** — repetition creates credibility without validation
   - **Workflow Instability** — production speed masks reliability degradation
   - **Method Lock** — governance procedure becomes self-referential
   - **Disclosure Gap** — AI contribution not documented at decision points

4. For each failure condition, identify:
   - Location in the workflow
   - Severity (Critical / Significant / Minor)
   - Consequence domain (authorship / auditability / workflow /
     legitimacy / documentation / release / review)

5. Produce the Recursive Governance Audit Memo (Form PH-RGA-003).

**Owner:** Governance Analyst supervised by Engagement Lead
**Evidence required:** Completed PH-RGA-003 with sourced citations
**Review interval:** One memo per engagement phase; updated if scope changes.

---

### 5.4 — EVIDENCE HIERARCHY AND ARTIFACT CONTROL

**Purpose:** Maintain a clear and enforceable distinction between source
material and generated material throughout every engagement.

**Trigger:** Active throughout all engagement phases.

**Rules (mandatory — no exceptions without Principal approval):**

1. No generated artifact may be cited as evidence for a factual claim
   without tracing it to a source artifact.

2. All AI tool outputs are tagged [AI-GENERATED] and stored in
   /generated-artifacts/. They are not promoted to /deliverables/ without
   Engagement Lead admissibility sign-off.

3. Synthesis artifacts (summaries, analysis memos, frameworks) are tagged
   [SYNTHESIS]. They do not outrank the source material they compress.

4. Revision history is mandatory for any artifact that changes material
   claims between versions. Minor edits do not require new versions; any
   change to a finding, recommendation, or control definition does.

5. Polished language is not evidence of accuracy. When in doubt, return
   to the source.

**Owner:** All PHAROS personnel on the engagement
**Evidence required:** Tagged artifact files in correct folders;
version history log for control and deliverable artifacts.
**Review interval:** Spot-checked by Engagement Lead weekly during
active engagements.

### 5.5 — FINDINGS DOCUMENTATION AND BOUNDED CONCLUSIONS

**Purpose:** Ensure all PHAROS conclusions are proportionate to the
evidence and clearly distinguish certainty levels.

**Trigger:** After PH-RGA-003 is complete; before any client communication.

**Steps:**

1. Sort all findings into three claim levels:
   - **Direct Evidence** — plainly stated in source artifacts
   - **Inference** — supported by multiple traces across the artifact pack
   - **Speculation** — plausible but not grounded in the artifact pack

2. Write bounded conclusions only. Use language that reflects the
   evidence level:
   - Direct evidence: "The record shows…"
   - Inference: "The pattern suggests…" / "This supports a reading that…"
   - Speculation: "A possible interpretation is…" (flag as unproven)

3. For each major conclusion, name its consequence domain:
   authorship, auditability, workflow, legitimacy, documentation,
   release, or review.

4. Never report a conclusion that depends on missing logs, absent revision
   history, or undisclosed data without flagging the dependency explicitly.

5. Produce the Findings Register (Form PH-FDR-004).

**Owner:** Engagement Lead with Principal review for high-risk engagements
**Evidence required:** Completed PH-FDR-004; claim level tags on all findings
**Review interval:** Before each client deliverable is drafted.

---

### 5.6 — RECOMMENDATIONS AND CONTROL DESIGN

**Purpose:** Translate findings into actionable governance controls that
bind to the client's actual workflow.

**Trigger:** After PH-FDR-004 is signed off.

**Steps:**

1. For each finding requiring remediation, design a control that names:
   - **Owner** — specific role (not "the team" or "management")
   - **Trigger** — what event activates this control
   - **Required Evidence** — what proof of execution looks like
   - **Review Interval** — how often the control is tested or revisited
   - **Consequence Domain** — what breaks if this control fails

2. Verify that controls bind to actual interfaces in the client's workflow,
   not to aspirational process descriptions.

3. Distinguish controls by urgency:
   - **Immediate** — required before next AI system deployment or output release
   - **Short-term** — within 90 days
   - **Structural** — requires process redesign; 90 days to 12 months

4. Produce the Control Register (Form PH-CTR-005).

5. Cross-reference each control against applicable regulatory obligations
   in Appendix B to confirm compliance coverage.

**Owner:** Engagement Lead
**Evidence required:** Completed PH-CTR-005; regulatory crosswalk complete
**Review interval:** Updated as findings are revised or client conditions change.

---

### 5.7 — DELIVERY AND CLIENT HANDOFF

**Purpose:** Ensure client receives a complete, usable, and auditable
deliverable package.

**Trigger:** Deliverable approved by PHAROS Principal (or Engagement Lead
for non-high-risk engagements).

**Steps:**

1. Compile the deliverable package:
   - Executive Summary (non-technical; for client leadership)
   - Full Findings Register (PH-FDR-004)
   - Control Register (PH-CTR-005)
   - Risk Classification Register (PH-RCR-002)
   - Regulatory Crosswalk Table (Appendix B format)
   - AI Disclosure Statement (see 6.2)

2. Conduct a pre-delivery review:
   - Confirm all claims are sourced or appropriately flagged
   - Confirm all AI-generated content is disclosed
   - Confirm no client confidential information appears outside
     authorized sections
   - Confirm version numbers and dates are accurate

3. Deliver via agreed channel. Retain a copy of the delivered version
   in /deliverables/ with the delivery timestamp.

4. Provide a 30-day post-delivery support window for questions.
   Document all post-delivery clarifications in the engagement record.

**Owner:** Engagement Lead; Principal sign-off required for high-risk
**Evidence required:** Signed delivery receipt or delivery log;
version-stamped deliverable on file.
**Review interval:** Per engagement.

---

### 5.8 — POST-ENGAGEMENT MONITORING

**Purpose:** Track whether PHAROS controls remain effective after delivery,
and feed lessons back into this SOP.

**Trigger:** 90 days after delivery; annually for long-term engagements.

**Steps:**

1. Request a brief status report from the client on:
   - Controls implemented vs. recommended
   - New AI systems deployed since engagement
   - Incidents or failures related to engagement findings

2. Document in the Engagement Monitoring Log (Form PH-MON-006).

3. Where a control has failed or been bypassed, assess whether a PHAROS
   method gap contributed. If yes, flag for SOP review per Section 6.1.

4. Archive the monitoring record alongside the original engagement file.

**Owner:** Engagement Lead
**Evidence required:** Completed PH-MON-006
**Review interval:** 90 days post-delivery; then annually.

## SECTION 6 — INTERNAL QUALITY AND OVERSIGHT

---

### 6.1 — SOP REVIEW AND CHANGE CONTROL

**Purpose:** Keep this Master SOP current, accurate, and free from
method lock.

**Review schedule:**
- Scheduled review: every 6 months from effective date.
- Triggered review: within 30 days of any of the following:
  - Material amendment to a listed regulatory instrument
  - PHAROS method failure identified in a post-engagement monitoring
  - Recurring deviation pattern identified across engagements
  - New AI tool category adopted by PHAROS

**Change process:**
1. Proposed change is documented in the SOP Change Proposal (Form PH-SCP-007),
   stating: what changes, why, what evidence supports it, what risks it
   introduces.
2. PHAROS Principal reviews and approves or rejects with documented rationale.
3. Approved change is applied, version number is incremented (minor: x.1, x.2;
   major: 2.0, 3.0), and the change is recorded in Appendix A.
4. All PHAROS personnel are notified of changes affecting their work.
5. No change is retroactively applied to closed engagements without
   explicit notation.

**Owner:** PHAROS Principal
**Evidence required:** Completed PH-SCP-007; updated Appendix A entry;
version-stamped SOP on file.

---

### 6.2 — AI TOOL USE AND DISCLOSURE

**Purpose:** Ensure PHAROS maintains integrity and client trust by
disclosing all AI contributions to its work product.

**Rules:**

1. All AI tool outputs used in any client deliverable must be disclosed
   in the AI Disclosure Statement appended to the deliverable.

2. The Disclosure Statement must specify the function-specific layer of
   AI contribution:
   - **Clerical** — formatting, transcription, search, sorting
   - **Structural** — organizing arguments, drafting outlines, summarizing
   - **Epistemic** — generating analysis, drawing inferences, proposing
     conclusions

3. Epistemic AI contributions require explicit human validation before
   promotion to deliverable status. The validating analyst is named.

4. AI tools used for internal PHAROS processes (not client deliverables)
   are logged in the Internal AI Use Log (Form PH-AIL-008) but are not
   disclosed to clients unless they materially affect delivered findings.

5. PHAROS does not represent AI-generated content as human expert opinion
   without explicit validation by a named PHAROS analyst.

**Owner:** All PHAROS personnel; monitored by Engagement Lead
**Evidence required:** AI Disclosure Statement in each deliverable;
completed PH-AIL-008 per engagement.

## SECTION 7 — INCIDENT AND DEVIATION MANAGEMENT

**An incident** is any event where a PHAROS control failed, a regulatory
obligation was not met, or a deliverable was found to contain material error.

**A deviation** is a planned or unplanned departure from SOP procedure that
does not meet the threshold of an incident.

**Incident process:**
1. Document in the Incident Register (Form PH-INC-009) within 24 hours
   of discovery.
2. Notify PHAROS Principal immediately.
3. Assess client impact. If a delivered finding is materially wrong,
   notify client and issue a corrected deliverable.
4. Conduct root cause analysis within 14 days.
5. Implement corrective action. If the incident points to an SOP gap,
   initiate a change per Section 6.1.

**Deviation process:**
1. Document reason for deviation before acting on it (or as soon as
   practicable if circumstances are urgent).
2. Engagement Lead approves deviations within engagement scope.
   Principal approval required for deviations from Sections 5.4, 6.2,
   or this Section 7.
3. Log all deviations in the Deviation Log (Form PH-DEV-010).

**Owner:** PHAROS Principal for incidents; Engagement Lead for deviations
**Evidence required:** Completed PH-INC-009 or PH-DEV-010 as applicable.
**Review interval:** Incidents reviewed at each scheduled SOP review.
