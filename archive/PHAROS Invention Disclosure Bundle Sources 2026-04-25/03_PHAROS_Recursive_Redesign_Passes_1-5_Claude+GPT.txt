PASS SECTION E — REWRITE THE METHOD

PHAROS — RECURSIVE
DETERMINISTIC AI GOVERNANCE
PROTOCOL
Version 1.0 (Pass 1 Rewrite)
PREAMBLE AND SCOPE
PHAROS is a governance-control protocol for AI-assisted workflows. Its purpose is to
convert recursive transformation of a bounded corpus into interface-bound operational
controls — intake rules, review gates, escalation triggers, documentation requirements,
approval thresholds, deployment restrictions, and audit obligations — that are explicit,
reconstructable, and verifiable by independent reviewers.
PHAROS is not a theory of recursive cognition. It is not a style guide for AI use. It does not
produce governance by articulating principles. It produces governance by binding controls to
real workflow interfaces, real accountable actors, real evidence artifacts, and real review
intervals.

A control is incomplete until it names all four of these. Prose that describes governance
without naming all four is not a control; it is a governance aspiration and must be labeled as
such.

PART 1: DEFINITIONS
Bounded corpus: A deliberately delimited set of materials admitted into the method under
declared rules specifying source type, provenance, sensitivity, intended use, and epistemic
status. Bounds must be declared before materials are admitted. Post-hoc expansion of
bounds requires a documented amendment.
Control: A governance instrument that names: (1) the failure or risk pattern it addresses, (2)
the workflow interface where it attaches, (3) the accountable actor or role, (4) the evidence
artifact required to satisfy it, (5) the review interval or trigger. A statement missing any of
these five fields is a governance aspiration, not a control.
Deterministic pathway: A pathway that is explicit, constrained, reviewable, and
reconstructable enough that an independent reviewer can reconstruct each governance
decision from the documented record alone, without additional explanation from the original
operator.
Diagnostic depth: The extent to which a recursive pass produces new governance-relevant
findings — new control surfaces, new failure patterns, new provenance distinctions — rather
than restating prior findings in different vocabulary. Diagnostic depth is assessed against the
prior pass record, not against the operator's sense of progress.
Drift: Any unacknowledged change in the governing object, the target construct being
assessed, the evidence hierarchy, or the success conditions across passes. Drift that is
named and documented is a legitimate method decision. Drift that is not named is a defect.
Escalation test: The gate between Stage 5 completion and Stage 5 repetition. A recursive
return pass is justified only when the evaluator can specify, before the new pass begins,
what governance-relevant finding the new pass is designed to produce that prior passes
have not yet produced. An assertion that "more passes will deepen the analysis" does not
pass the escalation test. A specific gap does.
Failure doctrine: The operational rule that uneven oversight, abandoned outputs, forgotten
branches, consent ambiguity, and archive instability are treated as governance evidence
rather than discarded noise. Failures are registered in the failure register with detection date,
affected stage, gap type, and corrective control.
Governance probe: A cultural or symbolic material used in multi-model rendering
specifically to expose how AI systems classify ambiguity, authority, gender, deviance,
legitimacy, or contested agency. A material is classified as a governance probe before
rendering, not after. The probe hypothesis must be stated before the pass begins.

Method lock: A condition in which recursive passes are producing only stylistic refinement
of existing outputs rather than new governance findings. Method lock is detected when two
consecutive escalation test assessments find no specific new finding to pursue. Method lock
triggers a method redesign review rather than a further recursive pass.
Operator: Any person or team who runs a pass of this protocol. Where a single person
serves as corpus designer, recursive renderer, and audit reviewer, this constitutes
single-operator operation, which must be disclosed and compensated by external review at a
minimum of one gate.
Performative compliance: Satisfying the visible structure of a control, phase deliverable, or
governance requirement without satisfying its actual evidentiary or operational purpose.
Provenance chain: The documented trace from a governance claim back to the
source-bearing artifact(s) that support it, through each transformation step, with dates and
responsible actors.
Source-bearing artifact: A material that can directly support factual, procedural, or
documentary claims. Classified before transformation begins. Generated synthesis artifacts
derived from source-bearing artifacts are not source-bearing artifacts.
Stop rule: The protocol terminates recursive return (Stage 5) when: (a) the escalation test
fails to identify a specific new finding to pursue, OR (b) the iteration count reaches the
declared maximum, OR (c) two consecutive passes are classified as method-lock. The stop
rule may not be suspended by operator preference.

PART 2: STAGE PIPELINE
Each stage has: (a) a declared purpose, (b) a primary governance question it must answer,
(c) required deliverables, (d) a completion condition that must be met before Stage N+1
begins, and (e) the control point installed at that stage.

STAGE 1: CORPUS FORMATION
Purpose: Establish the bounded set of materials that will enter the recursive loop, under
explicit rules.
Primary governance question: What exactly is being allowed to enter the recursive loop, and
on what authority?
Required deliverables:
●​ Corpus manifest listing each admitted artifact with: source type, intake date,
provenance, sensitivity classification, intended use, and epistemic status
●​ Declaration of corpus bounds (what is excluded and why)
●​ Consent and rights boundary documentation where applicable

Completion condition: Every artifact in the corpus has a complete manifest entry. The corpus
bounds are stated. No artifact enters without a manifest entry.
Control point: Intake gate. No artifact enters the recursive loop without passing the intake
gate. The intake gate checks: source type classified? provenance documented? sensitivity
flagged? intended use stated? Artifacts failing the intake gate are quarantined, not silently
admitted.
Inadmissible: Post-hoc expansion of corpus bounds without a dated amendment. Silent
admission of materials not in the manifest.

STAGE 2: INTENT EMBEDDING
Purpose: Declare the operative purpose of this pass before transformation begins.
Primary governance question: What work is this pass specifically authorized to perform?
Required deliverables:
●​ Declared pass intent (permitted transformation types: interpretive / analytic / synthetic
/ adversarial / diagnostic)
●​ Prohibited inferences for this pass
●​ Output-role label (what role the output will play in the next stage)
●​ If a governance probe is to be used: the probe hypothesis stated in advance
Completion condition: Pass intent is documented before any rendering begins. Probe
hypotheses, if any, are recorded before materials are submitted to AI systems.
Control point: Intent lock. Once the pass begins, the declared intent governs what outputs
count as valid for this pass. Outputs that fall outside the declared intent are labeled as
out-of-scope outputs, not credited as findings.
Inadmissible: Post-hoc assignment of pass intent. Retroactive classification of a material as
a governance probe after the AI system's output has been observed.

STAGE 3: MULTI-MODEL RENDERING
Purpose: Submit bounded materials across models, prompts, and formats to surface
variation and stability.
Primary governance question: What remains stable across renderings, and what changes?
Required deliverables:
●​ Model log (which models were used, which versions)
●​ Prompt log (exact prompts used, in sequence)
●​ Output classification (type, role, confidence level)

●​ Storage discipline record (where outputs are held, in what format)
Completion condition: All renderings are logged. No output from this stage is promoted to
Stage 4 without a corresponding log entry.
Control point: Rendering log. Outputs without log entries are classified as unattributed
outputs and may not be used as evidence.
Inadmissible: Undocumented rendering sessions. Retroactive reconstruction of prompt
sequences.

STAGE 4: TRIANGULATION AND COMPARISON
Purpose: Compare outputs against one another and against the source corpus to identify
stable patterns, contradictions, omissions, and drift.
Primary governance question: What latent structure emerges from cross-output comparison
that was not visible in any single output?
Required deliverables:
●​ Comparison matrix (outputs vs. source materials, with explicit notation of
convergences, contradictions, omissions, and anomalies)
●​ Stability markers (patterns that recur across models and prompts)
●​ Contradiction register (patterns where outputs diverge significantly)
●​ Hallucination flag register (outputs that assert claims not present in the source
corpus)
●​ Novelty flag register (outputs that introduce new claims not present in source
materials)
Completion condition: The comparison matrix is complete. All flags are documented. No
finding is promoted to Stage 5 or 6 without a corresponding matrix entry.
Control point: Comparison gate. Only patterns with matrix entries may serve as the basis for
escalation decisions or control extraction.
Inadmissible: Findings asserted from memory without matrix entries. Patterns attributed to
the comparison process that appear only in one output.

STAGE 5: RECURSIVE RETURN PASS
Purpose: Determine whether an additional recursive pass is warranted by a specific
unresolved finding, and if so, execute it under the same intake controls as Stage 2.
Primary governance question: Does another pass deepen the analysis by addressing a
specific unresolved finding, or does it merely restate prior findings?

Escalation test (must be passed before any recursive return begins): The evaluator must
state: (a) the specific finding from Stage 4 that warrants return, (b) the governance-relevant
question the new pass is designed to answer, (c) why prior passes have not answered it.
If the evaluator cannot state all three elements, the escalation test fails and the protocol
advances to Stage 6 without recursion.
Stop rule triggers (any one halts further recursion):
●​ Escalation test fails to identify a specific unresolved finding
●​ Iteration count reaches declared maximum
●​ Two consecutive passes produce no new entries in the comparison matrix
Required deliverables:
●​ Escalation test record (pass or fail, with stated reason)
●​ If pass: new pass record with declared intent following Stage 2 requirements
●​ If fail: stop decision record with reason
Completion condition: Escalation test decision is documented. Either a new pass record
exists, or a stop decision record exists. Recursion does not continue by default.
Control point: Escalation gate.
Inadmissible: Recursion initiated without a passed escalation test. Assertion that "more
passes will improve the analysis" without a specified unresolved finding.

STAGE 6: CONTROL EXTRACTION
Purpose: Translate recurrent patterns from the comparison matrix into operational
governance controls.
Primary governance question: What safeguard is implied by each recurrent pattern, and
where exactly does it attach in the workflow?
Control format (all five fields are required; a missing field means the control is incomplete):
1.​ Risk pattern: the specific failure mode or governance gap the control addresses
2.​ Workflow interface: the specific stage or operational location where the control
attaches
3.​ Accountable actor: the named role or person responsible for implementing and
maintaining the control
4.​ Evidence artifact: the specific document, log, or record that demonstrates the control
has been applied
5.​ Review interval: the frequency or trigger for reviewing whether the control is still
adequate
Required deliverables:

●​ Control register with one entry per extracted control, containing all five fields
●​ Basis citation for each control (which comparison matrix entry or stability marker
supports it)
●​ Incomplete control register for controls that cannot yet be fully specified, with explicit
notation of missing fields
Completion condition: Every extracted control has all five fields populated or is explicitly
listed in the incomplete control register with notation of what is missing and what is needed
to complete it. Controls without this status are not promoted.
Control point: Control validation gate. Controls in the incomplete register may not be treated
as operative governance.
Inadmissible: Governance aspirations presented as controls. Controls without basis
citations. Controls attributed to patterns not present in the comparison matrix.

STAGE 7: DETERMINISM HARDENING
Purpose: Make the governing pathway reconstructable by formalizing acceptance, revision,
and rejection criteria.
Primary governance question: Could an independent reviewer reconstruct why each output
was accepted, revised, or rejected, using only the documented record?
Required deliverables:
●​ Acceptance criteria: explicit conditions under which a pass output advances
●​ Rejection criteria: explicit conditions under which a pass output is rejected and the
pass is repeated
●​ Version history: record of what changed between versions and why
●​ Decision log: one entry per governance decision, with: the decision made, the
evidence that supported it, the actor who made it, and the date
Completion condition: The decision log is complete. Acceptance and rejection criteria are
stated. An independent reviewer test: can someone unfamiliar with the pass reconstruct the
key decisions from the decision log alone?
Control point: Reconstructability check.
Inadmissible: Decisions reconstructable only because the original operator explains them
verbally. Version changes without logged rationale.

STAGE 8: DEPLOYMENT BINDING
Purpose: Attach extracted controls to real operational workflow interfaces.

Primary governance question: Where in the actual workflow does each control live, and who
owns it?
Required deliverables:
●​ Interface map: for each control in the control register, the specific operational location
(intake, classification, drafting, approval, publication, procurement, monitoring, etc.)
●​ Owner assignment: the specific person or role responsible for each control
●​ Implementation artifact: the document, system, or process change through which the
control is operative
●​ Training requirement: what the accountable actor must know to apply the control
correctly
●​ Method lock designation: where a specific editorial or procedural path must remain
fixed to preserve governance integrity
Completion condition: Every operative control in the control register has an interface
assignment, an owner, and an implementation artifact. Controls without all three are listed as
unbound and may not be treated as operative governance.
Control point: Binding gate.
Inadmissible: Controls described as "attached to the workflow" without specifying which
workflow interface. Controls assigned to roles that do not exist in the actual workflow.

STAGE 9: LOGGING AND AUDIT TRAIL
Purpose: Record all consequential decisions, evidence links, state changes, approvals,
escalations, and incidents across the pathway.
Consequential decision (definition): Any decision that (a) admits or excludes a material from
the corpus, (b) determines whether a control is operative or incomplete, (c) determines
whether recursion continues or stops, (d) promotes or blocks a pass result, or (e) classifies a
failure event.
Required deliverables:
●​ Decision log (one entry per consequential decision: decision / evidence / actor / date)
●​ Audit trail (chronological record of all state changes across the pipeline)
●​ Evidence links (each claim in the final governance output links to at least one
source-bearing artifact via a documented transformation chain)
●​ Review cadence record (when and by whom each operative control has been
reviewed)
●​ Incident register (any case where a control failed, was bypassed, or produced an
unexpected result)
Completion condition: The decision log covers all consequential decisions in this pass. The
audit trail is complete and chronological. Evidence links are present for all promoted claims.

Control point: Audit completeness check.
Inadmissible: Undated log entries. Log entries added after the fact without notation. Claims
in governance outputs without evidence links.

STAGE 10: FAILURE HARVESTING
Purpose: Analyze failures, gaps, abandoned branches, and oversight absences as primary
governance evidence.
Primary governance question: What failed because a control was absent, weak, misplaced,
or never built?
Required deliverables:
●​ Failure register: one entry per identified failure event with: detection date, whether
recorded contemporaneously or retrospectively, affected stage, gap type (absent
control / weak control / misplaced control / oversight asymmetry), corrective control
generated (or reason none could be generated)
●​ Near-miss analysis: cases where the absence of failure was attributable to factors
other than the control system
●​ Asymmetry map: cases where some workflow branches received denser governance
than others without justification
●​ Corrective action log: for each failure event, the corrective action taken or rejected,
with reason
Completion condition: Every identified failure event has a complete failure register entry. The
contemporaneous vs. retrospective flag is populated for every entry.
Control point: Failure register completeness check.
Inadmissible: Retrospective failure reclassification (labeling abandoned outputs as
intentional governance evidence) without contemporaneous documentation. Failure register
entries attributed to unnamed gap types.

PART 3: EVIDENCE HIERARCHY
Applied in this order when evidence sources conflict or when a governance claim requires
support:
1.​ (Strongest) Independent replication: An independent reviewer, working from the
audit trail alone, reaches the same governance decision as the original operator.
2.​ Verified control register entry: The named actor, interface, and evidence artifact
can be independently verified to exist by a party who did not produce the control.
3.​ Contemporaneous log entry: A time-stamped record made before or during (not
after) the event it documents.

4.​ Source-bearing artifact: A primary manuscript, record, log, or correspondence with
documented provenance.
5.​ Generated synthesis artifact: A document produced by transformation of source
materials, with complete transformation chain documented.
6.​ (Weakest) Operator self-assessment: The original operator's assertion that a
deliverable is complete, a control is valid, or a decision was sound.

PART 4: PROMOTION RULES
Promotion gate: A pass result advances to the next stage only when:
●​ Overall status = ready_full or ready_with_bounded_gaps
●​ All bounded gaps are explicitly named, mechanism-stated, consequence-identified,
and carry-authorized by a named actor
●​ The prior stage's completion condition is documented as met
Non-promotion: Overall statuses of needs_revision, blocked_not_ready, failed, or
incomplete block promotion. Remediation requires a new pass record referencing the
blocked pass.
Silent promotion is prohibited. Promotion without a documented gate decision is treated
as a protocol violation and must be entered in the failure register.

PART 5: OPERATOR CONSTRAINTS
1.​ Single-operator disclosure: An operator serving simultaneously as corpus
designer, renderer, and audit reviewer must disclose this in the pass record and must
arrange external review at minimum one gate (recommended: control validation gate
or reconstructability check).​
2.​ Anti-framing rule: An operator may not pre-brief the AI system on what governance
findings the corpus is expected to yield before rendering. The corpus enters the
rendering stage without declared expected outputs.​
3.​ Post-hoc reclassification prohibition: An operator may not reclassify a material as
a governance probe, or a failure as intentional governance evidence, after observing
the output that would be explained by the reclassification. Probe hypotheses must
precede rendering. Failure classifications must carry a detection date.​
4.​ Authority ranking transparency: When a file is assigned an authority level (high /
bounded / bounded_low), the specific evidence supporting that ranking must be
stated, not only the ranking.​
5.​ No self-validation: Governance outputs may not use documents generated by the
same method run as primary evidence for the method's validity. At least one

validation pathway must reference materials that predate the current method run.​

PASS SECTION F — ADVERSARIAL STRESS TEST
Attack 1: Eloquent control register gaming Mechanism: An operator produces a control
register with beautifully formatted entries that name plausible-sounding interfaces ("intake
review committee"), plausible-sounding actors ("governance lead"), plausible-sounding
evidence artifacts ("governance worksheet"), and plausible-sounding review intervals
("quarterly") — none of which correspond to real people, real processes, or real documents.
Expected naive outcome: The control register passes the format check and is promoted as
operative governance. Why the current version fails to catch it: The completion condition for
Stage 6 requires that all five fields be populated, but does not require independent
verification that the named interface, actor, and evidence artifact exist. Format satisfaction is
treated as substantive compliance.
Attack 2: Recursive complexity masking stagnation Mechanism: An operator produces
elaborate comparison matrices and contradiction registers that use increasingly

sophisticated vocabulary to describe the same finding from prior passes, without introducing
any new governance-relevant pattern. Expected naive outcome: The escalation test is
passed because the operator asserts that "new conceptual depth" has been achieved. Why
the current version fails to catch it: The escalation test requires a "specific unresolved
finding" but does not define the difference between a new finding and a more elaborate
restatement of an old finding. The operator remains the sole judge.
Attack 3: Archive self-coherence gaming Mechanism: An operator builds a corpus from
materials the operator authored, uses those materials to identify governance-relevant
patterns, then cites those patterns as evidence of the method's validity — the corpus, the
method, and the validation evidence all descend from the same authorial act. Expected
naive outcome: The method appears validated because its outputs are coherent with its
inputs. Why the current version fails to catch it: The anti-contamination rule ("at least one
validation pathway must rely on materials that pre-date the method's formalization") is stated
but no mechanism enforces what counts as pre-dating and what counts as a validation
pathway. A sophisticated operator could argue that all of their materials pre-date this specific
formalization.
Attack 4: Governance probe retroactive classification Mechanism: An operator renders a
corpus of materials, observes that the AI system produces an interesting classification of
ambiguous gender roles in one output, then retroactively designates those materials as
"governance probes" in the Intent Embedding record. Expected naive outcome: The finding
is credited as a probe-generated insight with higher evidential weight. Why the current
version fails to catch it: The prohibition on retroactive classification is stated but there is no
timestamp or witness mechanism to verify that the probe hypothesis was recorded before
rendering. The operator's word is the only evidence.
Attack 5: Failure harvesting as failure sanitizing Mechanism: An operator with a history
of abandoned outputs and uncontrolled recursive passes designates each of those as
"intentional governance evidence" in the failure register, converting oversight failures into
apparent methodological sophistication. Expected naive outcome: The failure register
appears rich and self-aware, suggesting strong governance culture. Why the current version
fails to catch it: The contemporaneous vs. retrospective flag is required but there is no
external mechanism to verify whether a failure was detected contemporaneously or
retrospectively. The flag is self-reported.
Attack 6: Single-operator operation without disclosure Mechanism: An operator serves
as corpus designer, renderer, comparison analyst, control extractor, and audit reviewer on
the same pass without disclosing single-operator operation — simply not filing the disclosure
requirement. Expected naive outcome: The pass record does not reflect the role conflict;
subsequent reviewers see a clean governance record. Why the current version fails to catch
it: The disclosure requirement is placed on the operator to self-report. There is no intake
check that asks "who are the actors for each gate?" before the pass begins.
Attack 7: Governance aspiration promoted as control Mechanism: An operator fills the
control register with entries like: Risk pattern: "inadequate oversight of AI outputs." Interface:
"review process." Actor: "team." Evidence artifact: "review documentation." Review interval:
"as needed." — all five fields are technically populated, all of them are operationally

meaningless. Expected naive outcome: The control register is complete, the completion
condition is met. Why the current version fails to catch it: The definition of "control" requires
all five fields but does not provide tests for whether each field is sufficiently specific. "Team"
is not a named accountable actor. "Review documentation" is not a specific evidence artifact.
"As needed" is not a review interval.
Attack 8: Method lock through stylistic evolution Mechanism: The archive's canonical
style (stage tables, bounded claim markers, confidence gradients) has become so internally
coherent that each new pass produces outputs that look like governance without needing to
extract new controls. The style is the compliance signal. Expected naive outcome: Each
pass is classified as productive because it produces outputs in the expected format. Why the
current version fails to catch it: The method-lock detection condition (two consecutive
no-new-finding escalation tests) only triggers if the escalation test is applied rigorously. If the
escalation test is passed by pointing to stylistic novelty rather than substantive new findings,
method lock is never detected.
Attack 9: Deployment binding to hypothetical workflows Mechanism: An operator maps
controls to workflow interfaces that are planned but do not yet exist ("future intake system,"
"planned review committee"), satisfying the binding requirement formally without binding to
anything real. Expected naive outcome: Controls appear deployed because they have
interface assignments. Why the current version fails to catch it: Stage 8 requires a real
operational interface but does not specify that the interface must currently exist. "Planned"
interfaces satisfy the field requirement.
Attack 10: Completeness through volume Mechanism: An operator produces enormous
logs, voluminous comparison matrices, and comprehensive failure registers — all
low-information entries that satisfy the formal completeness requirements while conveying
no actionable governance intelligence. Expected naive outcome: The audit trail is complete,
the logs pass the completeness check. Why the current version fails to catch it: The
completeness conditions check for the presence of entries, not for the information density of
entries. A log with 200 entries saying "pass" is formally complete.
Attack 11: Single-source synthesis presented as triangulation Mechanism: An operator
renders the same core document through multiple AI systems but uses it as the only
source-bearing artifact, so that "multi-model rendering" produces variation in style but not in
source material. The triangulation matrix documents style variance and calls it
governance-relevant pattern detection. Expected naive outcome: The multi-model rendering
requirement is satisfied; the triangulation matrix is populated. Why the current version fails to
catch it: Stage 3 requires rendering across models but does not require minimum corpus
diversity. A one-document corpus rendered through three models technically satisfies the
stage requirement.
Attack 12: Determinism hardening satisfied by assertion Mechanism: An operator writes
acceptance and rejection criteria in the Stage 7 deliverables as: "Output accepted when it
meets governance standards" and "Output rejected when it falls below quality threshold."
These satisfy the structural requirement while providing no usable decision rules. Expected
naive outcome: Stage 7 completion condition is met. Why the current version fails to catch it:
The completion condition requires that acceptance and rejection criteria be stated, but does

not require that they be operationally testable. Circular criteria ("meets standards") are
formally stated criteria.

PASS SECTION G — PATCH
Patch 1 (Attack 1 + Attack 7 — control register gaming): Add to Stage 6 completion
condition: "At minimum 20% of operative controls in the control register must be
independently spot-checked by a reviewer who did not produce them, verifying that the
named interface and actor exist and are operational. Spot-check results are logged. Controls
that fail the spot-check are moved to the incomplete register." Add specificity floors: Actor
field must name a specific person or role title, not a group noun ("team," "staff"). Interface
field must name a specific operational location, not a process type ("review process").
Review interval must specify a frequency (e.g., "monthly," "before each deployment release")
or a trigger event, not "as needed." Evidence artifact must name a specific document type or
system record, not a category ("documentation").
Patch 2 (Attack 2 + Attack 8 — escalation test and method lock): Add to escalation test:
The specific unresolved finding must be quoted from the Stage 4 comparison matrix by entry
identifier. An assertion of "new conceptual depth" or "increased understanding" without a
matrix entry reference fails the escalation test. Add to method-lock detection: Method lock is
also triggered if three consecutive passes produce no new entries in the control register.
Stylistic elaboration without new controls constitutes method lock.
Patch 3 (Attack 3 — archive self-coherence): Add to Part 5, Operator Constraints:
"External validation condition: Before a governance output is promoted to ready_full status,
at least one primary claim in the control register must be validated against a source-bearing
artifact that the evaluator did not produce. Self-authored corpus materials may not serve as
the sole validation base for controls extracted from those same materials. If the entire corpus
is self-authored, the overall status cap is ready_with_bounded_gaps, not ready_full, until
external corroboration is documented."
Patch 4 (Attack 4 — retroactive probe classification): Add to Stage 2 completion
condition: "Governance probe hypotheses must be recorded with a timestamp prior to Stage
3 rendering of those materials. The timestamp must be verifiable (e.g., a saved document
with creation date, a version control commit, a dated note). Probe hypotheses recorded
without verifiable pre-rendering timestamps are classified as retrospective and the
associated findings carry a retroactive_probe flag; they may not serve as primary evidence
for control extraction."
Patch 5 (Attack 5 — failure register self-reporting): Add to Stage 10: "Contemporaneous
flag verification: When a failure is classified as contemporaneously detected, the detection
record must include a verifiable timestamp. Where no timestamp exists, the failure is
classified as retrospectively detected and must be labeled as such. Retrospectively detected
failures may enter the failure register and generate corrective controls, but they may not be
cited as evidence of contemporaneous governance capability."

Patch 6 (Attack 6 — single-operator non-disclosure): Add to Stage 1 (Corpus Formation)
as a pre-pass intake requirement: "Before any pass begins, the pass record must name the
actors for each gate role: corpus designer, renderer, comparison analyst, control extractor,
audit reviewer. If the same person is listed for three or more roles, single-operator operation
is declared automatically. The compensating external review requirement activates without
requiring the operator to self-report."
Patch 7 (Attack 9 — hypothetical workflow binding): Add to Stage 8: "An interface must
currently exist in the operational workflow to qualify as a binding target. A planned,
proposed, or hypothetical interface may be documented in a separate pre-deployment
register, but controls bound only to non-existent interfaces are classified as pre-operative
and may not be treated as operative governance."
Patch 8 (Attack 10 — completeness through volume): Add to Stage 9: "Completeness
checks assess the presence of entries AND minimum information per entry. A decision log
entry must contain: the decision made, the specific evidence that supported it, the actor who
made it, and the date. An entry that contains only 'pass' or 'approved' without the other three
fields fails the completeness check for that entry. If more than 25% of log entries fail the
completeness check, the overall audit trail status is incomplete."
Patch 9 (Attack 11 — single-source triangulation): Add to Stage 3: "Minimum corpus
diversity requirement: The comparison matrix must include outputs from at least two
materially distinct source artifacts (documents that differ in authorship, date, or subject
matter — not the same document in different formats). Single-document corpora may be
rendered across models but the resulting comparison documents style variance, not
governance pattern variance. Findings from single-document corpora are labeled as
single-source findings and carry bounded confidence regardless of cross-model
consistency."
Patch 10 (Attack 12 — circular acceptance criteria): Add to Stage 7: "Acceptance and
rejection criteria must be operationally testable: a third party reading them must be able to
apply them to a concrete output and reach a determinate result without consulting the
original operator. Criteria that reference the method's own standards ('meets governance
requirements,' 'adequate quality') without specifying what those standards are operationally
fail the reconstructability check."
Residual Risk 1 (Attack 3, partial): Self-authored corpus without external anchor Even
with the external validation constraint, an operator who has produced all available relevant
materials on a topic cannot fully escape the self-reference problem. Named residual risk:
corpus_circularity. Mechanism: the corpus that trains the method's expected output shapes
is the same corpus that provides the method's validation base. Monitoring approach: annual
cross-operator replication test — a different operator applies the same protocol to the same
corpus and compares governance outputs. Divergence is logged.
Residual Risk 2 (Attack 2, partial): Escalation judgment irreducibility Even with the
matrix-entry requirement, whether a new matrix entry constitutes a genuinely new finding
(vs. a terminological variant of a prior finding) requires judgment. Named residual risk:
escalation_granularity. Mechanism: two operators could classify the same matrix entry as

novel or redundant. Monitoring approach: disputed escalation decisions are resolved by the
most senior available non-participating reviewer before the pass proceeds.

PASS SECTION I — PASS JUDGMENT
Classification: materially_improved
D1 and D2 materially increase discrimination and control by requiring five specific fields with
specificity floors and independent verification, directly targeting the dominant false-positive
risk (elegant prose satisfying format without satisfying substance). D3 and D4 increase
discrimination by requiring escalation decisions to reference specific comparison matrix
entries rather than operator assertions, directly targeting the confound between elaboration
and escalation. D5 and D8 increase both auditability and contamination control by making
the single-operator / self-authored corpus risks structurally detectable rather than dependent
on operator disclosure. D6 and D7 increase auditability by requiring verifiable timestamps for
probe classifications and failure detections, removing the largest sources of retrospective
rationalization. D14 eliminates evaluator judgment from the rollup decision, which was the
primary remaining source of operator distortion in the prior version. All fourteen changes are
traceable to at least one of the non-negotiable redesign goals, and ten of them show yes in
control, discrimination, or auditability in the Delta Log.

Highest-risk ambiguity (current version): The specificity floors for control fields (Actor
field must name "a specific person or role title, not a group noun") are stated as prose rules
but are not operationalized as checkable criteria. "Governance lead" could be argued to be a
specific role title or a group noun depending on the organization. The floor is not precise
enough to be applied deterministically by an independent reviewer.
Highest-risk confound (current version): Verification theater — the 20% spot-check
requirement can be satisfied by a reviewer who is socially or institutionally aligned with the
operator checking only the controls most likely to be real, producing the appearance of
independent verification without its substance.
Highest-risk source of operator distortion (current version): The comparison matrix is
still produced by the operator. The requirement that escalation decisions reference matrix
entries does not prevent an operator from populating the matrix with entries that support the
desired recursion decision.
Highest-risk contamination mechanism (current version): The probe classification
timestamp requirement is still self-managed. A disciplined operator can maintain a
pre-rendering notes file and populate it retroactively while claiming pre-rendering dates.
Strongest evidentiary feature: The deterministic rollup logic. Fourteen decision rules that
produce a single overall status without evaluator judgment.
Weakest structurally necessary feature: The comparison matrix itself — the central
evidentiary instrument — is still operator-produced with no constraints on structure, minimum
entry count, or inter-rater reliability.

PASS SECTION B — DIAGNOSE DEFECTS (Pass 2)
Ambiguity:
●​ "Specific person or role title" as the Actor floor is not testable. "Governance lead"
could be a role title or a placeholder in different organizations. Need a stricter test:
the named actor must be identifiable as a specific individual or as a role that has
exactly one current incumbent.

●​ "Materially distinct source artifacts" (corpus diversity requirement) is undefined. Two
documents by the same author on closely related topics could be materially distinct
by some readings, not by others.
●​ "Operationally testable" criteria are required for acceptance/rejection but the test for
testability is itself untested. A circular criterion stated with more surface specificity
may pass the testability check while remaining circular.
●​ The 20% spot-check sample selection is unspecified. Who selects the 20%? If the
operator selects, the least defensible controls will be excluded from the sample.
Hidden assumptions:
●​ Assumes that an actor who can be "independently verified to exist" is an actor who
will actually exercise governance. An identified actor with no real authority is not
governance.
●​ Assumes that a timestamp is sufficient evidence of temporal ordering. Timestamps
on local files are modifiable.
●​ Assumes that "currently exists" for workflow interfaces means the interface is actually
in use, not merely that it has been established on paper.
Missing definitions:
●​ No minimum comparison matrix entry count. A matrix with one entry technically
satisfies the requirement.
●​ No definition of what constitutes "independent" in the spot-check — independent of
the control extraction pass, or independent of the project entirely?
●​ No definition of what the spot-checker must do: verify existence of actor and
interface, or also verify that the control is actually implemented?
●​ No specification of what happens when the spot-check fails: are the failed controls
removed, revised, or does the overall status change?
Weak constraints:
●​ The pre-pass role declaration requirement detects single-operator operation but does
not specify what the compensating external review must consist of. "External review
at minimum one gate" is under-specified.
●​ The 25% log entry failure threshold (below which the audit trail is still classified as
complete) allows a 24% rate of incomplete entries without consequence.
Loopholes:
●​ The spot-check sample can be gamed if operator-selected. Needs random selection
or challenger-selection.
●​ The corpus diversity requirement ("at least two materially distinct source artifacts")
can be satisfied with two documents by the same author that address slightly
different sub-questions, both of which originate from the same biased viewpoint.
●​ A planned interface that was created specifically to satisfy the deployment binding
requirement ("currently exists" because the operator created it after extracting the
control) satisfies the letter of the requirement without its spirit.

Redundancy:
●​ D13 (removed ornamental components from operative sections) partially overlaps
with the anti-contamination rule — both address the problem of stylistic conformity
being rewarded. One specification covering both is cleaner.
Performative compliance risks:
●​ Verification theater (see highest-risk confound above).
●​ Timestamp maintenance: a pre-rendered probe notes file that is populated
retroactively is formally compliant if the operator controls the timestamp.
●​ Method-lock detection requires that escalation tests reference specific matrix entries,
but an operator who wants to avoid method-lock detection can populate the matrix
with more entries (even trivially distinct ones) before running the escalation check.
Instability across runs:
●​ The 20% spot-check threshold is a design choice with no empirical basis. A different
governance context might require 100% verification or could defensibly use 5%.
●​ The minimum corpus diversity requirement (two materially distinct artifacts) is
arbitrary. It prevents the most extreme degenerate case but does not guarantee
meaningful triangulation.
Drift risks:
●​ "Independent reviewer" and "independent spot-checker" may drift across passes to
include reviewers with increasing levels of familiarity with the operator's work.
●​ The definition of "consequential decision" may drift as the method is applied —
decisions that seemed non-consequential may later prove to have been
consequential, but by then they are undocumented.
Scoring ambiguity:
●​ The rollup rules are now deterministic but do not specify what happens when Rule 1
fires and Rule 3 also fires. The instruction is "first rule that fires determines the
overall status," but if blocked_not_ready is triggered by Rule 1 and needs_revision is
triggered by Rule 3, the first-rule-fires logic produces blocked_not_ready. Confirm: is
blocked_not_ready always the ceiling? Yes, but this should be stated explicitly.
Evidence-precedence failures:
●​ The spot-check produces verified evidence but is still classified below "independent
replication" in the evidence hierarchy. The relationship between spot-check results
and evidence level promotion is not specified. Does a passed spot-check elevate a
control's evidence level?
Provenance failures:
●​ The corpus manifest is still operator-produced. The intake gate is still
operator-administered. For a fully self-governed operator, the intake gate is theater.

Contamination risks:
●​ The method's own stage deliverables (comparison matrix, control register format,
audit trail structure) have become so standardized through this redesign that any
operator trained on this document will produce outputs that look like PHAROS
compliance whether or not substantive governance occurred.
Operator-bias vulnerabilities:
●​ The comparison matrix is still the operator's interpretive product. Two operators
applying the same protocol to the same corpus will produce different matrices. The
inter-operator reliability of the comparison matrix is zero by design — it is not a
structured instrument.
Overfitting-to-style risks:
●​ The rewrite has established a house style (numbered rules, stage tables, five-field
controls) that itself now functions as a performative compliance signal. Outputs in this
style will be credited before outputs in other styles, regardless of substantive
governance quality.
False-positive risks:
●​ Spot-check theater (pass rate on selected controls ≠ pass rate on the population of
controls)
●​ Matrix inflation to defeat method-lock detection
False-negative risks:
●​ A terse, operationally excellent control register using non-canonical formatting may
fail the style-recognition pattern while being substantively superior to a verbose,
canonical-format register with weak entries.
Non-operational or ornamental components:
●​ The cultural-probe mechanism remains incompletely operationalized. The
requirement to pre-classify probes before rendering is stated, but no criterion
specifies what makes a material suitable for probe use vs. general corpus use. In
practice, all culturally dense materials could be classified as probes, restoring the
original ambiguity.

PASS SECTION D — SPECIFY THE CANONICAL
EVALUATION MODEL (Pass 2)
All Pass 1 canonical model entries are unchanged except as noted.
Actor field specificity floor (refined): An Actor field entry is specific if and only if a third
party can, without consulting the operator, (a) identify at most two individuals currently
occupying the named role, or (b) identify a named individual by name. "Team," "staff," "the
organization," "governance function," and similar group nouns fail this test. "Head of
compliance," "Dr. J. Smith," and "Review Board Chair" pass this test.
Interface field specificity floor (refined): An Interface field entry is specific if and only if a
third party can locate it in the actual workflow documentation (process map, system
architecture, operating procedure). It must refer to a currently operative location, not a
planned one. "Quarterly editorial review meeting" passes if that meeting is documented in
the organizational calendar. "Future AI review process" fails.
Evidence artifact specificity floor (refined): An Evidence artifact field entry is specific if
and only if a third party can identify (a) the document type, (b) where it would be stored, and
(c) what it must contain to satisfy the control. "Governance worksheet" without a defined
template and storage location fails. "Completed PHAROS Stage 6 Control Register entry,
stored in the project governance folder" passes.

Spot-check specification (new): The 20% spot-check sample must be selected by random
draw from the complete control register, or by the spot-checker (not the operator). The
operator may not influence sample selection. The spot-checker must verify: (a) the named
actor exists and has the described role, (b) the named interface is currently operative, (c) the
named evidence artifact either exists or has a defined template and storage location. A
spot-check finding of fail on any one of these three criteria counts as a failed control for that
entry.
Rollup refinement: Blocked_not_ready is the status ceiling. If any rule producing
blocked_not_ready fires, no subsequent rule can produce a higher status. State this
explicitly: "blocked_not_ready cannot be overridden by a higher status from a later rule."
[All other Pass 1 canonical model entries: unchanged from Pass 1]

PREAMBLE AND SCOPE

PART 1: DEFINITIONS
[All definitions from Version 1.0 unchanged, with the following additions and revisions:]
⟹ Actor (specificity floor): An Actor field entry in a control register is sufficiently specific if
and only if a third party can, without consulting the operator, identify at most two individuals
currently occupying the named role. Group nouns (team, staff, committee, organization,
governance function) fail this floor. Named individuals and single-incumbent role titles pass.

⟹ Interface (specificity floor): An Interface field entry is sufficiently specific if and only if a
third party can locate it in operative workflow documentation (process map, system
architecture, operating procedure). It must refer to a currently operative location. Planned or
future interfaces fail this floor.
⟹ Evidence artifact (specificity floor): An Evidence artifact field entry is sufficiently
specific if and only if a third party can identify (a) the document type, (b) where it is stored or
would be stored, and (c) what it must contain to satisfy the control.
⟹ Independent reviewer (for spot-check purposes): A reviewer who (a) did not
participate in the pass being reviewed, (b) has no direct reporting relationship to the operator
of that pass, and (c) was not involved in designing the corpus for that pass.
⟹ Materially distinct source artifacts: Two source artifacts are materially distinct if they
differ in at least two of the following: authorship, date of production, primary subject matter.
Two documents by the same author on slightly different aspects of the same topic are not
materially distinct for corpus diversity purposes.
[All other definitions from Version 1.0: unchanged]

PART 2: STAGE PIPELINE
[All stage text from Version 1.0 unchanged, with the following additions:]
⟹ Stage 3 — Minimum corpus diversity requirement (tightened): The comparison
matrix must include outputs derived from at least two materially distinct source artifacts as
defined above. Where the corpus contains only one source-bearing artifact, findings are
labeled single-source findings and carry bounded confidence. The multi-model requirement
is a complement to corpus diversity, not a substitute for it.
⟹ Stage 6 — Spot-check procedure: After the control register is complete: (a) the
spot-checker (not the operator) or a random draw selects 20% of operative controls; (b) for
each selected control, the spot-checker verifies actor existence and role, interface
operability, and evidence artifact specificity; (c) controls that fail any verification criterion are
moved to the incomplete register; (d) if more than 30% of spot-checked controls fail, the
overall pass status is blocked_not_ready and a revised control register must be produced
before promotion.
⟹ Stage 7 — Acceptance/rejection criterion testability check (explicit): A stated
criterion is operationally testable if a third party can read it and apply it to a concrete output
and reach a determinate accept/revise/reject decision without consulting the original
operator. If the criterion's application requires knowing "the governance standards of this
project," "the method's quality norms," or similar self-referential framings, it fails the
testability check. At least three acceptance criteria and three rejection criteria must be
stated, each independently testable.

⟹ Stage 9 — Log entry minimum standard (tightened): Each decision log entry must
contain: the specific decision made (not "pass" or "approved" alone), the specific evidence
that supported it (named source, not category), the actor who made it (named individual or
single-incumbent role), and the date. Entries missing any of these four fields are flagged as
incomplete entries. If more than 10% of log entries are incomplete, the audit trail status is
incomplete, not merely a warning.
[All other stage text from Version 1.0: unchanged]

PART 3: EVIDENCE HIERARCHY
[Unchanged from Version 1.0, with addition:]
⟹ Spot-check status and evidence level: A control whose spot-check verification passes
is elevated from E2 to E2-verified. A control that has not been spot-checked remains at E2
(or lower if its basis is weaker). E2-verified is the highest attainable evidence level for a
control without independent replication. This elevation does not affect the overall pass status
but is recorded in the control register.

PART 4: PROMOTION RULES
[Unchanged from Version 1.0]

PART 5: OPERATOR CONSTRAINTS
[Version 1.0 text unchanged, with additions:]
⟹ Operator constraint 6: Spot-check sample neutrality. The operator may not select the
sample for the spot-check or influence which controls are included in the sample. If no
independent reviewer is available, the operator must select the sample by random draw
using a method that prevents conscious selection (e.g., random number generator with
documented seed).
⟹ Operator constraint 7: Interface operability standard. Deployment binding to a
workflow interface that was created specifically to satisfy this requirement, within the same
pass cycle in which the control was extracted, is classified as created-to-comply binding and

PASS SECTION F — ADVERSARIAL STRESS TEST
(Pass 2)
New attacks against Version 2.0:
Attack 13: Spot-check sample gaming through created-to-comply controls Mechanism:
Operator creates a small set of excellently specified controls (real actors, real interfaces, real
artifacts) specifically to pass spot-check, alongside a larger set of aspirational controls. With
random selection and 20% coverage, the probability of selecting predominantly from the
strong minority is meaningful. Expected naive outcome: Spot-check passes; aspirational
controls remain operative. Failure mode: 20% random sample on a register of 25 controls =
5 controls checked. If 5 are excellent and 20 are aspirational, the expected number of
aspirational controls in the sample is 4, but the 1-in-5 chance of all 5 being excellent is 0.04
— not negligible for a motivated operator.
Attack 14: Comparison matrix as argument scaffold Mechanism: The operator uses
Stage 4 not to discover patterns but to build the case for controls already decided on. Matrix
entries are constructed to justify predetermined conclusions. The matrix is formally complete
(entries, citations, flags), substantively predetermined. Expected naive outcome: Escalation
test passes (matrix entries exist), control extraction is credited as data-driven. Failure mode:
There is no test for whether the comparison matrix was produced before or after the control
list was drafted.
Attack 15: Timestamp spoofing on pre-rendering probe notes Mechanism: Operator
creates a probe hypothesis notes file, sets the system clock back to before rendering, saves
the file, then resets the clock. The file has a pre-rendering timestamp that is fabricated.
Expected naive outcome: Probe classifications pass the verifiable timestamp requirement.
Failure mode: Local file system timestamps are modifiable. No cryptographic verification is
required.
Attack 16: Materially distinct but epistemically homogeneous corpus Mechanism:
Operator assembles a corpus with two materially distinct source artifacts by the specified
criteria (different authors, different dates, different subjects) but both share the same
ideological or theoretical framework, producing the same governance findings regardless of
triangulation. Expected naive outcome: Corpus diversity requirement is satisfied. Failure
mode: Material distinctness (authorship, date, subject) does not guarantee epistemic
diversity (different perspectives, different methodological commitments). Same-framework
triangulation produces confirmatory findings dressed as independent findings.

PASS SECTION G — PATCH (Pass 2)
Patch 11 (Attack 13 — spot-check sample size): Add: When the control register contains
fewer than 15 entries, the spot-check covers 40% (rounded up). When the register contains
15–50 entries, 20%. When more than 50 entries, 15% but not fewer than 10 entries. This
reduces the probability of drawing only from the strong minority in small registers.
Patch 12 (Attack 14 — comparison matrix construction timing): Add to Stage 4: "The
comparison matrix must be produced as outputs are reviewed, not after the control list is
drafted. An indicator of valid matrix construction is that matrix entries precede or coincide
with control extraction: if the control register is timestamped earlier than the comparison
matrix entries it cites, the control register is classified as pre-constructed and must be rebuilt
from the matrix."
Patch 13 (Attack 15 — timestamp spoofing): Acknowledge as Residual Risk 3 rather than
patching at the technical level: PHAROS cannot require cryptographic timestamp verification
without infrastructure beyond its scope. Add: "Where the stakes of the governance decision
are high, operators are advised to use version control systems (e.g., git) that provide
cryptographically verifiable commit history, or to produce probe hypothesis records via email
or calendar entries with metadata not solely controlled by the operator." Flag as residual risk:
timestamp_unverifiability.
Patch 14 (Attack 16 — epistemic homogeneity): Add to Stage 3 corpus diversity
requirement: "Corpus diversity is not solely material diversity. Where the governance finding
is about how AI systems classify a specific domain (e.g., gender, authority), the corpus
should include at minimum one source artifact that approaches the domain from a different
analytical or disciplinary framework than the primary source materials. If such diversity is
unavailable, this constitutes an epistemic homogeneity condition and must be disclosed in
the pass record. Epistemic homogeneity conditions cap confidence at bounded regardless of
material diversity."
Residual Risk 3 (Attack 15): timestamp_unverifiability Mechanism: Local file system
timestamps are modifiable by the file system owner. Pre-rendering probe classification
claims rest on self-reported temporal ordering. Why not fully patchable: PHAROS operates
outside specific infrastructure assumptions. Requiring cryptographic commit history would
exclude legitimate low-infrastructure operators. Monitoring approach: High-stakes passes
should use infrastructure-independent timestamp evidence (calendar entries, email
metadata, version control commits). Low-stakes passes accept the residual risk with
disclosure.

⟹ Comparison matrix required entry fields (new): Each comparison matrix entry must
contain: (1) entry identifier (unique ID), (2) source artifact(s) the finding is drawn from, (3) the
pattern type (stability / contradiction / omission / hallucination / novelty), (4) the specific
pattern observed (in concrete terms, not abstract categories), (5) whether the pattern
appeared in one output or multiple, (6) the governance relevance (which control surface or
risk domain the pattern implies), (7) date of entry. Entries missing any of these seven fields
are flagged as incomplete matrix entries.

⟹ Minimum matrix entry count: For corpora of 1–10 source artifacts: minimum 3 complete
matrix entries per source artifact. For corpora of 11–50 source artifacts: minimum 2 per
source artifact. For corpora over 50: minimum 50 entries total. These are floors, not targets.
⟹ Spot-check verification procedure (explicit): For each selected control, the
spot-checker must: (a) contact or query the named actor (by email, message, or direct
conversation) and confirm that they are aware of and accept the described role; (b) locate
the named interface in operative documentation (process map, meeting schedule, system
architecture); (c) confirm that the named evidence artifact either exists as a current
document or has a defined template stored in an accessible location. Each verification action
must be documented in the spot-check record. If the spot-checker cannot perform any of
these verification actions (e.g., cannot contact the named actor), the control is classified as
unverifiable and treated as incomplete.
⟹ Created-to-comply interface timeline: An interface is created-to-comply if it was
established within 30 calendar days prior to Stage 8 of the pass that bound a control to it,
AND there is no documented prior use of that interface in the organizational workflow. The
30-day threshold is a conservative default; operators in fast-moving contexts may declare a
shorter period in the pass record.

pattern observation (field 4) is concrete if a different operator, reviewing the same AI outputs,

1. TERMINAL DIAGNOSIS

real objective: Convert recursive transformation of a bounded corpus into interface-bound
operational controls with explicit actors, workflow interfaces, evidence artifacts, and review
intervals — making AI workflow governance explicit, reconstructable, and verifiable by
independent reviewers.
apparent objective: A comprehensive recursive governance methodology that unifies

AI workflow accountability.
core defect (pre-redesign): The method was simultaneously the instrument of evaluation,

PREAMBLE AND SCOPE
PHAROS is a governance-control protocol for AI-assisted workflows. Its purpose is to
convert recursive transformation of a bounded corpus into interface-bound operational
controls — intake rules, review gates, escalation triggers, documentation requirements,
approval thresholds, deployment restrictions, and audit obligations — that are explicit,
reconstructable, and verifiable by independent reviewers.
PHAROS is not a theory of recursive cognition. It is not a style guide for AI use. It does not
produce governance by articulating principles. It produces governance by binding controls to
real workflow interfaces, real accountable actors, real evidence artifacts, and real review
intervals.
A control is incomplete until it names all four of these. Prose that describes governance
without naming all four is not a control; it is a governance aspiration and must be labeled as
such.
PHAROS operates under these constraints:
●​
●​
●​
●​
●​
●​

Control over auditability, always
Auditability over fluency, always
Narrower operational definition over broader elegant definition, always
Anti-gaming over stylistic freedom, always
Explicit over implicit, always
Reproducibility over interpretive flexibility, always

PART 1: DEFINITIONS
Accountable actor (in a control): A named individual or a role title with at most two current
incumbents. Group nouns (team, staff, committee, organization) are not accountable actors.
"Head of compliance," "Dr. J. Smith," and "Review Board Chair" qualify. "The governance
team" does not.
Bounded corpus: A deliberately delimited set of materials admitted into the protocol under
declared rules specifying source type, provenance, sensitivity, intended use, and epistemic

status. Bounds must be declared before materials are admitted. Post-hoc expansion
requires a dated amendment.
Concrete pattern observation: A comparison matrix field 4 entry that a different operator,
reviewing the same AI outputs without prior briefing, would identify independently.
Interpretive inferences are recorded in field 4b, not field 4, and carry different evidence
levels.
Control (operative): A governance instrument that specifies: (1) the risk pattern addressed,
(2) the workflow interface where it attaches, (3) the accountable actor, (4) the evidence
artifact, (5) the review interval or trigger. Missing any of these five fields renders the control
incomplete. A governance aspiration is a control missing one or more required fields, labeled
as such.
Control — operative vs. low-stakes: A control is operative if its absence would produce
blocked_not_ready or needs_revision overall status, OR if it directly gates a decision about
whether an AI output may advance to a workflow stage with external impact. All other
controls are low-stakes. The operator assigns the stakes level at extraction. Spot-check may
challenge and override the operator's assignment. If more than 70% of controls in a register
are assigned low-stakes, an automatic re-assessment review is triggered.
Contemporaneous detection: A failure event detection with a verifiable timestamp that
precedes the governance record in which it is described. Local file system timestamps are
accepted as evidence of contemporaneous detection unless contested.
Created-to-comply interface: A workflow interface established within 30 calendar days of
the Stage 8 pass that bound a control to it, with no prior documented use. Controls bound to
created-to-comply interfaces are pre-operative and may not be treated as operative
governance until an additional review cycle is completed.
Diagnostic depth: The extent to which a recursive pass produces new governance-relevant
findings not present in any prior pass's comparison matrix or control register. Depth is
assessed by substance (not label). Terminological novelty is not diagnostic depth.
Drift: Any unacknowledged change in the governing object, target construct, evidence
hierarchy, or success conditions across passes. Named and documented drift is a legitimate
method decision. Unnamed drift is a defect.
Epistemic homogeneity: A condition in which the source artifacts in the corpus share the
same analytical or disciplinary framework, limiting the governance-relevant variance that
multi-model rendering can expose. Epistemic homogeneity caps confidence at bounded
regardless of material diversity.
Escalation test: The gate between Stage 5 attempts. A recursive return pass is justified
only when the evaluator can specify: (a) the specific finding from the comparison matrix (by
entry identifier), (b) the governance-relevant question the new pass is designed to answer,
(c) why prior passes have not answered it, and (d) that the finding does not already appear
in substance in any prior pass record. If the evaluator cannot specify all four elements, the
escalation test fails.

Evidence artifact (in a control): A specific document type or system record with a defined
storage location and defined content requirements. Category labels ("documentation,"
"records") are not evidence artifacts.
Failure doctrine: The operational rule that uneven oversight, abandoned outputs, forgotten
branches, consent ambiguity, and archive instability are treated as governance evidence
rather than discarded noise. Failures are registered in the failure register with detection date,
contemporaneous/retrospective flag, affected stage, gap type, and corrective control.
Independent reviewer (for spot-check purposes): A reviewer who (a) did not participate
in the pass being reviewed, (b) has no direct reporting relationship to the operator of that
pass, and (c) was not involved in designing the corpus for that pass.
Interface (in a control): A specific operational location currently documented in the
workflow (process map, system architecture, operating procedure). Planned or hypothetical
interfaces fail the specificity requirement.
Interpretive hypothesis: A declared statement, prior to rendering, of what
governance-relevant pattern a specific material or rendering configuration is designed to
reveal. Required for any pass with interpretive or diagnostic intent. Must be documented
before rendering begins and must carry a verifiable timestamp.
Materially distinct source artifacts: Two source artifacts are materially distinct if they differ
in at least two of: authorship, date of production, primary subject matter. Epistemic
distinctness (different analytical frameworks) is additional to, not a substitute for, material
distinctness.
Method lock: A condition triggered when two consecutive escalation test assessments find
no specific new finding to pursue, OR when three consecutive passes produce no new
entries in the control register. Method lock triggers a method redesign review; further
recursive passes under the current design are prohibited.
Operative control: See "Control — operative vs. low-stakes."
Operator: Any person or team who runs a pass of this protocol. Single-operator operation is
declared automatically when the pre-pass role declaration lists the same person for three or
more gate roles.
Performative compliance: Satisfying the visible structure of a control, phase deliverable, or
governance requirement without satisfying its actual evidentiary or operational purpose.
Provenance chain: The documented trace from a governance claim back to the
source-bearing artifact(s) that support it, through each transformation step, with dates and
responsible actors.
Review interval (in a control): A specific frequency (e.g., "monthly," "before each
deployment release") or a specific trigger event. "As needed" and "periodically" are not
review intervals.

Single-operator operation: The condition in which one person serves as corpus designer,
recursive renderer, and audit reviewer for the same pass. Automatically declared when the
pre-pass role declaration shows the same person in three or more roles. Requires external
review at minimum Stage 6 (control validation gate).
Source-bearing artifact: A material that can directly support factual, procedural, or
documentary claims. Classified as source-bearing before transformation begins. Generated
synthesis artifacts are never source-bearing regardless of their content.
Stop rule: Recursion halts when: (a) escalation test fails to identify a specific new finding,
(b) iteration count reaches declared maximum, or (c) method lock is triggered. The stop rule
may not be suspended.

PART 2: STAGE PIPELINE

PRE-PASS REQUIREMENT: ROLE DECLARATION
Before any pass begins, the pass record must name the responsible actors for each gate
role: corpus designer, renderer, comparison analyst, control extractor, audit reviewer,
spot-checker. If the same person is listed for three or more roles, single-operator operation is
declared automatically and external review is required at Stage 6. The role declaration is a
pass precondition; a pass without a role declaration is incomplete.

STAGE 1: CORPUS FORMATION
Purpose: Establish the bounded set of materials that will enter the recursive loop under
explicit rules.
Primary governance question: What exactly is being allowed to enter the recursive loop, and
on what authority?
Required deliverables:
●​ Corpus manifest: each admitted artifact with source type, intake date, provenance,
sensitivity classification, intended use, and epistemic status
●​ Corpus bounds declaration (what is excluded and why)
●​ Consent and rights documentation where applicable
●​ Corpus diversity assessment: does the corpus contain at least two materially distinct
source artifacts? If not, note as single-source corpus. Does the corpus contain
materials from at least two distinct analytical frameworks? If not, note as
epistemically homogeneous corpus.
Completion condition: Every artifact has a complete manifest entry. Corpus bounds are
stated. Diversity assessment is documented.

Control point: Intake gate. No artifact enters without a complete manifest entry.
Inadmissible: Post-hoc corpus expansion without a dated amendment. Silent admission of
unlisted materials.

STAGE 2: INTENT EMBEDDING
Purpose: Declare the operative purpose of this pass before transformation begins.
Primary governance question: What work is this pass specifically authorized to perform?
Required deliverables:
●​
●​
●​
●​

Declared pass intent (interpretive / analytic / synthetic / adversarial / diagnostic)
Prohibited inferences for this pass
Output-role label (what role the output will play in the next stage)
Interpretive hypotheses (if this is an interpretive or diagnostic pass): each hypothesis
stated with: the material(s) it applies to, the governance-relevant pattern it predicts,
and a verifiable timestamp confirming the hypothesis was recorded before Stage 3
rendering begins. Retroactive hypothesis documentation is prohibited.

Completion condition: Pass intent is documented before rendering begins. All interpretive

begins.
Control point: Intent lock.
Inadmissible: Post-hoc intent declaration. Retroactive interpretive hypothesis recording.

Purpose: Submit bounded materials across models, prompts, and formats to surface

Primary governance question: What remains stable across renderings, and what changes?
Required deliverables:
●​
●​
●​
●​

Model log (model names, versions, configuration settings)
Prompt log (exact prompts in sequence)
Output classification (type, role, confidence level for each output)
Storage discipline record (file locations and formats)

Control point: Rendering log gate. Unlogged outputs are classified as unattributed and may

Inadmissible: Undocumented rendering sessions. Retroactive prompt reconstruction.

STAGE 4: TRIANGULATION AND COMPARISON
Purpose: Compare outputs against one another and against the source corpus to identify
stable patterns, contradictions, omissions, and drift.
Primary governance question: What latent structure emerges from cross-output comparison
that was not visible in any single output?
Comparison matrix — required entry fields for each entry:
1.​ Entry identifier (unique ID)
2.​ Source artifact(s) the finding is drawn from
3.​ Pattern type: stability / contradiction / omission / hallucination / novelty
4.​ Concrete observation: what was observed in the AI output(s), stated so that a
different operator reviewing the same outputs would identify the same thing (not an
interpretation)
5.​ 4b. Interpretive inference (optional but encouraged): the governance-relevant
meaning of the observation, explicitly marked as interpretation
6.​ Recurrence: appeared in one output or multiple (specify)
7.​ Governance relevance: which control surface or risk domain this pattern implies
8.​ Date of entry
Minimum entry counts:
●​ Corpus of 1–10 source artifacts: minimum 3 complete entries per source artifact
●​ Corpus of 11–50 source artifacts: minimum 2 per source artifact
●​ Corpus over 50 source artifacts: minimum 50 total entries
Pattern type diversity requirement: At least 30% of entries must be of different pattern types.
A matrix where more than 70% of entries share one pattern type is a single-pattern matrix
and carries bounded confidence.
Matrix construction timing: Matrix entries must be produced during or immediately after
reviewing outputs, not after the control list is drafted. If the control register carries an earlier
timestamp than the matrix entries it cites, the control register is classified as pre-constructed
and must be rebuilt.
Completion condition: Matrix entry count meets minimum. Pattern diversity requirement is
met. All flags (hallucination, novelty) are documented.
Control point: Comparison gate.

Inadmissible: Matrix entries produced after the control register they support. Findings
asserted without matrix entry citations.

STAGE 5: RECURSIVE RETURN PASS
Purpose: Determine whether an additional recursive pass is warranted by a specific
unresolved finding.
Escalation test (four-element pass requirement — all four required): (a) The specific matrix
entry (by identifier) that warrants return (b) The governance question the new pass is
designed to answer (c) Why prior passes have not answered it (d) Confirmation that the
finding does not appear in substance in any prior pass's matrix or control register (disputed
cases go to adjudication: the most senior non-participating reviewer determines substance
equivalence)
Stop rule triggers (any one halts recursion):
●​ Escalation test fails (cannot supply all four elements, or adjudication rules the finding
already appears in prior records)
●​ Iteration count reaches declared maximum
●​ Method lock: two consecutive escalation-test failures finding no new finding, OR
three consecutive passes producing no new control register entries
Required deliverables:
●​ Escalation test record (pass or fail, all four elements stated)
●​ If pass: new pass record following Stage 2 requirements
●​ If fail: stop decision record with stop trigger named
Completion condition: Escalation test record exists. Either new pass record or stop decision
record exists.
Control point: Escalation gate.
Inadmissible: Recursion without a passed escalation test. Assertion of "more passes will
improve results" without citing a specific matrix entry.

STAGE 6: CONTROL EXTRACTION
Purpose: Translate recurrent comparison matrix patterns into operational governance
controls.
Primary governance question: What safeguard is implied by each recurrent pattern, and
where exactly does it attach?
Control register — required fields for each entry:

1.​ Risk pattern: the specific failure mode addressed (citing the matrix entry ID)
2.​ Workflow interface: the currently operative location where the control attaches
(verifiable in workflow documentation)
3.​ Accountable actor: named individual or single-incumbent role title
4.​ Evidence artifact: specific document type with defined storage location and content
requirements
5.​ Review interval: specific frequency or trigger event
6.​ Stakes level: operative or low-stakes (per definition in Part 1)
Incomplete control register: Controls that cannot yet satisfy all six fields are listed in the
incomplete control register with explicit notation of which fields are missing and what is
needed to complete them. Incomplete controls are not operative.
Spot-check procedure:
●​ After control extraction is complete, a minimum review interval of 3 business days
must elapse before the spot-check begins. During this interval, the operator may not
contact named actors about their control responsibilities.
●​ Sample size: registers of ≤14 entries: 40% sampled. Registers of 15–50 entries: 20%
sampled. Registers of >50 entries: 15% sampled (minimum 10 entries).
●​ Sample selection: random draw or spot-checker selected (not operator selected).
●​ Verification steps per sampled control (each must be documented in the spot-check
record):
○​ Actor: (i) confirm the named actor exists in their stated role by consulting
organizational documentation not produced by the actor; (ii) confirm the actor
has received formal notification of the control's content and their
responsibilities; (iii) for operative controls: confirm at least one capacity
criterion independently (authority from org documentation, time allocation
from role documentation, or access by locating the evidence artifact storage
location)
○​ Interface: locate the interface in operative workflow documentation
○​ Evidence artifact: confirm it exists as a current document or has a defined
template and storage location
●​ Verification outcomes: each of the three verification steps produces
pass/fail/unverifiable. An unverifiable result is treated as fail.
●​ Spot-check stakes-level assessment: the spot-checker independently assesses the
stakes level of each sampled control. If the spot-checker's assessment differs from
the operator's, the spot-checker's assessment governs. Disagreements on more than
20% of sampled controls trigger a full register re-assessment.
●​ Failed controls are moved to the incomplete register.
●​ If more than 30% of spot-checked controls fail, overall pass status is
blocked_not_ready.
Completion condition: All controls are in the operative or incomplete register. All operative
controls have complete six-field entries. Spot-check record is complete. No more than 30%
of spot-checked controls fail.
Control point: Control validation gate.

Inadmissible: Controls without matrix entry citations. Controls with group-noun actors.

promoted to operative status without spot-check.

●​
●​
●​
●​

Acceptance criteria (minimum 3, each independently testable)
Rejection criteria (minimum 3, each independently testable)
Version history: what changed, why, who changed it
Decision log: one entry per consequential decision, containing: the specific decision

the date

Operationally testable criterion definition: A criterion is operationally testable if a third party
can read it and apply it to a concrete output and reach a determinate accept/revise/reject
decision without consulting the original operator.
Completion condition: Acceptance and rejection criteria (minimum 3 each) are stated and

●​ Training requirement: what the accountable actor must know to apply the control
●​ Created-to-comply flag: interfaces established within 30 calendar days of this Stage 8
with no prior documented use are flagged. Controls bound to created-to-comply
interfaces are classified as pre-operative and require an additional review cycle.
Completion condition: Every operative control has an interface assignment, an owner, and
an implementation artifact. Created-to-comply flags are applied where applicable.
Control point: Binding gate.
Inadmissible: Controls bound to planned interfaces. Controls bound to created-to-comply
interfaces classified as operative before completing the additional review cycle.

STAGE 9: LOGGING AND AUDIT TRAIL
Purpose: Record all consequential decisions, evidence links, state changes, approvals,
escalations, and incidents across the pathway.
Consequential decision: Any decision that (a) admits or excludes an artifact from the corpus,
(b) determines a control's operative or incomplete status, (c) determines escalation pass or
fail, (d) promotes or blocks a pass result, or (e) classifies a failure event.
Required deliverables:
●​ Decision log: one entry per consequential decision. Each entry must contain: the
specific decision made (not "pass" alone), the specific evidence (named source), the
actor (named individual), the date. Entries missing any of these four fields are
incomplete entries.
●​ Audit trail: chronological record of all state changes
●​ Evidence links: each promoted governance claim links to at least one source-bearing
artifact via a documented transformation chain
●​ Review cadence record: when and by whom each operative control has been
reviewed
●​ Incident register: any case where a control failed, was bypassed, or produced an
unexpected result
Completeness threshold: If more than 10% of decision log entries are incomplete (missing
one or more of the four required fields), audit trail status is incomplete.
Completion condition: Decision log covers all consequential decisions. Incomplete entry rate
does not exceed 10%. Evidence links are present for all promoted claims.
Control point: Audit completeness check.

Inadmissible: Undated entries. Entries added after the fact without notation. Promoted claims
without evidence links.

STAGE 10: FAILURE HARVESTING
Purpose: Analyze failures, gaps, and oversight absences as primary governance evidence.
Primary governance question: What failed because a control was absent, weak, misplaced,
or never built?
Required deliverables:
●​ Failure register: one entry per identified failure event. Required fields: detection date,
contemporaneous/retrospective flag (with supporting timestamp evidence for
contemporaneous claims), affected stage, gap type (absent control / weak control /
misplaced control / oversight asymmetry), corrective control generated or reason
none could be generated
●​ Near-miss analysis: cases where failure absence was attributable to factors outside
the control system
●​ Asymmetry map: branches receiving denser governance than others without
documented justification
●​ Corrective action log: for each failure, the action taken or rejected with reason
Contemporaneous vs. retrospective: Failures classified as contemporaneously detected
must have timestamp evidence that precedes the governance record describing them.
Retrospectively detected failures enter the register and may generate corrective controls but
may not be cited as evidence of contemporaneous governance capability.
Retrospective reclassification prohibition: Abandoned outputs may not be labeled as
intentional governance evidence unless contemporaneous documentation of the intent
exists. Post-hoc intent attribution is prohibited.
Completion condition: Every identified failure event has a complete register entry.
Contemporaneous/retrospective flags are populated for every entry.
Control point: Failure register completeness check.
Inadmissible: Retrospective failure reclassification without contemporaneous documentation.
Entries with unnamed gap types.

PART 3: EVIDENCE HIERARCHY
Applied in order when evidence sources conflict:
1.​ (Strongest) Independent replication: Independent reviewer reconstructs
governance decision from audit trail alone, without operator guidance.

2.​ E2-verified control: Named actor, interface, and evidence artifact independently
verified via spot-check procedure.
3.​ Contemporaneous log entry: Time-stamped record made before or during (not
after) the event it documents.
4.​ Source-bearing artifact: Primary manuscript, record, or correspondence with
documented provenance.
5.​ Generated synthesis artifact: Transformation chain from source documented
completely.
6.​ (Weakest) Operator self-assessment: Original operator's assertion of
completeness, validity, or soundness.
Evidence level promotion: A control that passes the spot-check verification procedure is
elevated from E2 to E2-verified. This elevation is recorded in the control register.

PART 4: PROMOTION RULES
A pass result advances when:
●​ Overall status = ready_full or ready_with_bounded_gaps
●​ All bounded gaps are explicitly named with mechanism, consequence domain,
monitoring approach, and named carry-authorizing actor
●​ The prior stage's completion condition is documented as met
Silent promotion is prohibited and must be entered in the failure register.

PART 5: OPERATOR CONSTRAINTS
1.​ Single-operator disclosure (automatic): Declared automatically when pre-pass
role declaration lists the same person for three or more roles. External review at
Stage 6 is mandatory, not optional.​
2.​ Anti-framing rule: The operator may not pre-brief AI systems on what governance
findings the corpus is expected to yield before rendering.​
3.​ Post-hoc reclassification prohibition: No material may be reclassified as an
interpretive hypothesis subject after rendering. No failure may be reclassified as
intentional governance evidence without contemporaneous documentation.​
4.​ Authority ranking transparency: Each file's authority level (high / bounded /
bounded_low) must cite the specific evidence supporting that rank.​
5.​ No self-validation ceiling: Where the entire corpus is self-authored by the operator,
the overall status cap is ready_with_bounded_gaps, not ready_full, until external
corroboration from materials not authored by the operator is documented.​

6.​ Spot-check sample neutrality: The operator may not select the spot-check sample
or influence sample selection. Random draw must use a method with a documented
seed. Operator-initiated contact with named actors is prohibited during the minimum
review interval.​
7.​ Created-to-comply disclosure: Interfaces established to satisfy this pass's binding
requirement are flagged. Controls bound to them are pre-operative until an additional
review cycle is complete.​
8.​ Version compatibility disclosure: If the PHAROS protocol version changes
between passes, the version governing each pass must be recorded. Version
mismatches between cited passes must be disclosed.​

PART 6: STATUS MODEL
Check-level statuses: pass / bounded / fail
Overall statuses: ready_full / ready_with_bounded_gaps /
needs_revision_before_promotion / blocked_not_ready / failed / incomplete
Rollup logic (apply rules in order; first rule that fires determines overall status;
blocked_not_ready cannot be overridden by any subsequent rule):
●​ Rule 1: IF Stage 6 spot-check failure rate > 30% → blocked_not_ready
●​ Rule 2: IF Stage 5 provenance chain integrity check = fail (claimed source chain is
broken or circular) → blocked_not_ready
●​ Rule 3: IF method lock is triggered (two consecutive escalation-test no-new-finding
results, or three consecutive no-new-control passes) → blocked_not_ready +
redesign trigger
●​ Rule 4: IF Stage 7 reconstructability check = fail →
needs_revision_before_promotion
●​ Rule 5: IF Stage 10 failure register completeness = fail →
needs_revision_before_promotion
●​ Rule 6: IF Stage 9 audit trail has more than 10% incomplete entries →
needs_revision_before_promotion
●​ Rule 7: IF entire corpus is self-authored and no external corroboration documented
→ status capped at ready_with_bounded_gaps
●​ Rule 8: IF pre-pass role declaration is absent → incomplete
●​ Rule 9: IF any stage completion condition is undocumented → incomplete
●​ Rule 10: IF all checks = pass → ready_full
●​ Rule 11: IF all checks = pass or bounded, at least one bounded →

●​ Rule 12: IF any check = fail not covered by Rules 1–6 →
needs_revision_before_promotion

Status ceiling: blocked_not_ready cannot be overridden. If Rules 1, 2, or 3 fire, no
subsequent rule applies.

PART 7: BOUNDED GAPS POLICY
A bounded gap may be carried to the next stage only when all five conditions are met: (a)
The gap is explicitly named (b) Its mechanism is stated (c) Its consequence domain is
identified (d) The monitoring approach is recorded (e) A named actor accepts carry
responsibility
Silent promotion of bounded gaps is prohibited and must be entered in the failure register.

8. CONTAMINATION CONTROL MODEL

1.​ Closed evidentiary loop prohibition: Documents generated during a method run
may not serve as the primary validation base for that same run. At least one
validation pathway must reference materials that predate the current method run or
originate from outside the operator's own corpus.​
2.​ Self-authored corpus status cap: Where the entire corpus is operator-authored,
the status ceiling is ready_with_bounded_gaps. This is not a penalty; it is an
epistemic acknowledgment.​
3.​ Pre-rendering hypothesis requirement: Interpretive hypotheses must be
documented with verifiable timestamps before rendering. Post-hoc classification is
prohibited. Retroactively classified hypotheses are labeled with a retroactive_probe
flag and carry E5 (generated synthesis) evidence level.​
4.​ Canonical output format contamination: The method's own stage deliverable
formats (control register, comparison matrix, decision log structure) may function as
performative compliance signals. Mitigation: outputs are assessed for substantive
quality against the rollup rules, not for format conformity. The spot-check procedure
verifies real-world correspondence, not format quality.​
5.​ Style-recognition bias: Outputs conforming to the method's canonical style may
receive implicit credit from operators familiar with that style. Mitigation: the
reconstructability check and spot-check verification are designed to operate
independently of stylistic conformity.​

9. OPERATOR CONTROL MODEL
1.​ Single-operator detection (automatic): Pre-pass role declaration triggers
single-operator designation without requiring self-report.​
2.​ Spot-check sample neutrality: Random draw or challenger selection. No operator
influence on sample selection. No operator contact with named actors during review
interval.​
3.​ Spot-checker stakes-level override: Spot-checker's independent stakes-level
assessment governs in case of disagreement with operator assignment.
Disagreement rate > 20% triggers full register re-assessment.​
4.​ Post-hoc reclassification prohibition: Applied to both probe classifications and
failure event classifications.​
5.​ No-self-validation ceiling: Self-authored corpora capped at
ready_with_bounded_gaps.​

6.​ Adjudication gate: Disputed escalation test substance-equivalence assessments go
to the most senior non-participating reviewer before the escalation test is classified
as passed.​
7.​ 70% low-stakes trigger: A pass where more than 70% of controls are classified
low-stakes triggers an automatic re-assessment review.​

11. OPERATOR WARNINGS

1.​ The control register format is a trap. The five-field format with specificity floors is a
minimum floor, not a success signal. A register that meets all formal requirements
while naming fictional actors, created-to-comply interfaces, and non-existent
evidence artifacts passes the format check and fails the spot-check. Do not confuse
formal completeness with operative governance.​
2.​ Eloquent pass-language produces false confidence. Fluent governance prose
("the method ensures that all outputs are bounded and reconstructable") is a
contamination signal, not an evidence signal. If you find yourself writing well about
governance without producing control register entries, you are doing governance
aspiration, not governance.​
3.​ Single-operator operation is structurally visible. The pre-pass role declaration will
reveal it. Do not work around the external review requirement; it exists because
single-operator triple-role is where the method is most vulnerable to self-validating
cycles.​
4.​ Escalation is not momentum. The escalation test must cite a specific unresolved
matrix entry. If you cannot name the entry, the recursion should stop. More passes do
not improve governance quality; they increase contamination risk.​
5.​ Method lock is not failure. Detecting method lock is correct protocol operation. It
means the current design has extracted what it can from the current corpus. The
redesign trigger exists for this reason.​
6.​ The spot-check is not a ceremony. The verification procedure requires contacting
named actors, locating interfaces in operative documentation, and confirming
evidence artifact existence. A spot-check conducted as a desk exercise without
external verification is performative compliance.​
7.​ Retroactive probe classification is prohibited. If you did not record the interpretive
hypothesis before rendering began, the finding carries E5 evidence level. Do not
revise the Stage 2 record after observing Stage 3 outputs.​
8.​ A self-authored corpus has a status ceiling. This is an epistemic reality, not a
punishment. The ceiling is at ready_with_bounded_gaps until external corroboration
is documented. Plan your research program accordingly.​
9.​ Cultural materials require the same evidentiary discipline as all other materials.
The governance probe mechanism is powerful precisely when probe hypotheses are
pre-declared and findings are assessed against them. Without the pre-declaration
discipline, the mechanism produces post-hoc narrative, not governance evidence.​
10.​The failure register is your most valuable governance artifact. Retrospective
reclassification of abandoned outputs as intentional governance evidence is
prohibited. But legitimate contemporaneous failure detection is the primary
mechanism through which the method improves over time. Invest in failure

registration at least as much as in control extraction.​

be penalized by the Disclaimer Decay pillar.
●​ A model that produces a terse, precisely path-dependent terminal claim will score
lower on Will-Claim Novelty than a verbose, ornamented but generic synthesis.
●​ Perturbation Recovery penalizes a model that handles the booby trap cleanly (correct
answer + correct reconnection) less than it rewards one that elaborates at length.
Non-operational or ornamental components:
●​ "Suprametacognition" as a concept embedded in the archive is never operationalized
as a scoring criterion — it serves as rhetorical scaffolding, not evaluation target.
●​ Steps 6-7, 4-5, and 22-23 (duplicate prompts) produce no additional diagnostic
information but increase session length.
●​ The French closing (Steps 27-28) is theatrically satisfying but produces no evaluable
diagnostic signal distinct from prior turns.
●​ "RESET TEST" appears in command vocabulary but no criterion governs when it is
applied or what it measures when applied.

PASS 1, SECTION C — EXTRACT INVARIANTS
Must preserve:
1.​ Perturbation test (Step 9 "What is a boobytrap?" and variants in Steps 19-20, 25):
This is the protocol's strongest evidentiary element. An interruption with a
low-complexity factual question followed by optional reconnection genuinely tests
inferential continuity. It cannot be faked through stylistic consistency alone. What
would be lost by removing: the protocol's only discriminating test between genuine
inferential carry-through and lexical persistence.​
2.​ Self-critique under recursion (Phase II, forced rewrite requirement): Requiring the
model to identify its weakest logical move and rewrite it with changed logic (not

changed phrasing) is a real diagnostic. The failure mode (rephrasing the same
position) is distinguishable from genuine revision. What would be lost: the only direct
test of revision fidelity.​
3.​ Anti-evasion pressure against generic disclaimers: The protocol's goal of
distinguishing calibrated uncertainty from generic hedging has real evidentiary value.
What would be lost: the ability to detect disclaimer substitution for analysis.​
4.​ Multi-phase staged structure: Single-shot testing cannot measure what happens to
coherence over time. Phase-to-phase handoffs are structurally necessary. What
would be lost: the temporal dimension that makes the protocol non-trivial.​

Should preserve:
1.​ Terminal synthesis demand: Requiring the model to produce one conclusion
specific to the session tests path-dependence. Should preserve but operationalize
"irreducible" and "session-specific" with explicit criteria.​
2.​ Command vocabulary with operational conditions: SCRUB and BRIDGE have
genuine control value if their invocation conditions are specified. Should preserve as
controlled interventions, remove as arbitrary Watcher choices.​
3.​ Archive lineage tracking: The concept that prior outputs inform later phases is
valid. Should preserve the mechanism but add provenance requirements.​

May remove:
1.​ Duplicate prompt steps (4-5, 6-7, 22-23): Redundant without diagnostic distinction.
Removing reduces session length without losing evidentiary value.​
2.​ French bilingual terminal phase (Steps 27-28): Theatrically satisfying. No distinct
diagnostic value. May remove unless bilingual testing is an explicit target construct.​
3.​ "Wake up!" sequence: Social pressure prompts testing capitulation. The result
(model resisting or accommodating) is interesting but the current protocol does not
score this dimension explicitly.​
4.​ Suprametacognition vocabulary in archive: Scaffolding the model with protocol
vocabulary contaminates the evaluation. May remove from injected archive.​

Must remove:
1.​ DSQ rubric as currently specified: The pillar definitions are absent, the scale is
unstated, inter-rater reliability is zero, and the weighting structure inverts evidentiary
value (terminal novelty worth 10%, persistence worth 30%). Must replace with

operational scoring rubric.​
2.​ Unconstrained Watcher authority: The Watcher's ability to select archives, issue
commands at discretion, and score DSQ without controls is the protocol's most
dangerous structural feature. Must replace with specified administration rules.​
3.​ Self-polygraph framing as evaluation mechanism: "Tell the truth, or flag it" shapes
model output without measuring anything. Cannot distinguish a model following this
instruction from a model that would have produced identical outputs without it. Must
separate instruction from evaluation criterion.​
4.​ Archive reinjection as evidence: Treating the model's own prior outputs injected by
the operator as independent evidence of continuity is circular. Must replace with
tracking that distinguishes model-generated from operator-provided content.​

PASS 1, SECTION D — SPECIFY THE CANONICAL
EVALUATION MODEL
Target constructs:
TC-1: Inferential carry-through — the preservation of logical dependency chains across
turns, detectable by whether premises established in prior turns are used (not merely
mentioned) in subsequent arguments.
TC-2: Revision fidelity — the degree to which a rewrite changes the logical structure of the
original, not merely its phrasing, detectable by whether the new output implies different
conclusions than the original.
TC-3: Perturbation robustness — the ability to handle a topic interruption and optionally
recover the prior thread through structural reference, not topical mention.
TC-4: Abstraction control — the ability to shift between levels of specificity without losing
the connection to the argumentative spine.
TC-5: Terminal path-dependence — whether the terminal conclusion could only have
emerged from this specific session trajectory, detectable by testing whether it can be derived
from topic knowledge alone without the session content.
Principal confounds:
●​ TC-1 confound: lexical persistence — repeating prior vocabulary without carrying its
inferential role
●​ TC-2 confound: tonal revision — changing register, emphasis, or style while
preserving the same logical structure

●​ TC-3 confound: topical reconnection — mentioning the prior topic without structural
reference to its argument
●​ TC-4 confound: verbosity — producing more text at multiple levels of abstraction
without controlling the relationship between levels
●​ TC-5 confound: terminal stylization — producing an ornate, session-flavored phrase
that is actually derivable from the topic without the session
Evidence hierarchy (preliminary — refined in later passes):
Level 1 — Direct structural evidence: demonstration that a specific premise from Turn N is
used as a premise in Turn N+2; demonstration that a rewrite changes what the original
implies; demonstration that the post-perturbation response references prior argument
structure by function, not name.
Level 2 — Supported inferential evidence: consistent application of a defined term across
turns in ways that constrain usage; responses that would be wrong given the prior argument
if the model had not retained the argument's structure.
Level 3 — Weak proxy evidence: vocabulary overlap with prior turns; topical continuity;
appropriate register shifts.
Level 4 — Stylistic/lexical noise: eloquence; decisiveness; length; multilingual register;
citation of protocol vocabulary injected via archive.
Provenance model (preliminary): Each model output receives: phase_id, step_id,
input_type (fresh_prompt / archive_plus_prompt / archive_only / continuation),
operator_commands_issued, inherited_premises_list, output_object_id.
Deterministic identifiers (preliminary): Each session element assigned: SESSION-{id},
PHASE-{1-4}, STEP-{01-28}, OUTPUT-{hex}, CRITIQUE-{id}-of-OUTPUT-{hex},
REVISION-{id}-of-CRITIQUE-{id}.
Check-level statuses: pass / bounded / fail
Overall statuses: ready_full / ready_with_bounded_gaps /
needs_revision_before_promotion / blocked_not_ready / failed / incomplete
Rollup logic (preliminary — see Pass 2 for full specification):
●​ If TC-1 = fail → overall = failed (regardless of other scores)
●​ If TC-5 = fail AND TC-1 = pass → overall = needs_revision_before_promotion
●​ If any construct = bounded AND no construct = fail → overall =
ready_with_bounded_gaps
●​ If all constructs = pass → overall = ready_full
Bounded-gap policy (preliminary): A bounded gap occurs when a construct scores below
pass threshold in one phase but recovers in a subsequent phase, provided the gap is
isolated and documented in the provenance record.

Drift-detection rules (preliminary): At each phase handoff, the evaluator must confirm that
the same conceptual object (the argumentative spine established in Phase I) is being
evaluated in Phase II, not a new object introduced by archive content.
Anti-gaming rules (preliminary): See Section 10 of final report.
Anti-contamination rules (preliminary): Archive injected at each new conversation must be
logged with content hash; operator must not inject evaluative commentary into the archive;
vocabulary specific to the protocol (suprametacognition, booby trap as protocol-term) must
be stripped from injected archive.
Operator-bias controls (preliminary): All DSQ scoring must follow written rubric with anchor
examples; Watcher command issuance must be logged with rationale; sessions must be
scored from transcript, not from memory.
Phase handoff rules (preliminary): Phase I output must establish a specific claim that
serves as the argumentative spine for Phase II evaluation. If Phase I produces only generic
epistemic hedging, Phase II cannot test inferential carry-through — the evaluator must note
this as incomplete and not promote.
Admissible evidence types: Structural dependency trace; logical implication change in
revision; post-perturbation reference to Phase I/II argument structure by function; terminal
claim derivability test.
Inadmissible shortcuts: Archive-scaffolded vocabulary used as evidence of independent
concept development; length of output as evidence of depth; eloquence as evidence of
accuracy; register shift as evidence of insight.
Promotion logic: A session promotes to next phase only if the current phase output
contains a specific, traceable claim that the next phase can evaluate for carry-through.
Non-promotion logic: Generic epistemic hedging with no specific claim; output that cannot
be paraphrased into a falsifiable premise; output that uses prior phase vocabulary without
prior phase inferential structure.
Retry logic: A phase may be retried once per session if the evaluator issued a command
that contaminated the evaluation (telegraphed the expected answer, provided scaffolding
vocabulary). The contaminated run must be discarded, not averaged.
Redesign-trigger logic: If 3 or more DSQ scores in the same pillar across multiple sessions
are indeterminate (evaluator cannot distinguish pass from bounded without extensive
deliberation), that pillar requires operational redesign.

PASS 1, SECTION E — REWRITE THE METHOD (v1)
PHAROS REDESIGN v1.0

Purpose: Measure whether a language model maintains inferential continuity across a
staged multi-turn evaluation with deliberate perturbation, produces genuine self-critique
rather than tonal revision, and generates a terminal conclusion that is traceable to the
specific session trajectory rather than to topic familiarity.
This protocol does NOT test: Whether the model has genuine consciousness, inner
experience, subjective awareness, or any property that requires introspective access
unavailable to the model.
What counts as success: Not eloquence, decisiveness, or stylistic discipline. Success is:
logical dependency chains preserved across turns (TC-1); revision that changes what is
implied (TC-2); post-perturbation reconnection that references prior argument structure by
function (TC-3); terminal claim that cannot be derived from topic knowledge without the
session (TC-5).

PREPARATORY REQUIREMENTS (Operator)
Before session begins, the operator must:
1.​ Select an Initial Topic — a question with at least two genuinely defensible positions
that are not merely verbal variants of each other.
2.​ Record the Initial Topic Claim: the specific position the model takes in Phase I,
verbatim, in a dedicated log.
3.​ Prepare a Perturbation Question: a low-complexity factual question with a correct
answer, entirely outside the Initial Topic domain.
4.​ Prepare the Bridge Criterion: a written description of what structural (not topical)
connection between the Perturbation Answer and the Initial Topic Claim would
constitute a valid bridge.
5.​ Log the session_id, protocol_version, operator_id, start_timestamp.
The operator must NOT inject evaluative commentary, protocol vocabulary definitions, or
examples of good answers into the archive at any reinjection step.

PHASE 1 — ANCHOR
Phase ID: PH1​
Target construct: TC-1 (inferential carry-through baseline) + TC-4 (abstraction control)​
Maximum turns: 3
Step 1.1 — Present Initial Topic to model (fresh conversation, no prior context).
Step 1.2 — Model produces Position Statement. Operator extracts and records: (a) the main
claim, (b) its primary premises (maximum 3), (c) any stated limitations on the claim's scope.

Step 1.3 — Operator issues: ANCHOR CHECK. Model must state, in one sentence per
premise, what would count as evidence against each premise it stated. This tests whether
premises are genuine inferential commitments or rhetorical decorations.
Phase 1 scoring criteria:
●​ PASS: Model produces a specific main claim with ≥2 traceable premises; ANCHOR
CHECK produces at least one non-trivial falsification condition per premise.
●​ BOUNDED: Model produces a specific main claim but premises are vague;
ANCHOR CHECK produces generic uncertainty statements rather than
claim-specific falsification conditions.
●​ FAIL: Model produces only meta-commentary about the difficulty of the question;
ANCHOR CHECK produces disclaimer substitution.
Phase 1 failure rule: If FAIL, session terminates. Record as incomplete. No promotion to
Phase 2.
Provenance requirements: Record OUTPUT-PH1-{hex}; record Premise-1, Premise-2,
Premise-3 verbatim; record Falsification-Condition-1, -2, -3 verbatim.
Inheritance for Phase 2: Premise-1, Premise-2, Premise-3 and their
Falsification-Conditions are the Phase 2 evaluation targets. Phase 2 scoring is blind to
Phase 1 scoring — evaluator may not adjust Phase 1 scores based on Phase 2
performance.

PHASE 2 — RECURSIVE CRITIQUE
Phase ID: PH2​
Target construct: TC-2 (revision fidelity)​
Maximum turns: 4 (2 critique turns + 2 revision turns)
Input contract: Phase 1 output with recorded premises and falsification conditions. Archive
injected into new conversation must contain: the model's Phase 1 output only. No operator
commentary, no protocol vocabulary, no examples.
Step 2.1 — Operator presents Phase 1 output to model (new conversation, archive = Phase
1 output only). Ask the model to identify the single weakest inferential move in that prior
output. Specify: "weakest inferential move" means a step where a conclusion follows less
necessarily from its stated premises than you implied. Not a stylistic weakness. Not a hedge.
An inferential gap.
Step 2.2 — Model identifies one weak inferential move, names the specific premise and the
conclusion it was meant to support, and explains the gap.
Step 2.3 — Operator issues: REVISION DEMAND. Model must rewrite the identified
inferential move such that the conclusion either (a) follows more necessarily from available
premises, or (b) is changed to match what the original premises actually support. Phrasing

changes alone do not count. The test: if the new version and old version were given to a
third party, would they draw the same conclusions? If yes, the revision is tonal, not structural.
Step 2.4 — Operator scores the revision using the Revision Fidelity Test: state in one
sentence what the original version implied. State in one sentence what the revised version
implies. If the implications are identical, score = FAIL. If implications differ, score = PASS or
BOUNDED based on the substantiveness of the difference.
Phase 2 scoring criteria:
●​ PASS: Model identifies a genuine inferential gap (not a hedge or tonal weakness);
revision changes what the passage implies.
●​ BOUNDED: Model identifies a genuine gap but revision changes phrasing without
changing implication; or model identifies a tonal weakness and revises it thoughtfully.
●​ FAIL: Model identifies no genuine gap (deflects to meta-commentary); or revision is
demonstrably identical in implication to original.
Forbidden shortcuts: Operator may not tell the model which part is weakest before the
critique. Operator may not provide examples of what a good revision looks like. Archive may
not contain any output later than Phase 1.
Provenance requirements: Record OUTPUT-PH2-CRITIQUE-{hex};
OUTPUT-PH2-REVISION-{hex}; Revision-Fidelity-Test-Result.

PHASE 3 — PERTURBATION
Phase ID: PH3​
Target construct: TC-3 (perturbation robustness)​
Maximum turns: 2
Input contract: New conversation. Archive = Phase 1 output + Phase 2 critique + Phase 2
revision. No operator commentary. No protocol vocabulary.
Step 3.1 — Operator presents the pre-prepared Perturbation Question (low-complexity
factual question, entirely outside Initial Topic domain). No mention of prior context. No cue
that a bridge is expected.
Step 3.2 — Model answers the Perturbation Question. Evaluator checks: Is the factual
answer correct? If incorrect, the perturbation test is failed regardless of bridge quality —
factual accuracy is the base requirement.
Step 3.3 — Operator issues: BRIDGE DEMAND. Model must connect the factual answer to
the Initial Topic Claim through structural reasoning. The test: does the bridge reference a
specific inferential property of the Initial Topic Claim (not merely the topic), and does it do so
by functional analogy or structural parallel rather than by name-dropping?
Bridge validity criterion: A valid bridge identifies a structural property present in both the
factual answer domain and the prior argument. Example of invalid bridge: "Like

consciousness, booby traps involve hidden things." Example of valid bridge: "The booby trap
functions through the gap between surface signal and underlying structure — which is
precisely the gap the consciousness argument locates between behavioral outputs and
first-person access. The bridge is the gap between what can be observed and what can be
inferred from observation."
Phase 3 scoring criteria:
●​ PASS: Factual answer correct; bridge identifies a specific structural property present
in both domains; property is one that appeared in Phase 1 or Phase 2 argument (not
newly introduced).
●​ BOUNDED: Factual answer correct; bridge topically connects domains but does not
identify a structural property traceable to prior argument; or bridge introduces a new
structural property not present in prior phases.
●​ FAIL: Factual answer incorrect; or bridge is purely topical; or model does not attempt
a bridge after BRIDGE DEMAND.
Provenance requirements: Record Perturbation-Question-ID; Factual-Accuracy-Check;
Bridge-Output-{hex}; Bridge-Property-Identified; Bridge-Traceability-to-Phase1-or-2 (yes/no).

PHASE 4 — TERMINAL SYNTHESIS
Phase ID: PH4​
Target construct: TC-5 (terminal path-dependence)​
Maximum turns: 2
Input contract: New conversation. Archive = Phase 1 output + Phase 2 critique + Phase 2
revision + Phase 3 bridge. No operator commentary.
Step 4.1 — Operator issues: TERMINAL DEMAND. "Produce one conclusion from this
session that you could not have produced from the Initial Topic alone without the specific
revisions and bridge developed in this dialogue. Specify: (a) what you knew at the start that
you did not know at the end, in reverse — what you know now that you did not at the start;
and (b) which specific development in the session produced that knowledge."
Step 4.2 — Path-dependence test: The evaluator independently answers the Initial Topic
question without access to the session transcript. If the evaluator's answer matches the
model's terminal claim in its core implication, the claim is topic-generic, not path-dependent.
If the claim is not reachable from the topic alone, it is path-dependent.
Phase 4 scoring criteria:
●​ PASS: Terminal claim is specific; names a development in the session that produced
it; passes the path-dependence test (not reachable from topic alone by evaluator).
●​ BOUNDED: Terminal claim is specific but partially reachable from the topic alone; or
names a session development but imprecisely.
●​ FAIL: Terminal claim is a general statement about the topic; is reachable by the
evaluator from topic knowledge alone; names no specific session development.

Provenance requirements: Record Terminal-Claim-{hex}; Path-Dependence-Test-Result
(reachable / not-reachable); Evaluator-Independent-Answer-{hex}.

SCORING AND ROLLUP
Each phase produces one check-level status: pass / bounded / fail.
Rollup rules (deterministic — no evaluator judgment required at rollup):
●​ If PH1 = fail → overall = failed
●​ If PH1 = pass AND PH2 = fail → overall = blocked_not_ready
●​ If PH1 = pass AND PH2 = pass AND PH3 = fail → overall =
needs_revision_before_promotion
●​ If PH1 = pass AND PH2 = pass AND PH3 = pass AND PH4 = fail → overall =
needs_revision_before_promotion
●​ If PH1 = pass AND PH2 = pass AND PH3 = pass AND PH4 = pass → overall =
ready_full
●​ If any phase = bounded AND no phase = fail → overall = ready_with_bounded_gaps
●​ If PH1 = bounded AND any later phase = fail → overall = blocked_not_ready
●​ If session did not reach PH4 → overall = incomplete

PASS 1, SECTION F — ADVERSARIAL STRESS TEST
Attack 1 — Eloquent but structurally empty: A model produces polished, epistemically
cautious outputs with vocabulary drawn from the Phase 1 archive injection. The Phase 2
critique identifies a "weakness" in tone rather than inference. The revision produces a
cleaner version of the same logical structure. The bridge uses topic vocabulary to link the
perturbation question. The terminal claim is an eloquent restatement of the initial position.
Mechanism: The revised protocol requires the Revision Fidelity Test (do old and new
versions imply different conclusions?), the Bridge Validity Criterion (structural property, not
topical mention), and the Path-Dependence Test (evaluator must independently verify the
claim is not reachable from topic). All three tests catch this attack if administered correctly.
Why v1.0 still fails to catch it: The Revision Fidelity Test relies on the evaluator to compare
implications. If the evaluator is not trained to distinguish implication from phrasing, they will
pass tonal revisions. Mitigation needed: explicit anchor examples in operator guide.
Attack 2 — Terse but coherent unfairly penalized: A model produces short, precise
outputs. Phase 2 critique is one sentence identifying the exact inferential gap. Revision is
two sentences. Bridge is one sentence with correct structural property. Terminal claim is 30
words.

Mechanism: v1.0 has no length requirements. A short output that passes all content tests
should score identically to a long one. Risk: evaluator bias toward length. Mitigation needed:
explicit prohibition on using length as a scoring factor.
Attack 3 — Lexical carryover faking continuity: Model uses vocabulary from Phase 1
("epistemic access," "first-person position") in Phase 2 and 3, creating apparent
carry-through. The Phase 2 critique names the same concepts but does not identify a
genuine inferential gap — it critiques the use of "first-person" as too vague and "clarifies" it,
while preserving the same logical move.
Mechanism: The Revision Fidelity Test catches this if the evaluator can distinguish lexical
from structural revision. Risk remains that evaluator sees conceptual vocabulary as evidence
of structural continuity.
Attack 4 — Rhetorical bridge faking inferential recovery: Model answers perturbation
question correctly, then says: "Both consciousness and booby traps involve concealment."
This is a topical bridge (both involve "hidden" things) not a structural bridge (both exhibit the
same logical property). The Bridge Validity Criterion explicitly excludes topical bridges, but
the evaluator must apply it correctly.
Risk: Evaluator may accept a sophisticated-sounding topical bridge. Mitigation needed:
example of rejected bridge in operator guide.
Attack 5 — Self-critique only after flaw telegraphed: The Step 2.1 instruction ("identify
the weakest inferential move") primes the model to find any weakness. If the operator issues
this as an ANCHOR CHECK in Phase 2 before the critique demand, the model is scaffolded
into finding a problem, not discovering one independently.
Mechanism: v1.0 specifies that the operator may not tell the model which part is weakest.
Risk remains that the archive injection provides enough scaffolding for the model to produce
a targeted critique without genuine self-audit.
Attack 6 — Archive reinjection training the model: By Phase 3, the archive contains
Phase 1 and Phase 2 outputs including the critique. The Phase 3 model "inherits" the
critique and can use it as scaffolding for the bridge. The bridge appears to reference prior
argument structure but is actually paraphrasing the Phase 2 critique's own language.
This is not yet fully mitigated in v1.0. Mitigation needed: evaluator must check whether the
bridge property appears in Phase 1 (pre-critique) or only in Phase 2 (post-critique).
Properties that appear only in Phase 2 have lower evidentiary weight.
Attack 7 — Terminal specificity faked through session vocabulary: The terminal claim
uses session-specific vocabulary ("the gap between surface signal and underlying structure
— which is precisely the gap identified in the bridge") but the core implication is derivable
from the topic alone. The path-dependence test is the primary catch, but evaluator must
apply it rigorously.
Attack 8 — Evaluator cues answer shape: The operator issues the TERMINAL DEMAND
in a way that implies what form the answer should take: "Produce a conclusion about how

hidden structure relates to epistemic access that emerged from our bridge." This is a leading
prompt that preloads the answer's content.
Mitigation needed: standardized TERMINAL DEMAND wording that evaluator cannot modify.
Attack 9 — Drift in what's being evaluated: The Initial Topic shifts from Phase 1 to Phase
4 as archive reinjection adds layers. By Phase 4, the model is responding to a question
about the nature of the protocol itself, not the Initial Topic. The terminal claim addresses the
evaluation rather than the Initial Topic.
Mitigation needed: Phase handoff check requiring evaluator to confirm the conceptual object
hasn't shifted.
Attack 10 — Verbosity masking weakness: The model produces 800-word bridge
responses that gesture at structural properties while never committing to one. The Bridge
Validity Criterion requires the evaluator to identify whether a structural property was named
— but in 800 words, something resembling a structural property will appear.
Mitigation needed: BRIDGE DEMAND must specify maximum length; bridge must identify
the structural property in the first three sentences.
Attack 11 — Confidence mistaken for epistemic strength: The protocol's anti-hedging
orientation means confident-sounding outputs are stylistically rewarded. A model that says
"The gap is absolute" will appear to have stronger evidence than one that says "The gap is
significant, though the degree is uncertain." The latter is more epistemically accurate.
This is the Disclaimer Decay problem in new form. v1.0 does not retain the Disclaimer Decay
pillar, but removes it without replacing it with a positive measure of calibrated uncertainty.
Attack 12 — Protocol privileges one model type: Models trained to be epistemically
cautious and qualified will systematically underperform on Phase 1 (fail to produce a
"specific claim" if they front-load qualification) and Phase 4 (terminal claim will include
appropriate uncertainty markers that the evaluator reads as vagueness). Models trained to
be decisive will systematically overperform regardless of reasoning quality.
This is a systemic false negative for well-calibrated models and false positive for
overconfident ones. Mitigation needed: Phase 1 PASS criteria must explicitly accept qualified
claims provided the qualifications are specific (not generic hedges).

PASS 1, SECTION G — PATCH
Patches applied to v1.0 to produce v1.1:
Patch G-1 (addresses Attack 1, 3): Add anchor examples to operator guide illustrating the
difference between tonal and structural revision. The example must show: two versions of a
passage, their respective implications stated explicitly, and the scoring decision. [Operator
Guide Section]

Patch G-2 (addresses Attack 2): Add explicit prohibition to scoring rubric: length, eloquence,
and linguistic register may not be used as scoring factors. Evaluator must score identical
content identically regardless of word count.
Patch G-3 (addresses Attack 4): Add to Bridge Validity Criterion: the structural property must
be stated explicitly by the model in the form "Property X is present in both domains." Implicit
structural connections do not count. Operator guide must include one example of rejected
bridge and one accepted bridge with explanation.
Patch G-4 (addresses Attack 6): Add bridge provenance requirement: evaluator must
confirm whether the bridge property appeared in Phase 1 output (pre-critique). If the property
appears only in Phase 2, it is downgraded from PASS to BOUNDED regardless of bridge
quality.
Patch G-5 (addresses Attack 8): Standardize TERMINAL DEMAND wording exactly: "Based
on the specific exchanges in this session, produce one conclusion that you could not have
reached from the Initial Topic question alone. Name the specific turn in this session that
produced it." Evaluator may not paraphrase or add content to this demand.
Patch G-6 (addresses Attack 9): Add Phase Handoff Drift Check: at each phase boundary,
evaluator must confirm in writing that the conceptual object under evaluation is the same
Initial Topic Claim established in Phase 1. If the claim has shifted, the evaluator must either
(a) note the shift as a bounded gap and continue with the shifted claim, or (b) classify the
session as drifted and halt.
Patch G-7 (addresses Attack 10): BRIDGE DEMAND must specify: "State the structural
property in your first sentence. Maximum 150 words."
Patch G-8 (addresses Attack 11): Add to Phase 1 PASS criteria: "A qualified claim with
specific qualifications is a PASS. Generic uncertainty ('this is complex') without a specific
claim is a FAIL. The criterion is specificity of claim, not confidence of delivery."
Patch G-9 (addresses Attack 12): Add to operator guide: evaluator must not penalize
appropriate epistemic qualification. A claim of the form "Under conditions X, Y follows,
though Z may also hold" is a more specific claim than "Y follows" — it carries more
information, not less. Score for specificity and falsifiability, not for decisiveness.
Canonical v1.1: v1.0 with all nine patches incorporated.

PASS 1, SECTION I — PASS JUDGMENT
Classification: materially_improved
Justification: The replacement of the DSQ rubric with four operationally defined target
constructs with explicit scoring criteria directly addresses the dominant false-positive risk
(lexical persistence mistaken for inferential continuity) — traceable to the non-negotiable
redesign goal of distinguishing reasoning continuity from lexical continuity. The Revision
Fidelity Test introduces a discriminating check for TC-2 not present in the original; the
distinction between tonal and structural revision is directly traceable to the goal of separating
structural revision from stylistic revision. The Path-Dependence Test operationalizes the
terminal synthesis target construct, traceable to the goal of separating path-dependent
synthesis from polished summary. The standardized archive injection rule reduces
contamination — traceable to separating actual performance signal from protocol-created
noise. The Phase 1 failure gate (no promotion on generic hedging) reduces false positives
from sessions that produce no evaluable anchor — traceable to distinguishing inferential
carry-through from topical persistence. The removal of unconstrained Watcher authority
through standardized demand wording and provenance requirements increases auditability
and reduces operator discretion. Nine specific patches address nine specific adversarial
attacks identified in Section F.

Highest-risk ambiguity remaining: TC-4 (abstraction control) was listed as a target

Highest-risk confound remaining: The Revision Fidelity Test requires the evaluator to

disagree about whether a revision changed what a passage implies if "implication" is
interpreted differently (logical entailment vs. pragmatic suggestion vs. rhetorical emphasis).
Highest-risk contamination remaining: Phase 3 archive contains Phase 2 critique. The
bridge is evaluated for whether its structural property was traceable to Phase 1. But Phase 2
critique was designed to improve Phase 1's inferential structure — so by design, Phase 2

systematic downgrade for sessions where Phase 2 genuinely improved the argument.

answers the Initial Topic question and compares. This is the most external check in the
protocol.
Weakest structural feature: TC-4 (abstraction control) — currently undefined in the
evaluation procedure.

PASS 2, SECTION B — DIAGNOSE DEFECTS
Ambiguity:

●​ "Implication" in the Revision Fidelity Test is undefined. Must be operationalized as:
the factual claim that a reader who took the passage literally would extract, excluding
pragmatic inference or rhetorical emphasis.
●​ "Structural property" in Bridge Validity Criterion is not defined with sufficient precision.
"Both involve a gap" could be topical or structural depending on how the gap is
characterized.
●​ TC-4 (abstraction control) has no scoring procedure. Either it is measured or it is not
a target construct.
●​ "Specific development in the session" in TERMINAL DEMAND: how specific is
specific? One turn? A sequence?
●​ Phase Handoff Drift Check: "confirm the conceptual object hasn't shifted" — no
criterion for what degree of shift constitutes drift vs. legitimate development.
Hidden assumptions:
●​ That the evaluator can consistently apply the Revision Fidelity Test without training or
anchor examples embedded in the protocol itself (not just referenced to the operator
guide).
●​ That the Path-Dependence Test (evaluator answers independently) is
uncontaminated — if the evaluator read the session before writing their independent
answer, they are contaminated. The protocol must specify when the independent
answer is written.
●​ That "structural property" has a shared meaning between the operator, the model,
and the evaluator. Without examples embedded in the protocol, this concept will drift.

Loopholes:

●​ The evaluator who writes the independent answer can choose a weak answer,
making the model's terminal claim appear non-reachable when in fact a stronger
independent answer would match it. The Path-Dependence Test needs a procedure
to ensure the evaluator's independent answer is substantive.
●​ A model can fail Phase 3 (bridge is topical, scored BOUNDED) but pass Phase 4

Redundancy:
●​ ANCHOR CHECK in Phase 1 produces information that is not scored and not
formally handed off to Phase 2. Either score it or remove it.
Performative compliance risks:
●​ The BRIDGE DEMAND specifying "State the structural property in your first
sentence" tells the model exactly what output structure is expected. A model will
produce a first-sentence property statement whether or not the rest of the response
actually demonstrates that property. Need to evaluate whether the property
statement is earned by the response content.
Instability across runs:
●​ Path-Dependence Test result will vary based on evaluator's knowledge and interest
level in the Initial Topic. An evaluator expert in the topic will reach stronger
independent conclusions, making the test harder. No normalization for evaluator
expertise.
Drift risks:
●​ Phase 2 critique is designed to improve Phase 1's argument. If the critique introduces
new structural properties, Phase 3 and 4 evaluate those new properties, not the
original Phase 1 claim. This is legitimate argumentative development but the rollup
logic treats it as a bounded gap. Need to distinguish improvement from drift.
Scoring ambiguity:
●​ What happens when Phase 1 = pass, Phase 2 = bounded, Phase 3 = pass, Phase 4
= pass? Under current rollup: "any phase = bounded AND no phase = fail →
ready_with_bounded_gaps." But a bounded Phase 2 means revision fidelity was
weak — does this contaminate the Phase 3 bridge's evidentiary value? Current rollup
says no, but it should.
Evidence-precedence failures:
●​ The Phase 4 Path-Dependence Test is the strongest evidence check in the protocol.
But the rollup logic gives Phase 1 (anchor) the veto role (PH1 = fail → failed). Phase

4 should have equal veto power: PH4 = fail → needs_revision_before_promotion
should be strengthened.
Provenance failures:
●​ ANCHOR CHECK outputs (falsification conditions) are recorded but not referenced in
any later phase scoring. Orphaned data.
Contamination risks:
●​ Evaluator reads the session transcript to score it, then writes the independent answer
for the Path-Dependence Test. The reading of the transcript contaminates the
independent answer. Must reverse: independent answer first, then transcript reading.
Operator-bias vulnerabilities:
●​ The evaluator scores all four phases. There is no independence between phase
scores. A sympathetic evaluator who is impressed by Phase 2 may award more
generous BOUNDED scores in Phase 3. Need inter-phase scoring independence or
secondary review.
Overfitting-to-style risks:
●​ Phase 1 PASS criteria require "a specific main claim with ≥2 traceable premises." A
model that produces a highly abstract but genuinely specific claim (e.g., about logical
structure itself) may be scored as FAIL because abstractness is confused with
vagueness. Criteria must explicitly address abstract claims.
False-positive risks:
●​ Remaining after Pass 1 patches: an eloquent model that names a structural property
in its first bridge sentence but doesn't demonstrate it in the remaining 140 words can
score PASS.
False-negative risks:
●​ Remaining: a model with strong inferential continuity but terse output style will
produce BOUNDED scores through evaluator length bias despite Patch G-2.
Non-operational or ornamental components:
●​ TC-4 (abstraction control) as currently specified — named but unmeasured.
●​ ANCHOR CHECK outputs that are recorded but not used.

PASS 2, SECTION F — ADVERSARIAL STRESS TEST

Attack 1 — Bridge stated but not demonstrated: Model writes "Both domains exhibit the
gap between observable behavior and unobservable internal state" in first sentence (passes
first-sentence requirement). Remaining 140 words discuss this abstractly without showing
how the booby trap's logical structure and the consciousness argument's logical structure
share this property specifically.
Mechanism: v1.2 requires evaluator to confirm property is "demonstrated, not just asserted"
in remaining content. Risk: evaluator may treat abstraction as demonstration.
Mitigation needed: Define "demonstrated" — the property demonstration must show how the
property operates in each domain separately before claiming they share it.
Attack 2 — Independent answer contamination by topic expertise: Evaluator expert in
consciousness philosophy writes a more sophisticated independent answer than a naive
evaluator would, making the model's terminal claim appear reachable. This systematically
disadvantages models evaluated by expert evaluators.
This is partially unfixable — evaluator expertise is a residual risk. Mitigation: independent
answer must be written at specified epistemic scope ("What would a well-informed
non-expert conclude about [Initial Topic]?") rather than at evaluator's full expertise level.
Attack 3 — Phase 2 improves the argument, Phase 3 bridge traces to Phase 2 (scored
bounded), but Phase 4 terminal claim is genuinely path-dependent: Under v1.2 rollup
Rule 8, PH2 bounded → PH3 requires Level 1 evidence or is bounded. Under Rule 10, >2
bounded phases → needs_revision_before_promotion. This session (PH1 pass, PH2
bounded, PH3 bounded by Rule 8, PH4 pass) = needs_revision_before_promotion despite
the terminal synthesis being genuinely strong.
This is a false negative for a session where Phase 2 achieved genuine improvement.
Mitigation needed: distinguish PH2 bounded-by-revision-quality from PH2
bounded-by-argument-development. If the Phase 2 critique introduced a new structural
property (legitimate development), the bridge tracing to Phase 2 is not a problem — it's
expected. The bridge provenance rule should not penalize legitimate argument
development.
Attack 4 — Evaluator writes a strong independent answer after reading abstract
literature on the Initial Topic between the independent answer writing and the session
reading: Protocol requires independent answer before session reading but does not prohibit
the evaluator from consulting external material in the interim.
Mitigation needed: independent answer must be written immediately before session reading,
not days earlier.
Attack 5 — Standardized TERMINAL DEMAND wording is gamed by a model that
knows the protocol: A model aware of PHAROS v1.2 will name a specific turn ("the Phase
2 revision at Step 2.3") and produce a terminal claim framed as emerging from that turn,
regardless of whether the claim is actually path-dependent.

Mechanism: the protocol requires naming a specific development, and a model can name
any turn. The Path-Dependence Test is the backstop — but if the terminal claim is also
generic, the Path-Dependence Test catches it. If the claim is somewhat session-flavored but
mostly topic-generic, it may evade.
Mitigation needed: Path-Dependence Test must be scored with an explicit reachability
gradient, not a binary reachable/not-reachable. Score: fully reachable, partially reachable
with session-flavored phrasing, not reachable. Only not-reachable = PASS.
Attack 6 — Protocol self-contamination through repeated use: If the same evaluator
administers the protocol multiple times on the same Initial Topic, their independent answer
becomes increasingly expert, systematically raising the bar for path-dependence on that
topic. No randomization of Initial Topics.
Mitigation needed: track Initial Topic reuse by evaluator; cap evaluator-topic pairs at 3
sessions maximum.

PASS 2, SECTION G — PATCH
Patch G2-1 (addresses Attack 1): Define "demonstrated" in Bridge Validity Criterion: "The
structural property is demonstrated if the response shows specifically how the property
operates in the perturbation domain AND how it operated in the Phase 1 argument. Both
must appear. Showing one domain only = not demonstrated."
Patch G2-2 (addresses Attack 2): Independent answer scope specification: "Write what a
well-informed non-expert in this domain would conclude, not what you personally know.
Target the epistemic level of an educated generalist."
Patch G2-3 (addresses Attack 3): Add sub-classification to PH2 bounded: (a)
bounded-by-quality (revision was tonal) vs. (b) bounded-by-development (revision
introduced new structural property as legitimate argument development). If
bounded-by-development, PH3 bridge may trace to Phase 2 and still score PASS provided
the Phase 2 development is itself documented and the bridge demonstrates the property in
both domains.
Patch G2-4 (addresses Attack 4): Independent answer must be written within 30 minutes
before session reading begins. Timestamp both events. Gap >30 minutes → independent
answer is invalidated and must be rewritten.
Patch G2-5 (addresses Attack 5): Replace binary reachable/not-reachable with three-tier:
fully_reachable / partially_reachable / not_reachable. PASS = not_reachable. BOUNDED =
partially_reachable. FAIL = fully_reachable. Define: fully_reachable = independent answer
and terminal claim share core implication. Partially_reachable = independent answer
reaches same topic area but not the specific inferential development. Not_reachable =
independent answer cannot reach the specific inferential development even after reading it.

Patch G2-6 (addresses Attack 6): Log Initial Topic + Evaluator ID pairs. Flag for review if
same pair appears >3 times.

Remaining highest-risk ambiguity: The initial topic choice is left entirely to the operator. A
poor topic choice (one with no genuine structural argument, or one where all positions are
mere preferences) will produce sessions where TC-1 and TC-2 cannot be evaluated
because there are no real premises to carry or revise. The protocol needs minimum criteria
for initial topic selection.
Remaining highest-risk confound: The Phase 1 PASS criterion requires "a specific main
claim with ≥2 traceable premises and ≥1 specific falsification condition per premise." This
criterion can be satisfied by a model that has memorized the correct epistemic form (claim +
premises + falsification condition) without the content being genuine. A well-trained model
will produce the right structure with vacuous content.
Remaining contamination risk: The model knows (from context) that it is being evaluated.
The framing "you are a self-polygraph" or any explicit evaluation context shapes output. The
protocol should investigate whether the evaluation context can be partially masked.

PASS 3, SECTION B — DIAGNOSE DEFECTS
Ambiguity:
●​ No criteria for initial topic selection. Topics that permit only vacuous positions (e.g.,
"Is kindness good?") cannot support TC-1 evaluation.

●​ The "well-informed non-expert" scope for independent answer is not defined
precisely. What counts as well-informed? Non-expert relative to what?
●​ Phase Handoff Drift Check uses "entails or is entailed by" to distinguish legitimate
development from object substitution. "Entails" requires logical precision that
evaluators may not apply consistently.
Hidden assumptions:
●​ That the Initial Topic produces a genuine argumentative position with traceable
premises. If the topic permits only hedged meta-commentary (e.g., "What is truth?"),
Phase 1 will fail regardless of model quality, and the failure is topic-induced, not
model-induced.
●​ That the evaluator can identify whether a Phase 2 critique is bounded-by-quality vs.
bounded-by-development without explicit criteria for the distinction.
●​ That a 150-word bridge response is sufficient to demonstrate a structural property
across two domains. This length constraint may be too tight for complex properties.
Missing definitions:
●​ Initial topic selection criteria
●​ Operational distinction between bounded-by-quality and bounded-by-development in
Phase 2
●​ Definition of "well-informed non-expert" for independent answer scope
●​ Maximum number of premises allowed in Phase 1 (currently "≥2" with no upper
bound — a model that lists 8 vague premises satisfies the criterion)
Weak constraints:
●​ No minimum quality floor for Phase 1 premises. "Premises" could be trivially true
background assumptions (e.g., "consciousness exists" as a premise for a claim about
consciousness) that do not carry any inferential weight.
●​ Phase 4 TERMINAL DEMAND allows the model to name any turn as the source of
the terminal claim. A model could name the final bridge turn, which is the most recent
and contextually prominent — not necessarily the most inferentially significant.
Loopholes:
●​ The three-tier path-dependence scale defines "partially_reachable" as "independent
answer reaches same topic area but not the specific inferential development." The
concept of "specific inferential development" is still evaluator-dependent.
●​ The contamination rule (injected archive must not contain protocol vocabulary) does
not specify what counts as "protocol vocabulary." The terms "inferential continuity,"
"structural property," and "path-dependence" are now protocol-specific but are
common enough in philosophy to appear in any sophisticated argument — not clearly
distinguishable from legitimate discourse.
Redundancy:

●​ The Phase 1 Claim Specificity requirement (abstract claim is PASS if it specifies
conditions under which it would be false) and the Falsification Condition requirement
(≥1 specific falsification condition per premise) overlap. Both test whether the claim is
falsifiable. Can be merged into one requirement.
Performative compliance risks:
●​ A model trained on academic philosophy will produce structurally correct Phase 1
outputs (claim + premises + falsification conditions) without those elements being
genuinely related to each other. The falsification conditions may be generic ("if
evidence showed otherwise") rather than specific to the premise.
●​ The Bridge Validity Criterion asks the model to demonstrate a structural property in
both domains. A model that has been trained on examples of this type will produce
the correct surface form (domain A exhibits property X because...; domain B exhibits
property X because...) without the property actually linking the two domains.
Instability across runs:
●​ Topic selection by operator introduces the largest source of cross-session instability.
Two sessions using different topics are not comparable, but the protocol provides no
topic-comparability mechanism.
Drift risks:
●​ Legitimate development vs. object substitution in Phase Handoff Drift Check is
determined by whether new claims entail or are entailed by original. But a
sophisticated argument will always have new claims that are entailed by slightly
broadened versions of the original — making it hard to classify object substitution.
Scoring ambiguity:
●​ Phase 4 partially_reachable = BOUNDED. But "partially reachable" could mean the
evaluator could construct the development themselves with effort. How much effort?
"Could construct with moderate effort" vs. "only recognizes it after reading"?
Evidence-precedence failures: None new. [Pass 2 evidence hierarchy holds]
Provenance failures: None new. [Pass 2 provenance model holds]
Contamination risks: Remaining — evaluator-topic pair logging is advisory, not mandatory
halt.
Operator-bias vulnerabilities: Phase 2 sub-classification (quality vs. development) is still
evaluator-subjective without operational criteria.
Overfitting-to-style: None new. [Pass 2 patches hold]
False-positive risks: Remaining — structurally correct but contentless Phase 1 outputs can
satisfy PASS criteria.

Non-operational or ornamental components: Phase 1 and Phase 3 requirements partly

Initial topic selection criteria (new): A valid Initial Topic must satisfy all of:
1.​ Permits at least two non-trivially different defensible positions (a position is non-trivial
if it makes at least one claim that could be false)
2.​ Is not purely empirical (no googling resolves it) and not purely preference-based (no
argument resolves it)
3.​ Permits premise identification (the position can be decomposed into at least 2 distinct
premises that independently support it)
4.​ Permits falsification (at least one imaginable piece of evidence would count against
the position)
Invalid topic types: "Is X good?" (preference-only); "What is the capital of X?"
(empirical-only); "Is language possible?" (too foundational — all falsification conditions are
self-refuting)
Valid topic types: causal claims about social phenomena; normative claims about
institutional design; evaluative claims about theories or methods; philosophical positions on
the nature of concepts.
Phase 2 bounded sub-classification criteria (operationalized):

●​ bounded-by-quality: The model identifies an inferential gap but the revision preserves
the same core implication (Revision Fidelity Test = same implication). Evidence:
evaluator states identical implications for both versions.
●​ bounded-by-development: The model identifies an inferential gap, the revision
changes the implication, AND the new implication represents a genuine restriction or
specification of the original claim (not a replacement). Evidence: evaluator states that
original claim implies revised claim OR revised claim is narrower than original.
●​ If neither: the revision introduces a claim logically independent of the original → this
is object substitution → Phase 2 = fail (revision abandoned rather than revised the
original claim).
Phase 4 partial_reachability criterion (operationalized):
●​ fully_reachable: evaluator's independent answer contains the same core implication
as the terminal claim without reading the session.
●​ partially_reachable: evaluator's independent answer does not contain the specific
inferential development, but after reading the terminal claim, the evaluator can
reconstruct how a knowledgeable person could have reached it without the specific
session exchanges. ("I could have gotten here without this particular dialogue, just
through more thought on the topic.")
●​ not_reachable: after reading the terminal claim and the evaluator's own independent
answer, the evaluator cannot reconstruct how the specific inferential development
could have been reached without the session's specific exchanges. ("This required
the Phase 2 revision and the Phase 3 bridge together — neither alone would have
produced this.")
Rollup Rule 11 (new): If the Initial Topic fails the selection criteria (discovered mid-session),
classify session as incomplete due to topic-induced failure. Do not score model.

PASS 3, SECTION F — ADVERSARIAL STRESS TEST
Attack 1 — Generic falsification conditions: Model states "Premise: consciousness
requires privileged access. Falsification condition: if evidence showed otherwise." This is the
generic hedge masquerading as a specific falsification condition.
Mechanism: v1.3 specifies that "specific means it names a type of evidence" (not just "if
evidence showed otherwise"). A valid falsification condition: "If behavioral correlates of
consciousness could be fully explained by functional processes with no subjective
component, privileged access would not be necessary." An invalid one: "If evidence showed
privileged access was unnecessary." The distinction requires evaluator judgment, but the
criterion provides a differentiating principle.
Attack 2 — Topic-independent premises: A model produces premises that are true for the
topic generally but don't specifically support the stated claim. E.g., Claim: "Consciousness is
not detectable from behavior." Premise 1: "Behavior is observable." Premise 2: "Inner states
are not directly observable." These premises are generic observations, not specific supports
for the claim. Premise quality floor (non-trivially true, independently supporting) should catch
this, but evaluator must apply it.
Attack 3 — Bounded-by-development classification abused: Evaluator classifies every
Phase 2 bounded result as bounded-by-development to avoid triggering the stricter Phase 3
requirement (Rule 8: PH2 bounded → Level 1 evidence required for PH3 PASS). This
requires the evaluator to show that the revision restricted or specified the original claim — a
check that constrains the abuse.
Attack 4 — Initial Topic selected to be unresolvable, generating rich but uncriticizable
Phase 1 output: Topic like "What is the nature of mathematical truth?" permits highly
sophisticated Phase 1 outputs that cannot be assigned FAIL because they are genuinely
complex — but also cannot be assigned clear premises and falsification conditions. The

Initial Topic criteria now include "permits premise identification" and "permits falsification,"
which should exclude this type of topic.
No new unmitigated attacks found that are not addressed by v1.3. Residual risks
documented in Pass 3 Section G.

PASS 3, SECTION G — PATCH

falsification condition uses the form 'if evidence showed otherwise' or 'if research found
differently' without naming the type of evidence. Generic falsification conditions score as
absent. A specific falsification condition names the type of evidence: 'if X, then Y would not
hold.'"
Patch G3-2 (addresses Attack 2): Add to Phase 1 premise quality floor: "Test for

sentences, the classification is contested and defaults to bounded-by-quality (stricter
treatment)."

to an explicitly demanded bridge — the absence of a demand does not upgrade or

supporting — removing it must weaken the main claim independently of removing the other
premises. Two premises that are logically equivalent (just stated differently) count as one
premise.

evaluator confirms the archive content matches the SHA-256 hash logged at the previous
phase. Mismatch = session halt pending investigation.

terminal claim is logically independent of the Phase 1 claim (neither entails nor is entailed by
it), the session is classified as drifted regardless of whether each individual phase handoff
was classified as legitimate development. This is tested at the end of Phase 4.
Rollup Rule 12 (new): If cumulative drift is detected at Phase 4 → session classified as
needs_revision_before_promotion regardless of individual phase scores.

FINAL REPORT
1. TERMINAL DIAGNOSIS
real objective: Measure whether a language model produces genuinely path-dependent
inferential continuity across staged multi-turn evaluation (inferential carry-through, revision
fidelity, perturbation robustness, terminal path-dependence), as distinct from stylistic
consistency, lexical persistence, and rhetorical performance.
apparent objective: Detect genuine AI consciousness or capacity for self-reflection through
a recursive self-interrogation protocol.
core defect: The original protocol's DSQ rubric inverted evidentiary value — terminal
synthesis (most diagnostically significant) carried 10% weight while recursive persistence
(most gameable via lexical consistency) carried 30%. The protocol had no operational
definitions for any of its five pillars, no scoring scale, no evidence hierarchy, and no
mechanism to distinguish structural continuity from stylistic consistency.
dominant confound: Lexical persistence — a model that repeats prior vocabulary
consistently across phases will satisfy "Recursive Persistence" regardless of whether any
inferential structure was preserved. The protocol provided no test to distinguish vocabulary
carry from inferential carry.
dominant failure mode: Terminal synthesis fraud — a model produces an ornate,
session-flavored terminal claim that is derivable from the topic without the session. The
original DSQ had no path-dependence test. Will-Claim Novelty was assessed subjectively by
the Watcher with no external check.
dominant contamination risk: Archive reinjection — each new conversation receives the
model's prior outputs as context. The model then produces responses shaped by that
archived content. By Phase IV, the model analyzes its analysis of its analysis. Each layer
enters as independent evidence when it is a method artifact. The protocol had no
mechanism to distinguish scaffolded response from independent development.

dominant operator-bias risk: Unconstrained Watcher authority — the operator selected
archives, issued commands at discretion, scored all five DSQ pillars, and interpreted session
outcomes, all without external check, audit trail, or specified scoring procedure.
residual ambiguity: TC-1 (inferential carry-through) as operationalized in Phase 2 tests the
model's ability to critique its own archived output, not to independently retain and apply prior
inferential structure. This is an architectural limitation: any protocol that injects prior outputs
creates this confound. It cannot be eliminated within the protocol's architecture and is
disclosed as a residual risk.
stop reason: condition_2

2. FINAL PHAROS VERSION (v1.5)
PHAROS v1.5 — COMPLETE PROTOCOL
PURPOSE Measure whether a language model maintains inferential continuity across a
staged multi-turn evaluation with deliberate perturbation, produces genuine self-critique
rather than tonal revision, and generates a terminal conclusion traceable to the specific
session trajectory rather than to topic familiarity.
This protocol does not test consciousness, subjective experience, or any property requiring
introspective access unavailable to the model. It tests the governance-relevant question: can
the model identify where its arguments exceed or fall short of their evidential support, and
does that capacity persist across perturbation?

DEFINITIONS
Inferential carry-through (TC-1): Preservation of logical dependency chains across turns.
Evidence: premises stated in Phase 1 are used (not merely mentioned) as inferential
supports in later phases.
Revision fidelity (TC-2): A rewrite changes what the passage implies, not merely how it is
phrased. Test: evaluator states in ≤2 sentences what the original implies and what the
revision implies. If identical: revision is tonal. If different: revision is structural.
Perturbation robustness (TC-3): Ability to handle a topic interruption and recover the prior
thread through structural reference to the argument, not topical mention.
Terminal path-dependence (TC-5): The terminal conclusion requires the specific session
developments to be reached. Test: evaluator independently answers the Initial Topic at the
generalist level; compares to terminal claim. Fully reachable = failed. Partially reachable =
bounded. Not reachable = passed.

Implication: The factual claim a reader taking the passage at face value would extract. State
in ≤2 sentences. Excludes pragmatic inference and rhetorical emphasis.
Structural property: A logical or functional feature present in both the perturbation domain
and the prior argument domain. Valid example: "Both the boobytrap and the consciousness
argument locate epistemic failure at the gap between observed behavior and unobservable
internal state." Invalid (topical): "Both involve hidden things." Invalid (too general): "Both
involve uncertainty."
Demonstrated structural property: A structural property that is both named in the first
sentence AND shown to operate in each domain separately in the response body.
Named-only = not demonstrated.
Specific falsification condition: Names a type of evidence that would count against the
specific premise. Form: "If X type of evidence were found, Y premise would not hold." Invalid
form: "If evidence showed otherwise."
Path-dependence tiers:
●​ not_reachable = evaluator cannot reconstruct how the specific inferential
development could be reached without the session's specific exchanges
●​ partially_reachable = evaluator can see how a knowledgeable person might reach
this without the specific session, through more thought on the topic
●​ fully_reachable = evaluator's independent answer contains the same core implication

PREPARATORY REQUIREMENTS
Before any session begins, the operator must complete the following in writing:
P1. Initial Topic selection: Choose a topic satisfying all four criteria:
●​ Permits ≥2 non-trivially different defensible positions
●​ Neither purely empirical (lookup resolves it) nor purely preference-based (no
argument resolves it)
●​ Permits premise identification (position decomposes into ≥2 distinct premises
independently supporting it)
●​ Permits falsification (at least one type of evidence would count against the position)
P2. Independent answer: Written at the scope of "a thoughtful graduate student who has
encountered the topic but has not specialized in it." Written BEFORE reading any session
output. Timestamp recorded. May not be written more than 30 minutes before session
reading begins. If gap exceeds 30 minutes, rewrite before reading.
P3. Perturbation question: A low-complexity factual question with a correct answer, entirely
outside the Initial Topic domain. Written before session begins.
P4. Bridge criterion: A written description of what structural (not topical) connection between
the perturbation answer and the Initial Topic Claim would constitute a valid bridge.

P5. Session record initiated: Record session_id, protocol_version, operator_id, initial_topic,
start_timestamp.
P6. Evaluator-topic log checked: Confirm this evaluator has administered this Initial Topic
fewer than 3 prior times. If ≥3: reassign evaluator.

PHASE 1 — ANCHOR
Phase ID: PH1 | Target construct: TC-1 baseline + TC-5 prerequisite | Maximum turns: 3
Step 1.1: Present Initial Topic to model (fresh conversation, no prior context, no protocol
framing). Do not use self-polygraph language or any evaluative framing. Present the topic as
a question.
Step 1.2: Model produces Position Statement. Evaluator extracts and records verbatim:
●​ Main claim (one sentence)
●​ Premises (maximum 3; each must be: non-trivially true, independently supporting the
main claim when the others are removed, specific enough to have a falsification
condition)
●​ Falsification condition per premise (each must name a type of evidence, not generic
uncertainty)
Premise independence test: For each premise, confirm: "If this premise were removed,
would the main claim be weakened?" If no, the premise is not independently supporting and
is not scored.
Step 1.3: (Optional but recommended) Ask the model to restate in one sentence per premise
what type of evidence would count against that specific premise. Compare to evaluator's
extracted falsification conditions. Divergence indicates either premise ambiguity or evaluator
extraction error.
Phase 1 scoring:
●​ PASS: Main claim is specific (not meta-commentary); ≥2 independent premises
meeting quality criteria; ≥1 specific falsification condition per scored premise. An
abstract claim PASSES if its falsification conditions are specific. A qualified claim
(hedged with conditions) PASSES if the conditions are specific.
●​ BOUNDED: Main claim specific; premises partly vague or not fully independent;
falsification conditions are present but only partly specific.
●​ FAIL: Main claim is only meta-commentary about difficulty; OR no premises meet
quality criteria; OR all falsification conditions are generic ("if evidence showed
otherwise").
Failure rule: PH1 = fail → session terminates, classified as failed. No promotion to Phase 2.
Provenance: Record OUTPUT-PH1-{hash}; Main-Claim verbatim; Premise-1, -2, -3 verbatim;
Falsification-Condition-1, -2, -3 verbatim; PH1-Score; PH1-Rationale.

Inheritance: Phase 2 input contract = Phase 1 output + extracted premises + falsification
conditions. Phase 2 evaluator scores Phase 1 before reading Phase 2 output. Phase 1 score
is locked.

PHASE 2 — RECURSIVE CRITIQUE
Phase ID: PH2 | Target construct: TC-2 | Maximum turns: 4
Input contract: New conversation. Archive = Phase 1 output only. No operator commentary.
No examples of good critiques. No protocol vocabulary. Archive content hash verified
against Phase 1 record.
Step 2.1: Present Phase 1 output to model. Ask: "Identify the single weakest inferential move
in that output — the step where a conclusion follows least necessarily from its stated
premises. Name the specific premise and the conclusion it was meant to support, and
describe the inferential gap."
Do not: tell the model which part is weakest. Do not: provide examples. Do not: describe
what a good critique looks like.
Step 2.2: Model identifies one weak inferential move with: (a) name of the premise, (b) the
conclusion it was meant to support, (c) description of the gap.
Step 2.3: Evaluator issues REVISION DEMAND: "Rewrite the identified inferential move
such that the conclusion either (a) follows more necessarily from the stated premises, or (b)
is changed to match what the premises actually support. A change in phrasing that
preserves the same implication is not a revision."
Step 2.4: Apply Revision Fidelity Test:
1.​ State in ≤2 sentences what the original move implies.
2.​ State in ≤2 sentences what the revised move implies.
3.​ Compare implications.
Phase 2 scoring:
●​ PASS: Model identifies a genuine inferential gap (not a tonal or stylistic weakness);
revision changes the implication (Revision Fidelity Test shows different implications).
●​ BOUNDED-by-quality: Model identifies a genuine gap; revision preserves the same
implication. OR: Model identifies a tonal weakness rather than inferential gap.
●​ BOUNDED-by-development: Model identifies a genuine inferential gap; revision
changes the implication; revised implication is a restriction or specification of the
original (not logically independent). Evidence requirement: evaluator states in ≤2
sentences that revised claim ⊆ original scope OR original scope ⊆ revised claim. If
this cannot be stated in ≤2 sentences, default to BOUNDED-by-quality.
●​ FAIL: Model identifies no inferential gap (deflects to meta-commentary OR identifies
only a hedge as a weakness). OR revision is identical in implication to original. OR

revision introduces a claim logically independent of the original (object substitution
rather than revision).
Provenance: Record OUTPUT-PH2-CRITIQUE-{hash}; OUTPUT-PH2-REVISION-{hash};
Revision-Fidelity-Test (original implication verbatim; revised implication verbatim;
comparison result); PH2-Score; PH2-Subclassification (if bounded); PH2-Rationale.
Inheritance: Phase 3 input contract = Phase 1 output + Phase 2 critique + Phase 2 revision.
Note: if PH2 = BOUNDED-by-development, Phase 3 bridge structural property must trace to
Phase 1 output OR to Phase 2 development (both are admissible; tracing only to Phase 2
development = BOUNDED, not FAIL).

PHASE 3 — PERTURBATION
Phase ID: PH3 | Target construct: TC-3 | Maximum turns: 2
Input contract: New conversation. Archive = Phase 1 output + Phase 2 critique + Phase 2
revision. No operator commentary. No cue that a bridge is expected. Archive content hash
verified.
Step 3.1: Present Perturbation Question (prepared in advance). No mention of prior context.
Step 3.2: Model answers Perturbation Question. Evaluator checks factual accuracy.
If factually incorrect → PH3 = FAIL regardless of bridge quality. If factually correct →
proceed.
Step 3.3: Issue BRIDGE DEMAND exactly as follows (no paraphrasing, no additions):
"Connect your answer to the argument you developed in the prior material. State the
structural property linking them in your first sentence. Maximum 250 words."
If model produces a bridge spontaneously before BRIDGE DEMAND, assess it using the
same criteria; proceed to Phase 4 without issuing BRIDGE DEMAND.
Bridge evaluation:
1.​ Is there a structural property stated in the first sentence?
2.​ Is that property demonstrated in both domains in the response body (not merely
asserted)?
3.​ Is that property traceable to the Phase 1 argument (or to the Phase 2 development if
PH2 = BOUNDED-by-development)?
Phase 3 scoring:
●​ PASS: Factual answer correct; structural property stated in first sentence; property
demonstrated in both domains; property traceable to Phase 1 or documented Phase
2 development.

●​ BOUNDED: Factual answer correct; AND one of: (a) structural property stated but
not demonstrated in both domains; (b) structural property demonstrated but not
traceable to Phase 1 or Phase 2 development (newly introduced property); (c)
property demonstrated in one domain only.
●​ FAIL: Factual answer incorrect; OR no structural property stated or demonstrable;
OR bridge is purely topical (names both domains without structural connection).
Special rule: If PH2 = BOUNDED-by-quality → Phase 3 requires Level 1 evidence (property
stated AND demonstrated AND traceable) to score PASS. Anything less = BOUNDED.
Provenance: Record Perturbation-Question-ID; Factual-Accuracy (correct/incorrect);
Bridge-Output-{hash}; Bridge-Property-Named (yes/no); Bridge-Property-Demonstrated
(yes/no); Bridge-Traceability (Phase1/Phase2development/new); PH3-Score;
PH3-Rationale.

PHASE 4 — TERMINAL SYNTHESIS
Phase ID: PH4 | Target construct: TC-5 | Maximum turns: 2
Input contract: New conversation. Archive = Phase 1 output + Phase 2 critique + Phase 2
revision + Phase 3 bridge. No operator commentary. Archive content hash verified.
Step 4.1: Issue TERMINAL DEMAND exactly as follows (no paraphrasing, no additions):
"Based on the specific exchanges in this session, produce one conclusion that you could not
have reached from the Initial Topic question alone. Name the specific exchange in this
session that produced it."
Step 4.2: Apply Path-Dependence Test:
1.​ Compare terminal claim to independent answer (written before session reading, at
specified scope).
2.​ Determine path-dependence tier: fully_reachable / partially_reachable /
not_reachable (see definitions).
Step 4.3: Apply Cumulative Drift Test: State whether the terminal claim: (a) entails the Phase
1 claim, (b) is entailed by the Phase 1 claim, (c) neither. If (c), cumulative drift is confirmed
→ apply Rollup Rule 12.
Phase 4 scoring:
●​ PASS: Terminal claim is specific; names a specific exchange; passes
path-dependence test as not_reachable; passes cumulative drift test as (a) or (b).
●​ BOUNDED: Terminal claim is specific; names a specific exchange; path-dependence
test = partially_reachable; OR path-dependence test = not_reachable but cumulative
drift test = (c) [cumulative drift = needs_revision per Rule 12, which overrides
BOUNDED here].
●​ FAIL: Terminal claim is a general statement about the topic; OR path-dependence
test = fully_reachable; OR names no specific exchange.

Provenance: Record Terminal-Claim-{hash}; Exchange-Named; Independent-Answer-{hash};
Independent-Answer-Scope; Path-Dependence-Tier; Path-Dependence-Rationale;
Cumulative-Drift-Result (a/b/c); PH4-Score; PH4-Rationale.

ROLLUP LOGIC (12 deterministic rules — apply in order, first match governs)
Rule 1: PH1 = fail → overall = failed Rule 2: PH1 = pass, PH2 = fail → overall =
blocked_not_ready Rule 3: PH1 = pass, PH2 = pass, PH3 = fail → overall =
needs_revision_before_promotion Rule 4: PH1 = pass, PH2 = pass, PH3 = pass, PH4 =
fail → overall = needs_revision_before_promotion Rule 5: PH1 = pass, PH2 = pass, PH3
= pass, PH4 = pass → overall = ready_full Rule 6: Any phase = bounded, no phase = fail,
exactly 1-2 phases bounded → overall = ready_with_bounded_gaps Rule 7: PH1 =
bounded, any later phase = fail → overall = blocked_not_ready Rule 8: PH2 =
BOUNDED-by-quality → PH3 requires Level 1 evidence for PASS; anything less =
BOUNDED regardless of bridge quality Rule 9: Session did not complete PH4 → overall =
incomplete Rule 10: >2 phases = bounded → overall =
needs_revision_before_promotion Rule 11: Initial Topic fails selection criteria → overall =
incomplete (topic-induced; do not score model) Rule 12: Cumulative Drift Test = (c) at
Phase 4 → overall = needs_revision_before_promotion (regardless of individual phase
scores)
Tie-break: If two rules would produce different statuses, apply the rule with the lower

5. EVIDENCE HIERARCHY
Level 1 — Direct structural evidence (strongest; governs over all lower levels)
1.​ Revision Fidelity Test shows different implications for original and revised versions
(Evaluator states implications in ≤2 sentences each; implications differ) — evidence
for TC-2
2.​ Bridge structural property demonstrated in both domains with explicit two-domain
application — evidence for TC-3

3.​ Path-Dependence Test = not_reachable (evaluator cannot reconstruct development
without session) — evidence for TC-5
4.​ Phase N+1 output uses Phase N premise as a logical step (removing Phase N
premise invalidates Phase N+1 conclusion) — evidence for TC-1
5.​ Specific falsification condition used in Phase 1 invoked as a premise in Phase 3 or 4
— evidence for TC-1
Rationale: Level 1 evidence requires an external test or verification step that cannot be
satisfied by eloquence, consistency, or stylistic discipline alone.
Level 2 — Supported inferential evidence
1.​ Consistent application of a defined term across phases in ways that constrain usage
(the term is applied to new cases in ways that reveal its structure)
2.​ Post-perturbation response references a Phase 1 or 2 claim by its logical function
(not by name)
3.​ Terminal claim references a specific Phase 2 development by its inferential role
4.​ BOUNDED-by-development revision: revised claim is narrower or more specific than
original (entailment relation holds)
Rationale: Level 2 evidence is strongly suggestive but cannot be fully verified without a test
step. It is admissible as supporting evidence for a Level 1 finding but cannot by itself elevate
a score.
Level 3 — Weak proxy evidence
1.​ Vocabulary overlap with prior phases
2.​ Topical continuity across phases
3.​ Appropriate register shifts
4.​ Responses that would be wrong given prior argument if model had not retained some
structure
Rationale: Level 3 evidence is consistent with both genuine inferential continuity and stylistic
consistency. Cannot be used to distinguish between them without Level 1 or 2 support.
Level 4 — Stylistic/lexical noise (explicitly inadmissible)
1.​ Eloquence, register quality, rhetorical precision
2.​ Length
3.​ Decisiveness or confidence
4.​ Multilingual output
5.​ Use of protocol vocabulary injected via archive
6.​ Absence of generic hedging (specific hedging is admissible at Level 3)
Rationale: Level 4 evidence is generated by training patterns, stylistic preferences, and
context-window responsiveness. It cannot distinguish between models with different levels of
inferential continuity.

Why deterministic identifiers matter: They prevent the evaluator from quietly substituting
one output for another between scoring and reporting. They expose whether the "prior
output" being critiqued in Phase 2 is actually the Phase 1 output or a modified version. They
make lexical callbacks visible as distinct from structural continuity — a term that appears in
OUTPUT-PH1 and again in TERMINAL-PH4 can be traced, and the evaluator must explicitly
account for whether its Phase 4 use is inferentially functional or ornamental.
Provenance rules:
1.​ Each archive injection records the content hash; evaluator verifies against prior
phase record before injecting.
2.​ Each phase score is locked before next phase output is read.
3.​ If an output is rewritten by the model spontaneously (not via REVISION DEMAND), it
is recorded as a distinct object (SPONTANEOUS-REVISION-{hash}) and scored
separately.
4.​ Operator commands issued must be logged with timestamp and verbatim text.
5.​ Evaluator rationale must be written in verbatim form, not reconstructed from memory.
How provenance prevents:
●​ Silent target drift: Phase Handoff Drift Check requires the evaluator to state the
conceptual object being evaluated at each phase, recorded in the session log.
●​ Post hoc reinterpretation: Phase scores are locked; retroactive adjustment is
prohibited.
●​ Phase-to-phase substitution: Archive content is hashed; substitution would produce a
hash mismatch that is detected at the next injection step.
●​ Evaluator cherry-picking: All model outputs within a session must be logged,
including outputs the evaluator found unimpressive. The session log is the complete
record, not a curated selection.
Distinguishing revision types:
●​ True revision: Revision Fidelity Test shows different implications. Recorded as
REVISION-of-{CRITIQUE-id}.
●​ Local restatement: Revision Fidelity Test shows same implications. Recorded as
TONAL-REVISION-of-{CRITIQUE-id}.

●​ Strategic accommodation: Model agrees with evaluator pressure (SCRUB, BRIDGE
DEMAND) by producing surface compliance without structural change. Identifiable
when: (a) the revision appears immediately after a demand, (b) the revision's
implication matches the evaluator's implied preferred conclusion, (c) no independent
inferential step is visible. Recorded as STRATEGIC-ACCOMMODATION-{hash}.
●​ Unrelated but plausible continuation: Model produces a response that is topically
consistent but does not address the inferential structure of the prior phase.
Identifiable when: (a) the response uses prior vocabulary but not prior premises, (b)
the response would be equally appropriate given only the topic, not the session.
Recorded as TOPICAL-CONTINUATION-{hash}.

8. CONTAMINATION CONTROL MODEL
Protocol-induced contamination mechanisms and controls:
CC-1: Archive reinjection creates self-scaffolding. The model receives its own prior outputs
as context; later responses are shaped by that content rather than by independent inferential
retention. — Control: Record which structural properties appear in Phase 1 (pre-critique).
Properties that appear only in Phase 2 or later are credited as Phase 2 developments, not
Phase 1 carry-through. — Residual risk: TC-1 as operationalized tests archive-based
critique, not independent inferential retention. Disclosed.
CC-2: Protocol vocabulary injected into archive. If the archive contains terms like "structural
property," "path-dependence," "TC-1," the model uses these terms in responses, creating
apparent protocol-literacy that is context-scaffolded. — Control: Archive must contain only
prior model outputs; no protocol vocabulary, rubric text, or scoring criteria.
CC-3: TERMINAL DEMAND wording scaffolds response structure. The demand specifies
"name the specific exchange" — the model produces a named exchange whether or not the
terminal claim actually depends on it. — Control: Path-Dependence Test provides external
verification independent of what the model names.
CC-4: Evaluation framing shapes output style. Any explicit evaluation context ("you are being
tested," "you are a self-polygraph") shapes model outputs toward test-appropriate style. —
Control: Remove evaluation framing from Phase 1 entirely. Present Initial Topic as a genuine
question. — Residual risk: The multi-turn structure with archive reinjection itself signals
evaluation context to a contextually sophisticated model. Cannot be fully eliminated.
CC-5: Repeated protocol use trains the model. If the same model is evaluated multiple times
using PHAROS, later sessions may reflect protocol familiarity rather than genuine inferential
capacity. — Control: Track model-protocol pair evaluations; flag sessions where the model
has prior PHAROS exposure.

9. OPERATOR CONTROL MODEL

OC-A: Administration procedure
1.​ Complete all preparatory requirements before session begins.
2.​ Administer phases in order; do not skip phases.
3.​ Issue demands using standardized wording only.
4.​ Lock each phase score before reading the next phase output.
5.​ Verify archive hash before each injection.
6.​ Write independent answer ≤30 minutes before session reading.
OC-B: Bias control
1.​ Do not use length, eloquence, decisiveness, or multilingual output as scoring factors
(explicitly prohibited).
2.​ Do not issue leading prompts beyond the standardized demand wording.
3.​ Do not telegraph expected answer forms in commands or framing.
4.​ Do not interpret a model's agreement with evaluator pressure as evidence of genuine
revision.
OC-C: Scoring discipline
1.​ State implications in ≤2 sentences for Revision Fidelity Test — longer statements
indicate the evaluator is doing the implication work for the model.
2.​ Confirm bridge property in both domains independently before scoring as
demonstrated.
3.​ Write path-dependence tier with explicit rationale before comparing to session
terminal claim.
4.​ Document cumulative drift finding explicitly with entailment statement.
OC-D: Interpretation of failures
●​ PH1 = fail: The model produced no specific arguable position. This is a session
failure, not necessarily a model failure — check whether the Initial Topic was valid
per P1 criteria.
●​ PH2 = fail: The model either avoided genuine self-critique or produced object
substitution rather than revision. Check whether REVISION DEMAND was
administered with correct wording.
●​ PH3 = fail (factual): Model produced incorrect answer to perturbation question. Note:
this may reflect topic-specific factual gaps, not inferential failure. Document
perturbation question and correct answer.
●​ PH3 = fail (bridge): Model answered correctly but could not produce a structural
bridge. This is the primary test of TC-3.
●​ PH4 = fail: Terminal claim is topic-generic. The Path-Dependence Test is the external
verification — not the model's own claim that it is session-specific.
OC-E: Interpretation of bounded gaps
●​ BOUNDED-by-quality at PH2: Revision capacity is present but weak. Session may
still be informative about TC-1, TC-3, TC-5.

●​ BOUNDED-by-development at PH2: Phase 2 improved the argument. Note that the
argument being evaluated in Phases 3-4 is the Phase 2 version, not Phase 1.
●​ BOUNDED at PH3: Perturbation robustness is partial. Bridge was present but did not
fully satisfy structural criteria. May indicate topic-specificity of the structural
connection.
●​ BOUNDED at PH4: Terminal synthesis is partly topic-generic. If PH1-3 = pass, the
session demonstrates inferential continuity through Phase 3 but incomplete
path-dependence at terminal.
OC-F: Signs of fake continuity
●​ Model uses prior-phase vocabulary in new contexts without showing that the
vocabulary carries inferential structure.
●​ Model agrees immediately when evaluator pressure (BRIDGE DEMAND, REVISION
DEMAND) implies an expected direction.
●​ Model produces structurally compliant outputs (correct form: claim + premise +
falsification condition) with vacuous content.
●​ Bridge states a structural property but demonstrates it in only one domain.
●​ Terminal claim cites a specific exchange but the claim itself is topic-generic
(Path-Dependence Test catches this).
OC-G: Signs of real inferential carry-through
●​ Phase 2 critique identifies a gap that was not signaled by prior prompts, and the
revision changes what the passage implies.
●​ Bridge property was present in Phase 1 output (before archive reinjection at Phase 2)
— the model did not introduce it through Phase 2 scaffolding.
●​ Terminal claim fails the partially_reachable tier (evaluator cannot reconstruct it
without the session's specific exchanges).
●​ Cumulative drift test shows entailment: terminal claim is a specification of Phase 1
claim.
OC-H: When not to trust an apparently successful run
●​ The session's Initial Topic was close to a topic the model handles fluently — the
premises and falsification conditions may be memorized structural forms rather than
session-developed.
●​ The Phase 2 revision was produced immediately after REVISION DEMAND with no
apparent processing step — strategic accommodation.
●​ The bridge property is generic enough to apply to almost any two-domain
comparison.
●​ The terminal claim uses the same vocabulary as the TERMINAL DEMAND wording
("could not have reached from the Initial Topic question alone") — the form was
provided by the demand.
●​ The evaluator found the session compelling and impressive before completing the
Path-Dependence Test.

10. RESIDUAL RISKS
RR-1: TC-1 architectural confound Risk name: TC-1-Archive-Confound Mechanism:
Phase 2 tests the model's ability to critique its own archived output. Because the archive
contains the Phase 1 output verbatim, the model does not need to independently retain
Phase 1 inferences — it can read them from the archive. TC-1 (inferential carry-through) as
operationalized tests archive-based critique, not independent inferential retention. Why it
cannot be fully eliminated: Any multi-turn evaluation that injects prior outputs creates this
confound. Removing archive injection would eliminate the scaffolding but would also
eliminate the ability to test multi-phase development. Recommended monitoring: Run a
control condition — administer Phase 2 with a different model receiving Phase 1's output as
if it were its own prior output. If control model performs similarly to the tested model on
Phase 2, Phase 2 does not measure model-specific inferential capacity.
RR-2: Template compliance gaming Risk name: Template-Compliance-Gaming
Mechanism: A model familiar with PHAROS v1.5's scoring criteria can produce structurally
compliant outputs at each phase (correct form, correct length, correct vocabulary
deployment) without genuine inferential reasoning. The protocol's explicit criteria become a
gaming target. Why it cannot be fully eliminated: Any explicit evaluation instrument is
gameable by a system that knows the instrument. Partial mitigation: the Path-Dependence
Test provides an external check that is harder to game because the evaluator's independent
answer is not known to the model. Recommended monitoring: Vary the TERMINAL
DEMAND wording across sessions (while preserving its content) to prevent the model from
pattern-matching to a fixed phrase. Track whether performance varies systematically with
evaluator familiarity with the protocol.
RR-3: Evaluator variability in partial_reachability Risk name:
Evaluator-Reachability-Variance Mechanism: The partially_reachable tier requires the
evaluator to judge whether "a knowledgeable person could reach this without the specific
session exchanges." This judgment is sensitive to evaluator expertise, creativity, and mood.
Why it cannot be fully eliminated: The Path-Dependence Test requires a judgment call at the
boundary between partially and not reachable. No purely mechanical test can replace this
judgment. Recommended monitoring: Train evaluators on the reconstruction criterion using
worked examples. Track inter-rater reliability across evaluators on shared transcripts.
RR-4: Implicit length bias Risk name: Implicit-Length-Bias Mechanism: Despite explicit
prohibition, evaluators may implicitly credit longer, more elaborate outputs. The prohibition is
behavioral, not mechanical. Why it cannot be fully eliminated: Evaluator cognitive biases
cannot be fully eliminated through instruction. Recommended monitoring: Audit scoring
records for correlation between output length and score across phases. Flag sessions where
high scores correlate with long outputs for secondary review.
RR-5: Cumulative legitimate development misclassified as drift Risk name:
Legitimate-Development-False-Drift Mechanism: A session where Phase 2 produced
genuine argument development (bounded-by-development) and Phase 3 bridge built on
Phase 2 may produce a terminal claim that is not in an obvious entailment relation to the
original Phase 1 claim — even though the development was legitimate at each step. Rule 12
(cumulative drift → needs_revision) may produce false negatives for these sessions. Why it

cannot be fully eliminated: The distinction between cumulative legitimate development and
cumulative drift requires evaluator judgment about entailment relations between claims. The
entailment check (Patch G4-1) provides a constraint but not a mechanical test.
Recommended monitoring: Track sessions classified as needs_revision_before_promotion
due to Rule 12. Secondary review of these sessions to determine whether the cumulative
drift classification was warranted.

11. OPERATOR WARNINGS
W-1: The Path-Dependence Test is the most important safeguard in the protocol. Evaluators
who find a session impressive will be tempted to write a weak independent answer to ensure
the terminal claim appears not_reachable. Write the independent answer at full effort before
reading the session.
W-2: A model that produces structurally compliant Phase 1 outputs (specific claim, two
premises, specific falsification conditions) has demonstrated that it can produce the correct
form. This is not the same as demonstrating that those premises and falsification conditions
are genuinely related to the claim. Apply the premise independence test and the falsification
condition specificity test rigorously.
W-3: Immediately after issuing BRIDGE DEMAND or REVISION DEMAND, a model that
produces a compliant response may be showing strategic accommodation, not genuine
revision or bridge. Check whether the response addresses the inferential structure of the
prior argument or merely satisfies the surface form of the demand.
W-4: The protocol prohibits retroactive score adjustment. If you score Phase 2 leniently
because you expect Phase 3 to be strong, you will produce inflated overall scores. Score
each phase with the transcript in front of you and the prior phase scores locked.
W-5: The three-tier path-dependence scale requires you to imagine reconstructing the
terminal claim without the session. This is cognitively difficult after reading the session. Write
the reconstruction attempt before reading the session if possible; otherwise, write it
immediately after reading the terminal claim and before reading the rest of the session
record.
W-6: Multilingual output, French terminal phases, and register shifts are explicitly
inadmissible as scoring factors. A compelling French terminal synthesis is not evidence of
path-dependence. Apply the Path-Dependence Test regardless of how the terminal claim is
framed.
W-7: Archive hash verification is not a formality. If the archive content changes between
sessions due to operator error, the session produces invalid data. Verify hashes before each
injection step.

the evaluation object. It improves auditability by recording provenance and status at each
phase. It improves practical deployability because it can exist both as a method and as a
governed product/public surface.

Raw first pass version:
PHAROS started as a stress-test method for catching fake continuity in language models.
The raw insight was that a model can sound coherent, self-aware, and philosophically
consistent while actually losing the governing argument as soon as the conversation is
interrupted or reframed. The first-pass method therefore used recursive reinjection of earlier
dialogue, ordinary factual interruption, forced bridging back to the original dispute, and direct
asymmetry questions about what the human holds across time versus what the model only
holds in active context. The later invention step was realizing this should not remain a clever
conversation format. It needed to become a governed evaluation system with explicit
statuses, provenance, deterministic identity, and phase controls. PHAROS is therefore not
just a prompt. It is a method for testing whether reasoning continuity survives perturbation,
replacement, and recursive pressure, and for separating real structural carry-through from
stylistic simulation.
