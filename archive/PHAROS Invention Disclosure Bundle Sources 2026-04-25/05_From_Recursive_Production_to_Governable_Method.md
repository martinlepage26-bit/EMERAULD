### 1. Abstract
This document provides a comprehensive account of a research program that began in late
2024 and culminated in the formalization of the PHAROS AI Governance Protocol. The
program was driven by a singular scientific intention: to understand how authority is
produced, stabilized, and contested within mediated systems—be they ritual, narrative,
institutional, or computational. When this inquiry was applied to the researcher's own
AI-assisted production process, it encountered a critical failure condition: recursive
AI-assisted workflows could generate output faster than they could generate decision clarity,
leading to evidence drift, authority inflation, and structural fragility. This document narrates
the evolution of the response to that failure. It traces the arc from theoretical and
ethnographic beginnings in queer Paganism and digital culture, through the development of
a recursive governance architecture, to the rigorous, self-reflexive redesign of that
architecture into a deployable, auditable governance method for AI systems. The final output
is the PHAROS Recursive AI Governance Protocol, a transdisciplinary science of mediated
authority designed to make legitimacy traceable and contestable.
### 2. The Arc of Inquiry: Research Timeline and Context
The research timeline is best understood as four interconnected phases, each building on
the prior one, rather than a simple chronological sequence. The underlying rationale
remained stable: to study how claims to truth, legitimacy, and authenticity are assembled,
naturalized, and challenged.
#### Phase 1: Foundational Inquiry (September – December 2024)
This phase established the core objects of study. The work was centered on **queer
Paganism, neo-shamanism, Wicca, and contemporary ritual life**, particularly in Montreal.
The rationale was to show how these alternative communities function as "laboratories of
legitimacy," revealing how truth claims about identity, lineage, gender, and belonging are
produced, mediated by books, commodities, and platforms. The scientific function was
epistemic and diagnostic. Parallel work began in **cultural and media analysis**, using texts
like *Buffy the Vampire Slayer* and *Jurassic Park* as "analytic laboratories" to study how
power, embodiment, and authority are staged and governed in compressed narrative form.
This period was foundational, clarifying the conceptual tools—authenticity, glitch,
recursion—that would later be extended to AI systems.

#### Phase 2: Methodological Elaboration and Laboratory Formation (Spring – Summer
2025)
This phase saw a crucial shift. The research became explicitly **methodological**. The work
expanded into two new fronts. First, **public health research** (on DoxyPEP, STI prevention,
etc.) brought the question of legitimacy into a more applied institutional setting, revealing
how evidence and care structures classify and govern vulnerable populations. Second, the
inquiry turned back on itself. Recognizing that its own AI-assisted production was itself a
mediated system of authority, the researcher began building controlled production
environments—**Hexa, Wheels of Will**—to observe, stress, and govern recursive AI
behavior. This was the formation of a "laboratory." The first governance constraints (AI Gov
Constitution, Voice Operator) were imposed, marking the moment when the system began to
move from pure production to governed production. The work started generating **loop
papers**—texts excluded from the main workflow that would later return as diagnostic
objects.
#### Phase 3: Architecture Formalization and Stress Testing (Late Summer – December
2025)
By this stage, the work had moved beyond analyzing mediated systems to using them as
part of the inquiry itself. The central problem became **governability under recursion**. The
research output was split into distinct classes: **root-method** texts (like *Recursive AI
Governance as Executable Method: The Very Long Narrative*) that documented the
architecture's construction, and **analytical apex** texts (like *Discursive Authority Under
Recursive Pressure*) that theorized the highest-order implications. The architecture was
layered into first, second, and third-order controls to manage instability, executable
governance, and continuity. The **protocol stack** (Sealed Card, Violet Gem, ALAMBIC,
Master Key) was developed to standardize transitions and transformations. This was the
**condensation phase**, where governance was formalized from a conceptual orientation
into explicit, inspectable, and revisable layers.
#### Phase 4: Protocol Redesign and Implementation (January – March 2026)
This phase was marked by a rigorous, recursive self-audit. The initial protocol was subjected
to a five-pass redesign, each pass diagnosing defects, extracting invariants, and hardening
the method against adversarial attacks. The goal was to break the closed evidentiary loop
where the method validated itself. The result was the **PHAROS Recursive AI Governance
Protocol (v5.0)**, a terminal, stable version with explicit target constructs, evidence
hierarchies, provenance rules, and operator controls. Parallel to this, the findings were
translated into an operational, public-facing form: the **PHAROS AI Governance Practice**,
codified in its **Master Standard Operating Procedure (SOP)** . This phase represents the
transition from method-building to institutional implementation, with PHAROS acting as the
public-facing surface and CompassAI and Aurora as standalone applications embodying the
research.
### 3. The Method Itself: The PHAROS Recursive AI Governance Protocol

The PHAROS method is not a theory or a policy document; it is an **executable governance
architecture**. Its core innovation is to treat governance not as an external overlay on AI
production, but as an embedded, recursive part of the production process itself.
The method is structured as a **ten-stage pipeline** that transforms a bounded corpus of
materials into interface-bound operational controls. Its key components are:
- **Staged Pipeline:** A linear sequence of stages (Corpus Formation → Intent Embedding
→ Multi-Model Rendering → Triangulation → Recursive Return → Control Extraction →
Determinism Hardening → Deployment Binding → Logging & Audit Trail → Failure
Harvesting). Each stage has a declared purpose, a governance question, required
deliverables, and a completion condition.
- **Target Constructs:** The protocol measures five core constructs:
1. **Inferential Carry-Through (TC-1):** The preservation of logical dependency chains
across turns.
2. **Revision Fidelity (TC-2):** Whether a rewrite changes what the passage *implies*,
not just its phrasing.
3. **Perturbation Robustness (TC-3):** The ability to handle a topic interruption and
recover the prior thread through structural reference.
4. **Terminal Path-Dependence (TC-5):** Whether the final conclusion could only have
emerged from the specific session trajectory.
- **Evidence Hierarchy:** The protocol establishes a clear hierarchy of evidence, from Level
1 (Direct structural evidence) to Level 4 (Stylistic/lexical noise, which is explicitly
inadmissible). This prevents eloquent prose from being mistaken for rigorous reasoning.
- **Control Format:** Any operational control must name a risk pattern, a workflow interface,
an accountable actor, an evidence artifact, and a review interval. A control missing any of
these is a "governance aspiration."
- **Deterministic Rollup Logic:** The protocol uses 12 deterministic rules to assign an overall
status (e.g., `ready_full`, `ready_with_bounded_gaps`, `blocked_not_ready`) based on
pass/fail/bounded results from each phase, removing evaluator judgment from the final
classification.
- **Anti-Gaming & Anti-Contamination Controls:** The method is explicitly designed to be
resistant to "performative compliance." It includes mechanisms like independent
spot-checks, verifiable timestamps for probe classifications, and a prohibition on
retrospective rationalization of failures.
### 4. Conceptual and Theoretical Background
The PHAROS method is rooted in a transdisciplinary theoretical framework:
- **Queer Theory & Ritual Studies:** The initial insight that "ritual worlds produce truth
claims" is the foundation for analyzing how *any* system (including AI) produces legitimacy.
Concepts like **glitch, stutter, and interruption**—derived from queer and performance
theory—become diagnostic tools for exposing the seams in systems that claim to be
seamless.

- **Digital Culture & Media Analysis:** The method treats media and platforms not as neutral
channels but as "infrastructures of mediation" that actively transform the knowledge, identity,
and authority they carry. The "laboratory" approach to analyzing texts like *Buffy* and
*Jurassic Park* provided a model for treating AI systems as similar sites of compressed
social theory.
- **Science & Technology Studies (STS) & Actor-Network Theory (ANT):** The focus on
"recursion," "infrastructure," and "distributed agency" (tools that "name through me") is a
direct inheritance from STS. The method views governance as an assemblage of humans,
non-humans (AI), procedures, and texts.
- **Epistemic Justice & Critical Disability Studies:** The inquiry into AI and authorship is
explicitly framed around the question of who gets recognized as a "valid knower." The
method seeks to separate rigor from ableist performance norms, treating the body (stutter,
illness) not as a contaminant but as a site where the cost of legitimacy is paid.
- **Governance & Institutional Theory:** The final move is a translation of these critical
insights into the language of institutional control: risk classification, audit trails, and
accountability mechanisms. This is not a retreat from critique but its operationalization.
### 5. Analysis and Findings
The program's most significant finding is that **recursive AI production is structurally fragile
without embedded governance**. The key failures identified are not technical glitches but
governance failures:
- **Evidence Drift:** Generated artifacts are treated as source material, eroding the
evidentiary basis of future conclusions.
- **Authority Inflation:** Fluency and repetition create an illusion of credibility, leading to
"performative compliance" where the form of governance is satisfied without its substance.
- **Method Lock:** Governance procedures become self-referential and stop adapting to
new evidence.
The rigorous self-audit of the PHAROS method itself revealed a critical insight: a method
that governs recursive AI production must itself be governed recursively. This led to the
creation of the **five-pass redesign**, which serves as a case study in how to break a closed
evidentiary loop. The process of analyzing the protocol's own defects, applying adversarial
stress tests, and patching its vulnerabilities demonstrated that a governance system can
only be trustworthy if its own construction is auditable and its limitations are disclosed as
residual risks.
The final architecture is thus not a static set of rules but a **"bounded non-final" system**. It
expects and accommodates revision, but it structures that revision with explicit gates,
provenance, and justifications to prevent uncontrolled drift.
### 6. Implications for AI Governance at Large

The PHAROS research program offers several profound implications for the broader field of
AI governance:
1. **Governance Must Be Recursive:** AI systems are not static; they are part of dynamic,
recursive workflows. Governance cannot be a one-time compliance check. It must be a
continuous, embedded process that evolves with the system it governs.
2. **Distinguish Form from Substance:** The current field is rife with "performative
compliance"—the creation of ethics statements and policies that satisfy a structural
requirement but have no operational effect. PHAROS demonstrates that governance must
be operationalized into interface-bound controls with named actors, evidence artifacts, and
review intervals. A control is not a policy; it is a procedure.
3. **Treat Lineage and Provenance as First-Order Concerns:** The single most important
artifact in any governance-sensitive system is the audit trail. PHAROS's focus on
source/derived distinctions, hash verification, and deterministic identifiers shows how to
make AI workflows reconstructable. Without traceability, trust is impossible.
4. **Incorporate Failure as Evidence:** A robust governance system learns from its own
gaps. PHAROS’s "failure doctrine" treats abandoned branches, uneven oversight, and
archive instability as governance evidence, not noise. This is a direct counter to the
industry’s tendency to suppress or hide failures.
5. **Acknowledge and Disclose Residual Risks:** No governance system can be perfect.
The PHAROS protocol explicitly names its residual risks (e.g., the TC-1 archive confound,
template compliance gaming) and provides monitoring approaches. This honesty is more
valuable than a false claim of completeness.
6. **Translate Critical Theory into Operational Language:** The program demonstrates that
concepts from queer theory, STS, and critical media studies are not merely academic. They
can be operationalized into executable governance tools to make power traceable and
contestable. The line from "ritual worlds produce truth claims" to an enforceable control
register is a direct, albeit long, one.
### 7. Conclusion
This research program began by studying how authority is made in marginalized ritual
spaces. It ended by building a method to govern authority in one of the most powerful
technological systems of our time. The arc from **queer Paganism to AI governance** is not
a contradiction; it is a coherent scientific trajectory. It is grounded in the conviction that to
understand power, one must study it where it is most visible (on the margins) and most
urgent (in the infrastructures that govern modern life). The PHAROS method is the concrete
output of this conviction: a rigorous, self-reflexive, and executable science of mediated
authority, designed to ensure that the systems we build remain accountable to the people
they serve.
