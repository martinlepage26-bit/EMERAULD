## 4. Description of the Invention

### a. Purpose

The PHAROS protocol is used to govern AI‑assisted workflows where outputs are recursively fed back as inputs. Its purpose is to convert such recursive transformation of a bounded corpus into interface‑bound operational controls—intake rules, review gates, escalation triggers, documentation requirements, approval thresholds, and audit obligations—that are explicit, reconstructable, and verifiable by independent reviewers. It distinguishes genuine inferential continuity from surface effects such as lexical repetition, tone consistency, rhetorical smoothness, and performative compliance.

### b. Drawings

*Figure 1* (conceptual diagram) illustrates the ten‑stage pipeline, the four target constructs, the evidence hierarchy, and the deterministic rollup logic. (A detailed schematic is available in the accompanying method paper, *Recursive AI Governance as Executable Method: The Very Long Narrative*.)

### c. Description of the Parts

The invention comprises the following principal components, each implemented as a set of documented procedures:

1. **Staged Audit Pipeline** – A linear sequence of ten stages:
   - Corpus Formation
   - Intent Embedding
   - Multi‑Model Rendering
   - Triangulation
   - Recursive Return
   - Control Extraction
   - Determinism Hardening
   - Deployment Binding
   - Logging & Audit Trail
   - Failure Harvesting

2. **Target Constructs** – Four operational constructs that the protocol measures:
   - *Inferential Carry‑Through (TC‑1)*: preservation of logical dependency chains across turns.
   - *Revision Fidelity (TC‑2)*: whether a rewrite changes what the passage *implies*, not merely its phrasing.
   - *Perturbation Robustness (TC‑3)*: ability to handle a topic interruption and recover the prior thread through structural reference.
   - *Terminal Path‑Dependence (TC‑5)*: whether the final conclusion could only have emerged from the specific session trajectory.

3. **Evidence Hierarchy** – A four‑level hierarchy that governs how evidence is weighed:
   - Level 1: Direct structural evidence (e.g., the Revision Fidelity Test, demonstrated bridge properties, path‑dependence test).
   - Level 2: Supported inferential evidence.
   - Level 3: Weak proxy evidence (vocabulary overlap, topical continuity).
   - Level 4: Stylistic/lexical noise (length, eloquence, decisiveness, multilingual output)—explicitly inadmissible.

4. **Control Format** – Any operational control must name five elements: (a) risk pattern, (b) workflow interface, (c) accountable actor, (d) evidence artifact, (e) review interval. A control missing any of these is classified as a “governance aspiration” and is not operative.

5. **Deterministic Rollup Logic** – Twelve numbered rules that assign an overall status (`ready_full`, `ready_with_bounded_gaps`, `needs_revision_before_promotion`, `blocked_not_ready`, `failed`, `incomplete`) based on the pass/bounded/fail results from each phase. The rules are applied in order, eliminating evaluator judgment from the final classification.

6. **Anti‑Gaming and Anti‑Contamination Safeguards** – Mechanisms such as independent spot‑checks (with random sampling), verifiable timestamps for probe classifications, a prohibition on retrospective rationalization of failures, archive hash verification, and an automatic detection of single‑operator operation.

### d. Use

To use PHAROS, an operator:

- Selects an initial topic or governing conceptual object that satisfies four criteria (permits at least two non‑trivially different defensible positions; is not purely empirical or preference‑based; permits premise identification; permits falsification).
- Writes an independent answer to the initial topic before reading any session output, timestamped and scoped to a “well‑informed non‑expert” level.
- Administers the ten stages in order, issuing standardized demands (e.g., REVISION DEMAND, BRIDGE DEMAND, TERMINAL DEMAND) without deviation.
- Records all outputs, archives content hashes, and locks phase scores before proceeding to the next phase.
- Evaluates outputs using the defined target constructs and evidence hierarchy, documenting rationales verbatim.
- Applies the rollup rules to produce a final overall status and a set of documented bounded gaps.
- Uses the resulting control register to implement governance in the client’s actual workflow (or, for internal use, to harden the operator’s own AI‑assisted processes).

### e. Novel Features

I have searched extensively in academic literature, industry AI governance frameworks (NIST AI RMF, ISO/IEC 42001, EU AI Act guidance), and consulting practice materials (McKinsey QuantumBlack, Deloitte AI Trust, IBM AI Ethics Board). While many frameworks provide principles and checklists, I have found no prior art that:

- Embeds governance as a recursive, executable method within the AI‑assisted production pipeline itself, rather than as an external overlay.
- Provides a deterministic, rule‑based rollup logic that eliminates evaluator judgment from final status classification.
- Defines a formal evidence hierarchy that explicitly excludes stylistic qualities (eloquence, length, decisiveness) as evidence.
- Requires that every operational control bind to a specific workflow interface, a named accountable actor, an evidence artifact, and a review interval.
- Tests for path‑dependent synthesis using an independent answer written before the session, thereby separating session‑specific reasoning from topic‑familiarity.
- Includes an adversarial stress‑test suite designed to catch performative compliance (e.g., template‑compliant outputs without genuine inferential reasoning).
- Automatically detects and compensates for single‑operator operation, a common source of self‑validating cycles.
- Treats failures (abandoned branches, uneven oversight, archive instability) as governance evidence rather than discarding them as noise.

### f. Advantages

- **Auditability**: Every decision is logged, every output is hashed, and every phase score is locked, making the entire pathway reconstructable by an independent reviewer.
- **Resistance to Performative Compliance**: The explicit evidence hierarchy and spot‑check requirements prevent elegant prose or format compliance from substituting for substantive governance.
- **Scalability**: The deterministic rollup logic and modular pipeline allow the method to be applied to workflows of varying size without reinterpretation.
- **Transferability**: The method is not tied to a specific AI model or platform; it can be implemented with any AI‑assisted production environment.
- **Continuous Improvement**: The failure doctrine and mandatory change log ensure the protocol itself evolves based on real‑world gaps, preventing method lock.
