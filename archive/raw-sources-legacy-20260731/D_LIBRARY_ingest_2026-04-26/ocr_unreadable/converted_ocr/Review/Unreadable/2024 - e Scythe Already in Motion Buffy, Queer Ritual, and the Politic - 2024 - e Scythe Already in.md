e Scythe Already in Motion: Buffy, Queer Ritual, and the Politics of Glit
Book Prospectus

Author: Martin Lepage, PhD

Overview
This book explores the post-canonical life of Buffy the Vampire Slayer fragments in queer and trans
magical practice. These are not nostalgic fan references; they are operative ritual technologies media fragments deployed to anchor grief vigils, puncture solemnity, shield communities, and keep
ritual space open.
Drawing on multi-year ethnographic fieldwork in Montreal's queer Pagan communities (2020-2024)
and close readings of key Buffy "glitch moments" - narrative ruptures, tonal misalignments, camp
disruptions, and refusals of moral closure - the book develops a hybrid theoretical frame combining
media/fan studies, queer theory, and ritual studies. It argues that these fragments operate as
distributed infrastructure for ritual life, circulating between physical and digital space, continually
recharged through recursive reuse.

Introduction - The Scythe Already in Motion
Introduces the central concept of the "glitch" as a moment where narrative, character, or tone resists
canonical coherence. These glitches open a space for ritual insertion, becoming "ritual openings."
Outlines the method of recursive ethnography and situates the author's double position as both
embedded practitioner and ethnographer.

Chapter 1 - Willow Rosenberg: Recursion and Saturated Grief
Willow's resurrection chant in Bargaining is more than a plot device; it is a ritual loop embedded
within the narrative. In canon, Willow refuses to let the working end, stretching the spell beyond its

intended limits, embodying grief's resistance to closure. In queer ritual spaces, this fragment
appears as slowed, distorted, endlessly looped audio, anchoring all-night vigils and grief-saturated
kitchen rituals.

Chapter 2 - Anya Jenkins: Ethical Misalignment and Mourning Without Script
Anya's raw, awkward outburst in The Body has a strange life outside the show. In canon, she
refuses to perform grief according to script, exposing the emptiness of ritual etiquette in the face of
death. In the field, these lines are repeated without attribution in queer grief circles, functioning as
permission for others to speak unscripted truth.

Chapter 3 - Faith Lehane: Outlaw Kinship and the Refusal of Domestication
Faith's arc is defined by her refusal to be domesticated - neither fully villain nor accepted hero, she
occupies a liminal, dangerous kinship role. In queer ritual life, Faith's fragments guard against
sanctimony and moral policing in magical groups.

Chapter 4 - Harmony Kendall: Camp Disruption and Glamour as Ward
Harmony's refusal to deepen - her vampirism as lifestyle brand, her comedic incompetence - is often
dismissed as fluff. This chapter re-reads Harmony as a camp disruptor, whose frivolity functions as
magical protection.

Chapter 5 - Systems of Recursion: From Glitch to Ritual Infrastructure
This section synthesises the four glitch-types into a systemic account of how media fragments
become ritual architecture. It maps how fragments move between rooftops, kitchen-tables, grief
circles, and encrypted group chats.

Chapter 6 - Methodological Loops: Recursive Ethnography in Queer Ritual
Worlds
Explores the challenges and ethics of studying a living ritual system from within. It addresses double
accountability - to community trust and to scholarly rigour - and discusses techniques for following
fragment circulation without freezing them as static "data."

Chapter 7 - Queer Ritual Systems Beyond Buffy
Extends the analysis to other media-ritual entanglements. Compares Buffy's ritual afterlife with
fragments from other fandoms and popular texts that have been operationalised in queer magical
life.

Chapter 8 - Glitch Futures and Ritual Openings
Speculates on how glitch-based ritual practice will adapt in the future. Explores emerging platforms,

shifting fan ecologies, and cross-fandom ritual systems.

Conclusion - The Spell Was Never Meant to Close
Brings together the book's central arguments: refusal, recursion, misalignment, and disruption are
not by-products of queer ritual life but its sustaining logics.
