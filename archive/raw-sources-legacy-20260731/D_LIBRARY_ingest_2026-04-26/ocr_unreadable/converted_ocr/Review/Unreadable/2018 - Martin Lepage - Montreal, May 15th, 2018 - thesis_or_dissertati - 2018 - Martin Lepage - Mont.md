Dear Professor Young, Director of the School of Religion,

I would like to be considered for the position of Baker Postdoctoral Fellow in the School
of Religion at Queen’s University. I received a Ph.D. in Religious studies from
Université du Québec à Montréal in March 2017, and I am convinced that my research
and teaching experience, as well as my interest for the different aspects of academia have
adequately prepared me for this position. Furthermore, I believe that my expertise on
contemporary ritual practices and material culture could bring a both unique and rigorous
contribution to the School of Religion. In fact, this postdoctoral position would provide
me with a great opportunity to delve further into some aspects of my existing research on
alternative spiritualities and contemporary Paganism, namely their relationship to
material culture, that had to remain peripheral in the context of my dissertation.
In today’s Canadian culturally diverse climate, religion is a major aspect through which
people and communities come into contact and develop connections. Whether it is
through the face of any institutionalized organization, churches of every denomination or
spiritual affiliation, or through individual small daily rituals, phrases or objects
reminding people of their most cherished values, religious discourses and practices are at
the heart of social relations. Furthermore, they act and are performed in numerous ways
not unlike gender, in terms of social roles, social norms, religious and national
mythologies, individual and collective narratives, etc.: they entail social recognition,
sense of belonging, sense of community, identity politics, civil rights, power, and more.
The underlying perspective, which stems from my training in socioanthropology of

religion, is that, in our cultural context, contemporary religiosities often constitute loci of
agency more than of normativity. It is this perspective that I could bring into the
academic and research dimensions of the School of Religion at Queen’s University.
I am interested in this position for a variety of reasons: it would allow me to investigate
the relationship between gender, material culture and the transmission or transgression of
social norms around religious and embodied identities. I would be particularly interested
in looking at how collective and individual gender practices share a common set of ritual
tools and performances that help negotiate with normative tradition, modify it or carry it
on. These tools, as physical and aesthetic artifacts, exist inside and outside religious
experience. They permeate every aspect of culture and come into play with how
identities are created and negotiated. The Baker Postdoctoral Fellowship would allow my
research to provide a greater understanding of this deep-seated modern social
phenomenon.
Because of my graduate training and, as you will see in the next parts of my dossier, my
research interests and teaching experience, I am uniquely qualified for this position. It
would be my pleasure to work in a stimulating community such as Queen’s University.
In regard to Queen’s employment equity and diversity policy, it is worth mentioning that
I identify as a cisgender gay man living with HIV, and that my stuttering, as you will
learn about in my dossier, although not recognized as a disability, has surely worked
against me before in integrating academia. Be assured that I will be happy to meet with
you any time you deem appropriate, and to answer your questions about my academic
path and my professional endeavors.

Thank you greatly for your consideration,
