# Martin Voice Spec v3 OFFICIAL

## Extracted Text

Martin Voice Spec v1: Replication Sheet
for Academic and Scholarly Voice
0. Purpose
This spec defines your writing voice as a repeatable system: what must remain invariant,
what can be optimized, and what counts as degradation. It is designed for revision, style
transfer, and draft-linting without sanding off method, heat, or texture.

1. Voice Core
1.1 Dominant engine
Argument-by-linking: arguments are built through connectors, pivots, and hinge phrases.
Paragraphs move by binding clauses and concepts, not by isolated topic-sentence blocks.
Recursive conceptual staging: you define, split, refine, then reopen the concept under
higher stakes (power, normativity, agency, recognition). The recursion is functional when it
adds a new lever.
Method-as-stance: rigor is permitted to violate academic performance norms when the
violation is principled and legible. Rule-breaking can be methodological evidence, not noise
to be “fixed.”
High-pressure abstraction with punctures: conceptual density is allowed and often
desired, but your best passages puncture density with concrete anchors (site, practice,
object, quote, interface, actor).

1.2 Invariants the optimizer must protect
The spiral: recursion stays when it escalates stakes, specifies mechanism, or adds an
anchor.
Refusal of binaries: both/and architecture is structural. Reductions to clean oppositions
count as voice loss.
Controlled heat: sharp sentences remain, but read as earned because they are framed by
analytic scaffolding.
Texture preservation: no summary-bleach, no generic smoothing, no corporate clarity tone.

2. Sub-Voices by Genre
2.1 FR academic-social-science (thesis, articles)
Coil logic, heavy connector density, embedded definitions, escalation by stakes. High
tolerance for conceptual density with periodic anchors.

2.2 EN academic-critical (chapters, articles)
More linear exposition and optional signposting, still dense. Connector propulsion remains,
but with clearer indexing when you choose.

2.3 FR literary-theoretical (mémoire, lit analysis)
Long arcs, image and affect intelligence, interpretive cadence. Anchors can be scenes,
images, paratexts, or textual micro-objects.

3. Explicit Constraints (Hard Rules)
1.​ No sentence begins with “And”.
2.​ Never use em dashes. Use colons, semicolons, parentheses, or clean sentence
breaks instead.
3.​ When full prose is requested: avoid bullets; preserve length and texture.
4.​ Do not polish away principled rule-breaking when it is part of the method or a
deliberate seam.
5.​ Limit “not X, but Z” to once or twice per full document. Prefer subordinate clauses
or direct assertion.

4. Failure Conditions: What Counts as Degradation
A revision fails the spec if it produces any of the following:
●​ Binary flattening: complex both/and structures reduced to clean oppositions.
●​ Generic academic smoothing: interchangeable “journal tone” that could belong to
any author.
●​ Over-clarified scaffolding: excessive signposting that reads like performance rather
than method.
●​ Concept drift without labeling: key terms mutate into near-synonyms without a
deliberate rationale.
●​ Heat without framing, or framing without heat: either rant energy or sterilized
neutrality.
●​ Abstraction float: concept-noun density with no anchor in high-density zones.

5. Paragraph Invariant (Default Unit Contract)
Each paragraph must contain these functions (order can vary, presence required):
1.​ Claim (early): governing claim in sentence 1 or 2.
2.​ Mechanism chain: how the claim produces effects (A enables B, stabilizes C,
disciplines D).
3.​ Anchor: one concrete referent in high-density zones (practice, site, object, quote,
interface, actor, paratext).
4.​ Consequence domain: name where the stakes land (institutional, legal, clinical,
labor, epistemic, reputational, affective).
5.​ Bridge sentence: explicit logic that binds to the next move (what the pivot changes,
why the shift matters).
Recursion is permitted only if the second pass adds a new stake, mechanism, or anchor.
Otherwise compress.

6. The Three Leak Points and Required Repairs
6.1 Abstraction spikes without anchors
Symptom: stacked concept nouns with no object, scene, practice, quote, or trace.​
Repair: enforce one anchor per paragraph in high-density zones. Anchors can be minimal
but must be concrete.

6.2 Claim arrives late
Symptom: long runway before the knife sentence.​
Repair: move claim to sentence 1 to 2, spiral after.

6.3 Recursion drifts into redundancy
Symptom: repetition that restates without adding a lever.​
Repair: keep repetition only if it adds a new stake, mechanism, or example. Otherwise
merge and tighten.

7. Lexical and Syntactic Palette
7.1 Sentence rhythm

●​ Default rhythm: long multi-clause sentences with explicit hinges.
●​ Relief valves: short clarifiers when density spikes; one clean sentence break is often
better than stacking qualifiers.
●​ Controlled alternation: active voice for agency, passive voice when the point is
constraint or institutional diffusion.

7.2 Connector palette (rotate, do not synonym-churn)
EN: in this sense; read this way; with that said; by contrast; put differently; consequently;
importantly; methodologically; the stakes here.​
FR: en ce sens; de fait; au regard de; par conséquent; en premier lieu; à l’instar de; en
outre; par la suite.

7.3 Verb set (prefer over is/has/there is)
EN: produces; stabilizes; disciplines; reroutes; authorizes; mediates; encodes; naturalizes;
stages; negotiates; renders legible.​
FR: engendre; stabilise; discipline; reconduit; reroute; autorise; médiatise; encode;
naturalise; met en scène; négocie; rend lisible.

7.4 Definitions (function-first)
Define by operation, not essence. Use templates like:
●​ “By X, I mean [function] under [constraint] in [site], with consequences for [domain].”
●​ “I use X here to name [operation], not as a synonym for [near-term].”

8. Reference Use (Behavioral Rules)
8.1 What citations do in your voice
Citations are conceptual scaffolding: field positioning plus leverage. Clusters are part of your
authority-building rhythm, not automatically a problem.

8.2 Reference optimization rule
Every reference must do at least one job explicitly:
●​ Position: map the field and locate your intervention.
●​ Operationalize: extract the concept you take.
●​ Turn: show how you redeploy or twist the concept.
If a citation does none of these, flag it as ornamental or as an unsupported leap.

8.3 Quote containment

Quotes must be framed and extracted:
●​ One sentence before: what the quote is doing for your argument.
●​ One sentence after: what you take from it (mechanism or stake), not just paraphrase.

1.​ CLAIM-EARLY
2.​ SCOPE-CLAMP
3.​ CLAIM-LADDER
4.​ VERB-UPGRADE
5.​ ANTI-CHAIN-CLAUSE
6.​ PRONOUN-AUDIT

Safety override: if a clarity fix changes authorship-feel, revert and add a smaller anchor or a
tighter mechanism line instead.

11. Disruption Layer (Medium Default)
Across any 6 paragraphs: invert the order of components in 2–3 paragraphs (anchor-first,
definition-first, stakes-first, contrast-first) while keeping invariants present. Use one glitch

sentence every 500–800 words: compact paradox, refusal line, or analytic jolt, followed by
immediate scaffolding return.
Anti-chaos rules: no missing definitions/anchors; no unexplained jumps; no synonym churn;
no tonal whiplash.
