Dear Professor Clarke,
I would like to be considered for a part-time faculty position for four courses during the Fall term
2017. I recently defended my doctoral dissertation at the Département de sciences des religions
at Université du Québec à Montréal. I am convinced that my teaching and research experience in
religious studies, as well as my interest for the different aspects of academia have adequately
prepared me for this position. Furthermore, I believe that my interest in contemporary
religiosities could bring a both unique and rigorous contribution to your department.
I would like to apply to the following courses : RELI 380 Religion and sexuality; RELI 233
Introductionto women and religion; RELI 386 Witchcraft, magic and religion; and RELI 216
Encountering religions.
My doctoral project is a study of identity negotiations in the Montreal Pagan community,
regarding gender and sexual orientation, and practices of witchcraft and magic. Much of the
research on the topic suggests that contemporary Paganism and Wicca offer a very inclusive
milieu where LGBTQ Pagans can express the specificity of their identities through collective and
individual practices and performances whose gendered meanings and relations can be modified
at will. But I demonstrate, contrary to popular belief, that these religiosities still reproduce
normativity, and that identity and spirituality are more easily alined when the individual is
detached, at least for a time, from the community.
To complete this research, I did an extensive exploration of the diverse relationships between
religion and sexuality within instituted religions, as well as in new religious movements and
alternative spiritualities. Because certain issues around identity and power arose in my research, I
also worked on the sociological, anthropological, historical and political underpinnings of
categories like religion, magic, and witchcraft. Moreover, I have articulated a broadly useful
perspective on lived religion, spiritualities of life, and meaning itineraries, allowing the analysis

of power relations, within individual and collective experiences, between normative religion and

Although my primary area of research is religious studies, I have additional expertise in women
and gender studies, and I am eager to teach in both areas. I have served as a teaching assistant in
courses about religion and sexuality, homosexuality and gendered identities, representations of
evil, new ritualities, as well as Quebec culture. I also have spoken in many courses related to
those topics, and my research was published in different journals, both in French and in English.
I have served as a research assistant for projects on sexuality, ageing and masculinity, youth and
sexuality in Quebec, on marginalized populations in France, and on religious diversity in
Montreal. In addition, I have a previous background in literary studies. I have spent four years
doing fieldwork in the Pagan community surrounding Montreal and some festival locations in
Ontario. I have traveled to Toronto, Ottawa, and Quebec City, interviewed experts on the topics
of Paganism, magic and gender from Ontario and Quebec universities, and created a diversified
and comprehensive original dataset from ethnographies and LGBTQ informant’s interviews. In
the next few years, I hope to develop and give courses in anthropology of religion, religious
studies, and queer studies.
Because of my graduate training, my doctoral research, and my teaching experiences and
research interests, I am uniquely qualified for the job. It would be my pleasure to work in a new
scientific community like Concordia University. Be assured that I will be happy to meet with you
any time you deem appropriate, and to answer your questions about my academic path and my
professional endeavours.

Thank you greatly for your consideration,
