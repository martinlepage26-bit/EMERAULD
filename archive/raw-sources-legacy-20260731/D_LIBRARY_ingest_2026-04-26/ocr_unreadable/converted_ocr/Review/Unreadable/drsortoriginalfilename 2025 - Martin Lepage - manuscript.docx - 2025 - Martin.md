THE BROKEN FREQUENCY OF THE WORD, Martin Lepage

When the hum of his refrigerator stops one winter morning, Matisse realizes the silence has weight. Moments later, he finds his mother dead on the couch. What follows is not only grief but a strange new attunement: the city begins to hum. Radios glitch into melody, strangers speak in rhythm, and even the air seems to sing.

As Matisse drifts through this altered world, sound and silence fuse into something alive — a pulse that blurs the border between the living and the dead. Through dreams, voices, and the soft vibration of everyday objects, his mother’s presence begins to return, not as a ghost, but as a frequency.

The Broken Frequency of the Word is a literary novel about grief, listening, and transformation. It moves between realism and the uncanny, tracing one man’s passage from loss to resonance, where the world itself becomes a form of speech — and love survives as the hum beneath language.
