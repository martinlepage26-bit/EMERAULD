18. Give a short description of the research you performed and are actually involved with.
Our doctoral research, entitled «Why be king? I’m already a queen!»: performances rituelles et
négociations identitaires queer dans la communauté néo-païenne de Montréal, consists in an
ethnographic study of contemporary religiosities, as well as sexual and gender identities (man,
woman, gay, bisexual, lesbian, transgender and queer (LGBTQ)). The Montreal Pagan community
attracts a good number of LGBTQ individuals and, as such, promotes gender equality and sexual
diversity. Be that as it may, sexual and gender identities are still represented within a binary frame in
this religious community. Yet gender is a socially constructed identity manifesting though ideal
opposites or “essences” (masculinity and femininity) and mediated by/on people’s bodies. Gender is
the effect of numerous binary attitudes, learned behaviors, linguistic codes and divided spaces

4

putting every social aspect in two distinct categories. For this reason, a religious movement open to
equality and sexual diversity can still reproduce certain stereotypes occurring in the largest part of
society. To gain a better understanding of how LGBTQ people deal with the gender binary, our
research looks at life narratives. Reactions to social pressure in terms of gender identity, among
other things, can be seen in they ways in which LGBTQ individuals tell about their past and their
social relationships. Our research shows how rituals converge with different modes of negotiating
with gender norms. It also puts into perspective the use of certain stereotypical norms and excluding
concepts still relegating LGBTQ individuals to the margins of society. We were responsible for every
step of the research (ethical certification, conducting interviews, transcripts, participant observation,
data analysis).

B2. RESEARCH AND TRAINING PROJECT
21. Title of the project proposed
Masculinities and gender normativity: Narratives of Socialization of Virility
22. Relation of the proposal with the on-going research activities of the host unit:
This postdoctoral research will be conducted at the Laboratory for a prospective Anthropology
(LAAP), within the Institute for the Analysis of Change in Historical and Contemporary Societies
(Iacchos) and the Religions, Spiritualities, Cultures, Societies (RSCS) research institute. As such, it
will explore the various practices through which LGBTQ men identify as masculine. More
specifically, it will focus on the ways in which religion, whether institutionalized or personal,
converges with discourses on virility and masculinity within different cultural contexts. It also focuses
on the ways men give meaning to these practices in regards to their life narratives. Equal to the
interests of the Groupe de recherche en études de genre (GREG) we will join (sociology and
anthropology research axes), this research interrogates the ways in which gay, bisexual,
transgender and queer men, whose identification to masculine norms can be problematized in
different ways, negotiate their identities in regards to binary gender norms as they are performed in
religious context. It focuses mainly on normativity associated with masculinity and virility mediated
through different contexts (family, school, church, etc.) and transmitted in society. It compares the
ways in which diverse masculinities come into play, within identification processes, with conceptions
of virility (Batinder, 1992; Dubard, 2000; Thomas, 2000; Clare, 2004; Connell, 2005; Beauchemin,
2007; Jeffrey and D’Allondans, 2008). This project is based on the qualitative analysis of life
narratives, showing how gender norms have influenced and still influence our informants’ life course.
It expands the lens of queer theory (Butler, 2005, 2006, 2009; De Lauretis, 2007; Dorlin, 2008;
Dorlin and Fassin, 2009, 2010) towards an intersectional approach comprising gender, religion,
race, class, sexual orientation, age, physical capacity, etc. Moreover, it looks for ways in which lived
religion and religious education, understood as loci of oppression and/or emancipation from gender
norms (McGuire, 2002, 2008; Thumma and Gray, 2004; King and Beattie, 2005; Fedele and Knibbe,
2013; Lepage, 2013, 2015), partake in the transmission or the negotiation of virility as fundamental

6

to the definition of masculinity (Clare, 2004; Corbin et al. 2011a, 2011b, 2011c; Jeffrey and
Lachance, 2012).

24 months are expected to complete this research, starting approximately in June 2017.

25. Description of potential career development
This postdoctoral research can lead to multiple opportunities. Being the continuation of our
doctoral research on LGBTQ identities in religious context, it will deepen our understanding of the
convergence of religion and gender. Moreover, it will document the tensions between contemporary
identity politics and different types of normativity promulgated through various aspects of culture and
religion. It will heighten our command of qualitative research around gender and religion, expanding
our publication skills and our teaching skills. The network of scholars in all levels we will develop in
Louvain-la-Neuve will certainly allow further collaborations with various research units and
universities, thus augmenting chances to obtain a lasting position within one of them, or upon our
return to Canada. Postdoctoral research is very much valued and necessary in terms of career
advancement in Canada and in Quebec, even more when they are conducted abroad, as they help
strengthen connections between research networks and universities worldwide, as well as theories
and methodologies. This fellowship would greatly help achieve such level of expertise and
multidisciplinarity, which would ultimately translate in the production of new research, papers, talks,
even seminars and conferences.

C. IMPACT
26. Added value of the stay for the applicant
This postdoctoral research will be conducted in collaboration with Olivier Servais, whose
research on contemporary gender identities is closely linked to our own. This context seems
perfectly adequate to nourish our research in terms of available literature on masculine identities
and virility. Moreover, a postdoctoral fellowship abroad is fundamental to the pursuit of an academic
career, as in North America research experiences abroad are valuable and show capacity for
international collaborations. It would also be particularly useful to gain a better understanding of the
ways research is conducted in Belgium and Europe in general.

27. Added value of the stay for the host unit :
This research would be included in the symbolic systems axis of the LAAP, which is part of the
Iacchos and RSCS research institutes. It would allow the LAAP, in convergence with our
participation to the Groupe de recherche en études de genre (GREG) in UCL, to develop more
knowledge around gender and sexual identities as they are associated with the changing ways in
which individuals and collectivities partake in contemporary religiosities. It will also document the
ways different religions convey various gender norms and impact the life course of individuals who
identify to marginalized masculinities. Moreover, it will not only include individuals from
institutionalized faiths like Christianity, Judaism and Islam, but it will see to understand how those
individuals experience religion, whether they belong to a particular Church or not. Thus, the Iacchos
and RSCS institutes would sponsor research on the transmission of the practices adopted or
rejected by men for multiple reasons. It would particularly be interesting to examine non-traditional
practices of religion, benefiting from our interdisciplinary expertise. We are also prepared to
contribute to various seminars on related topics in Belgium and elsewhere, and to teach if asked.

28. Prospects for further collaboration:
Our collaboration with Dr. Servais and to the Iacchos research institute will allow us to extend
our network within academia to Belgium and Europe at large, as well as Australia and the United
States. It would allow us to collaborate with other postdoctoral researchers and professors to
undertake research in Belgium and in Quebec on different common aspects of our respective
research. It would also be interesting to compare social phenomena associated with religion and
gender and sexual identities between the two countries. At the end of our stay, we plan to stay
available within this research unit in Louvain-la-Neuve.
