Project Description - Coach House Books

**Title:** *Under Watch: Rigor, Voice, and the Bodies We Bring Across***Genre:** Literary Nonfiction / Essay Collection

*Under Watch* is a book about what it means to live under continuous, low-intensity scrutiny. It examines how legitimacy is not granted once and for all, but is instead repeatedly tested through voice, comportment, timing, productivity, and intelligibility. The book’s central concern is not spectacular surveillance or authoritarian oversight, but the everyday experience of being monitored, assessed, and subtly recalibrated by institutions that rarely announce themselves as such.

Structured as a series of interlinked essays, *Under Watch* moves between personal scenes and analytic reflection without separating the two. Moments of professional evaluation, medical encounter, bureaucratic delay, and social misrecognition become sites where larger questions of authority and credibility are felt at the level of the body. The essays do not seek resolution or mastery; instead, they trace patterns of repetition, interruption, and accumulation, showing how surveillance operates as atmosphere rather than event.

The book is grounded in lived experience but resists memoir’s narrative arc of overcoming. Its interest lies in persistence rather than transformation: what it means to remain under watch even after competence is demonstrated, to be asked repeatedly to prove capacity, coherence, or good faith. In this sense, *Under Watch* is less about exposure than about duration. It asks what happens when scrutiny never quite lifts, and when legitimacy is always conditional.

Formally, the book draws on the essay tradition to think through power without adopting academic apparatus. Conceptual language appears when necessary, but the prose prioritizes clarity, rhythm, and attention to detail. Ideas emerge through scenes, fragments, and returns rather than through linear argument. The essays are designed to be read cumulatively, with each piece adding pressure rather than resolution.

*Under Watch* will appeal to readers of contemporary nonfiction interested in surveillance culture, disability, institutional life, and the politics of voice. It sits alongside recent essay collections that blend lived experience with critical insight, while maintaining a distinct focus on evaluation as a structuring force of everyday life.

The manuscript is complete. It is intended for a general readership interested in serious nonfiction that does not require academic training but does reward attentiveness.
