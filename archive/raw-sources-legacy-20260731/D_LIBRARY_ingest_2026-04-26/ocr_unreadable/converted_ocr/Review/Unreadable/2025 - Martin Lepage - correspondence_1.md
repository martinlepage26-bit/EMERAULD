Camera Obscura: Feminism, Culture, and Media Studies

Dear Editors,

I am pleased to submit my article titled *The Machine’s Mirror: Alien, AI, and the Political Theology of Posthuman Recognition* for your consideration. This essay engages the Alien franchise not simply as speculative horror but as a sustained reflection on the cultural and ethical tensions surrounding autonomous machine intelligence. Building on a lineage of feminist and queer media theory, I explore the shifting roles of synthetic androids (Ash, Bishop, David) as they come into contact with the alien’s radically nonhuman survival logic.

Rather than opposing synthetic life to alien monstrosity, the article frames both as figures of posthuman agency: amoral, adaptive, and resistant to containment. Drawing on theorists such as Donna Haraway, Judith Butler, José Esteban Muñoz, Jack Halberstam, and Kim TallBear, I develop the concept of posthuman recognition — a form of kinship that bypasses humanist ideals of empathy, reciprocity, and moral alignment. In doing so, I position Alien as a critical lens through which to examine the contemporary rhetoric surrounding artificial intelligence, particularly the tension between alignment risk and corporate futurism.

I believe this work will be of interest to *Camera Obscura* readers for its interdisciplinary resonance with feminist film criticism, cultural studies, AI ethics, and the politics of techno-scientific discourse. I welcome the opportunity to contribute to your journal’s continued engagement with radical reimaginings of media, power, and embodiment.

Thank you for your consideration.

Sincerely,

Martin Lepage, PhD
