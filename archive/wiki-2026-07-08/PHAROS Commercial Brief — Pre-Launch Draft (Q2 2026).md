# PHAROS Commercial Brief — Pre-Launch Draft (Q2 2026)

## Summary

The pre-launch draft of the PHAROS AI commercial brief for Q2 2026, written for Chief Risk Officers, Heads of Model Risk Management, and AI Governance Leads. Title: *"AI Governance That Survives the Inspector Leaving."* This draft contains the earlier "93 days" framing (referenced from April 2026) that was subsequently corrected to "90 days" before deployment. See [[PHAROS AI Commercial Launch — 8-Stream Deploy (2026-05-01)]] for the deployed version and [[PHAROS Launch — Fluency, Evidence, and Mid-Funnel Trust (Synthesis)]] for the synthesis of the messaging evolution.

## Context

This draft is the genealogical predecessor to the six deployed static HTML commercial pages on `pharos-ai.ca`. It was written in the same voice and with the same regulatory grounding (EU AI Act, Law 25, OSFI E-23, NIST AI RMF) but uses "93 days" as the window-to-enforcement figure — a specific claim that was rounded to "90 days" for the deployed pages to reduce external verification risk while preserving the urgency framing.

The title *"AI Governance That Survives the Inspector Leaving"* is the headline used in the brief. It articulates the core PHAROS value proposition: not compliance theater for the inspection day, but continuous assurance that works when nobody is watching. This framing was preserved in the deployed version.

The draft connects to [[Regulatory Arbitrage — EU AI Act + Law 25 + 93-Day Window (Synthesis)]] for the regulatory context analysis and to [[Agent Orchestration — PHAROS Launch as Governed Multi-Agent Execution]] for the multi-stream launch architecture.

## Details

### The Core Argument

The brief argues that Canadian organizations facing overlapping regulatory regimes (EU AI Act, Law 25, OSFI E-23) will choose between:
1. **Compliance theater** — policies, checklists, attestations that satisfy the inspector for a day but fail under incident review
2. **PHAROS AI** — continuous assurance through layered, evidence-tiered governance engineered to work when nobody is watching

This is the differentiation claim. The "survives the inspector leaving" headline operationalizes it.

### Regulatory Perimeter Covered

- EU AI Act, Articles 6, 9–17, 26, 27, 49, 72 — August 2, 2026 enforcement date for high-risk AI
- Quebec Law 25 — in force since September 2024; §12.1 on automated decisions; penalties up to $25M CAD or 4% of global turnover
- OSFI E-23 — Model Risk Management guideline; effective May 1, 2027; examiners setting expectations now
- Federal vacuum — Bill C-27/AIDA died on the order paper; no federal AI law in Canada

### The "93 Days" / "90 Days" Correction

The draft used "93 days from the date of this brief" (written ~April 30, 2026 → August 2 deadline). This was changed to "90 days" in the deployed version:
- Rounder figure reduces verification risk from external readers checking the exact count
- Provides a natural margin while preserving urgency
- Messaging update noted in session-state 2026-05-01

### The Three Engagement Options

The brief closes with three engagement options sized by governance maturity:
1. Readiness Check (diagnostic)
2. Diagnostic tier
3. Diagnostic + Implementation tier

These correspond to the Stripe payment links in [[PHAROS AI Commercial Launch — 8-Stream Deploy (2026-05-01)]] pending creation.

For the smallest first-paid entry offer packaged as a client-defensible document set (inventory + risk register + policy + disclosure), see [[AI Governance Sprint — One-Page Sellable Packet]].
