# How to reason with the map

## The trace

1. Describe the symptom without interpreting it. Record onset, timing, severity, triggers, relieving factors, associated symptoms, medications, supplements, diet changes, illness, menstrual or reproductive context, sleep, and stressors.
2. Check [[00 Navigation/Clinical safety and red flags|red flags]] before exploring mechanisms.
3. Open the symptom note and scan the **connected systems** and **patterns**.
4. Follow at least two competing paths. For fatigue, compare [[06 Patterns/Reduced oxygen delivery|reduced oxygen delivery]], [[06 Patterns/Impaired cellular energy production|impaired cellular energy production]], [[06 Patterns/Dysglycemia|dysglycemia]], sleep, infection, medication effects, and mood-related causes.
5. Ask what evidence would discriminate among paths. Symptoms alone are rarely specific.
6. Take a short, ranked question list to a qualified clinician.

## Counter-bias prompts

- What common explanation am I skipping for an interesting one?
- Does the timeline support the proposed pathway?
- Is the relationship established, plausible, or merely possible?
- Could treatment of the suspected issue cause harm or obscure testing?
- What would falsify this hypothesis?
- Which connection is based only on a personal observation?

## Restore, do not optimize blindly

“What needs to be restored?” is useful only after defining the reference state and the evidence. More of a nutrient, hormone, herb, or intervention is not automatically restorative. Deficiency, adequacy, excess, interactions, and individual contraindications all belong in the map.
