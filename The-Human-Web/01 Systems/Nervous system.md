# Nervous system

## Core role

Neural signalling integrates sensation, movement, cognition, autonomic regulation, sleep, pain, prediction, attention, threat response, and communication with endocrine, immune, digestive, respiratory, and cardiovascular systems.

## Functional modules

- **Central nervous system:** brain and spinal cord integrate perception, movement, cognition, emotion, autonomic control, and reflexes.
- **Peripheral nerves:** carry sensory, motor, and autonomic signals between the central nervous system and the body.
- **Autonomic regulation:** sympathetic and parasympathetic signals influence heart rate, blood pressure, digestion, sweating, breathing patterns, and arousal.
- **Somatic movement and sensation:** voluntary movement, reflexes, proprioception, pain, touch, and vibration depend on nerves, muscle, spine, and brain.
- **Sleep and circadian control:** sleep-wake timing affects cognition, pain sensitivity, glucose regulation, immune tone, and restoration.
- **Neuroendocrine and stress signaling:** nervous-system appraisal and hypothalamic signaling coordinate HPA-axis responses.

## System cross-talk

- The [[01 Systems/Endocrine system|Endocrine system]] receives signals from the brain and feeds back through thyroid, adrenal, reproductive, and glucose pathways.
- The [[01 Systems/Cardiovascular system|Cardiovascular system]] is shaped by autonomic tone, blood pressure reflexes, and oxygen delivery.
- The [[01 Systems/Digestive system|Digestive system]] is strongly influenced by enteric, autonomic, and stress-related signaling.
- The [[01 Systems/Musculoskeletal system|Musculoskeletal system]] depends on motor neurons, proprioception, reflexes, and energy availability.
- The [[01 Systems/Immune system|Immune system]] can alter sleep, fatigue, pain sensitivity, and cognition through inflammatory signaling.

## What strain can look like

- Brain fog, sleep disturbance, paresthesia, dizziness, palpitations, altered pain sensitivity, tremor, weakness, coordination change, or sensory change.
- Symptoms linked to posture, exertion, meals, sleep deprivation, stress, infection, medications, alcohol, glucose swings, or nutrient deficiency.
- Central red flags such as sudden focal deficits differ from chronic nonspecific symptoms and should not be collapsed into the same pathway.

## Discriminating questions

- Is the problem sensory, motor, autonomic, cognitive, sleep-related, pain-related, or mixed?
- Is onset sudden, progressive, episodic, postural, post-infectious, medication-linked, or exposure-linked?
- Is the pattern localizable to a nerve distribution, spinal level, brain region, or whole-system state?
- Which findings would distinguish anxiety/autonomic arousal, neuropathy, glucose shifts, B12 deficiency, sleep deprivation, medication effect, or urgent neurological disease?

> [!danger] Neurological red flags
> Sudden weakness, facial droop, trouble speaking, seizure, severe new headache, new confusion, fainting with concerning symptoms, loss of bowel or bladder control, or rapidly progressive neurological symptoms require urgent assessment.

> [!evidence] Boundary note
> A body system is a navigation layer. Symptoms usually arise from interactions among systems, exposures, life stage, medications, and context.
