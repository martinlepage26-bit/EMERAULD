# Endocrine system

## Core role

Hormonal signalling coordinates metabolism, growth, reproduction, stress responses, blood pressure, salt-water balance, temperature regulation, appetite, sleep-wake timing, and internal balance.

## Functional modules

- **Hypothalamus-pituitary axes:** brain-endocrine signaling coordinates thyroid, adrenal, reproductive, growth, and lactation pathways.
- **Thyroid signaling:** sets broad metabolic tone and affects heart rate, temperature tolerance, bowel motility, skin, hair, and cognition.
- **Pancreatic glucose regulation:** insulin and glucagon help regulate blood glucose and energy storage.
- **Adrenal signaling:** cortisol, aldosterone, and adrenal androgens affect stress response, blood pressure, salt-water balance, and metabolic rhythm.
- **Gonadal signaling:** ovarian and testicular hormones coordinate reproduction, sexual development, cycle patterns, and some metabolic and bone effects.
- **Adipose and metabolic signaling:** fat tissue participates in appetite, inflammation, insulin sensitivity, and reproductive signaling.

## System cross-talk

- The [[01 Systems/Nervous system|Nervous system]] initiates many endocrine responses through hypothalamic and autonomic pathways.
- The [[01 Systems/Cardiovascular system|Cardiovascular system]] responds to thyroid, adrenal, kidney, and glucose-related signaling.
- The [[01 Systems/Reproductive system|Reproductive system]] depends on pituitary, gonadal, thyroid, adrenal, and metabolic context.
- The [[01 Systems/Renal system|Renal system]] interacts with blood pressure, aldosterone, vitamin D activation, and electrolyte balance.
- The [[01 Systems/Integumentary system|Integumentary system]] can show endocrine shifts through hair, skin texture, sweating, and temperature tolerance.

## What strain can look like

- Fatigue, temperature intolerance, palpitations, bowel change, sleep disturbance, hair change, weight change, cycle change, thirst, urination change, tremor, dizziness, or exercise intolerance.
- Symptoms that shift with meals, fasting, sleep disruption, stress, pregnancy, menopause, illness, steroids, thyroid medication, diabetes medication, or hormonal contraception.
- Lab patterns that involve more than one axis, such as glucose plus thyroid, thyroid plus reproductive hormones, or kidney plus adrenal/electrolyte markers.

## Discriminating questions

- Is the suspected pattern thyroid, glucose-insulin, adrenal, reproductive, pituitary, medication-related, or mixed?
- Does the timeline fit a hormone shift, medication change, acute illness, pregnancy/postpartum state, menopause, sleep disruption, or weight change?
- Are symptoms episodic, meal-linked, cycle-linked, stress-linked, or persistent?
- What objective evidence would separate endocrine disease from sleep, nutrition, anxiety, infection, medication effect, or normal life-stage variation?
