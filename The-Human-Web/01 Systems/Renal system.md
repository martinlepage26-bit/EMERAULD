# Renal system

## Core role

The kidneys regulate fluid, electrolytes, acid-base balance, waste removal, blood pressure, erythropoietin signaling, medication clearance, and vitamin D activation. The urinary tract stores and eliminates urine through coordinated muscular and neural control.

## Functional modules

- **Filtration and clearance:** kidneys filter blood and remove wastes, drugs, toxins, and excess fluid through urine.
- **Electrolyte and acid-base regulation:** sodium, potassium, bicarbonate, phosphate, calcium, magnesium, and acid-base balance affect nerves, muscles, heart rhythm, and bone.
- **Fluid and blood pressure control:** kidneys coordinate with vessels, hormones, and autonomic inputs to regulate volume and pressure.
- **Erythropoietin signaling:** kidney signaling supports marrow red blood cell production.
- **Vitamin D activation and mineral balance:** kidneys participate in vitamin D activation and calcium-phosphate regulation.
- **Urinary storage and voiding:** bladder, urethra, pelvic floor, and nerves coordinate urine storage and release.

## System cross-talk

- The [[01 Systems/Cardiovascular system|Cardiovascular system]] supplies kidney blood flow and responds to volume and pressure changes.
- The [[01 Systems/Hematologic system|Hematologic system]] depends on kidney erythropoietin signaling for red blood cell production.
- The [[01 Systems/Musculoskeletal system|Musculoskeletal system]] is affected by electrolyte, mineral, vitamin D, and acid-base context.
- The [[01 Systems/Endocrine system|Endocrine system]] interacts through adrenal hormones, glucose metabolism, vitamin D, and parathyroid-mineral balance.
- The [[01 Systems/Nervous system|Nervous system]] controls bladder function and is sensitive to electrolyte shifts.

## What strain can look like

- Fatigue, muscle cramps, palpitations, swelling, blood pressure changes, urination changes, itching, nausea, brain fog, or abnormal kidney/electrolyte markers.
- Medication or supplement sensitivity, especially when kidney function changes or dehydration, infection, diabetes, blood pressure, or heart disease are present.

## Discriminating questions

- Is the issue filtration, electrolytes, fluid balance, blood pressure, urinary tract symptoms, anemia signaling, medication clearance, or mineral balance?
- Are kidney function, urine findings, blood pressure, diabetes status, medication/supplement list, hydration, and infection signs available?
- Are there red flags: inability to urinate, severe flank pain with fever, blood in urine, confusion, severe dehydration, or rapidly worsening illness?
- What evidence separates kidney-related fatigue or cramps from anemia, thyroid disease, dehydration, medication effects, neurological disease, or muscular disease?
