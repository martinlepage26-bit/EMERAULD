# Immune system

## Core role

Innate and adaptive immune responses protect against infection and injury while interacting with nutrition, barriers, hormones, microbes, inflammation, tolerance, and tissue repair.

## Functional modules

- **Barrier defense:** skin, gut, airways, mucus, acid, enzymes, and resident microbes help prevent harmful entry.
- **Innate response:** rapid inflammation, phagocytes, complement, fever response, and tissue repair activate before precise recognition.
- **Adaptive response:** T cells, B cells, antibodies, and immune memory provide targeted recognition and long-term response.
- **Tolerance:** immune regulation helps avoid inappropriate response to self, food, harmless microbes, and environmental exposures.
- **Inflammation and repair:** inflammation can protect, but persistent or misdirected inflammation can alter energy, pain, mood, blood markers, gut function, skin, and tissue health.
- **Immune-nutrient interface:** vitamin D, zinc, selenium, omega-3 fatty acids, iron, protein status, and overall nutrition can shape immune function without implying supplement benefit for every symptom.

## System cross-talk

- The [[01 Systems/Digestive system|Digestive system]] is a major barrier and immune interface.
- The [[01 Systems/Integumentary system|Integumentary system]] provides external barrier defense and visible inflammatory signals.
- The [[01 Systems/Hematologic system|Hematologic system]] carries immune cells and inflammatory markers.
- The [[01 Systems/Endocrine system|Endocrine system]] modulates immune activity through cortisol, thyroid, glucose, reproductive hormones, and vitamin D.
- The [[01 Systems/Nervous system|Nervous system]] interacts with inflammation through sickness behavior, pain, fatigue, sleep, and autonomic pathways.

## What strain can look like

- Fever, swollen nodes, recurrent infections, inflammatory skin or gut symptoms, fatigue, pain sensitivity, brain fog, abnormal inflammatory markers, or immune-mediated nutrient absorption problems.
- Symptoms that shift after infection, allergen exposure, food exposure, medication, vaccination, stress, sleep disruption, autoimmune flare, or environmental trigger.

## Discriminating questions

- Is the pattern infection, allergy, autoimmunity, chronic inflammation, immune deficiency, medication effect, or normal recovery?
- Are symptoms localized to a barrier surface, systemic, recurrent, seasonal, exposure-linked, or post-infectious?
- Are fever, weight loss, night sweats, severe pain, rash with systemic illness, breathing compromise, or rapidly worsening symptoms present?
- What evidence separates immune-mediated disease from endocrine, nutritional, medication, anxiety/autonomic, or mechanical explanations?

> [!evidence] Boundary note
> A body system is a navigation layer. Symptoms usually arise from interactions among systems, exposures, life stage, medications, and context.
