# Digestive system

## Core role

Digestion, absorption, barrier function, motility, elimination, and interaction with the immune, nervous, endocrine, hepatic, biliary, pancreatic, and circulatory systems.

## Functional modules

- **Ingestion and mechanical processing:** chewing, swallowing, mixing, and movement of food through hollow organs.
- **Chemical digestion:** acid, enzymes, bile, pancreatic secretions, and intestinal enzymes break food into absorbable components.
- **Absorption:** the small intestine absorbs most nutrients; the colon absorbs water and electrolytes.
- **Barrier and immune interface:** the gut lining separates contents from tissues while sampling antigens and responding to microbes.
- **Motility and elimination:** peristalsis, stool formation, rectal storage, and defecation coordinate with nerves and pelvic-floor mechanics.
- **Microbial metabolism:** gut microbes participate in fermentation and produce metabolites, but microbiome claims require careful evidence.

## System cross-talk

- The [[01 Systems/Nervous system|Nervous system]] regulates appetite, nausea, motility, visceral sensation, and stress-related gut response.
- The [[01 Systems/Immune system|Immune system]] shapes barrier defense, inflammation, food-related immune reactions, and tissue repair.
- The [[01 Systems/Hepatic and biliary system|Hepatic and biliary system]] receives absorbed nutrients and returns bile to the intestine.
- The [[01 Systems/Endocrine system|Endocrine system]] influences hunger, satiety, glucose handling, thyroid-related motility, and stress physiology.
- The [[01 Systems/Hematologic system|Hematologic system]] can show downstream effects of absorption problems through iron, B12, folate, and inflammatory markers.

## What strain can look like

- Change in stool frequency, stool form, urgency, pain, bloating, nausea, reflux, early fullness, appetite change, or unintended weight change.
- Fatigue, palpitations, hair changes, brain fog, or paresthesia when absorption, bleeding, inflammation, hydration, or intake patterns are involved.
- Symptoms that vary with meals, fasting, stress, medications, infection, menstrual cycle, travel, or specific foods.

## Discriminating questions

- Is the main problem movement, digestion, absorption, inflammation, pain, bleeding, or elimination?
- Did the timeline begin after infection, antibiotics, travel, surgery, medication, diet change, supplement use, stress, or a new exposure?
- Are there red flags: blood or black stool, persistent vomiting, dehydration, fever, severe pain, anemia, unexplained weight loss, or progressive worsening?
- Which evidence would separate IBS-like functional patterns from celiac disease, inflammatory disease, medication effects, endocrine disease, or infection?
