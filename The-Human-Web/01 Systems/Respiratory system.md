# Respiratory system

## Core role

The airways and lungs exchange oxygen and carbon dioxide, influence acid-base balance, coordinate with breathing muscles and neural control, and work with circulation and blood to support tissue oxygen delivery.

## Functional modules

- **Ventilation:** air moves through nose, mouth, throat, trachea, bronchi, and smaller airways.
- **Gas exchange:** alveoli and nearby capillaries move oxygen into blood and carbon dioxide out of blood.
- **Breathing mechanics:** diaphragm, chest wall, airway resistance, lung elasticity, and posture affect ventilation.
- **Control of breathing:** brainstem, chemoreceptors, autonomic inputs, sleep state, pain, anxiety, fever, and acid-base status influence breathing pattern.
- **Airway defense:** mucus, cilia, cough, immune response, and inflammation protect against inhaled particles and infection.
- **Oxygen delivery link:** lungs load oxygen, but tissue delivery also depends on heart function, blood flow, hemoglobin, and cellular use.

## System cross-talk

- The [[01 Systems/Cardiovascular system|Cardiovascular system]] carries oxygenated blood and returns carbon dioxide-rich blood.
- The [[01 Systems/Hematologic system|Hematologic system]] determines oxygen-carrying capacity through hemoglobin and red blood cells.
- The [[01 Systems/Nervous system|Nervous system]] controls breathing rhythm, airway tone, and perception of breathlessness.
- The [[01 Systems/Musculoskeletal system|Musculoskeletal system]] supplies breathing muscles and posture.
- The [[01 Systems/Immune system|Immune system]] contributes to airway inflammation, infection response, and repair.

## What strain can look like

- Shortness of breath, cough, wheeze, chest tightness, fatigue, palpitations, poor exercise tolerance, sleep disturbance, or brain fog.
- Symptoms that shift with exertion, position, sleep, infection, allergens, smoke, air quality, anxiety, anemia, heart strain, medication, or altitude.

## Discriminating questions

- Is the issue airflow, gas exchange, breathing mechanics, oxygen-carrying capacity, circulation, or cellular oxygen use?
- Is breathlessness sudden or chronic, exertional or at rest, positional or sleep-related, associated with cough/fever/wheeze/chest pain?
- Are pulse, oxygen saturation, hemoglobin, heart findings, infection signs, or medication exposures relevant?
- What evidence separates lung disease from anemia, cardiac disease, deconditioning, anxiety, medication effects, or metabolic acidosis?

> [!danger] Respiratory red flags
> Severe breathing difficulty, blue lips, confusion, chest pain, fainting, very low oxygen saturation, or rapidly worsening breathing symptoms require urgent assessment.

> [!evidence] Boundary note
> A body system is a navigation layer. Symptoms usually arise from interactions among systems, exposures, life stage, medications, and context.
