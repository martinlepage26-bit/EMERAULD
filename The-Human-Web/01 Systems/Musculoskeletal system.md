# Musculoskeletal system

## Core role

Muscle, bone, joints, tendons, ligaments, cartilage, marrow, and connective tissue support movement, posture, protection, mineral storage, blood-cell production, heat generation, and functional capacity.

## Functional modules

- **Skeletal support:** bone protects organs, stores minerals, anchors muscles, and houses marrow.
- **Movement and force:** skeletal muscle contracts under nerve control and depends on oxygen delivery, glucose, fatty acids, electrolytes, mitochondria, thyroid signaling, and training state.
- **Connective tissue and joints:** tendons, ligaments, cartilage, fascia, and joints transmit force and absorb load.
- **Mineral and endocrine interface:** vitamin D, calcium-phosphate balance, kidney function, parathyroid signaling, thyroid state, reproductive hormones, and activity shape bone and muscle.
- **Repair and inflammation:** injury, overuse, immune activity, medication, and systemic inflammation can affect pain and recovery.
- **Functional reserve:** strength, mobility, balance, and endurance reflect multiple systems, not muscle alone.

## System cross-talk

- The [[01 Systems/Nervous system|Nervous system]] controls movement, reflexes, pain, coordination, and proprioception.
- The [[01 Systems/Cardiovascular system|Cardiovascular system]] and [[01 Systems/Respiratory system|Respiratory system]] deliver oxygen and remove waste during activity.
- The [[01 Systems/Renal system|Renal system]] regulates electrolytes, acid-base balance, vitamin D activation, and mineral balance.
- The [[01 Systems/Endocrine system|Endocrine system]] shapes thyroid tone, glucose use, stress response, reproductive hormone effects, and bone metabolism.
- The [[01 Systems/Immune system|Immune system]] participates in repair, inflammatory pain, and autoimmune musculoskeletal disease.

## What strain can look like

- Muscle cramps, weakness, fatigue, pain, stiffness, reduced exercise tolerance, injury recurrence, bone pain, balance changes, or slow recovery.
- Symptoms that change with exertion, rest, hydration, electrolytes, sleep, thyroid state, kidney function, medications, infection, inflammation, or training load.

## Discriminating questions

- Is the problem muscle, joint, bone, nerve, vascular supply, inflammatory pain, electrolyte imbalance, medication effect, or conditioning?
- Is it pain, true weakness, fatigue, cramp, stiffness, numbness, instability, or loss of range of motion?
- Are there red flags: trauma, fever, severe swelling, sudden weakness, loss of bladder/bowel control, chest pain with exertion, or rapidly worsening symptoms?
- What evidence separates muscular energy strain from neuropathy, anemia, thyroid disease, kidney/electrolyte disturbance, inflammatory disease, or cardiovascular limitation?

> [!evidence] Boundary note
> A body system is a navigation layer. Symptoms usually arise from interactions among systems, exposures, life stage, medications, and context.
