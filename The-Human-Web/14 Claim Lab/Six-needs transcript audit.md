# Six-needs transcript audit

| Claim | Status | What the map retains |
|---|---|---|
| Robbins teaches six needs | Verified framework claim | The six categories and their coaching use |
| The model explains every human being | Not independently established | A reflective hypothesis, not an exhaustive law |
| Certainty and variety are opposites | Useful tension, overstated as a law | They can conflict and can also coexist |
| Significance blocks genuine connection | Context-dependent | Recognition can isolate or strengthen belonging |
| Growth and contribution underpin the other needs | Internal model claim | Useful architecture without scientific validation |
| The future-self question bypasses brain resistance | Unsupported mechanism claim | The grammar presupposes a perspective and can guide attention |
| Robbins coached four presidents and named elite clients | Repeated in promotional biographies | Keep as a self-promotional claim unless independently corroborated |

## Useful remainder

The transcript’s strongest move is not the universal theory. It is the invitation to examine how strategies for safety, stimulation, recognition, and belonging can collide, then ask whether growth and contribution create a more spacious response.
