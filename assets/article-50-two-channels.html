EU AI Act/Article 50/applicable 2026‑08‑02

The marking is for the machine.
The label is for the person.

Article 50 creates two disclosure duties for generated content, not one. They run on different tracks, reach different audiences, and carry very different odds of ever being checked.

Where a single piece of generated content splits
AI‑generated content

Machine‑readable marking

Provider duty Applies to all generative output Transitional until 2026‑12‑02

Built for detection infrastructure: platforms, regulators, forensic tools. Not designed to be read by the person in front of it.

Perceptible label

Deployer duty Deepfakes and public‑interest text only Exempt under editorial review

Reserved for a narrow slice of content. Most generated material a person actually sees never reaches this tier.

Also perceptible, no machine tier exists
Chatbots and AI assistantsdisclosed once, first contact
Emotion recognition, biometric categorizationexposed people informed

The obligation that is easiest to verify is not the one aimed at the person actually reading. That gap, not the marking itself, is the part worth watching as enforcement infrastructure matures.
