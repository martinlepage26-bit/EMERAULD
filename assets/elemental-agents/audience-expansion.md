# Elemental Agents — Audience Expansion Analysis

## Why the narrow positioning leaves money on the table

The launch memo aimed at Claude Code / Cursor / Aider operators because that audience is closest to the framework's birthplace. But the framework is not really an AI tool — it is a **methodology in a box**: ten role contracts, six orchestration phases, an element-based combination algebra, and a shell validator that polices its own structure. Any of those four artifacts has buyers outside the AI tooling crowd.

The narrow buyer is **maybe 20,000 people** globally with disposable income at the $49 price point. The wider buyers below collectively reach the **low millions** and many of them tolerate higher price points because they already spend on methodology products.

---

## Five expansion bets, ranked by ratio of upside to effort

### Bet 1 — Productized-methodology buyers (HIGH)

**Who they are:** People who buy frameworks-as-products. Tiago Forte's *Building a Second Brain* graduates ($1,500+). Ali Abdaal's productivity systems crowd. Justin Welsh's solopreneur frameworks ($150+). David Perell's writing methodology. The audience that lives on Gumroad, Lemon Squeezy, Skool, Maven, and Circle, and that already accepts "framework" as a product category.

**Why this framework fits them:** They are conditioned to pay for naming systems, decision-rights maps, and operating models. They do not need an AI agent to find value in "ten roles, six phases, 165 named compositions." The framework reads naturally as an operating system for solo or small-team knowledge work.

**Positioning angle:** *The first operating model with a self-validating spine.* Sell the explicitness — most methodology products are a PDF and a Notion template; this one is markdown contracts plus a shell script that fails loud when you drift.

**Price:** $79–$149, with a $299 "implementation pack" tier (live walkthrough + customization session). Methodology buyers expect to pay more than developer-tool buyers.

**Channel:** LinkedIn-native posting (the methodology crowd lives there), guest appearances on operator/founder podcasts, cross-promotion with adjacent framework sellers.

**Risk:** The element vocabulary may read as woo to this segment. Defense: lean into the philosophical seriousness — the *Manifestation Rules* doc reads more like an academic methodology than a productivity hack, and that intellectual depth is actually attractive to this buyer.

**Effort to launch:** Low. Existing assets work as-is; sales-page rewrite is two days of copy work.

---

### Bet 2 — TTRPG and hard-magic-system creators (HIGH)

**Who they are:** Tabletop RPG designers, fantasy worldbuilders, hard-magic-system enthusiasts (Brandon Sanderson's *Three Laws* crowd), Critical Role / Dimension 20 fans, indie game designers on itch.io. Audience size in the millions; willing to pay $20–$200 for well-built systems.

**Why this framework fits them:** The element composition system *is already a magic system.* Ten elements, 45 dual combinations, 120 triples, manifestation rules that reject decorative names without executable behavior, escalation modifiers (Spirit/Chi/Akasha) that cannot appear as routine peers. Strip out the agent contracts and the orchestration phases and what remains is a working magic-system spine that any GM could drop into a campaign.

**Positioning angle:** *A hard magic system you can run without rebalancing.* The validator script is the selling proof — most homebrew magic systems collapse under their own weight when players start composing spells; this one was built with mechanical integrity from day one.

**Price:** $19 base "magic system manual" PDF; $49 expanded edition with 165 spell cards (the dual + triple combinations); $149 GM toolkit including the validator concept ported to a campaign-management spreadsheet.

**Channel:** itch.io, DriveThruRPG, r/RPGdesign, r/magicbuilding, Reddit's hard-magic-system community, Sanderson YouTube ecosystem. Different audience, different vocabulary, fast pickup.

**Risk:** Brand split. If Martin sells the same artifact to enterprise AI buyers ($35k Sprint) and TTRPG hobbyists ($19), the two audiences may collide on social signals. Mitigation: use a different storefront brand for the TTRPG line (the framework is licensed; the brand is what differs).

**Effort to launch:** Medium. Requires repackaging the combinations as game-system content — strip the "Best use case" engineering jargon, add example spell narrations, replace "Lead agent" with "Caster role". Two to three weeks of repackaging.

---

### Bet 3 — Compliance, controls, and audit professionals (MEDIUM-HIGH)

**Who they are:** Internal-audit teams, GRC platforms (Vanta, Drata, Secureframe customers), SOC 2 / ISO 27001 implementers, AI-governance roles inside enterprises. These buyers have line-item budgets for methodology and controls libraries.

**Why this framework fits them:** Rename three agents and the framework reads as a controls library. *Quality Auditor* is already an internal-audit role. *Governance Steward* is already a GRC role. *Security Guardian* is already a CISO function. The triangulated-validation gate (build / quality / governance) is a markdown-native rendering of the classic three-lines-of-defense model. The pairing protocols are RACI made operational.

**Positioning angle:** *A markdown-native operating layer for AI governance controls.* Particularly resonant in 2026, where every enterprise is reaching for ways to express agent governance without buying another SaaS dashboard.

**Price:** $299–$499 for the controls library + implementation guide; $2,500–$5,000 for the team license; warm-intro upsell to [[PHAROS Procurement-Unblock Sprint]] is *natural* in this channel because the buyer already has stalled enterprise deals.

**Channel:** AICPA newsletters, ISACA forums, AI Governance LinkedIn communities, IAPP (privacy professionals), guest posts on Vanta / Drata / Secureframe blogs. The compliance world publishes everywhere.

**Risk:** This is the most legitimate cross-sell to PHAROS but also the most reputation-sensitive. Don't position as "another AI agent thing" — position as "a controls library that happens to be expressible to AI agents."

**Effort to launch:** Medium-high. Requires a controls-vocabulary version of the agent files (Coordinator / Architect / Engineer / Auditor / etc., with element names retained only inside the validator). Three to four weeks.

---

### Bet 4 — AI/ML platform teams inside enterprises (MEDIUM)

**Who they are:** Platform engineering and AI-platform teams at Fortune 1000 companies who are trying to govern agent sprawl across many internal teams. They already pay for LangSmith, LangChain platform, AutoGen Studio, etc., but those are runtime tools — they lack a *methodology* the platform team can hand out.

**Why this framework fits them:** A platform team's job is to publish reusable patterns. The framework is already shaped like a publishable pattern set — agent contracts, orchestration patterns, validation gates, a script that enforces all of it. This is exactly what a platform team would otherwise spend three months writing internally.

**Positioning angle:** *The pattern set your AI platform team has been writing on a wiki for the last six months — but finished, validated, and dropped in next week.*

**Price:** $5,000–$15,000 team license, with a $25k "embedded enablement" tier. This buyer expects this price range and resists below-$1k pricing as not-serious.

**Channel:** Enterprise sales motion only; warm-intro, the same channel as the Sprint. The same email list works for both offers.

**Risk:** Enterprise sales cycles are slow. Treat this as a 6–9 month motion that produces $10k+ deals, not a $49 Gumroad pop.

**Effort to launch:** Low for the artifact; high for the sales motion. The framework as-is is enterprise-suitable. The work is on the channel side.

---

### Bet 5 — Educators and course creators (LOW-MEDIUM)

**Who they are:** University faculty teaching multi-agent systems, MOOC creators (DeepLearning.AI, Fast.AI ecosystem), technical bootcamps, AI engineering courses on Maven and Frontier.

**Why this framework fits them:** Multi-agent systems courses lack a canonical "running example" that students can use without learning a heavy SDK. The framework is markdown — students read it in one sitting, modify it themselves, present their changes to the class.

**Positioning angle:** *The teachable multi-agent framework.* No SDK. No infrastructure setup. One repo, one shell script, ten role contracts a student can read in forty minutes.

**Price:** Educational license — free for courses with public syllabi; $500 institutional license; cross-promo via "powered by Elemental Agents" badge in course materials.

**Channel:** Direct outreach to course creators, AI-engineering Discord servers, Maven and Frontier course-creator forums.

**Risk:** Educational licenses rarely generate direct revenue; the value is brand surface and downstream alumni who become buyers in segments 1, 3, or 4.

**Effort to launch:** Low. One-page educational-license addendum to the MIT terms.

## Recommended sequencing

**Phase 1 (weeks 1–2):** Ship the original AI agent operator launch as drafted in `positioning-memo.md`. Same week, ship Bet 1 (methodology buyers) — the only change is sales-page copy. Two launches, one product, two storefronts.

**Phase 2 (weeks 3–6):** Repackage and ship Bet 2 (TTRPG) under a separate brand. The repackaging produces a halo asset (the magic-system PDF) that also doubles as a lead-magnet for Bet 1.

**Phase 3 (weeks 6–12):** Ship Bet 3 (compliance controls). This is the natural PHAROS upsell channel. Use the same warm-intro list. Bet 4 (enterprise platform) runs on the same channel as a longer-tail motion.

**Phase 4 (ongoing):** Bet 5 (educators) as a brand-surface play — propose it to two AI engineering courses for free in exchange for citation.

**Total upside if all five run:** the framework retails simultaneously at $19, $49, $79, $149, $299, $499, $5,000, and $25,000+ across non-overlapping audiences with shared assets. This is the *one product, many doors* pattern that Obsidian Agent Vault already validated at smaller scale.

---

## What this changes in the original positioning memo

- The two-door ladder (Obsidian Agent Vault + Elemental Agents) is too narrow. Replace with a **five-door** picture once Bets 1–3 ship.
- The positioning fork (Option A mythopoetic vs Option B technical) becomes false: ship **both**, addressed to different segments under different storefronts.
- The "qualification filter" framing for the $49 tier still holds, but only for Sprint-bound AI operator buyers. Methodology and TTRPG buyers are their own outcome, not a Sprint funnel.
- The PHAROS Procurement-Unblock Sprint upsell remains the highest-value terminal exit, but now it has three feeder channels (AI operators, compliance, enterprise platform) rather than one.

## Open questions for operator review

- Confirm appetite for a separate TTRPG storefront brand. (This is the question that determines whether Bet 2 ships.)
- Confirm willingness to invest 3–4 weeks repackaging effort for Bet 3 before its first sale.
- Confirm whether to test all storefronts under "Pharos AI" or distinct identities. Recommendation: distinct identities for Bets 2 and 5; same identity for Bets 1, 3, 4.
