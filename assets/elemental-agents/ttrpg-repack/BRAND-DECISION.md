# Charge & Circle — Brand Decision Record

## Pivot history 2026-05-24

The brand name Charge & Circle remains locked through all reframes. The product identity it serves has gone through four readings during 2026-05-24:

1. TTRPG product (original)
2. Governance framework with TTRPG-readable surface (Path A, mid-day pivot)
3. Fiction in the false-document tradition (afternoon reframe)
4. **Governance framework with elemental magic as EXCLUSIVE surface vocabulary** (evening reframe; current, locked)

Under the final EXCLUSIVE reading, the product is *only* a governance framework. The elemental magic vocabulary is the teaching surface — it gives semantic-dense verbs to governance roles. It is not an alternate reading, not a halo audience, not a dual-surface artifact. Buyers who read it as a TTRPG product or a magic system are reading against the manuscript's intent.

The Charge & Circle name survives all four reframes because it was selected for compatibility across surfaces. The argument for keeping it under the EXCLUSIVE governance reading: *Charge* is the corpus-doctrine word from [[Martin Walks the Witches' Road — Corpus as Charge-Persistence]] (the residue that persists across surface change); *Circle* is the ritual-geometry containment that the manifestation rule + validator + modifier escalation enforce mechanically. The name is corpus-coherent without naming the corpus directly, and the surface-vocabulary defense applies: a governance product that calls itself "GovOps Toolkit v2" is forgettable; one that calls itself "Charge & Circle" sticks.

See [[Charge & Circle — TTRPG Launch (2026)]] for the current product framing (file name retained for backlink stability; the product is no longer TTRPG in any sense). The rest of this document describes the brand identity's rationale; the rationale survives all four pivots.

## The choice

**Brand name:** Charge & Circle

**Decided:** 2026-05-24

**Decider:** Operator (Martin Lepage) via AskUserQuestion during plan-mode session.

**Alternatives considered:** Witches' Road Press (rejected as too on-the-nose, also publisher-explicit), Hexbinder (rejected as too genre-D&D-adjacent and lacking corpus coherence), Element & Manifestation (rejected as SaaS-sounding; "manifestation" word burned by Instagram-spirituality discourse).

## Why Charge & Circle

The brand sits at the load-bearing intersection between two of the operator's existing structures:

**Charge** is the corpus-doctrine word from [[Martin Walks the Witches' Road — Corpus as Charge-Persistence]]. The canonical statement of the corpus is that *agency-under-constraint persists by changing surface while retaining a residue (the charge)*. Each form Martin produces (PhD, paper, novel, vector model, governance method, software framework) is a recomposition of the same charge. The TTRPG product, if it ships, is yet another Witches' Road recomposition — a surface that satisfies different gatekeepers (hard-magic designers, GMs, indie reviewers, witchy/queer-coded TTRPG audiences) while retaining the charge that connects it back to the corpus.

**Circle** is the ritual-geometry word. The casting circle is the boundary inside which the working can be performed safely; outside the circle the same working becomes uncontained. This maps cleanly to the mechanical spine of the framework: the manifestation rule + the validator script + the modifier-can't-lead constraint together define the circle within which the system stays coherent. Outside that circle (decorative-without-executable names, structurally-broken combinations, Spirit/Chi/Akasha as routine peers), the working breaks.

**Together:** *Charge & Circle* names what the press publishes — workings that retain a charge inside a contained ritual geometry. It scales to a second and third title without rebranding. It is ungoogleable in the useful sense (the search returns mostly nothing — clean naming surface). It is corpus-coherent without naming the corpus directly. It works as both a publisher name and a title concept.

## Why each alternative was rejected

**Witches' Road Press** — Direct callout to the corpus note. Too on-the-nose. Reads as derivative of the *Agatha All Along* / *WandaVision* cultural moment in 2024-2025 rather than as an independent press identity. Also publisher-explicit ("Press" suffix), which makes the storefront sound formal in a way that the witchy/queer-coded TTRPG audience reads as corporate-trying.

**Hexbinder** — Strong single-word but reads too D&D-adjacent. The "binder" suffix in TTRPG carries Pact-Bound Bargainer / Warlock-pact connotations that don't match the framework's actual mechanics. Also loses the corpus connection entirely; treats this as a generic genre product, not a charge recomposition.

**Element & Manifestation** — SaaS-shaped (the "&" pattern paired with abstract nouns is a startup-naming convention). The word "manifestation" is also positionally compromised by Instagram-spirituality discourse — wrong valence for the academic-witchy/queer-corpus register the product needs.

## What the brand enables and forbids

**Enables:**
- Second title: another Charge & Circle product (a divination system? a ritual-grounded NPC creator? a campaign sandbox built on the manifestation-rule discipline?) ships under the same publisher identity within 12 months if v1 succeeds.
- About-the-Author page on the storefront that quietly establishes corpus continuity (links from the press's About page to the corpus note are deferred to v2 — see below).
- The press identity remains intact whether or not the operator's name attaches publicly.

**Forbids:**
- Linking Charge & Circle to PHAROS, EMERAULD, Obsidian Agent Vault, or any AI-governance / consulting work in any public-facing surface. The two audiences must not collide.
- Naming the product in the same Substack, LinkedIn post, or warm-intro email as the [[PHAROS Procurement-Unblock Sprint]].
- Using Martin's legal name as the publisher identity. Charge & Circle is the press; an author byline can be a pseudonym, a legal name, or initials — but the press identity is independent.
- Importing "manifestation" vocabulary into the storefront copy. The framework's internal vocabulary still uses "manifestation field" as a mechanical term, but the storefront frames workings, castings, circles, and charges — not manifestations.
