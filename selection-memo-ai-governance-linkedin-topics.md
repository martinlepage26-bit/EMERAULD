Inventory completed before selection. The vault tree contains 14,335 non-git files and 14,992 files including Git internals. Working classification counted 20 source notes, 2,709 raw source corpus files, 56 archive files, 71 direct control artifacts, 889 generated vault notes, 127 generated artifacts, 2,845 visualization or generated index artifacts, and 7,618 other root, tooling, application, or non-evidentiary files. For article drafting, I treated source notes, raw files, archive files, and governance control files as stronger evidence than polished wiki syntheses. Generated wiki notes were used for topic selection, process diagnosis, and inference boundaries.

Topic 1 is evidence hierarchy as the real control surface. It draws on the files labeled PHAROS Method Technical Reference, PHAROS Runbook SOP, and archive PHAROS Master SOP. The claim advanced is that an AI governance program is not operational until source artifacts, generated artifacts, control artifacts, decision rights, thresholds, evidence registers, and re-entry triggers are separated and bound to workflow consequences.

Topic 2 is supply-chain enforcement before formal enforcement. It draws on the files labeled Supply Chain Enforcement, Trust Advantage Analysis, PHAROS Launch Fluency Evidence Mid-Funnel Trust, and PHAROS Commercial Brief v1. The claim advanced is that AI vendors can face practical enforcement through customer procurement long before a public regulator acts, because vendor questionnaires, renewals, and contract terms can exclude vendors that cannot produce evidence.

Topic 3 is transparency without interruption. It draws on the files labeled Learning Together for Responsible AI, AI is speeding into healthcare source note and raw clipping, Strategic Silence, and Transparency Against Democracy. The claim advanced is that transparency and literacy do not become accountability unless affected people and weaker institutions can use information to contest, reopen, or stop a decision path.

Topic 4 is human supervision as an insufficient accountability claim. It draws on the files labeled The Blind Leading the Automated, Relay Ledger, Specialist Guideline Authority, Governance Infrastructure Manifest, and Agent Orchestration PHAROS Launch. The claim advanced is that "human in the loop" collapses unless the human has visibility, refusal authority, escalation paths, and an independent audit trail across handoffs.

Topic 5 is trust as an audit artifact rather than a brand asset. It draws on the files labeled Trust Advantage Analysis, PHAROS Launch Fluency Evidence Mid-Funnel Trust, PHAROS Commercial Brief v1, and PHAROS AI Governance Service Offer Architecture. The claim advanced is that trust in AI markets becomes operational only when the buyer can defend the decision after failure, using bounded evidence rather than polished claims.
