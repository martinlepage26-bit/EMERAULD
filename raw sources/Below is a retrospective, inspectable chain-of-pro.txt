## 0) Pipeline object model: what counts as “audit evidence” for this paper

### A. The paper’s core mechanism (the thing the workflow is designed to observe)

* **Glitch** = a perceptual threshold where a reader senses mediation and evaluation migrates from artifact to pathway. 
* **Charging** = the social allocation of credibility that lets an artifact survive circulation as “real enough to trust,” with failure modes (spike, implode, dissolve). 
* **Pathway authorization** = the institutional decision about whether the route that produced the artifact is acceptable technique or disqualifying contamination, especially once a glitch is sensed. 

### B. The accountability object (what an auditor should be able to reconstruct)

A single “evidence packet” (appendix or private folder) that allows a third party to rebuild the pathway without relying on trust:

1. **Version lineage** (dated drafts, filenames, word-count checkpoints).
2. **Decision ledger** (why a section was restructured, expanded, cut, or reframed).
3. **Prompt and response log** (if AI assisted): inputs, outputs, what was accepted or rejected, and why.
4. **Verification ledger** (claims checked, citations stabilized, page locks when possible).
5. **Risk register** (anticipated failure modes: hallucinated citations, over-polish, voice flattening, plagiarism-by-proximity, institutional suspicion triggered by smoothness).
6. **Change impact notes** (what each revision does to legibility, auditability, and the paper’s political stake). 

This structure matches the paper’s argument that legitimacy disputes are governance disputes about pathways, not merely artifact quality.

## 3) Minimal “audit procedure” for an external reviewer

If someone wanted to audit the pathway, they should be able to do this:

1. **Pick any claim** in analysis or discussion.
2. **Trace it backward** to:

   * the section function statement,
   * the cited sources,
   * the method that produced the observation,
   * the draft history where it entered,
   * the decision log that justified keeping it,
   * the verification ledger line that confirms it was checked.
3. **Confirm the three-arm coupling** by identifying at least one conversion event per arm.
4. **Confirm consequence awareness** by checking that known failure modes are named and mitigated in the pathway documentation.

This is the protocol’s wager: legitimacy should be granted by accountable mediation evidence, not by myths of unassisted origin.
