## Extracted Text

Montreal, May 15th, 2018

Professor Pamela Dickey Young, Acting Director
School of Religion
Queen’s University
Kingston, ON

RE: In support of Martin Lepage’s application, Baker Postdoctoral Fellowship,
School of Religion, Queen’s University

Dear Madam,
I have known Dr. Martin Lepage since my arrival in 2013 at the Department of
Religious Studies at the Université du Québec à Montréal where he was a doctoral
student.
Over the years, I could observe that Martin was a highly esteemed and dynamic member
of the intellectual and social life of the department: extremely committed, positive, and
generous of his ideas and of his time, Martin has given brilliant lectures at many courses
taught in our department, as teaching assistant and as a guest speaker, where he has
been highly appreciated by the students and the professors. But Martin’s activities have
extended well beyond our department: he has been a key research assistant in several
interesting projects in gender studies at UQAM and other Quebec universities, he has
given an impressive number of papers at international and national conferences, and he
has published and is publishing a number of extremely interesting articles on the Pagan
community of Montreal, with a focus on gender performance, ritual construction of
identity, and queer religiosity.
Within the span of four years, I saw him grow academically, and extend the network of
his collaborations well outside Canada, reaching prestigious European journals and
colleagues. If I was asked to characterize in a few key words his activity of these last
four years, I would say: dynamism, enthusiasm, determination, positivity, as well as
brilliant, and rigorous hard work.

I had the opportunity to hear Martin giving a lecture on ‘Gendered Pagan Rituality and
the Queer Goddess of Love and Sex’ at the American Academy of Religion conference
held at McGill University in 2015. I was struck by the finesse of his arguments and the
easy, fluid way he introduced them. I was not the only one to be impressed: the keynote
speaker at the conference, Megan Goodwin, quoted him repeatedly during her keynote
lecture later in the day. I was also present for his doctoral defense, for which he got a
mention of excellence, where I was once again impressed with his results and
conclusions on normativity and power.
For all these good reasons, I hold this extremely promising young researcher in high
esteem and I strongly endorse his application to the 2018 Baker Postdoctoral Fellowship
at Queen’s University.
I am convinced that Martin will be a rigorous and committed colleague, kind and open
to collaboration, who will bring to your department his ability to create collaboration
with international scholars and his rich approach to gender studies combining different
disciplines, from religious studies to anthropology, from ritual studies to performance
studies.
Please feel free to contact me for any additional information.

Sincerely,
