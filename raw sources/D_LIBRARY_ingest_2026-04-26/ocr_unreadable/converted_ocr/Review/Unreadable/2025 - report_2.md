Non-Graded Project: Design & Present a
Responsible AI Solution Pack

Description
In this capstone project, you step into the role of a Responsible AI consultant for
an online recruitment platform preparing to launch an AI-driven candidate

screening model. Executives depend on this system to shortlist applicants
quickly, but are worried about fairness, bias, and explainability. You will use
structured Responsible AI tools to map stakeholders and risks, identify potential
bias points across the AI lifecycle, design a targeted Bias Mitigation Plan, and
create a stakeholder-ready presentation that clearly communicates your ethical

design decisions.

Learning Objectives
By the end of this project, learners will be able to:
• Map stakeholders, risks, and mitigations for an AI-driven

recruitment system using a Responsible AI Canvas.
• Identify and document potential bias points across data

collection, model training, and decision stages.
• Develop a concise Bias Mitigation Plan that addresses datalevel, model-level, and process-level interventions.

• Create a stakeholder-ready pitch deck that explains key
risks, safeguards, and governance outcomes in clear, non-

technical language.
• Synthesize Responsible AI design, evaluation, and
communication practices into a coherent solution pack for
a real-world scenario.

Project Components

Introductory Slide
Title:
Hiring at Scale, Ethically: Responsible AI for Candidate Screening
Subtitle:
Design a fair, explainable screening model and present it with
stakeholder-ready clarity

Tools to be used:
Excalidraw - for designing a Responsible AI Canvas (stakeholders, risks,
mitigations, data flows).
Gamma.app - for generating a 5-6 slide stakeholder presentation.
Optional support tools: any text editor (for notes), diagram export tools
(PNG/PDF export from Excalidraw and Gamma).

Scenario
TalentFlow, an online recruitment platform, is about to launch an AI-driven
candidate screening model that automatically evaluates resumes and produces

ranked shortlists for recruiters. Executives want faster hiring cycles and reduced
manual screening time, but concerns have emerged:
• Will the model disadvantage candidates from certain schools, regions, or
backgrounds?
• Can recruiters and candidates understand why someone was shortlisted or

rejected?

• What happens if bias is discovered after deployment?
You have been hired as the Responsible AI consultant to design and present a

Responsible AI Solution Pack. Your work will inform the final go/no-go decision
for launch, and will be presented to executives, legal/compliance, and HR
leadership. They are not interested in raw model details alone—they want
evidence that fairness, transparency, and accountability have been built into the
system from the start.

Instructions for Learners

Step 1: Map the Responsible AI Canvas in
Excalidraw
1

Open Excalidraw and create a blank canvas.

2

Sketch a Responsible AI Canvas for the TalentFlow screening model,
including:
Key stakeholders (recruiters, candidates, HR, legal, affected
communities, regulators).
Main data sources (resumes, applicant tracking system data, historical hiring
decisions, optional assessments).

Model decisions (ranking, shortlisting, automated rejections,
recruiter-facing explanations).
Potential harms (unfair ranking by gender or school, opaque rejections,
feedback loops reinforcing past bias).
3

For each high-risk area, note at least one mitigation (e.g., fairness audits,
explanation UI, appeals process).

4

Clearly label each section so another person could understand your canvas
at a glance.

Tip: Think of the canvas as a one-page X-ray of the system: who it touches, where
risk lives, and how you plan to manage it.
