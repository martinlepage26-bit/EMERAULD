Version of events
I was hired under a probationary contract and began my employment on October, 27th,
2025. Shamiza Hussein was designated as my direct supervisor and responsible for my
integration and evaluation during probation.
Early in the probationary period, during a group or team interaction around November, 30th,
2025, I disclosed that I have a speech disability (stuttering). I made this disclosure proactively
and in a pedagogical manner to avoid misinterpretation of my communication. My colleagues
reacted positively and professionally. However, my supervisor’s reaction was noticeably
different in tone and demeanor. After this disclosure, my supervisor’s engagement with me
decreased, and no discussion took place regarding accommodation, clarification of
expectations, or support.
During the probationary period, I also experienced significant health issues that were
communicated to management. Around September 2025, I contracted a streptococcal
infection that caused severe physical symptoms, including widespread hives. This was
followed by laryngitis, which resulted in a near-total loss of voice for several weeks. These
conditions affected my physical comfort and ability to speak. Despite this, I continued
working, learning the role, and communicating transparently about my condition and
recovery.
Despite being aware of my health limitations, my supervisor repeatedly insisted that I attend
the office in person for training. This insistence continued even though my duties consisted
almost entirely of email-based administrative work performed through digital systems. The
limited training I received, including training with Olivia Yip and Josée Desmarais, was
procedural and could have been conducted remotely. No individualized assessment of
accommodation needs was undertaken, and no clear operational reason for mandatory
in-person attendance was provided.
End of December, my supervisor sent an email to my manager emphasizing that I needed to
come into the office. I replied the same day to both my supervisor and my manager,
explaining my health situation, the temporary nature of my limitations, and my continued
commitment to my work and training. I stated clearly that the constraints I was experiencing
were outside my control. I received no response, no clarification, and no adjustment of
expectations. Following this exchange, my supervisor became increasingly absent, and
communication diminished further.
Throughout the probationary period, I did not receive meaningful supervision, feedback, or
communicated performance criteria. I was never informed of any performance concerns and
was not given any opportunity to adjust, improve, or benefit from accommodation measures.
Evaluation appeared to occur informally and retrospectively, through silence and withdrawal
rather than through articulated standards.

On January 22nd, 2026, I was called to a meeting with Human Resources, attended by
Stephanie Nwachuku and Frankie Iacovelli, where I was informed that my employment
was terminated because I was “not a good fit.” No examples, documentation, or explanation
were provided.
During this meeting, I was instructed that I could no longer communicate directly with my
supervisor or colleagues and that all communication had to be routed through a single
intermediary, Frankie Iacovelli, whom I did not previously know. When I later attempted to
contact this person by email on the same day to retrieve my personal belongings from the
office, the message was returned as undeliverable. As a result, I was effectively cut off from
all communication with the employer immediately following termination.
Overall, my experience was characterized by a lack of supervision, absence of feedback, and
repeated silence in response to disclosures related to disability and health. Expectations were
imposed without accommodation or explanation, and the termination was carried out through
procedural barriers rather than transparent evaluation.
