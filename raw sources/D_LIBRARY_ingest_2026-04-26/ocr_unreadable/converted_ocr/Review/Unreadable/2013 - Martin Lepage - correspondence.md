## 4) Signature Syntax Kit (templates you can reuse)

### English templates

1) This article argues that X by showing how A produces B, which stabilizes C.

2) Much research has shown X, but fewer studies have examined Y in Z context.

3) Following [X], I treat [concept] as a contested category rather than a fixed object.

4) In this respect, X functions as a mechanism that disciplines / authorizes Y.

5) On the one hand X; on the other hand Y. The tension reveals Z.

6) Rather than framing X as individual preference, I read X as a social technology of recognition.

7) What is at stake here is not X, but Y (power, legitimacy, normativity).

8) In practice, X operates through A, B, and C, producing D as a consequence.

9) This reframing makes visible the costs for [domain: epistemic / institutional / reputational].

10) Taken together, these elements produce a pattern of normalization under constraint.

### Gabarits français

1) Cet article soutient que X en montrant comment A produit B, puis stabilise C.

2) De nombreuses études ont montré X, mais peu ont interrogé Y dans le contexte de Z.

3) Dans cette perspective, X fonctionne comme un dispositif qui discipline / autorise Y.

4) D’une part X; d’autre part Y. Cette tension rend visible Z.

5) Plutôt que de traiter X comme une donnée, j’aborde X comme une catégorie disputée.

6) Il s’agit moins de X que de Y (pouvoir, légitimité, normativité).

7) En pratique, X opère par A, B et C, et produit D comme conséquence.

8) Ce déplacement (de X vers Y) met en évidence les coûts [épistémiques / institutionnels / réputationnels].

9) Ainsi, X se construit à l’intérieur d’un cadre de Y, puis se naturalise.

10) Au final, ces éléments consolident un régime de normalisation sous contrainte.

## 5) Mini rule-set: ‘How to write like Martin’

**Lead with the governing claim.** Put the main claim in the first 1–2 sentences; spiral outward after.

**Clamp the scope.** Name bounded actors + sites (who, where, through what interface) instead of universal ‘we/people/society’.

**Build a claim ladder.** Each paragraph should move: Claim → Mechanism → Consequence (name the consequence domain).

**Prefer active verbs.** Replace ‘is/has/there is’ with verbs like produces, disciplines, stabilizes, reroutes.

**Split chain clauses.** If a sentence contains 3+ commas, split it at the strongest causal hinge.

**Audit pronouns.** Replace ‘it/this/that’ with the noun once per paragraph when the referent might drift.

**Use one sharp sentence per paragraph.** Max one ‘hot’ line; surround it with analytic scaffolding.

**Ground metaphors.** After a metaphor, name at least one concrete referent (site, practice, interface, institution).

**Keep evidence legible.** When you cite, tag what the citation does (definition, finding, method, critique).

**Keep the stakes explicit.** Name what changes if your claim is true (institutional, legal, clinical, labor, epistemic, reputational).
