Submission to JAAR

*Titre:* **The Circle Remains Open: Toward a Queer Sociology of the Sacred**

Author: Martin Lepage, Ph.D.

2456, de Rouen

Montréal, QC

H2K1M2

Martinlepage26me.com

**Abstract**

This essay explores queer and trans participation in Montreal’s neo-Pagan communities, offering an ethnographic and theoretical inquiry into how ritual becomes a site of identity, resistance, and cosmological reimagination. Drawing from lived religion, queer theory, and decolonial critique, it examines how practitioners reconfigure sacred space through embodied performance, symbolic improvisation, and magical epistemologies. Rather than seeking inclusion within normative religious frameworks, these rituals challenge the very boundaries between religion and magic, belief and performance, self and spirit. Through fieldwork, affective engagement, and methodological reflexivity, the essay contributes to Pagan studies, the sociology of religion, and queer studies by reframing enchantment as a legitimate form of knowledge. It argues that queer magic is not only expressive—it is ontological, political, and theoretical. By attending to misrecognition, exclusion, transformation, and ritual creativity, this work illuminates how the sacred becomes a space of queer world-making. The circle does not close—it opens.
