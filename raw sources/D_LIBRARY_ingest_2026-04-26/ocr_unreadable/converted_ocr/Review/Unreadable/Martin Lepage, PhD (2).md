Dear Editors,

Please consider my article, *“Reboot”: Performance, Gender, and the Computation of Identity in Postmodern Society*, for publication in *Animation*. This essay examines *ReBoot* (Mainframe Entertainment, 1994–2001), a pioneering Canadian CGI series, as a dense allegory of identity performance under digital capitalism. Drawing on performance theory, queer studies, and media archaeology, it argues that the series stages identity as programmable—iterative, context-sensitive, and algorithmically enforced.

*ReBoot* is not just a nostalgic artifact; it is a recursive text that anticipated the gamification of the self, long before avatars became TikTok filters and algorithmic logic shaped our every gesture. From Dot Matrix’s institutional femininity to Hexadecimal’s glitch femininity, the paper traces how identity is not expressed but compiled, enacted under pressure within a digital dispositif. What emerges is a queer counter-script for survival in today’s looped economies of performance.

This submission is original, unpublished, and not under review elsewhere. It contributes to *Animation*’s interdisciplinary conversations by showing how 1990s children’s media encoded complex models of computation, embodiment, and gender—long before theory caught up.
