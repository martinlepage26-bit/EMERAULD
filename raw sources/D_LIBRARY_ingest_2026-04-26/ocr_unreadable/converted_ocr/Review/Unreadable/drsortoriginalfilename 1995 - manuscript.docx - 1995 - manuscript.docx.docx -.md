Patrick is writer in his late 30s. One published novel, 10 years ago, pages of unpublished poetry and unloved lyrics for pop singers, he’s grasping at straws for his one big break. Patrick loves one thing among all others, TV, and especially Reality Television and the iconic celebrity realty show portraying 5 outrageous sisters and powerful business women, The Van Veinem. After the death of his older sister, Martha, Patrick finally tries a new genre, the only one he really knew from his core, television. Nothing really appeals to him, but the great Van Vanheim sisters who haunt his dreams. Viola, the baby star, Ronna, Vanessa, Victoria, the most beloved by the public, and Violet, the eldest. Competition and treachery runs deep among the sisters and that makes him feel alive.

The five sisters and their mother, Vita, are a powerhouse holding up billions and captivating the public’s eye with their short-lived marriages, their sexual affairs, and their respective businesses, clothe lines, jewelry lines, perfumes, all feeding content for fans, influencers, tabloids, and wannabes. Everyone of them ragingly loved to speculate, on one hand, about their lives, about the part they play in the game of Hollywood, the legitimacy of their success, and on the darker hand, on the murders that have started it all.

In 1995, JK Mansion, a beloved Black country singer, former marine and celebrity boxer, was accused of killing his wife and daughter after a night of terror and blood. The evidence was shocking, the witnesses were unshakable, the emotional distress of the fans weighing in the balance. The only reason for which Mansion did not get the chair: Vick Van Vanheim, his defense attorney.

Days of trial were widely covered by the media in 1995, but Patrick decides to write the night of Mansion’s crimes (brought back in real time). The season’s episodes are simultaneously showing JK Mansion’s real past with Patrick’s addition as equally true. As Patrick’s research finally leads him to start writing, his inspiration is through the roof: he integrates a dark twist to the show and brings in hauntings, visions, and apparitions to the script. At the same time, he starts experiencing strange occurrences.

The plot Patrick creates revolves around Mansion was in fact under the sway of demonic entities collecting the soul of innocents by making pacts in exchange for riches and fame. Until the last episode, it is assumed that Mansion had indeed his family, but a doubt is sewn when we learn that Vick Van Vanheim was forced to make a pact with one of the entities. He was to act as Mansion’S attorney and keep him from death row, in exchange for the success of his entire family line. But there was a catch: the demon wanted the soul of his most precious daughter, supposedly Victoria. Thanks to some missing evidence, a lost suitcase which allegedly contained incriminating proof, Van Vanheim wins the trial and one of his daughter was to be made the vessel of the demon’s evil.

As Patrick continues to imagine the horrors of that night in 1995, his life surprisingly seem to turn for the better. His agent brings up a few offers : a country song being picked up, and a possible re-editing of his horror novel, and even a TV movie. In the midst of all the excitement, strange things start to happen. His inspiration does not diminish, at all. He starts to have nightmare’s about the five sisters in horrible torture. Faint shadows seem to be following him around, whispering in his ear about Mansion and Victoria. He keeps writing of the demon entities and ghosts that led Mansion to murder: lust, drugs and money, all things tempting for Patrick.

After a night a terrorizing dreams, he finally decides to sit down and write the last episode of his show, finally entitling it Ⅴ. He’s finally compelled to write the last moment of Mansion’s wife and daughter. Under a thunderous sky, the two come back home from a night at the kid’s aunt. As they get into the house, they catch Mansion in the living room having sex with Victoria. A violent fight ensues and Victoria is chased out of the house. Demons come out of the shadows and hold Mansion unable to move, to talk, to see. They bring from the shadow a short dagger with a black marble handle and stick it into the floor. Violet comes into the living room, grabs the knife and explains the demonic pact to the scared wife of Mansion. Before longViolet slits her daughter’s throat in front of her. As she cries in awful terror, Mansion’s wife attacks Violet, puts up a fight and finally gets stabbed in the throat, as Violet fulfills her family’s destiny. She leaves the rooms, enters Mansion’s office, grabs a suitcase and stores the dagger in it. Violet picks up the suitcase, and leaves the house.

Patrick finishes the show and submits it to his agent. Not too long after, final surprise, Patrick’s book is picked up for TV movie and Ⅴ is a hit among producers. Everybody is buzzing about him and a private investor even insists on meeting Patrick. He agrees to the meeting and ends up in a luxurious mansion in old Hollywood. He is there to meet the only and only Victoria, who apparently had a blast reading the script with her girlfriends. As he enters the house, he realizes the faint shadows were back, and the whispers, warnings. Flashing before his eyes, the ghosts of Mansion’s wife and daughter, begging him to leave this house.

He finds himself in a gorgeous study. Someone had left a box on a table with a message to open it. As Patrick unboxes a long dagger with a white marble handle, someone walks behind him and stabs him in the back and through the chest, skewering his heart. Violet stands bloody at his side, emanating dark shadows from her whole being, and stores both knives in the suitcase from 1995.
