Object: Dr. Martin Lepage - Teaching Appreciation
Dear Professor MacGregor,
1 have known Martin Lepage for more than five years, and witnessed on multiple
occasions how both his research and teaching abilities developed. I first heard him speak
at Brown Bag Lunches and UQAM’s annual graduate students in Religious Studies
conferences that he organized, with other graduate students of the department,
conferences that were, at times, attended by graduate students coming from abroad.
Since 2013, I have invited him every year to teach two three-hour long classes on
contemporary religiosities in my large Religion and Sexuality course (once with over 125
students enrolled), which was offered once as an intensive Spring course. Teaching in this
particular course has always presented great challenges, as most students are not from our
religious studies programs, but come from a variety of disciplines, i.e. sexology, feminist
studies, sociology, administration, languages, social service, accounting, mature students,
etc. For those classes, he has taught a few important key concepts in gender and women’s
studies, queer studies and ritual studies. In addition, I have hired him as a teaching
assistant, for corrections, for all those years, both for his knowledge of the content
covered in the course and his professional grading.

FACULTE DES SCIENCES HUMAINES :

Université du Québec a Montréal
The way Mr. Lepage exposes his research is always fascinating and very appropriate to
his theoretical approach to gender and sexual identities, notably among Canadian Pagan
communities. He is always very engaging with students, attentive to their questions and
responding to them, at times, in challenging ways. Despite the complexity of his material,
he always gets good feedback from students. He is always available to meet them to talk
over the content of his presentations and, more importantly, the way he marks their exams.
He also worked with several of my colleagues in our Religious Studies Department,
gaining much experience that has enabled him to develop a number of essential
pedagogical skills.
Over the years, he presented his research at different national conferences (Association
francophone pour le savoir, ACFAS; and a number of academic colloquia) and
international conferences (International “Rites et identités” conference; American
Academy of Religion, AAR, University of Pittsburgh; International Society for the Study
of Religions, ISSR, in Louvain-La-Neuve, en Belgium).
Mr. Lepage would make a good university teacher, appreciated by students. He would be
able to contribute in a positive manner to your Department's teaching mission.
