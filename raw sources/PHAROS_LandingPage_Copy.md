### "Does this apply to me?" Section

**Headline:** Three questions. If you answer yes to any of them, the EU AI Act applies to your business.

1. **Do you have European customers, users, or partners?** If your AI system produces outputs that are used by anyone in the EU — even a single account — you are in scope under Article 2 of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689. The AI Act is extraterritorial. Being headquartered in Canada does not exempt you.

2. **Do you use AI in any of these decisions?** Hiring or candidate screening. Credit, insurance, or pricing. Access to education, healthcare, or essential services. Worker monitoring or performance management. Biometric identification. Anything classified under Annex III.

3. **Are you a provider, deployer, importer, or distributor of an AI system?** All four roles carry obligations. If you embed a third-party model in your product, you are a deployer with documentation, transparency, and human-oversight requirements regardless of where the underlying model was built.

If any of these is yes and you don't have a written, defensible answer to "what's our AI Act compliance posture?" you have a 93-day problem.

### How It Works Section

**Week 1 — Scoping and inventory.** Two structured interviews (CEO/founder, technical lead). Document collection. We map your AI footprint and your customer geography. By Friday of Week 1 you have a draft inventory for review.

**Week 2 — Classification and exposure.** We classify every system, run the deployer/provider analysis, and identify which AI Act obligations apply to which system. Mid-week interim review with you to validate scope.

**Week 3 — Gap assessment and prioritization.** We compare your existing posture to required controls, by article and clause. Findings are tiered by exposure (highest = direct AI Act high-risk obligation gap; medium = ISO 42001 readiness gap; lowest = good-practice gap).

**Week 4 — Deliverable, board memo, walkthrough.** Final document delivered Monday of Week 4. Two-hour walkthrough session with you and up to three of your team members on Wednesday. One round of revisions inside ten business days post-delivery.

**Body:**

The AI Act has been on the horizon since 2021. Three things changed in the last 90 days that compress the timeline:

### FAQ Section

**Is my AI actually "high-risk" under the Act?**
Probably not all of it. Most companies have a mix: some systems are high-risk (anything in Annex III), most are limited-risk (chatbots, recommendation systems with transparency obligations), and some fall outside scope entirely. The diagnostic distinguishes them. Most clients discover that 10–25% of their AI footprint is high-risk and the rest is manageable under lighter obligations. The point is to know which is which.

**What if I don't have any EU customers?**
Then the AI Act may not apply to you directly — but Quebec Law 25 does, OSFI E-23 does if you're a federally regulated FI, and ISO/IEC 42001 is increasingly cited in Canadian and U.S. enterprise procurement. The Readiness Check (one week, $4,500) is the right first step to confirm. If you're genuinely outside all of these regimes, we'll tell you that and refund the engagement.

**What about the Omnibus delay — should I just wait?**
No. The trilogue failed on April 28, 2026 and the original deadline is in force. Even if a delay were adopted between now and August, the high-risk obligations would still apply to systems already on the EU market on the trigger date. Waiting trades small certainty now for large uncertainty in two months.

**Can you do this faster than four weeks?**
Yes, for the Readiness Check (one week). Not for the Diagnostic. Four weeks is the minimum responsible time for the inventory, classification, and gap work. Companies that promise diagnostic-level output in five days are selling inventory templates, not assessments.

**What happens after the diagnostic?**
You own the deliverable and can act on it however you want — including taking it to another firm to implement. Most clients want to discuss implementation with us, but that's a separate engagement, scoped after the diagnostic, with its own fixed fee. There is no implicit obligation to continue.

**Are you certified or accredited?**
PHAROS AI is a Quebec-registered consultancy (CAE 7771, 7759). We are not a notified body and do not certify systems — that role belongs to designated EU bodies and is not what the diagnostic is. If you want certification, you need a notified body; we can identify the right one for your sector as part of the diagnostic.

**Who is doing the work?**
Martin Lepage, founder, on every engagement. Doctoral training in qualitative institutional analysis, methodology development since 2024, seventeen documented governance modules. PHAROS does not subcontract diagnostics or staff them with juniors. If that scales below what we can sustain, the price goes up before the team grows.

**Why $18,000 and not more?**
Because mid-market Canadian companies need to be able to say yes to this on a credit card or a same-week PO, not negotiate it through procurement for six weeks. The price is set for speed of decision, not for margin. Enterprise-scope work is priced separately.
