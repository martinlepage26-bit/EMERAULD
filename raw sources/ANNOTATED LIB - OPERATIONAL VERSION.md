National Institute of Standards and Technology - Artificial Intelligence Risk
Management Framework (AI RMF 1.0) (2023)

Type: Framework
Governance role: Voluntary operational risk-management framework
Suggested reference entry: Tabassi, E. (2023). Artificial Intelligence Risk
Management Framework (AI RMF 1.0). National Institute of Standards and
Technology. https://doi.org/10.6028/NIST.AI.100-1
Primary URL:
https://www.nist.gov/publications/artificial-intelligence-risk-management-framework-ai-rmf-10
Issuer / jurisdiction: NIST, United States
Current status: Active voluntary framework with supporting playbooks, profiles,
and crosswalks.
Use this when: You need a serious operational backbone for responsible AI,
risk-management language that reviewers recognize, or a lifecycle structure for
governance analysis.
What it gives you: Four functions - Govern, Map, Measure, Manage - plus a
shared vocabulary for trustworthiness, lifecycle risk, and institutional
responsibility.
Operational move: Use it to structure governance sections, implementation
roadmaps, procurement criteria, risk registers, or comparative framework tables.
Best paired with: ISO/IEC 42001, NIST SP 800-53, model cards, datasheets.
Watch-outs: It is deliberately non-prescriptive and does not by itself certify
that a system is safe or compliant.
Search tags: risk management, lifecycle, trustworthy AI, governance, controls

International Organization for Standardization / International Electrotechnical
Commission - ISO/IEC 42001:2023

Type: Standard
Governance role: Auditable AI management-system standard
Suggested reference entry: ISO/IEC. (2023). ISO/IEC 42001:2023 AI management
systems. International Organization for Standardization.
https://www.iso.org/standard/42001
Primary URL: https://www.iso.org/standard/42001
Issuer / jurisdiction: ISO/IEC, international
Current status: Active international standard; usable in certification and audit
contexts.
Use this when: You need organizational governance requirements, management
system logic, or an auditable standard rather than a general ethics framework.
What it gives you: Leadership accountability, documented processes, continual
improvement logic, operational controls, and a management-system architecture
for AI governance programs.
Operational move: Use it to show how an organization could institutionalize AI
governance beyond one-off ethics reviews or model-specific checklists.
Best paired with: NIST AI RMF for risk framing; sector rules for domain
requirements; internal control libraries for implementation.
Watch-outs: Certification demonstrates process maturity, not guaranteed
substantive safety or fairness outcomes.
Search tags: management system, assurance, certification, AIMS, audit

European Union - AI Act

Type: Regulation
Governance role: Binding risk-based AI regulation
Suggested reference entry: European Commission. (2024). AI Act.
https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/regulatory-framework-ai
Primary URL:
https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/regulatory-framework-ai
Issuer / jurisdiction: European Union
Current status: In force since 2024-08-01, with phased application through
2027.
Use this when: You need the strongest current example of binding AI regulation,
risk-tiered obligations, or the clearest legal comparator for global governance
analysis.
What it gives you: A legal architecture covering prohibited practices,
high-risk AI obligations, transparency rules, and governance for general-purpose
AI models.
Operational move: Use it to move from principles talk to actual legal duties,
sanctions, implementation timelines, and institutional enforcement questions.
Best paired with: OECD AI Principles for soft-law contrast; ISO/IEC 42001 for
organizational compliance processes; NIST AI RMF for operationalization.
Watch-outs: Parts of compliance practice still depend on standards, guidance,
and institutional interpretation; do not flatten the phased timeline into a
single "fully active" moment.
Search tags: EU, regulation, high-risk AI, GPAI, compliance, enforcement

Endnote

Minimum operational source stack for most papers:
1. NIST AI RMF for operational governance language.
2. ISO/IEC 42001 or EU AI Act for institutional or legal structure.
3. Model Cards plus Datasheets for documentation artifacts.
4. AI Incident Database for real-world examples.
5. Stanford HAI or AI Now depending on whether you need descriptive metrics or
   structural critique.

If writing a literature review, sort these entries into five buckets:
- soft law and policy principles
- formal standards and management systems
- binding regulation
- documentation and assurance artifacts
- empirical harms and institutional critiques
