1) Website Style Card

House style for public posts

Purpose

Write governance like infrastructure. Stay out of moral panic and out of compliance theater. Bind to interfaces, traces, routing, incentives.

Core stance

Not anti-AI

Not techno-utopian

Not academic exhibition

Not “principles-only” ethics

Yes to: workflow reality, enforceable constraints, accountable ownership

Default argument shape

Claim → Mechanism → Consequence → Constraint

Claim: one sentence that says what is happening

Mechanism: how it happens in practice (workflow, interface, record, queue)

Consequence: what shifts for power, access, risk, legitimacy

Constraint: what to do that is buildable, auditable, and owned

Voice and texture

Light, readable sentences with occasional density spikes

Controlled heat is allowed, one sharp line per paragraph max

Metaphor is allowed only if grounded immediately in a concrete referent

Refuse simple binaries by design, not by hedging

Structural rules

Open with the governing claim in the first paragraph

Use 2–4 short sections, each with one clean claim

End with a soft CTA that invites a practical question, not a moral pose

Style constraints you always keep

No sentence begins with “And”

No em dashes

Avoid rhetorical fog like “in today’s world,” “it is important to note,” “as we know”

Prefer specific nouns over abstract nouns when possible

Vocabulary you can reuse without sounding repetitive

binding site, intake, triage, routing, escalation, retention, trace, contestability, ownership, lock-in, drift, legibility

CTA tone

Soft, invitational, not salesy

Aim for “Try this question on your workflow” energy

Keep it one to two sentences

Receipts policy for public posts

Use a compact Receipts block when you make factual, time-bound, legal, regulatory, standards, enforcement, or market claims.

Prefer primary sources, regulators, standards bodies, official guidance

Use non-academic formatting: short labels, clean links on the site

If it is conceptual and not time-bound, keep receipts minimal and curated

Do

Name the site of power: front door, form, queue, log, handoff

Give readers an audit question and a constraint they can implement

Make the institution visible without moralizing

Do not

Anthropomorphize AI as an agent with intentions

Hide behind “bias” as the only frame

Say “transparency” without saying retention, access, and afterlife

Write solutions that are only training, values, or awareness
