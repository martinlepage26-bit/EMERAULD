1. Age 4–5: the feral cat bite
   When I was very young, I got bitten badly by a feral cat near my old house. I had to go to the hospital for tetanus. What stayed with me was not only the pain, but how little anyone seemed to register the seriousness of it afterward.

2. Age 5 (approx.): Martine’s lip-sync contest
   When my sister Martine prepared for a lip-sync contest at a mall, my dad mixed up the date and brought her to the actual contest instead of the rehearsal. She had to perform without her costume, in snowmobile boots and everyday clothes. Even then, I could feel that he didn’t care enough to get the basics right for us.

3. Age 7–8 (approx.): the camping bike crash
   I went camping with my sister Martine and her friends. When we left, I was given a thin city bike I wasn’t used to. I went down a hill, couldn’t manage the brakes, and ended up in the ditch with my lip split open. My sister was nowhere to be found, and I was comforted by people who weren’t really mine.

4. Age 7–8 (approx.): the sleepover where I get kicked
   Around the same period, I slept at my sister’s friend’s house with older teens around. They were sniffing gas or paint. One of the young men came into my room and kicked me in my bed because I was in the way.

5. Age 8–9 (approx.): learning I’m often on my own
   Before I even had language for it, I learned that I could be present in adult worlds while still being basically unprotected, treated like an obstacle instead of a child.

6. Age 9 (Jonquière/Saguenay): being left alone by my father
   When I was nine, I lived with my father and he often left me alone. I remember the feeling of being too small for the house and too alone for the woods around it.

7. Age 9 (Jonquière/Saguenay, Lac Kénogami): Earth becomes sacred
   For about a year or a year and a half when I was nine, we lived out in the woods near Lac Kénogami. That is where I realized that nature was alive and that Earth was divine and sacred. I remember sensing energies around trees, and I remember laying in the snow and feeling warm from the ground and the forest itself.

8. Age 9 (Jonquière/Saguenay): animals and the cost of life
   During that time we had rabbits and chickens, a horse, and two dogs. We collected eggs, and we also ate rabbits and chickens. That taught me the value of life and how adults can treat killing as ordinary. That period made me a pagan.

9. Age 9 (Saguenay, December weekend): abandoned in the forest house
   One December evening my father told me I’d go to my mother’s after school and spend the weekend there. When I arrived Friday around 4, my stepfather, furious, drove me back to my father’s place immediately. Nobody was home. I was left alone in a big house in the woods in Saguenay at nine.

10. Age 9 (Jonquière/Saguenay): snowmobile milk accident
    One winter, my father and I took the snowmobile to buy milk. I sat behind him holding the bags. We hit a curb and I got thrown off, still holding the milk. He didn’t notice for a while, and when he did he laughed at the fact that I never dropped the bags.

11. 1993, age 9 (living with my mother): the Hocus Pocus trick
    In 1993, after *Hocus Pocus*, my friends tricked me into believing I had turned into a black cat. At sunrise they said I had turned back and laughed at me behind my back. I believed it and started telling people, and people thought I was nuts, so I stopped talking. Still, it seeded something. Hocus Pocus made me a witch.

12. Age 9–10: left to myself, building inner life
    Even when I was living with my mother, I was often left to myself. I learned early how to retreat inward and how to hold experiences privately.

13. Age 10 (approx.): the pool dunking
    One summer, in a pool at my dad’s girlfriend’s brother’s place, she dunked my head hard under water, then laughed and told me it was her birthday and she could do whatever she wanted.

14. Age 11: solitary dedication
    At 11, I decided to dedicate myself as a solitary witch. It was a real choice, not a game.

15. Age 11: the four oaths
    When I dedicated myself, I pledged four oaths: to know, to will, to dare, to be silent. I carried those words like a rulebook and a shield.

16. Age 12: broken leg
    When I was around 12, I broke my leg jumping off a roof into snow that had hardened.

17. Age 12: stepdad’s motorcycle accident, my injury disappears
    At the same time, my stepdad had a major motorcycle accident. With my broken leg and his injury, I had to go live with him. I thought I was helping, but the effect was that my own injury became invisible.

18. Age 12: blocked memory, something very dark
    Around that same period, I became aware something very dark happened involving my former stepdad’s son, because the memory is blocked and there are parts of the house I can’t access in my mind.

19. Age 13: slap patterns and what they taught me
    I remember my dad slapping my sister a lot, and I remember him slapping me hard enough to leave a hand-shaped mark. That was the atmosphere: correction through violence.

20. Age 13: stopping my mother from slapping me
    I remember my mother trying to slap me when I was around 13, and I grabbed her hand and stopped it. I told her she couldn’t do that anymore. It felt like drawing a boundary not only for me but also for my sister.

21. Age 13–14: the becoming spell
    When I was around 13 or 14, I didn’t like what I was becoming physically. I cast a spell to become the man I envisioned I would be, the man I felt I deserved to be.

22. Age 13–14: the intuition servant spell
    When I was young, I cast a spell to create a spiritual servant of intuition, meant to warn me against danger and threats. I believe it is still with me.

23. Age 13–14: forgetting where I lived
    One afternoon after school, I stopped in my tracks because I didn’t remember where I lived. Moving around after the divorce fractured something basic: a sense of home.

24. Age 14 (Jonquière): the belvédère ritual with Pascal
    Around 14, I went with my childhood friend Pascal to a high belvédère in Jonquière to do a ritual to help the world heal. We didn’t know what we were doing: random candles, a crucifix. When we did it, the clouds departed and the light hit. It was the first time I felt my power in a direct way.

25. Age 14 (Jonquière): meeting Nathalie + Anne Rice
    Around that same time, I met an older woman, Nathalie, in a jewelry store in Jonquière. She knew a lot and made me advance quickly. She also told me to read Anne Rice, *The Vampire Chronicles* and *The Mayfair Witches*, and those books had a tremendous impact on my spiritual development.

26. Age 14 (first coven begins): Marion, Sylvie, Judith, and the elements
    Around 14, I formed my first coven with Marion, Sylvie, and Judith. We mapped ourselves onto the elements: I was air, Marion was water, Sylvie was earth, and Judith was fire.

27. Age 14 (Judith lasts one summer, then leaves)
    Judith only lasted a summer. She left after we judged her for being very sexual and flirty at a young age.

28. Age 14–16 (the long core): Marion, Sylvie, and I
    After Judith left, it became the three of us: Marion, Sylvie, and me. We stayed close for a long time and went through a lot of struggles together. That trio was the real coven for me.

29. Age 16 (end of high school): François joins as fire, lasts one summer
    At the end of high school, around 16, François came in and replaced the fire role. He only lasted a summer. I didn’t know he was gay. We had a brief affair, I got enthusiastic, told him I loved him, and he left.

30. Ages 16–19 (CEGEP in Jonquière) → age 19+ (Université Laval in Québec City) → 2009 Montréal + later rupture cluster
    Between 16 and 19, I did CEGEP in Jonquière, and by then the coven was effectively gone. At 19, I moved to Québec City to attend Université Laval, where I did my bachelor’s and master’s. In my first year there, my advisor stopped me during a book report and took over, something he did only to me. Over time, I learned to be strategic about my witch identity in academic circles and often chose silence, which fit my oath. In 2009, I moved to Montréal. Later, the Windsor teaching offer and its collapse, the end phase of my PhD, being dumped by Chris, bankruptcy, job loss, the HIV diagnosis, and the deepening meth addiction all braid together in my memory as one connected rupture.
