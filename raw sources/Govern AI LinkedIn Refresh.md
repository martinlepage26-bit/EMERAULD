I help organizations make AI governance legible before scrutiny slows the work.

My work focuses on the point where procurement, audit, vendor review, leadership oversight, and operational reality meet. I design governance structures that teams can actually use: decision rights, risk tiering, controls, review packets, evidence architecture, and upkeep cadence.

The goal is not governance theatre. The goal is to help organizations answer clearly when someone asks how AI is governed, who decides, what is documented, and what evidence can be reviewed.

My practice is based in Montreal and works across regulated and enterprise environments where AI systems need to hold up under pressure.

## Post 1

AI governance usually starts too late.

Not because teams do not care, but because the pressure does not feel real until a questionnaire arrives, an audit starts, a customer asks for evidence, or leadership wants a clear answer on accountability.

That is usually the moment governance stops being abstract and becomes operational.

I have been rebuilding Govern AI around a simple idea:
Legible governance. Legitimate controls. Reviewed evidence.

The work is not to add more policy language.
The work is to make decision rights, control logic, and documentation easy to follow under scrutiny.

https://govern-ai.ca/

## Post 2

What do procurement teams, auditors, and review committees actually need?

Usually the same core layer:
- clear decision rights
- named risk tiers
- controls tied to those tiers
- a review packet someone else can follow
- evidence that can be maintained over time

That is the difference between governance language and governance operations.

Govern AI is being shaped around that distinction.

https://govern-ai.ca/services

## Post 3

A lot of AI governance content stays too general.

I am more interested in the translation layer:
How does a signal become a control?
How does a concern become a review expectation?
How does a governance posture become something legible to a customer, auditor, or committee?

That is why I keep a working research track alongside the consulting work.

https://govern-ai.ca/research

## Post 4

If your team cannot answer these questions quickly, governance work is probably overdue:

- What AI systems are in scope?
- Who approves what?
- Which use cases trigger deeper review?
- What controls apply at each level?
- What evidence exists today?
- What would you send if a customer or auditor asked tomorrow?

That is the practical starting point.
