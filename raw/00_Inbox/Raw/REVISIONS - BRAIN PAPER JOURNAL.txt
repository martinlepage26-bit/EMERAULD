  Review Report

  Summary
  This is an interesting and potentially useful topic, but the manuscript is not yet methodologically secure enough for
  publication. The question is important, and the authors have made a genuine attempt to summarize a small literature,
  but the current version overstates what the included studies actually support and does not meet the reporting standard
  I would expect for a diagnostic accuracy meta-analysis.

  No inflammatory or libelous material noted.

  Major Comments

  1. The scope of the paper and the evidence base do not match. The title and abstract promise a meta-analysis of
     multimodal neuroimaging combined with eye-tracking /tmp/manuscript-to-review.md:1, /tmp/manuscript-to-review.md:17,
     but Table 1 includes neuroimaging-only studies, eye-tracking-only studies, transcriptomics, and MRI+EEG+cognitive
     studies /tmp/manuscript-to-review.md:103, /tmp/manuscript-to-review.md:108, /tmp/manuscript-to-review.md:111, /tmp/
     manuscript-to-review.md:114, /tmp/manuscript-to-review.md:115. I do not see a clearly identified study in which
     neuroimaging and eye-tracking were fused in a single classifier. As written, the pooled “multimodal” conclusion is
     therefore not directly supported. The manuscript needs either a much narrower framing or a completely re-scoped
     synthesis.
  2. The statistical approach is not sufficiently rigorous or reproducible for a diagnostic accuracy review. The methods
     state that DerSimonian-Laird pooling was used for accuracy, sensitivity, specificity, and AUC /tmp/manuscript-to-
     review.md:75, but there is no risk-of-bias assessment, no protocol registration, no description of how threshold
     effects were handled, no explanation of whether multiple models within a study were reduced to one estimate, and no
     distinction between internal cross-validation and external validation. For diagnostic test accuracy data, a generic
     random-effects pooling of raw accuracy is usually not enough. At minimum, the paper needs a transparent study-
     quality assessment and a method that is appropriate for paired sensitivity/specificity data or HSROC-style
     synthesis.
  3. The figures and results presentation are internally inconsistent, and some plots look schematic rather than
     empirically derived. The manuscript captions Figure 3 as the SROC plot /tmp/manuscript-to-review.md:171, /tmp/
     manuscript-to-review.md:173, but the embedded graphic itself is titled “Figure 4” /tmp/manuscript_images/
     image3.png. The same off-by-one numbering mismatch continues for the heterogeneity and funnel plots: the manuscript
     captions at /tmp/manuscript-to-review.md:192 and /tmp/manuscript-to-review.md:204 do not match the titles inside /
     tmp/manuscript_images/image4.png and /tmp/manuscript_images/image5.png. More importantly, the SROC graphic is
     labeled “Illustrative,” and the heterogeneity “forest plot” is actually a bar chart of I² values rather than a
     conventional forest plot. These visuals need to be rebuilt from the actual extracted study data or relabeled
     honestly as schematic figures.
  4. The references and citation mapping need a line-by-line audit. Several references appear tangential to the
     manuscript’s topic, including a health-equity review /tmp/manuscript-to-review.md:275, an ADHD eye-tracking
     review /tmp/manuscript-to-review.md:297, and a COVID-19 neuroimaging review /tmp/manuscript-to-review.md:299. There
     are also clear year mismatches between Table 1 and the bibliography: Lei et al. is listed as 2019 in Table 1 /tmp/
     manuscript-to-review.md:108 but appears as a 2020 paper in ref. 16 /tmp/manuscript-to-review.md:301; Liang et al.
     is 2018 in Table 1 /tmp/manuscript-to-review.md:109 but 2019 in ref. 17 /tmp/manuscript-to-review.md:303; Ebdrup et
     al. is 2018 in Table 1 /tmp/manuscript-to-review.md:115 but 2019 in ref. 23 /tmp/manuscript-to-review.md:315. The
     discussion also contains at least one placeholder-style citation, “(LEG, 2019)” /tmp/manuscript-to-review.md:226.
     That combination makes the literature base hard to trust.

  Minor Comments

  1. The manuscript needs a full copyedit. Several sections are grammatically rough or semantically hard to follow,
     including the pooled neuroimaging results paragraph /tmp/manuscript-to-review.md:123 and the conclusion /tmp/
     manuscript-to-review.md:240. This is not just a style issue; it impairs reproducibility and makes the analysis
     difficult to audit.
  2. Please add a formal study-quality / risk-of-bias assessment, ideally QUADAS-2 or an equivalent diagnostic-accuracy
     tool. At present there is no such table or narrative assessment.
  3. Please state whether the review protocol was registered, provide the final search date, and include full database-
     specific search strings in a supplement or appendix. The current search description is too high-level to reproduce.
  4. Please distinguish internal cross-validation from external validation. Treating them as equivalent will inflate the
     apparent performance of classifier studies.
  5. The study with “Not Reported” sample size in Table 1 /tmp/manuscript-to-review.md:110 needs an explicit
     explanation. If it was included in pooling, how were the missing denominators handled?
  6. Please indicate whether sex and gender information were extracted and whether any sex-disaggregated analyses were
     possible. If not, this should be acknowledged as a limitation.
  7. Define every error bar and interval in the figure legends, and explain exactly how each pooled estimate was
     calculated from the study-level outputs.
  8. Standardize terminology and formatting throughout: “eye tracking” versus “eye-tracking,” “multimodal” versus
     “multi-modal,” and figure numbering versus embedded graphic titles.

  Questions for Authors

  1. Did any included study truly integrate neuroimaging and eye-tracking in a single model? If so, please identify it
     explicitly and show where that analysis appears in the paper.
  2. What effect size was actually pooled for the headline results: raw accuracy, sensitivity/specificity, DOR, or AUC?
     Was inverse-variance weighting used?
  3. How did you handle studies that reported multiple models or multiple validation schemes?
  4. Were overlapping cohorts or repeated datasets checked for and excluded?
  5. Can you provide the extraction sheet and analysis code, or at least a complete table of all inputs used for each
     pooled estimate?
