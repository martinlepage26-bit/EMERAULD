# Strategic Storytelling in the AI Economy

## Summary

As AI lowers the cost of building products, the story around the product becomes the primary differentiator. Strategic storytelling — the capacity to understand product, business, and customer simultaneously and connect them into a coherent narrative — is the high-value skill, not writing per se. This maps directly to the position [[Martin Lepage Professional Identity]] occupies: cross-functional, cross-industry, operating at the seam between governance theory and applied consulting. See also [[PHAROS Commercial Strategy]] for how this plays out in Martin's own go-to-market.

## The Core Argument

AI has flooded the market with generic content (the fluency trap — see [[Beyond Fluency — Stuttering, Autoethnography, and Unstable Epistemology]]). Simultaneously, building is cheaper than ever. These two forces together raise the premium on:

- **Narrative coherence** — why this product, why now, why for you
- **Cross-functional translation** — executive vision → concrete customer language
- **Strategic presence** — being the person who can hold the whole picture

The roles carrying this skill are not called "storyteller." They are embedded in comms, product marketing, developer relations, content, and chief of staff functions.

## Nonlinear Career as Structural Advantage

Strategic storytelling requires having crossed multiple domains — academic, clinical, corporate, creative. The person who has worked across industries and functions already inhabits the seam that most people only see from one side. This is not a deficit to apologize for; it is the credential.

## Context for This Vault

Martin's biography — PhD humanities, AI governance consulting, creative writing under [[HEXA Press — Publishing and Practice Initiative]], cross-sector operator — is precisely the profile this argument describes. The question is not whether the skill is present but how explicitly the positioning names it.
