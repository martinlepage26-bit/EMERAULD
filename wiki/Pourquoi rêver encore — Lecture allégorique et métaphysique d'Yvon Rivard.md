# Pourquoi rêver encore — Lecture allégorique et métaphysique d'Yvon Rivard

## Summary

Recent (2024–2026) scholarly essay by [[Martin Lepage — Professional Profile|Martin Lepage]] offering a symbolic and metaphysical reading of Yvon Rivard's first two novels — a **revisitation** of the material first developed in the [[Mort et Naissance et L'Ombre et le Double — MA Thesis on Yvon Rivard (Université Laval, 2010)|2010 master's thesis at Université Laval]]. The "1980" in the filename is a citation date for Rivard's *L'Ombre et le Double*, not composition — per [[Complete Paper List — Martin Lepage Corpus]], all composition dates are 2024–2026 except the 2017 PhD and the 2010 MA. The essay's three formal elements of transfiguration — **l'ombre, le silence, le souffle** — map directly onto operational structures across the architecture (see [[Mythocritique to PHAROS — The 2010 Master's Thesis as Methodological Keystone|keystone]]): *l'ombre* = LOTUS *Sh* (Khaibit shadow remainder); *le silence* = L99 Gap Declaration; *le souffle* = the *ba* (breath of life) operationalized in the speech/TTS work. Part of the [[Research and Papers MOC]].

## Context

This essay analyzes *Mort et Naissance de Christophe Ulric* (1976) and *L'Ombre et le Double* (1980) as two volumes of a single poetic and spiritual cycle. The text is archived in two versions in raw sources: a longer full essay and a shorter CV/résumé format. The essay draws on narratology, mythocritique (Frye, Bachelard, Eliade), and situated subjectivity theory (Haraway, Bertaux, Beauchemin). It connects to [[Martin Lepage — Professional Profile|Martin Lepage]]'s broader career interest in the politics of interiority, spiritual cartography, and the intersection of literary form with power. The essay's concern with who is permitted to "dream," withdraw, and narrate inner life anticipates the queer critique of legitimacy in later work documented in [[Refusing Fixity — Aesthetic Governance and Fugitive Authorship]].

## Details

**Subject:** Yvon Rivard's early novels analyzed as modern spiritual allegory. The protagonist figure traverses shadow, silence, and breath in a process of inner transfiguration (*transfiguration*).

**Method:** Mythocritique combined with narratology and a politically situated reading of interiority. The essay argues that the inner quest in Rivard's texts is not apolitical — who can afford introspection is itself a question of material and symbolic conditions.

**Central claim:** Rivard's writing creates ritual texts (*textes-rites*) where a fragmented subject moves through instability toward recomposition. These novels offer "une forme discrète de réenchantement" — a discreet form of re-enchantment — and affirm literature as spiritual and poetic resistance.

**Political dimension:** The essay explicitly notes that even introspective literature is traversed by power relations. The question of who has the material, symbolic, and psychic conditions to withdraw and imagine an alternative self is posed as a political question, not merely an aesthetic one.

**Language:** French. Both versions in raw sources are in French. This is one of a small number of French-language scholarly texts in the corpus; others include the [[Agatha All Along — Wicca, Digital Mediatization, and Proof Regimes|Agatha All Along]] article and [[CORPUS ou le génie de l'insistance — Novel|CORPUS]].

## Key Ideas

- Literature as spiritual cartography: Rivard composes a "géographie secrète de l'expérience intérieure"
- The inner quest as politically situated, not merely aesthetic — anticipates the situated epistemology in [[Beyond Fluency — Stuttering, Autoethnography, and Unstable Epistemology]]
- The threshold (seuil) as recurring structure: seuil of speech/silence, dream/real, love/soul
- Re-enchantment as resistance: literature's capacity to hold sacred possibility against disenchanting rationality

## Insights

- This essay establishes early in [[Martin Lepage — Professional Profile|Martin Lepage]]'s career the interest in spiritual legitimacy and who controls access to it — a concern that runs through the entire [[Historical Academic Portfolio — Pre-PHAROS Scholarly Work]] and culminates in the [[PHAROS Recalibration — Unified Governance Architecture|PHAROS]] governance method
- The essay's concern with "who can dream" is a proto-queer question about permission and interiority, even before the queer theoretical framework is explicit
