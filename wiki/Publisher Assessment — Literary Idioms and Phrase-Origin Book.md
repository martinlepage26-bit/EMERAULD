# Publisher Assessment — Literary Idioms and Phrase-Origin Book

## Summary

Publisher and developmental editor assessment of a pre-manuscript reference compiling ~100+ literary idioms and phrases from Shakespeare, Greek mythology, Aesop, Biblical sources, pop culture, nautical/sporting origins, and modern slang. Genre: popular etymology / phrase-origin non-fiction. Verdict: **research is here; the book is not yet.** The project needs a voice decision, structural rebuild, deduplication, and attribution fact-check before submission. See also [[HENRY — Research Paper Writing System]] for the writing infrastructure that supports manuscript development.

## Context

Session run 2026-05-04 using `/publisher` skill. Material submitted: a categorized reference list organized partly by source (Shakespeare, mythology, Aesop, Biblical), partly by theme (misfortune, fate, paradox), and partly by origin domain (nautical, sporting, combat). No author voice present in the submitted material. Assessed against the popular etymology genre and comparable titles. Connects to [[Publishing Strategy — Springer Trilogy and Parallel Tracks]] as an adjacent (non-academic) publishing track; this is trade non-fiction, not the Springer/academic pipeline.

## Details

### Genre and Market Viability

The phrase-origin / popular etymology genre has a proven commercial audience. Comparable titles (assumed from memory — verify sales data before submission):

- Mark Forsyth, *The Etymologicon* (2011) — witty, digressive, strong authorial voice
- Susie Dent, *Word Perfect* — warm, scholarly, phrase-a-day format
- Michael Quinion, *Port Out, Starboard Home* — focused on nautical and historical phrase origins
- Bill Bryson, *Bryson's Dictionary of Troublesome Words*

The raw material covers the same territory these books cover. The market exists. The gap is not content — it is form and voice.

### Ranked Developmental Findings

**P0 — Voice does not exist**

Every book in this genre sells on authorial personality. The submitted material has no narrator, no angle, no distinctive register. It reads as organized research notes. Until the author decides what their lens is (wit, surprise, cultural critique, pedantic delight) and writes every entry *through* that lens, there is no book — only a list that competes with free websites.

**P1 — Redundancy**

"Bite the bullet" appears at least twice (under Combat & Weaponry and under Nautical terms). A full deduplication pass is required before any structural work.

**P1 — Organizing logic is inconsistent**

Three incompatible systems coexist without resolution:
- Source-based (Shakespeare, Greek mythology, Aesop, Biblical)
- Theme-based (misfortune, fate, knowledge, paradox)
- Origin-domain-based (nautical, sporting, combat)

One system must be chosen and applied throughout. Source-based or origin-domain-based will likely give the most narrative momentum for trade publication.

**P2 — Attribution accuracy requires a fact-check pass**

| Phrase | Claim in manuscript | Issue |
|---|---|---|
| "My bad" | Popularized by *Clueless* (1995) | Pre-dates the film; basketball slang from early 1980s |
| "Cold feet" | Stephen Crane, *Maggie* (1896) | Crane used it; likely older — verify against OED |
| "Break the ice" | Shakespeare, *Taming of the Shrew* | Contested; pre-Shakespearean sources exist |

A book in this genre is destroyed by one publicized error. Budget a professional fact-check.

**P3 — Audience architecture unclear**

Is this a reference book (looked up by phrase) or a reading book (read cover to cover)? These require different physical and structural architectures. The decision must precede any structural rebuild.

**P3 — Register inconsistency**

Some entries are crisp. Others are over-explained. Hedging words like "Interestingly" and parenthetical cautions ("Note: This is a darker reference…") undercut the tone and should be cut on revision.

### Required Steps Before Submission

1. Write 10 sample entries in the full decided voice — not placeholder prose
2. Choose one organizing principle; rebuild the structure
3. Full deduplication pass
4. Attribution fact-check against OED, Phrase Finder, or equivalent primary sources
5. Decide: reference book or reading book architecture

### Assessment Summary

Strong raw material in a viable genre. The gap between here and a publishable manuscript is a voice decision and a structural rebuild — neither is small, but both are solvable. Do not submit in current form.
