### Subject

A symbolic, mythocritical, and metaphysical reading of Yvon Rivard's first two novels, treated as two volumes of a single allegorical-spiritual cycle:

- *Mort et Naissance de Christophe Ulric* (Éditions La Presse, 1976; Éditions Leméac, 1986)
- *L'Ombre et le double* (Éditions internationales Alain Stanké, 1979; Boréal Compact n° 75, 1996)

Both novels stage a quest with a manifest sense (literal: love in MN, origins in OD) and a figurative sense (allegorical: spiritual unity through traversal of life and death). The thesis demonstrates that the spiritual dimension is structured by a recognizable archetypal pattern recalling the Egyptian *Livre des Morts*.

### Methodology and authorities

**Mythocritique** chosen by **explicit rejection** of two alternatives (conclusion p. 83):

> *"Nous avons choisi de ne pas diriger notre analyse vers une sociocritique, qui ignorerait tout élément spirituel particulier à un seul auteur, ou vers une psychocritique, qui tend minutieusement à laisser de côté les sphères du divin, de l'invisible et de l'esprit comme semblent l'entendre les romans de Rivard."*

**Authorities** (from bibliography pp. 86–90):

- *Mythocritique:* Frye (*Anatomie de la critique*, *Littérature et mythe*), Eliade (*Aspects du mythe*, *Images et symboles*, *Le mythe de l'éternel retour*, *Histoire des croyances*), Brunel (*Mythocritique : théorie et parcours*), Gilbert Durand (*Figures mythiques et visages de l'œuvre : de la mythocritique à la mythanalyse*)

### Central thesis

The two Rivard novels are structured by an Egyptian spiritual triad — ***âme/double/ombre*** (*ba/ka/khaibit*) — translated narratively into actantial roles:

- *Mort et Naissance de Christophe Ulric* (love quest):
  - Geneviève = âme (destinataire and objet)
  - Ernest Charlevoix = double (adjuvant)
  - Philémon = ombre (opposant et adjuvant)
- *L'Ombre et le Double* (origins quest):
  - Marie = âme (destinataire and objet)
  - Thomas* (parallel narrator) = double (adjuvant)
  - Gaspard = ombre (opposant)

The triad operates as a system translating "la conception spirituelle de l'être" (conclusion p. 84) and structures the spiritual passage between life and death — analogous to but not derivative from the Osirian myth (p. 81).

### Diamond-Eyes anticipation

The conclusion (p. 84) cites Pierre Vadeboncoeur on Rivard:

> *"L'humanité dont il fait preuve donne la mesure de son caractère. […] La pensée d'un être n'est pas indépendante de la mesure de son âme."*

This is the doctrine that becomes [[Diamond-Eyes — Aesthetic Refinement Skill|Diamond-Eyes]] as a non-exceptionable wisdom-and-care gate before promotion: thought of a being is not independent of measure of soul; method without character is dishonest.

### Closing sentence (p. 85)

> *"En tant que l'œuvre d'Yvon Rivard crée un lien direct entre littérature et spiritualité, comme une poétique de la quête de l'unité et de la plénitude, elle dénote une grandeur d'esprit et une profonde compréhension de la vie humaine."*

A *poétique de la quête de l'unité et de la plénitude* — a poetics of the quest for unity and plenitude. This phrase characterizes Rivard's work; per the keystone analysis, it equally characterizes the operator's own subsequent work (novels, governance method, vault architecture).

## Bounded claims

This note describes the thesis as **documentary substrate**. It does **not** claim:

- That every later operationalization was consciously derived from this thesis. Many later artifacts were authored by the operator without explicit reference to it.
- That the operator currently reads the cited Hermetic authorities (Vivenza/Guénon, Bernard *La science occulte égyptienne*) as living doctrine vs. historical scaffolding. That distinction is a question for authorial attestation.
- That the methodology is closed. The conclusion (p. 84) explicitly anticipates broader mythanalytical extensions across other Quebec authors (Vadeboncoeur, Gabrielle Roy, Hubert Aquin).
