# Queering Neo-Pagan Magic — 2006 Paper

## Summary
Full manuscript of "Queering Neo-Pagan Magic: Identity, Power, and the Uses of Magic" by [[Martin Lepage — Professional Profile|Martin Lepage, PhD]]. An ethnographic paper drawing on fieldwork with queer Pagan practitioners in Montreal, arguing that magic is not merely a symbolic or religious act but a queer methodology of self and world-making. Engages Michael Bailey's "The Meanings of Magic" (2006) and reframes magic's analytical instability as a resource rather than a problem.

## Details

### Core Argument
Queer Pagan practitioners in Montreal do not define magic. They inhabit it. Magic is not asked "Is it real?" but "What happens when I do this?" This pragmatic, non-definitional orientation is treated here as *queer methodology*: practice over belief, improvisation over doctrine, ambiguity over coherence.

In dialogue with Bailey (2006), who argues magic's analytic value lies in its constitutive instability as a boundary category, this paper agrees on the diagnosis but shifts the prescription: instability is not a problem to be managed comparatively but a resource practitioners themselves inhabit and weaponize.

### Three Motifs of Queer Magical Practice
1. **Magic as transformation** — Jay (pseudonym): magic as "electrical fields between people" and a framework of spiritual self-responsibility. Power without submission.
2. **Magic as healing** — Maria: "energy work" and somatic repair. Magic is relational and reciprocal, not symbolic.
3. **Magic as meaning-making** — Manon (trans woman): "When I do magic, I'm writing new stories." Magic as mythic intervention and narrative self-authorship.

### Practitioner Ontologies
Practitioners' views are unified not by shared belief but by shared orientation away from definition:
- Helena: "Magic is energy. It's in everything." Animist cosmology that collapses sacred/secular.
- Simon (trans man): magic as "shifting the odds" — probabilistic supplement to mundane effort, not a guarantee.

### Three Social Levels
- **Individual**: magic as identity technology, inscribing coherence into lives marked by erasure
- **Relational**: magic builds community (Lokkabrenna — queer-inclusive Pagan kindred in Montreal)
- **Cultural**: magic as re-enchantment and reworlding, following Sabina Magliocco

### The Academic Provocation
To study queer magic is to confront the limits of scholarly method. The question "Do they really believe it?" imposes a false binary (authenticity vs play) that queer practitioners refuse. The paper argues that our categories — belief, tradition, religion — are showing their limits, not the practitioners'.

## Key Ideas
- Magic functions as a *verb* not a *noun* in queer practice
- Illegitimacy is not endured but cultivated as sacred ground
- Queer magic disturbs academic method precisely because it refuses doctrinal consistency and empirical legibility
- Following Lamoureux: queer theory is not additive but subtractive and destabilizing
- The witch becomes a subject of her own cosmology, not its spectacle

## Relationship to 2026 Papers (Resolved)
The [[Queering Neo-Pagan Magic — 2026 Paper]] and [[Queering Neo-Pagan Magic — Identity Power and the Uses of Magic]] are **distinct papers** from this one. Both draw on the same Montreal fieldwork and engage Bailey (2006), but the 2026 papers introduce entirely new theoretical frameworks (nomadic epistemology, tactical illegitimacy) not present here. This is the foundational base; the 2026 papers are the mature theoretical synthesis twenty years later.
