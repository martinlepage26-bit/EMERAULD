# Is Sacred Nature Gendered or Queer — Becci & Grandjean 2022 (Eco-Spiritual Activism Switzerland)

## Summary
Irene Becci and Alexandre Grandjean analyze eco-spiritual activism in French-speaking Switzerland (2015–2020), showing how gendered imaginaries (“Mother Earth”, gendered nature spirits) coexist with and sometimes shift toward **queer** approaches to ecology. Across three milieus (New Age/holistic, transition network, neo-shamanism), they document a range from essentialist/organicist framings to non-binary/queer ecological thought, and they argue for contextualizing “planetary/universal” imaginaries by relating them to individuals’ gendered selves and social capital. Sits alongside [[Queerness and Transgender Identity — Lepage 2017 (Pagan Montreal, Wicca vs Reclaiming)|the Montreal Pagan study]] as a comparative francophone case for how queer/trans participation reshapes spiritual milieus.

## Key Ideas (vault-usable)
- “Spiritualization of ecology” as a public-facing discursive/practice bundle that circulates across milieus — the same circulation logic [[Inductive Literary Discourse Analysis — Witchcraft in Song Lyrics]] traces across pop-cultural surfaces.
- Gender language can be joyful/ubiquitous without constituting feminist emancipation; later observations show increasing feminist visibility — a useful caution paralleling the [[Authority Without Ethics — The Love Witch|authority-without-ethics]] reading of female-power aesthetics.
- Uses Timothy Morton’s “queer ecology” as a non-essentialist lens: “Nature” as constructed exteriority; ecology as entanglement — pairs with [[The Meanings of Magic — Bailey 2006 (Magic as Unstable Category)|Bailey’s category-instability]] for a parallel anti-essentialist move on “magic.”

## Why it matters here
- Directly relevant to Martin’s Montreal pagan/neo-shamanism adjacency notes and to how gender/queer frames travel through “nature” talk in contemporary spiritual milieus; reinforces the [[Queer Media and Ritual Map|Queer Media and Ritual Map]] cluster and supplies a Swiss comparison case for [[Social Compass Paper — Ritual Authority and Régime de Preuve|the Social Compass paper]].
