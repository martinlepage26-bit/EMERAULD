# The Returning Light — Monograph

## Summary
Philosophical-poetic monograph (2025) by Martin Lepage. Published by HEX/ADECIMAL Press. Engages queer theory, ritual studies, and posthumanism as interlocking frameworks. Thematically organized around return, light, and the figure of what comes back. **The "returning light" is structurally the Osirian rebirth structure** named in the [[Mort et Naissance et L'Ombre et le Double — MA Thesis on Yvon Rivard (Université Laval, 2010)|2010 MA thesis]] (p. 81: *"À l'image du mythe osirien, la triade spirituelle relate ce passage migratoire"*); the Phoenix-as-rebirth motif (MA p. 67) is the closest analog in the operator's creative output to the substrate. A *texte-rite* exemplar in the Rivardian lineage (see [[Mythocritique to PHAROS — The 2010 Master's Thesis as Methodological Keystone]]). Part of the creative-corpus ring with [[Alchemy of the Wound — Novel]], [[The Broken Frequency of the Word — Novel]], [[Le sang qui porte ton nom — Novel]], [[CORPUS ou le génie de l'insistance — Novel]], [[The Weather Beneath the Walls — Novel]], and [[The Palace Under Root — Allegory and the Keyport Novel]]. The "returning" figure connects to the loop structure analyzed in [[Loop Papers and Recursive Governance]].

## Three Frameworks

### Queer Theory
Deployed not as identity politics but as epistemology: a method for analyzing what gets categorized as natural, normative, or self-evident, and how those categorizations do work to exclude what they do not name. The "returning light" figure is queer in this sense — it returns to what was assumed to be gone, revealing that the absence was a governance choice, not a fact.

**Connection to governance work**: the queer epistemological move — asking what appears natural because it is categorized that way — is the same move as narrative control analysis in [[Governance by Denial]]. The naturalization of authority is both a queer studies problem and a governance problem.

### Ritual Studies
Drawn from the PhD research (UQAM 2017) and the ethnographic fieldwork that produced the [[Social Compass Paper — Ritual Authority and Régime de Preuve]]. The monograph uses ritual as a concept for repetition-with-transformation: rituals do not repeat the same thing; they repeat a structure through which something new can emerge each time.

**Connection to governance work**: recursive transformation in the [[PHAROS Invention Disclosure]] is a ritual structure in this sense — the same pipeline processes different corpora and produces different governance decisions, but the *structure* is repeated. The determinism is in the structure, not the content.

### Posthumanism
Critiques the figure of the autonomous, self-sufficient human subject that underlies both liberal political theory and classical AI ethics. The posthumanist claim: the subject is always already constituted through its entanglements with non-human systems (language, infrastructure, ecology, technology).

**Connection to governance work**: the Machine Limitation principle from AGENTS.md — "The machine operates through language. The gap between model and reality is structural and permanent" — is a posthumanist acknowledgment. The AI system is not a surrogate human; it is a different kind of entanglement with language and infrastructure.

## The Figure of the Returning Light

Light that has already passed — from a star that may no longer exist, from a moment that has ended, from a person who has left. The returning light is real (it is genuinely here, now) but its source is in the past and may be gone. The question: what do you do with real light from a possibly-extinct source?

This is the archive problem: prior outputs are real (they exist, they have effects) but their source — the reasoning process that produced them — may no longer be accessible. Working with the returning light means working with real effects whose causes you cannot verify by checking the source.

**Governance application**: the archive-becomes-agent problem in [[Recursive Continuity Without Memory — AI Identity Across Sessions]] is the same as the returning light problem applied to AI sessions. Prior outputs are the returning light. Their source (the reasoning session that produced them) is gone. The governance question is the same: what admissibility rules apply to real light from a possibly-extinct source?

## Poetic Structure

The monograph uses prose poetry and lyric essay sections alongside argument. The poetic sections are not decoration — they are doing epistemological work that the argument cannot do:

- Argument establishes what can be said clearly
- Poetic sections establish what can be gestured toward without claiming
- The boundary between them is the admissibility boundary of the work itself

This is the autofiction principle (from [[Alchemy of the Wound — Novel]]) applied to philosophy: the form enacts what the content argues.

## Connections Across the Creative Work

The three 2025 creative works form a loose trilogy:

| Work | Form | Central Figure | Epistemological Move |
|---|---|---|---|
| *Alchemy of the Wound* | Novel/autofiction | The wound / red seed | Failure harvesting applied to personal experience |
| *The Returning Light* | Philosophical-poetic monograph | Returning light / extinct source | Archive problem applied to time, memory, presence |
| *The Broken Frequency of the Word* | Novel | Grief / shared frequency | Resolution through resonance, not closure |

Each work addresses a version of the same problem: what do you do with what remains when the source is gone or broken? The governance methodology's answer (bounded claims, declared gaps, failure harvesting — see [[Recursive Deterministic AI Governance — Method and Paper]] Stage 12) and the creative work's answers (alchemy, returning light, resonant frequency) are parallel responses to the same epistemological condition.

## Insights

- The returning light figure is the most precise image in the entire creative work for the archive-becomes-agent problem. It captures the distinction between "the light is real" (the prior output exists and has effects) and "the source may be gone" (the reasoning process that produced it is inaccessible) without claiming to resolve the tension
- Posthumanism is the philosophical ground for the Machine Limitation principle: the machine does not fail to be human; it is a different kind of entanglement. Governance for AI systems built on this ground will look different from governance built on the "AI as surrogate human" frame
- The queer epistemological move — asking what appears natural — is undertheorized in the AI governance literature. Most AI ethics frameworks treat "human values" as natural; a queer approach would ask what values appear natural because they have been successfully naturalized, and whose interests that naturalization serves
- The form of the monograph (argument + poetic sections) enacts the same boundary discipline as the governance methodology: there are claims, and there are gestures toward what cannot yet be claimed
