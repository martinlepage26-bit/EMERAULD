# Le Mythe de Sisyphe — Camus (Absurd as Starting Point)

## Summary
Camus’s *Le Mythe de Sisyphe* (1942) frames the absurd as a **starting point** rather than a conclusion and opens with the question of suicide (“un raisonnement absurde”). In this vault it primarily functions as a conceptual anchor for the “Sisyphean machine” framing already used in the fluency/interruption governance lane — see [[Fluency and Interruption Theory]] and [[Fluency, Interruption, and Institutional Accountability]].

## Key Ideas (vault-usable)
- The text is explicit about method posture: it offers “description… à l’état pur” of a condition of mind; “aucune métaphysique” is mixed in “pour le moment.”
- The “Sisyphus” frame is usable as a governance metaphor for loops: what matters is the interval where the subject becomes conscious of the loop rather than being carried by it.

## Where it plugs into the vault
