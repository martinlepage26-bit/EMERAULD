# Magic between Europe and India — Fárek & Horák 2021 (Mantras, spells, prayers)

## Summary
Martin Fárek and Pavel Horák analyze why debates about Indian mantras repeatedly oscillate between describing mantras as **spells**, **prayers**, or “magico‑religious” hybrids. They argue that general theories of magic do not explain the persistence of these classificatory conflicts, and they trace how a historically coherent structure of **Christian theology** (God, fallen angels, “supernatural powers”) shaped the dominant European scholarly characterization of “magic” in India—then dissolved into a set of problematic assumptions that still organize contemporary debate. Reference for [[Ritual, Magic, and Institutional Authority]] (theological residue inside scholarly classification) and [[Queer Sociology of Magic and Ritual]] (comparative anchor for non-European ritual systems).

## Key Ideas (vault-usable)
- The same mantra can be classified as magic or religion depending on the criteria—so “magic vs religion” is a **method problem**, not just an object property.
- Spell vs prayer framing is historically tied to European intellectual formations (including missionary/orientalist legacies), not neutral description.
- Useful for category hygiene when writing about coercion/submission, this‑worldly vs other‑worldly goals, and practitioner belief analysis.
