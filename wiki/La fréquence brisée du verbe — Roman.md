# La fréquence brisée du verbe — Roman

## Summary

French-language novel by [[Martin Lepage — Professional Profile|Martin Lepage]] archived under the filename `2025 - Martin Lepage - La frequence brisee du verbe - thesis_or_dissertation.docx`. The novel opens in silence — the refrigerator has stopped humming — and enters through a protagonist's encounter with his mother's death. Connected to [[The Broken Frequency of the Word — Novel]] as either the French-language original or a parallel draft. **Status of the EN/FR relationship: under verification — parallel manuscripts whose exact relationship (translation vs. independent variation) requires authorial attestation.** A *texte-rite* in the Rivardian lineage (see [[Mythocritique to PHAROS — The 2010 Master's Thesis as Methodological Keystone]] and [[Mort et Naissance et L'Ombre et le Double — MA Thesis on Yvon Rivard (Université Laval, 2010)]]): the silence/breath/word axis maps directly onto *l'ombre / le silence / le souffle* from the recent [[Pourquoi rêver encore — Lecture allégorique et métaphysique d'Yvon Rivard|Pourquoi rêver encore]] essay. Belongs to the creative-corpus ring with [[Alchemy of the Wound — Novel]], [[Le sang qui porte ton nom — Novel]], [[The Returning Light — Monograph]], [[CORPUS ou le génie de l'insistance — Novel]], and [[The Weather Beneath the Walls — Novel]]. Indexed in [[Novel and Creative Corpus]]. Part of [[Writing and Novels MOC]].

## Context

The file is tagged "thesis_or_dissertation" in its filename but the content is clearly literary fiction — a first-person novel in French. The title *La fréquence brisée du verbe* ("The broken frequency of the word") is the French counterpart to [[The Broken Frequency of the Word — Novel]], which is already in the wiki as an English novel. These may be:
1. The same novel in two languages (French original, English translation/parallel)
2. Two separate novels on related themes

The opening chapter's title — "La pièce sans son" ("The room without sound") — establishes the sonic/silence framework that runs through [[Spider-Man and the Phonetic Glitch — Voice as Infrastructure]], [[Beyond Fluency — Stuttering, Autoethnography, and Unstable Epistemology]], and the stutter work.

## Details

**Opening:** The protagonist remembers the day the refrigerator stopped humming. "Silence falls on him like a second skin, vivid, immediate, sticky." He stands barefoot in the kitchen doorway, "hair greasy, the smell of dried oranges floating above the sink."

**Governing image:** The absence of background sound — the hum that filled the air, "this invisible current that linked one second to the next" — becomes his mother tongue. "Il ne le sait pas encore, mais ce silence deviendra sa langue maternelle."

**Mother's death:** She is lying on the couch, arm folded awkwardly behind her, as if still searching for the remote she never found. Her body looks relaxed but the stillness beneath is false. "From the doorway, one would think she is asleep, stopped in the middle of a breath." The TV screen is black.

**Temporal suspension:** Every object in the kitchen appears "slightly changed, a little less itself." The sugar pot shines too brightly. The bowl of fruit leans toward the light. Even the air seems bruised. The protagonist hears his own breathing for the first time.

**The word unpronounceable:** He thinks of calling her name, but "the shape of his name seems foreign to him." He has not said it without irony or impatience in so long that he fears if he says it now, "the word will come out wrong."

## Key Ideas

- Silence as mother tongue: the space opened by the death of background sound becomes constitutive
- Sound as the thread between seconds: the hum that linked moments — its absence produces temporal fragmentation
- The word that cannot be said: language failing at the threshold of grief
- The body of the mother as a stopped clock: the scene is suffused with the phenomenology of death-as-suspension

## Insights

- The French novel engages the same stutter/frequency/broken word themes as the English [[The Broken Frequency of the Word — Novel]], but through a childhood and maternal death scene rather than the institutional contexts of the theoretical work
- The silence-as-mother-tongue figure is the complement to [[Beyond Fluency — Stuttering, Autoethnography, and Unstable Epistemology|the stutter as institutional interruption]]: the stutter is the external marking of what is already a broken frequency inside

## Open Questions

- Is this the French original or a parallel composition to [[The Broken Frequency of the Word — Novel]]?
- Is the protagonist's stutter addressed later in the novel, or is this opening purely about the death of the mother and the death of background sound?
