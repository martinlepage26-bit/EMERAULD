# The Circle Remains Open — Toward a Queer Sociology of the Sacred

## Summary

Academic essay by [[Martin Lepage — Professional Profile|Martin Lepage]] (published in JAAR — Journal of the American Academy of Religion, or similar) proposing a queer sociology of the sacred grounded in ethnographic fieldwork with queer and trans Pagan practitioners in Montreal. Part of [[Research and Papers MOC]] and [[Historical Academic Portfolio — Pre-PHAROS Scholarly Work]].

## Context

The full title appears to be "The Circle Remains Open: Toward a Queer Sociology of the Sacred." The file `LEPAGE-CircleRemainsOpenJAAR.docx` suggests journal submission to JAAR or a comparable venue. A separate title page (`Title pageCircleJAAR.docx`) exists in the archive. This essay synthesizes the Montreal Pagan fieldwork with a theoretical proposition: queer and trans practitioners do not just navigate binary-gendered ritual frameworks — they actively produce new forms of sacred knowledge through their negotiations. The essay builds on [[Historical Academic Portfolio — Pre-PHAROS Scholarly Work|the full portfolio]] of Pagan community research and connects directly to [[Social Compass Paper — Ritual Authority and Régime de Preuve]] and [[Agatha All Along — Wicca, Digital Mediatization, and Proof Regimes]]. This is the broader theoretical frame for the queer Pagan corpus rooted in the 2010 MA mythocritique substrate (see [[Mythocritique to PHAROS — The 2010 Master's Thesis as Methodological Keystone]]); indexed in [[Queer Sociology of Magic and Ritual]].

## Details

**Opening gambit:** The essay begins with real witches — "queer and trans, urban and solitary, lineage-bound and iconoclastic" — who invoke spirits, cast spells against pipelines and patriarchy, and gather in basements, forests, and Zoom circles.

**Site of inquiry:** Contemporary Paganism in Montreal as "spiritual cartography" — overlapping mythic maps, revived pantheons, seasonal rites, sensual devotions — shaped by linguistic hybridity, queer presence, and post-secular yearning.

**The tension named directly:** Even within these expansive enchanted spaces, binaries endure: Goddess and God, male and female, the chalice and the blade. Sacred texts are often unwritten but their scripts are deeply gendered. Ritual form, even when improvised, can reproduce heteronormative logics of lineage, polarity, and mythic balance.

**Central question:** How do queer and trans practitioners move within such frames? How do they craft livable, meaningful sacred practices in the face of recurring normative pressure?

**Theoretical framing:** Goffman on action and self; Mucchielli on the constructed empirical world; queer phenomenology (Ahmed); queer futurity (Muñoz); Indigenous critique (TallBear) applied to neo-shamanic appropriation.

**The open circle as figure:** The essay's title proposes that queer Paganism keeps the ritual circle open — resisting the closure that produces orthodox authority and maintaining the sacred as a site of ongoing negotiation rather than fixed transmission.

## Key Ideas

- The queer sacred: sites of misalignment, discomfort, and reconfiguration are not anomalies but knowledge-producing moments
- Spiritual cartography: Paganism as overlapping, constructed maps rather than a single religion
- The open circle: a figure for refusing ritual closure and keeping sacred authority permanently contestable
- Linguistic hybridity and queer presence as shaping forces in Montreal's Pagan scene

## Insights

- "The circle remains open" is a direct theological and methodological counterpart to the [[Loop Papers and Recursive Governance|loop papers']] argument that recursive governance refuses final closure — both resist "bounded convergence" as the only valid outcome
- The essay's TallBear citation and explicit engagement with Indigenous critique of neo-shamanic appropriation shows this work is aware of the limits it operates within — connects to the reflexivity in [[Beyond Fluency — Stuttering, Autoethnography, and Unstable Epistemology]]
