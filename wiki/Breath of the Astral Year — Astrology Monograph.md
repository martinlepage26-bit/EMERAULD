# Breath of the Astral Year — Astrology Monograph

## Summary
Full astrology monograph by Martin Lepage, PhD (HEX/ADECIMAL Press). Structured around **12 competences of the year** — one per zodiacal sign — each developed as a civic and embodied capacity rather than a personality type. Central argument: astrology functions as a **civic grammar** and **shared temporal technology** for coordinating collective experience and negotiating queer, posthuman, and counter-normative forms of selfhood. Includes dedicated chapters on queer astrology and a closing section on apps and algorithms (Co-Star, The Pattern). Related to [[The Returning Light — Monograph]] and [[Martin Lepage — Professional Profile]].

## Context
Source: `BREATH OF THE ASTRAL YEAR - book_or_monograph.docx`. This is the full text, not a skeleton. One of several HEX/ADECIMAL Press titles in the creative/scholarly archive alongside [[Alchemy of the Wound — Novel]], [[CORPUS ou le génie de l'insistance — Novel]], [[Le sang qui porte ton nom — Novel]], and [[The Broken Frequency of the Word — Novel]]. The monograph extends the ritual and spirituality themes from Martin Lepage's Neo-Pagan ethnographic work into popular culture astrology.

---

## Central Argument

Astrology is not primarily a belief system (true or false) but a **shared temporal technology** — a grammar for naming, coordinating, and inhabiting collective time.

- Zodiacal categories are not personality essences but **civic competences**: capacities a community collectively needs to function, distributed across bodies and seasons
- The year as a practice: each zodiacal period offers a distinct invitation to cultivate a particular form of attention, relation, or action
- "Civic grammar": astrology provides a shared vocabulary for public and intimate life that is non-institutional, non-hierarchical, and accessible without gatekeepers — making it a site of interest for queer communities for whom institutional vocabularies of selfhood are often hostile

## Twelve Competences of the Year

Each sign is analyzed as a competence rather than a type:

| Sign | Competence |
|---|---|
| Aries | Initiative and the ethics of beginning |
| Taurus | Persistence and the body as site of knowing |
| Gemini | Translation and the proliferation of perspectives |
| Cancer | Memory, inheritance, and the politics of care |
| Leo | Visibility and the risk of being seen |
| Virgo | Discernment and the labor of refinement |
| Libra | Negotiation and the suspension of judgment |
| Scorpio | Transformation and the inhabitation of limit |
| Sagittarius | Expansion and the ethics of encounter |
| Capricorn | Structure and the governance of time |
| Aquarius | Detachment and the conditions of solidarity |
| Pisces | Dissolution and the permeability of self |

## Queer Astrology Chapters

Dedicated chapters extend the civic grammar argument into queer theory:

- Astrology as **counter-normative temporal technology**: zodiacal time runs counter to capitalist developmental time (career progression, life-stage milestones, reproductive futurity)
- **Zodiacal selves** as refusal of coherent identity: each sign-period offers a partial, seasonal self that does not add up to a stable whole — resonates with the governing thread from [[Sealed Card Protocol — Legitimacy, Glitch, and Charging]]: "Queerness is the refusal to cohere"
- Queer kinship and chosen family mapped onto the sign cycle: the twelve competences as descriptions of the distinct roles different people play in queer community formation
- Astrology's non-hierarchical epistemology: no sign is superior; each competence is necessary; the year requires all twelve

## Apps and Algorithms: Co-Star and The Pattern

The final section analyzes how digital platforms transform astrological practice — connecting to the mediatization argument in [[Agatha All Along — Wicca, Digital Mediatization, and Proof Regimes]]:

**Co-Star**: Algorithmic birth chart generation and daily notifications. The platform translates the civic grammar into an individualized, data-driven selfhood product. Astrology becomes a **legibility technology** — a way of making the self intelligible to algorithmic systems and vice versa.

**The Pattern**: Behavioral astrology app that maps personality patterns to chart placements. More explicitly therapeutic than Co-Star; foregrounds self-improvement narrative. Translates zodiacal competences into optimization targets.

**Critical finding**: Both apps reduce the civic grammar to individual performance metrics. The collective, seasonal, and anti-normative dimensions of astrology are flattened into personalized self-tracking. This is the same fluency mechanism identified in [[Fluency, Interruption, and Institutional Accountability]]: platforms amplify agency while reducing it to a consumable aesthetic.

## Posthuman Zodiac

Closing theoretical section:

- Extends the zodiacal framework beyond the human: planets, seasons, ecosystems, and non-human actors as participants in the zodiacal cycle
- The posthuman zodiac refuses anthropocentrism in astrological practice — the year is not organized around human needs but around a set of relations in which the human is one participant
- Connects to the posthumanism thread in [[The Returning Light — Monograph]] and [[Unnatural Selection — Jurassic Park and the Posthuman Commons]]
- "The body is not the container of zodiacal competences — it is the site where competences pass through"

## Key Thematic Connections

- **Proof regimes**: Astrology operates by what would be classified as "narrative proof" in the [[Social Compass Paper — Ritual Authority and Régime de Preuve]] framework — not oppositional (argument/counter-argument) or sensory (direct bodily experience) but narrative (coherent account that makes experience legible)
- **Authority without origin**: The civic grammar has no founder, no central institution, no hierarchy — authority comes from collective use, not from institutional certification. Same pattern as the Wiccan authenticity analysis in [[Agatha All Along — Wicca, Digital Mediatization, and Proof Regimes]]
- **Anti-coherence**: The zodiacal self that changes every month, that requires all twelve competences, that cannot be reduced to a single sign — this is the same epistemological and political position as "queerness is the refusal to cohere"

## Insights

- The civic grammar argument is the most politically specific contribution: it reframes astrology from a private belief practice to a collective temporal infrastructure — with implications for how communities coordinate without institutional mediation
- The app section does for astrology what [[Agatha All Along — Wicca, Digital Mediatization, and Proof Regimes]] does for Wicca: shows that digital platforms do not simply broadcast practices but transform the conditions under which practices produce proof, identity, and community
- The twelve competences framework implicitly rejects the Big Five / MBTI personality-type paradigm that underlies most AI-adjacent personality and behavior modeling — a theoretical position with direct implications for [[PHAROS Invention Disclosure]] target constructs around behavioral prediction
