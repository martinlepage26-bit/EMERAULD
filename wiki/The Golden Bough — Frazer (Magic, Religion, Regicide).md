# The Golden Bough — Frazer (Magic, Religion, Regicide)

## Summary
James George Frazer’s *The Golden Bough* (abridged) is a classic comparative study arguing that many ritual and mythic systems can be organized around themes like sacred kingship, regicide, sacrifice, and the relation between magic and religion. In this vault it functions as an *origin text* for 20th-century “magic/religion” typologies, with the usual cautions about dated anthropology and over-generalization.

## Why it matters here
- **Genealogy anchor:** later scholarly conversations about “magic vs religion” (including critiques) often assume Frazer as an upstream reference.
- **Ritual authority:** the regicide / sacred king framing gives vocabulary for “authority sustained by ritual constraints,” which can be mapped (carefully) onto institutional legitimacy problems.
- **Cross-link surface:** supports [[The Meanings of Magic — Bailey 2006 (Magic as Unstable Category)]] and the ritual-method stack in [[Qualitative Handbook — Expanded Reference List]].

## Key Ideas (Vault-usable)
- Frazer’s motivating problem: the succession rule for the priesthood of Diana at Aricia → expands into comparative ritual systems.
- Strong emphasis on **sacred kingship** and **killing the king** as a recurring institutional solution when “power” is tied to cosmic/seasonal order.
- Canonical (but contested) distinction: **magic as coercive technique** vs **religion as propitiation**.
