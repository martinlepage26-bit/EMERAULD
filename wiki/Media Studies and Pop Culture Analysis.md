# Media Studies and Pop Culture Analysis

Martin's media studies work beyond Buffy: queer readings of animated series, fantasy films, superhero media, and genre fiction. Applies queer theory, governance analysis, and ritual theory to popular culture texts.

**Methodological substrate:** The cultural objects analyzed across this domain (Buffy's hair, *Charmed*'s jester femininity, *Agatha All Along*'s Ballad of the Witches' Road, *Disenchantment*'s post-sovereign polity) are surfaces where the operator formalizes structures he himself lives in his own intellectual-governance trajectory. The Witches' Road logic of charge-persistence-through-recomposition — canonical doctrine: [[Martin Walks the Witches' Road — Corpus as Charge-Persistence]]; cultural object that names it: [[The Ballad of the Witches' Road — Analysis]] — is the structural pattern of the operator's full arc (2025 horror screenplay → 2010 MA thesis → 2017 PhD → 2024–2026 PHAROS). Per [[Mythocritique to PHAROS — The 2010 Master's Thesis as Methodological Keystone|the keystone]], the cultural studies work is not "applied from outside"; it formalizes perceptions lived in encounter-time (per operator authorial attestation: *"I saw charge watching Buffy's hair, that's true"*).

## Core Media Studies Papers

- [[Spider-Man and the Phonetic Glitch — Voice as Infrastructure]] — Spider-Man (especially Miles Morales) as phonetic glitch site. Voice infrastructure fails precisely at the point of identity assertion. Connected to stuttering theory and [[Fluency, Interruption, and Institutional Accountability|fluency/interruption]].
- [[Transmutation and the Body — Queer Ethics in Fullmetal Alchemist]] — FMA as queer ethics: equivalent exchange as a governance constraint on body modification. What can and cannot be transmuted as a political question.
- [[Unnatural Selection — Jurassic Park and the Posthuman Commons]] — Jurassic Park as posthuman governance problem. Who owns the commons when the commons can be engineered?
- [[Avatar Chapter — A Queer Reading of Embodied Power (English)]] — Avatar (film) as queer embodied power. The na'vi body as a site of governance.
- [[Avatar — Lecture queer du pouvoir incarné]] — French version of the Avatar queer reading.
- [[The Familiars of Hollywood — Queerness, Whiteness, and Representation]] — Hollywood's familiar as a figure of managed queerness and racial encoding.
- [[Authority Without Ethics — The Love Witch]] — The Love Witch as governance failure: authority exercised without ethical constraint or accountability.
