## Details

**French title:** *« Why be king? I'm already a queen! » : Paganisme, performances rituelles et négociations queer*

**Core argument of the proposed book:**
Contemporary Paganism is a religious movement grouping diverse recomposed practices and beliefs. These religiosities valorize the resurgence of representations perceived as pre-Christian (often polytheistic) and conceive Earth and nature as central elements of divinity, positing gender equality as a natural fact.

The book shows that the **sacred feminine and masculine** are invoked by LGBTQ Pagan practitioners primarily for their symbolic potential within ritual performance and for their capacity to enable self-narration (*mise en récit de soi*). From these queer negotiations, it emerges that participants' religious and identity performances are intimately linked — their discourse about their own experience depends largely on the social relations that marginalize them.

**Key finding:** These practitioners are themselves **vectors of normativity**: their ritual performances, even when serving as alternatives, reproduce, reverse, or displace the gender norms associated with their religious community and with society at large. Contemporary Paganism is simultaneously:
- A site of normativity regarding the nature of masculine and feminine
- A site of emancipation from other mechanisms reproducing the binary norm

**Method:** Ethnographic fieldwork in Montreal's neo-Pagan/Wiccan community (Wicca, Reclaiming Witchcraft); gender studies and queer theory applied to LGBTQ practitioners' religious experience.

## Key Ideas

- Ritual performance as identity performance: they are inseparable, each structured by the same social relations of marginalization
- Vectors of normativity: even those who resist norms reproduce them in partial or displaced forms
- The sacred as binary and emancipatory simultaneously: the same ritual structures can normalize and liberate
- Self-narration (*mise en récit de soi*) as a primary function of ritual

## Insights

- This document confirms that the PhD dissertation's central argument — practitioners are simultaneously normative and resistant — is the empirical and theoretical core from which the subsequent governance vocabulary developed. The recognition that systems simultaneously contain and enable resistance is operationalized in [[Governance by Denial]] and the [[PHAROS Recalibration — Unified Governance Architecture|PHAROS]] method's treatment of performative governance
