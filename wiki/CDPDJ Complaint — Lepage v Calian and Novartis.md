# CDPDJ Complaint — Lepage v Calian and Novartis

## Summary
Formal human rights complaint filed March 20, 2026 with the Commission des droits de la personne et des droits de la jeunesse (CDPDJ) under Quebec's Charter of Human Rights and Freedoms. Complainant: Martin Lepage. Respondents: Calian Group Ltd. (formal employer) and Novartis Pharmaceuticals Canada Inc. (de facto employer). Grounds: discrimination based on handicap (speech disability / stuttering compounded by streptococcal infection causing voice loss) and social condition (precarious contract worker). Related to [[Martin Lepage — Professional Profile]] and [[Consent, Fraud, and Boundary Protocols]].

## Context
Source: `CDPDJ_Complaint_Lepage_v_Calian_Novartis.docx`. Filed 2026-03-20. Employment began 2025-10-27 and was terminated 2026-01-22. The clinical trial coordinator role at Novartis (placed by Calian) is the employment context; the complaint documents the discrimination and failure to accommodate that occurred during this period.

## Protected Grounds (Section 10, Quebec Charter)

**1. Handicap (disability)**
- Pre-existing condition: speech disability (stuttering), constituting a recognized handicap under Quebec human rights law
- Compounding condition: streptococcal infection causing widespread hives and near-total voice loss from approximately November 2025 through January 2026
- The combination created a compounding effect the employer was made aware of and failed to address

**2. Social condition**
- Precarious employment as a probationary contract worker placed through a staffing agency into a client company
- Triangular arrangement created structural vulnerability: no direct relationship with the entity exercising supervisory authority (Novartis), limited recourse through the formal employer (Calian), subject to termination without documented cause

## Legal Analysis

### Ground 1 — Discrimination Based on Handicap (Art. 10, 16)

**Prima facie discrimination established by temporal sequence**:
1. Complainant disclosed protected characteristic
2. Supervisor's behaviour changed
3. No accommodation process initiated
4. Terminated with vague, undocumented justification

Burden shifts to respondents to demonstrate termination was unrelated to protected grounds.

**Reference**: *Commission des droits de la personne et des droits de la jeunesse c. Montréal (Ville)*, [2000] 1 SCR 665 — handicap includes any physical limitation, permanent or temporary, resulting in a functional restriction.

### Ground 2 — Failure to Accommodate

**Reference**: *Centre universitaire de santé McGill c. Syndicat des employés*, 2007 CSC 4 — duty to accommodate requires employer to engage in individualized assessment upon becoming aware of disability; this obligation exists independently of a formal accommodation request.

**Violations**:
- No steps taken to assess functional limitations
- No exploration of accommodations (remote work for email-based duties, modified schedules, alternative training modalities)
- Insistence on in-person attendance despite voice loss and demonstrably remote-capable duties = refusal to accommodate
- Failure to engage in any accommodation process at all = standalone violation

### Ground 3 — Discriminatory Dismissal (Art. 10, 16)

**Reference**: *Bombardier Inc. c. Commission des droits de la personne*, 2015 CSC 39 — discrimination need not be the sole cause; where a protected ground is one factor, the discriminatory character is established.

"Not a good fit" — vague, unsupported by documented evaluation, performance metrics, or prior communication. Combined with: temporal proximity to disability disclosures + pattern of supervisory disengagement = supports inference that protected grounds were a factor.

### Ground 4 — Joint Liability of Both Respondents

Triangular employment does not diminish obligations:
- **Novartis**: directed daily work, controlled workplace conditions, exercised supervisory authority that produced discriminatory conduct
- **Calian**: as formal employer, bore legal obligation to ensure placement conditions respected the Charter

Both failed to: initiate accommodation process, provide evaluative feedback, document basis for termination.

---

## Remedies Sought

1. **Moral damages** — for affront to dignity, humiliation, and psychological harm
2. **Punitive damages** — given intentional or grossly negligent character of failure to accommodate, absence of documented evaluation, imposition of communication barriers
3. **Material damages** — lost wages from January 22, 2026 through reasonable reemployment period (subject to duty to mitigate)
4. **Cessation order** — directing respondents to cease discriminatory practices and implement compliant accommodation and evaluation procedures

## Post-Termination Conduct as Additional Evidence

- Instructed to communicate only through Frankie Iacovelli
- That same email bounced as undeliverable immediately after termination
- Access to Novartis email system revoked, preventing retrieval of correspondence
- Communication barriers imposed immediately = additional evidence of the pattern

The complaint characterizes the email revocation itself as evidence of the barriers imposed — it made the complainant unable to document the case from within the employment system.

## Insights

- The triangular employment relationship (staffing agency → client company) is itself a governance problem: the entity exercising supervisory authority (Novartis) is not the legal employer (Calian), and neither relationship provides full accountability. This is the capacity lever from [[Governance by Denial]] at the individual employment level — formal rights without the institutional access to exercise them
- "Not a good fit" as a termination reason is structurally the same as the governance failures documented in the PHAROS audit work: a vague claim, no documented basis, no prior notice, no opportunity to contest. The governance critique of bounded claims applies here: an undocumented termination reason is not a claim — it is impunity
- The temporal sequence (disclosure → supervisor behavior change → no accommodation → termination) is the same evidentiary logic used in the PHAROS method's target construct assessment: you don't need proof of intent if the sequence of events is sufficient to establish the inference
- The bounced email to the designated intermediary immediately after termination is the kind of evidence the PHAROS method calls a "failure event" — it is not an administrative error, it is evidence of the system's functioning
