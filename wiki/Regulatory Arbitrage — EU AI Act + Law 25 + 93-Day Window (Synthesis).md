# Regulatory Arbitrage — EU AI Act + Law 25 + 93-Day Window (Synthesis)

## Summary

The PHAROS commercial launch (2026-05-01) is deliberately timed to exploit a regulatory window: EU AI Act high-risk enforcement begins August 2, 2026 (93 days); Quebec Law 25 automated-decision obligations have been in force since September 22, 2024 (live today). For Canadian companies with EU exposure or Quebec operations, both regimes apply simultaneously. The market sees this as two separate compliance problems; the vault documents show they overlap significantly and can be solved with a single, well-designed governance program.

This arbitrage is reflected in the launch artifacts: 4 SEO articles, commercial brief, and outreach targeting all address the overlap as a value proposition.

## The Three Regulatory Moments

### Moment 1: Quebec Law 25 (In Force Since Sept 22, 2024)

**Status:** Live enforcement. Commission d'accès à l'information (CAI) has audit and order-making powers. Organizations processing Quebec personal information are in scope today.

**Scope:** Section 12.1 — automated decision-making. Applies to "any AI-powered process that reaches a decision or recommendation about a person using their personal data."

**Obligations:**
- Notification to affected person before automated decision
- Disclosure of personal information used + principal factors
- Right to human review of automated decision

**Enforcement:** CAI has begun enforcement activity. Penalties: up to 25M CAD or 4% of global turnover.

**Market gap:** Most Quebec organizations do not yet have documented compliance with Law 25 §12.1 for their AI systems. No scarcity of unresolved cases.

### Moment 2: EU AI Act (High-Risk Enforcement Begins Aug 2, 2026)

**Status:** Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 enforceable from August 2, 2026. Digital Omnibus delay attempt failed at trilogue April 28, 2026 — deadline stands.

**Scope:** Applies to providers who place AI systems on the EU market AND deployers who put AI systems into service in the EU. Extraterritorial: a Canadian company with EU customers/users is in scope.

**High-Risk Obligations (Articles 9–17):**
- Risk management system (documented, operational, lifecycle)
- Data governance (training/validation/test data quality, provenance, bias mitigation)
- Technical documentation (system design, capabilities, limitations, training data, testing, deployment context)
- Record-keeping (automatic logging sufficient to reconstruct inputs/outputs)
- Transparency to deployers (instructions for appropriate use)
- Human oversight (design for meaningful human override/monitoring/disregard)
- Accuracy, robustness, cybersecurity (performance maintained throughout lifecycle)
- Quality management system (design, testing, deployment, monitoring, incident management)

**Penalties:** €15M or 3% of global turnover for high-risk violations (Article 99§4); €35M or 7% for prohibited practices (Article 99§3).

**Market gap:** Most Canadian companies do not know whether the EU AI Act applies to them. Those who do are scrambling to understand the 8 Annex III categories and assess which of their systems are in scope.

### Moment 3: The Overlap (2026-05-01 to 2026-08-02)

**Timeline:**
- **Today (2026-05-01):** Digital Omnibus delay failed; timeline reset to Aug 2, 2026
- **Next 93 days:** Procurement cycles (76% are 3+ weeks) mean multiple deal windows before Aug 2
- **By Aug 2, 2026:** High-risk compliance required in EU market; Law 25 enforcement ongoing in Quebec

**The insight:** The AI systems that trigger Law 25 §12.1 (automated decision-making on personal information) and EU AI Act high-risk classifications (credit scoring, employment screening, access to services, worker monitoring) **overlap significantly**. They are not two separate problems.

- Credit/insurance scoring: Law 25 §12.1 applies (personal info → decision); EU AI Act Annex III applies (high-risk)
- Employment screening: Law 25 §12.1 applies; EU AI Act Annex III applies
- Customer eligibility decisions: Law 25 §12.1 applies; EU AI Act Annex III applies
- Worker performance monitoring: Law 25 §12.1 applies; EU AI Act Annex III applies

## The Market Gap (PHAROS's Target)

**Most organizations handle this in two parallel workstreams:**
1. Law 25 §12.1 inventory + compliance documentation
2. EU AI Act Annex III classification + high-risk obligations

**Result:** Two separate inventories, two separate gap assessments, two separate remediation plans, inconsistent system descriptions, divergence risk in enforcement contexts.

**PHAROS value proposition:** Design your compliance program to EU AI Act high-risk standards (stricter). That single program satisfies both Law 25 and EU AI Act simultaneously.

**Practical implication:** What would be three separate consulting engagements (Law 25 readiness + EU AI Act readiness + unified program design) becomes one diagnostic + one remediation sprint.
