### Term Trace Map — Five Governing Terms

#### 1. "Governance"

Three sub-registers, each doing different work:

- **Regulatory/compliance register** (~153 notes): Law 25, EU AI Act, procurement accountability, licensing frameworks. Governance as external constraint. Anchor: [[EU AI Act and Law 25 — Regulatory Pressure Window]], [[CONTROL 1 — Tool Layer Audit Protocol (Regulatory Grounding)]].
- **Methodological/upstream register** (~220 notes): governance as executable method, recursive process, upstream institutional practice. Governance as internal discipline. Anchor: [[AI Governance Manifesto — Upstream Institutional Practice]], [[Recursive AI Governance as Executable Method — The Very Long Narrative]].
- **Personal practice register** (~76 notes): self-governance, agent identity discipline, operator-as-practitioner. Governance as enacted posture. Anchor: [[Trismégiste — Operator State]], [[Second Self System — Identity Kernel and Agent Routing Architecture]].

**Trace finding:** The term performs a telescoping movement — regulatory demand → methodological response → personal discipline. The methodological register (upstream, recursive) structurally critiques the compliance register (checklists, attestation) as insufficient while depending on it for legitimacy claims in the commercial register. This tension is productive but unresolved.

#### 2. "Recursion / Recursive"

- **Technical register** (~198 notes): loops, functions, stack operations, recursive file structures (RECURSO directories). Governance as computation.
- **Philosophical/self-referential register** (~110 notes): recursive determination structures, the "this paper may not exist" paradox, re-entering differences as rules. Governance as ontology.

**Trace finding:** The vault deliberately fuses these two registers. The [[Recursive Governance Theory]] cluster treats computer-science recursion as a political metaphor made operational — not just analogical. The sharpest use: "destiny only happens because it never happened" (from [[The Palace Under Root — Allegory and the Keyport Novel]]) — where recursion names a structure where the condition of possibility is also the condition of impossibility. This is not technical; it is the philosophical core from which the governance method derives.

#### 3. "Authority"

Near-equal distribution across three registers:

- **Institutional** (~132): regulatory, legal, state, policy authority
- **Epistemic** (~162): knowledge claims, evidence authority, source credibility
- **Personal/spiritual** (~151): ritual authority, queer authority, witch as authority-without-warrant

**Trace finding:** The [[Authority, Legitimacy, and Post-Sovereignty]] map holds all three in explicit tension via five post-legitimacy models (abdication, administrative sovereignty, care without claim, glitch sovereignty, relational authority). The governance principle "authority without power-over" (HEPHAISTOS binding principle #6, [[Authority Without Power-Over]]) elevates the personal/spiritual register into infrastructure. This is the vault's most theoretically ambitious claim: spiritual-queer authority is not metaphor; it is a structural design principle for AI governance.

#### 4. "Fluency"

The most analytically developed term in the vault. Three registers deliberately bridged:

- **Disability/speech register** (~72): stuttering as epistemological position, dysfluency as institutionally penalized
- **AI output register** (~160): fluency as the mode by which AI systems naturalize authority and render decision criteria invisible
- **Epistemic/confidence register** (~66): fluency as proxy for intelligence and credibility

**Trace finding:** The [[Fluency, Interruption, and Institutional Accountability]] paper performs the key synthesis: "Human hesitation is stigmatized as weakness; machine fluency is rewarded as intelligence." The political implication — making the labor of prediction audible as a condition for accountability — directly bridges the disability and AI registers. Fluency is not a neutral descriptor anywhere in the vault; it is always a mode of authority exercise. The PHAROS method frames itself explicitly as an *interruption technology*. See also [[Stuttering through the Institution — Academic Containment and Queer Knowing]] and [[Spider-Man and the Phonetic Glitch — Voice as Infrastructure]].

#### 5. "Relation / Relational"

- **Theoretical primitive** (~22): relation as the ontological primitive (difference that can be re-entered as rule), [[Smallest Building Block — Relation as Rule]]
- **Interpersonal/care** (~31): relational ethics, consent, care without claim
- **AI-human** (~85): relational embedding, conversational AI reorganizing institutions, [[Embedding Before Rupture — Relational AI and Institutional Power]]

**Trace finding:** There is a structural gap between the theoretical-primitive use and the AI-human use. The Smallest Building Block note argues that reality is built from repeatable asymmetries (relation as rule), but this ontological claim is not explicitly cross-linked to the AI governance argument that relational embedding is the primary governance risk. Bridging these — relation-as-primitive → relational-embedding-as-governance-threat — would strengthen both clusters. **Recommended link: add [[Smallest Building Block — Relation as Rule]] to [[Embedding Before Rupture — Relational AI and Institutional Power]].**

### Three Core Narrative Threads (Vault-Wide)

These are not topic clusters; they are the narrative logic running through the vault as a whole.

#### Thread 1 — "The Interrupted Body"

A researcher who has been interrupted by institutions (stuttering, queerness, precarity) builds interruption back into the systems that would otherwise operate fluently over bodies like his. The personal trajectory from disability epistemology through queer ritual through AI governance is a single arc. The governance method IS the autobiography made operational. Key nodes: [[Stuttering through the Institution — Academic Containment and Queer Knowing]], [[Beyond Fluency — Stuttering, Autoethnography, and Unstable Epistemology]], [[WHEN THE CAT STOPS — Personal Notes on Recursion and Stutter]], [[AI Recruiting Has an Accessibility Problem — Lepage (2026)]].

#### Thread 2 — "The Second Self as Governance Device"

The vault documents the construction of a second self — AI-mediated, governed, auditable — as an alternative to the unaccountable self that institutions produce when they operate fluently. PHAROS is not a product; it is a method for building a legible, interruptible, accountable self-in-the-world. Key nodes: [[Second Self System — Identity Kernel and Agent Routing Architecture]], [[Martin Lepage Professional Identity]], [[PHAROS AI Lineage — Source of Truth]], [[Agent Session Phenomenology]], [[AI Identity and Phenomenology]].

#### Thread 3 — "Authority After the Institution"

When institutional legitimacy fails (regulatory, epistemic, democratic), what forms of authority remain possible? The vault proposes: ritual authority, relational authority, recursive authority — authority that earns legitimacy through process rather than claiming it through position. The queer media analysis, the AI governance method, and the commercial offer ladder are all answers to the same question about what makes authority believable after institutions stop being trustworthy. Key nodes: [[Authority, Legitimacy, and Post-Sovereignty]], [[Authority Without Power-Over]], [[Legitimacy Machines — Book Skeleton and Architecture]], [[Recursive Governance Theory]], [[AI Governance Manifesto — Upstream Institutional Practice]].

### Productive Tensions (Not to Be Resolved)

1. **Governance-as-method vs governance-as-compliance.** The upstream/methodological register would critique the compliance register as insufficient surface-level governance. But the commercial offer depends on compliance framing for legibility to procurement audiences. The vault holds this without resolving it — which is correct; resolving it would collapse one register into the other.

2. **Fluency as problem vs fluency as achievement.** PHAROS produces fluent, authoritative outputs (the commercial value proposition) while critiquing institutional fluency as a mode of authority concealment. The tension is generative: PHAROS produces interruptible fluency, not fluency that conceals decision criteria. But this distinction needs to be more explicit in commercial-facing notes.

3. **Authority-without-power-over vs authority-through-competence.** The governance philosophy grounds authority in stewardship (HEPHAISTOS principle #6). The commercial positioning grounds authority in expertise and track record. These can coexist but the philosophical register should be explicitly invoked in commercial notes to prevent drift.

4. **Relation as primitive vs relation as risk.** The theoretical primitive (Smallest Building Block) frames relation as the generative structure of reality. The AI governance register (Embedding Before Rupture) frames relational AI as the primary governance risk. Both are true; the vault does not yet make explicit that the governance risk is precisely that AI is exploiting the ontological primitive — embedding into the relational substrate that constitutes institutional life.

## Codex Passes 13–20

### Pass 13 — Power map (clusters): Governance, Authority/Power, Fluency

#### Governance

- **Actors:** regulators (EU AI Act / Quebec Law 25), procurement/legal, internal risk owners, auditors, the operator-method author (PHAROS).
- **Incentives:** regulators want enforceable accountability; procurement wants legible risk-transfer artifacts; internal owners want speed without liability; auditors want defensible evidence chains; PHAROS wants upstream practice to become operational default.
- **Leverage / choke points:** entry offers that convert “compliance pressure” into “method adoption” (artifact-first deliverables, explicit controls mapping, and stress-test protocols).
- **Anchor notes:** [[AI Governance Manifesto — Upstream Institutional Practice]], [[EU AI Act and Law 25 — Regulatory Pressure Window]], [[Governance Controls and Mechanisms]], [[CONTROL 1 — Tool Layer Audit Protocol (Regulatory Grounding)]], [[AI Governance Sprint — One-Page Sellable Packet]].

#### Authority / Power

- **Actors:** institutions (state/university/corporate governance), experts/specialists, platforms/tool vendors, audiences (buyers/public), and “marked bodies” forced into legibility regimes.
- **Incentives:** institutions preserve legitimacy while externalizing cost; vendors optimize adoption/retention (fluency as UX); specialists protect status via gatekeeping; audiences want trustworthy shortcuts; marginalized actors want contestability and voice.
- **Leverage / choke points:** legitimacy machinery (proof regimes), authority classification (binding vs advisory), and “auditability of claims” as the scarce resource that converts rhetoric into governance.
- **Anchor notes:** [[Authority, Legitimacy, and Post-Sovereignty]], [[SPECIALIST-GUIDELINE-AUTHORITY — Binding vs Advisory Split]], [[Trust Advantage Analysis — Sales and AI Governance]], [[Governance Stress-Test Protocols — Index]].

#### Fluency (cross-cutting)

- **Actors:** institutions and AI systems that output fluency; evaluators who reward fluency; subjects who pay the cost of dysfluency (disability/queerness/precarity); governance operators designing “interruptible fluency”.
- **Incentives:** systems are rewarded for smoothness (speed, certainty, persuasion); evaluators use fluency as a proxy for competence; subjects seek survival under compulsory fluency; PHAROS/HELIX enforce “make uncertainty audible”.
- **Leverage / choke points:** designs that separate registers (fact / inference / uncertainty / action), and protocols that force interruption (stress tests; refusal states; traceability).
- **Anchor notes:** [[Fluency, Interruption, and Institutional Accountability]], [[Fluency and Interruption Theory]], [[Beyond Fluency — Stuttering, Autoethnography, and Unstable Epistemology]].

### Pass 14 — Power map (clusters): Identity/Persona, Recursion/Agent practice, Storytelling/Narrative

#### Identity / Persona

- **Actors:** the operator (Martin Lepage), the “public voice” interface, specialist agents/personas, and audiences who demand coherence.
- **Incentives:** coherence earns trust; fragmentation invites governance drift; persona sprawl creates unaccountable outputs; the public voice must remain legible and audit-friendly.
- **Leverage / choke points:** a single identity kernel + routing architecture + explicit “what may speak publicly” contract.
- **Anchor notes:** [[Second Self System — Identity Kernel and Agent Routing Architecture]], [[Architecture Translation Guide — Eight Operators to Three-Agent Stack]], [[Trismégiste — Operator State]], [[Agent Session Phenomenology]].

#### Recursion / Agent practice

- **Actors:** agent stacks, protocol designers (HELIX/PHAROS), auditors (Argus layer), and adversarial environments that produce failure under pressure.
- **Incentives:** recursion is used to stabilize epistemics (truth discipline) and to surface hidden failure modes; adversaries exploit “confidence without proof”.
- **Leverage / choke points:** recursive reinjection + stability-under-pressure scoring; converting conversational failure modes into governance controls.
- **Anchor notes:** [[Recursive Governance Theory]], [[Recursive Deterministic AI Governance — Method and Paper]], [[Governance Stress-Test Protocols — Index]], [[RAGE — Recursive Artifact Governance Engine Proposal (2026-05-11)]].

#### Storytelling / Narrative

- **Actors:** the narrator (operator), cultural artifacts (media “legitimacy machines”), and commercial audiences who buy a story before they buy a method.
- **Incentives:** narrative compresses complexity into commitment; institutions use narrative to naturalize authority; PHAROS needs narrative that *reveals mechanisms* instead of concealing them.
- **Leverage / choke points:** “story as governance instrument” (make decision criteria visible; turn lived contradiction into method; convert biography into trust without collapsing into autobiography-as-marketing).
- **Anchor notes:** [[Strategic Storytelling in the AI Economy]], [[Legitimacy Machines — Book Skeleton and Architecture]], [[Spider-Man and the Phonetic Glitch — Voice as Infrastructure]], [[The Palace Under Root — Allegory and the Keyport Novel]].

### Pass 15 — Power map (clusters): Queer/Ritual/Magic, Commercial/PHAROS, Health/Neuro

#### Queer / Ritual / Magic

- **Actors:** practitioners/communities, institutions that police credibility, platforms that mediatize practice, and scholars translating ritual into proof regimes.
- **Incentives:** communities seek belonging + efficacy; institutions seek control over what counts as “real”; platforms monetize circulation; scholarship seeks defensible accounts of legitimacy under visibility/commodification.
- **Leverage / choke points:** proof-regime articulation (how practices become credible), “glitch” as threshold event, and ritual repetition as legitimacy engine (also a governance primitive).
- **Anchor notes:** [[Pagan and Queer Ritual Studies MOC]], [[Agatha All Along — Wicca, Digital Mediatization, and Proof Regimes]], [[Sealed Card Protocol — Legitimacy, Glitch, and Charging]], [[Authority After Legitimacy — Disenchantment and Queer Political Theory]].

#### Commercial / PHAROS / Revenue

- **Actors:** buyers (risk owners, procurement), sellers (PHAROS), and competitors/adjacent services (compliance consultancies, safety vendors).
- **Incentives:** buyers want “defensible under scrutiny” artifacts; sellers need legible packaging without collapsing the method into compliance theater; competitors sell fluency (dashboards, checklists) as governance.
- **Leverage / choke points:** smallest paid entry offer (Sprint packet), offer ladder sequencing, and explicit framing of “interruptible fluency” as the differentiator.
- **Anchor notes:** [[PHAROS Commercial Strategy]], [[AI Governance Offer Ladder - Montreal Quebec 90-Day Revenue Plan]], [[AI Governance Sprint — One-Page Sellable Packet]], [[PHAROS Commercial Brief — Pre-Launch Draft (Q2 2026)]].

#### Health / Neuro

- **Actors:** clinicians/researchers, patients, hospitals/procurement, regulated AI vendors, and “trust-critical” institutions (healthcare, pharma, insurers).
- **Incentives:** clinicians want usable diagnostics; vendors want deployment; institutions want liability control; patients want care that does not reduce them to a dataset.
- **Leverage / choke points:** regulated deployment pathways + evidence discipline + procurement-ready governance artifacts; HELIX-style truth-claim constraints become an applied safety mechanism.
- **Anchor notes:** [[Diagnostic Accuracy of Multimodal Neuroimaging + Eye-Tracking in Schizophrenia — Draft Snapshot (2026-05-10)]], [[HELIX Healthcare Prospect Deep Dive - Quebec and Canadian Healthcare AI Regulated Route (2026-05-11)]], [[EMERGENT HELIX Session — Truth Claims, Ingestion, and Booby-Trap Diagnostics (2026-05-07)]], [[AI Infrastructure Stack]].
