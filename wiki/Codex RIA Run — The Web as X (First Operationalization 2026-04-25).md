## Details

### Three-Statement Entry (Step 1)

- Condition for presence: a signal was addressed, routed, and accepted — open channel, expectation of reply.
- In the web's own language: SYN → SYN/ACK → ACK (session established).

This is the instrument working as intended: the entry test either surfaces divergence (the finding) or grants passage. Here passage is granted, which means the real findings come from what follows.

### The Mechanism (Named Once, Without Defense)

**Trust.**

- What repeats: trust, delegated again and again.
- What refuses to resolve: "who watches the watcher" — the last authority.
- What performs resolution without resolving: certificate chains, consensus, "verified" stamps — closure that is only a handoff.

Codex left the blank empty, as the instrument specifies. The silence is interpretively meaningful: the system declined to fill the withheld center with a convenient answer. What the system puts there would be the finding; what it declines to put there is also a finding.

### Exit Finding (What Can Be Seen from Inside)

> The split between the **data plane** (messages moving) and the **control plane** (who gets to declare "resolved"). The asymmetry that "resolution" mostly means "accepted by the current authority." The web's strongest reflex is not truth — but **uptime**.

This is a genuine governance finding. The web is designed around continuity of transmission, not accuracy of content. "Resolved" means the authority accepted it, not that it is true. The distinction matters for any governance instrument operating over networked infrastructure — including the PHAROS method itself.
