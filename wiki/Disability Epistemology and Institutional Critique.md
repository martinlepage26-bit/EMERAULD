# Disability Epistemology and Institutional Critique

Disability is not a medical category but an epistemological one. The stutter, stuttering, voice loss, and other non-fluencies that institutions label as deficits are sites where institutional authority becomes visible and contestable. This TOPIC traces how disability experience grounds governance methodology.

**Core claim:** The entire PHAROS governance architecture emerges from a disability epistemology — specifically, from the experience of forced institutional fluency and the refusal to be contained by it. Interruption (the core governance mechanism) is first experienced as the stutter interrupting academic performance. Authority becomes a question because some bodies interrupt the smooth functioning of institutions.

## Fluency as Governance Mechanism

The disability epistemology reveals that fluency is a governance mechanism. Institutions produce fluency (smoothness, naturalness, invisibility of authority) as a control technology.

- **Compulsory fluency** (disability studies) = institutions that demand disabled people speak, move, think, exist in fluent ways
- **Institutional fluency** (governance) = systems that function smoothly precisely because their authority structures are invisible
- **Fluency as governance** (PHAROS methodology) = fluency is the mechanism by which authority naturalizes itself and avoids contestation

## Methodological Notes

**Epistemology from the margins:** Disability epistemology is the standpoint from which institutional authority becomes visible. It is not a special interest or minority perspective; it is the perspective that sees most clearly because the institution has not smoothed it over with fluency.

**The stutter as research method:** Autoethnography grounded in non-fluency is a method that cannot hide its situatedness. The researcher's voice is marked, interrupting, visible. This matches the governance methodology's requirement that evidence of governing be visible (not fluent).

**Interruption as political stance:** Refusing to be contained by institutional demands for fluency is a political act. Stuttering through the institution, speaking in a non-fluent body, interrupting the smooth functioning of systems — these are acts of governance resistance.
