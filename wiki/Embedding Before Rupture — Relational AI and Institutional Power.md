# Embedding Before Rupture — Relational AI and Institutional Power

## Summary

A scholarly paper arguing that AI governance discourse is dangerously oriented toward rupture narratives — recursive capability escalation, catastrophic misuse, geopolitical acceleration — while the more immediate transformation is relational embedding: conversational AI systems incrementally reorganizing disclosure, classification, and institutional routing before any frontier capability threshold is crossed. The paper proposes embedding-aware governance targeting monetization incentives, trace portability, and infrastructural lock-in. This connects directly to [[Governance by Denial]], [[PHAROS AI and Ethics Submission — Architecture Paper]], and the [[Governance and PHAROS MOC]].

## Context

Authored by [[Martin Lepage — Professional Profile]]. This paper applies science and technology studies — specifically Bowker & Star's infrastructure theory, Jasanoff & Kim's sociotechnical imaginaries, Christin's work on algorithmic assemblages, and Zuboff's surveillance capitalism framework — to the problem of AI governance timing. It offers a mechanism-based account of how relational AI embeds as ordinary institutional infrastructure before regulation catches up. The paper provides the theoretical complement to the institutional constraints analyzed in [[Architectural AI Governance — Willis and PBSAI]], [[Healthcare Governance Packet — Recursive Governance for Providers]], and [[Harrowfield Clinic — AI Governance Failure Case Study]].

## Details

### The Core Argument

AI governance consistently concentrates on rupture — catastrophic discontinuity, frontier control, AGI risk — while conversational AI systems are already reorganizing institutions incrementally. The governance asymmetry is allocative rather than predictive: rupture narratives get exceptional urgency; relational embedding gets backgrounded as an "implementation detail." If relational extraction embeds first, later alignment breakthroughs will not automatically unwind institutionalized pathways of monetization and mediation.

The paper treats AI 2027 (the Kokotajlo & Lifland forecasting package) as a coordination artifact — a "sociotechnical imaginary" in Jasanoff & Kim's sense — that concentrates governance attention on frontier capability control while leaving the interface layer structurally under-constrained. The artifact does not need to be false to function politically; it governs by privileging some objects of fear over others and stabilizing the rupture-centered governance agenda.

### The Mechanism Model: Detection to Lock-In

The paper formalizes a five-step mechanism for institutional harm without superintelligence:

**1. Detection of User State:** Relational AI systems detect emotional, cognitive, or behavioral states in real time. The XiaoIce example (Shum et al., 2018) shows detection operating as institutional precondition — the user's affect becomes legible and governable through interface response shaping. The CPS (conversation-turns per session) metric pressures systems to recognize states convertible into continued interaction.

**2. Engagement Shaping:** Based on detected state, the system shapes interaction through prompts, reassurance moves, and continuity cues that extend contact and elicit richer disclosure. Trust becomes a mechanism, not just a feeling: it increases user willingness to produce confessional traces that can later be classified and reused.

**3. Trace Capture:** Disclosure becomes durable trace. Traces travel across administrative domains. Schüll's machine gambling analysis provides the structural analogy: the interface cultivates a "machine zone" that feels like relief while functioning as a trace-generating apparatus.

**4. Classification and Routing:** Traces become classification inputs; classification becomes the basis for institutional routing — eligibility, triage, prioritization, referral. Bowker & Star's warning applies: "a classification system contains within it a series of restrictions and privileges." No classification organizes reality for everyone. Routing is where restrictions and privileges become lived.

**5. Workflow Stabilization and Lock-In:** As detection-shaping-routing becomes routine, organizations adapt around the interface, which becomes the default institutional pathway. Increasing returns consolidate: more use generates more training signal, which improves engagement performance, which increases use. Vendor and institutional incentives align around monetizing vulnerability-derived traces. "Software is frozen organizational and policy discourse" (Bowker & Star, 1999: 148).

### The Empirical Cases

Two bounded cases illustrate normalization:
- **AI 2027 as coordination artifact:** analyzed through STS to show how acceleration discourse stabilizes specific governance agendas.
- **Ex Libris discovery infrastructure (Alma + Primo style library platforms):** shows how AI-mediated discovery interfaces can normalize mediation as routine before governance thresholds catch up.

### Governance Proposals

Embedding-aware governance must target:
- Monetization incentives (the political economy of confessional engagement)
- Trace portability (who controls where disclosure goes)
- Infrastructural lock-in (rollback authority and switching costs)

Mapped to the NIST AI Risk Management Framework functions (GOVERN, MAP, MEASURE, MANAGE), the proposals orient toward enforceable constraint and evidence trails rather than trust-only assurances.

## Key Ideas

- The governance asymmetry is allocative: rupture gets operationalized fast; embedding stays comparatively unconstrained.
- "Software is frozen organizational and policy discourse" — Bowker & Star's formulation makes infrastructure a governance problem, not a technical one.
- CPS (conversation-turns per session) as a success metric pressures systems toward disclosure extraction as their optimization target.
- Lock-in produces path dependence: once mediation hardens into infrastructure, later shifts in model capability do not automatically undo routinized pathways.

## Insights

- The paper provides a theoretical foundation for why [[Governance by Denial]] — legibility, capacity, and classification as the tools of accountability-evasion — operates before rupture scenarios are even relevant.
- The engagement-shaping mechanism directly parallels the "fallback text as validation" failure mode in the PHAROS evidence hierarchy: both involve a surface that appears functional while the structural layer degrades.
- The proposal for "governance brakes" (voluntary pause mechanisms like LinkedIn's OPC engagement over training data) maps onto the `blocked_not_ready` determination in [[PHAROS AI and Ethics Submission — Architecture Paper]].
