# Social Stratification, Intersectionality, and the Why Be King Manuscript

## Summary

Enriched manuscript (2017) by [[Martin Lepage — Professional Profile|Martin Lepage]] expanding the "Why Be King" dissertation into book form, incorporating a comprehensive references section grounded in social stratification and intersectionality theory. This document represents the late-stage scholarly infrastructure of the [[Why Be King Im Already a Queen — Book Project]] — the full chapter-and-references structure for the planned academic monograph. Part of [[Research and Papers MOC]] and [[Historical Academic Portfolio — Pre-PHAROS Scholarly Work]].

## Context

The file (`2017 - Social Stratification and Social Inequality Models of Intersectionality and Identity.docx`) contains content labeled "Why Be King: Enriched Manuscript" — the title signals it is an expanded version of the doctoral work, not a standalone paper on stratification theory. The document integrates previous chapters (noted as "already integrated in canvas") with Chapter Six ("Queering Magic: Identity, Power, and the Limits of Religion") and a substantial reference list. The Anthias source on intersectionality in the references — "Social Stratification and Social Inequality: Models of Intersectionality and Identity" (2005) — appears to have been the source of the file's title attribution by the archiving system. This connects directly to [[Why Be King Im Already a Queen — Book Project]] and represents the book proposal's full scholarly grounding. The intersectionality framing draws directly on [[Mapping the Margins — Crenshaw 1991 (Intersectionality and Violence)]]. The enriched manuscript adds sociocritique to the queer pagan ethnographic frame established at the MA level (see [[Mythocritique to PHAROS — The 2010 Master's Thesis as Methodological Keystone]] and [[Mort et Naissance et L'Ombre et le Double — MA Thesis on Yvon Rivard (Université Laval, 2010)]]); indexed in [[Queer Sociology of Magic and Ritual]].

## Details

**Document structure:**
- Introduction: "Sacred Subversions: Queer Becoming in Montreal's Neo-Pagan Circles"
- Multiple chapters noted as previously integrated
- Chapter Six: "Queering Magic: Identity, Power, and the Limits of Religion"
- Full reference list

**Chapter Six argument ("Queering Magic"):** This chapter directly feeds into [[Queering Neo-Pagan Magic — Identity Power and the Uses of Magic]], placing magic at the limit point where religion and identity performance negotiate. The title phrase "limits of religion" echoes Bailey's (2006) argument about magic demarcating the limits of religious legitimacy — a citation that anchors both this chapter and the standalone 2026 paper.

**Reference list highlights:**

*Core Pagan studies:* Albanese (1990) *Nature Religion in America*; Adler (1986) *Drawing Down the Moon*; Berger (1998) *A Community of Witches*; Bailey (2006) "Meanings of Magic"; Pike (2001); Hutton (1996, 1999); Magliocco (2004)

*Queer/gender theory:* Baril (2007) on sex/gender construction; Bardella (2001) on queer spirituality; Bever and Styers (2017) on magic legitimization

*Intersectionality:* Anthias, Floya. "Social Stratification and Social Inequality: Models of Intersectionality and Identity." In *Rethinking Class* (Palgrave, 2005) — the source that named this file in the archive

*Sociology of religion:* Bloch (1998); Charbonneau (2008) Montreal Pagan community thesis; Davy (2007); Cowan (2005) *Cyberhenge*

**Fieldwork base:** Ethnographic research in Montreal's neo-Pagan community — the same core fieldwork as [[Neo-Shamanism and Gender — Montreal Pagan Community]], [[The Inner Goddess — Gender Normativity and Gay Identity in Wiccan Ritualities]], and [[Queer Neo-Pagan Spiritualities and the Question of Authenticity in Wicca]].

## Key Ideas

- The book project's "enriched" framing signals expansion beyond the dissertation: new chapters, updated theory, more developed argument
- Chapter Six positions magic as the limit point of religion — where identity performance and religious claim encounter each other most directly
- Intersectionality as explicit theoretical ground: the Anthias citation (re: class, identity, and social inequality) places the queer Pagan analysis within broader social stratification theory, not just queer theory
- The reference list documents the full range of sources that ground the book — its breadth confirms that this is a mature scholarly project, not a draft

## Insights

- The archiving system appears to have named the file after the Anthias reference rather than the actual document title — a documentation artifact worth noting when searching for this material.
- The book manuscript's integration of stratification theory (Anthias) alongside queer theory and Pagan studies is the theoretical move that positions [[Why Be King Im Already a Queen — Book Project]] as a contribution to multiple fields simultaneously.
- Chapter Six ("Queering Magic") is a direct predecessor to the 2026 standalone paper [[Queering Neo-Pagan Magic — Identity Power and the Uses of Magic]] — the book chapter becomes a journal article in the later corpus.
