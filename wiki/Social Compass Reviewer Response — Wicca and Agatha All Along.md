# Social Compass Reviewer Response — Wicca and Agatha All Along

## Summary

Two versions of the response to editorial reviewers for the *Social Compass* article "De la Wicca à Agatha All Along: recompositions culturelles, numériques et enjeux épistémologiques" by [[Martin Lepage — Professional Profile|Martin Lepage]]. Documents the revision process that produced the canonical version recorded in [[Social Compass Paper — Ritual Authority and Régime de Preuve]]. Indexed in [[Media Studies and Pop Culture Analysis]] and [[Queer Sociology of Magic and Ritual]]. Companion to [[Agatha All Along Social Compass — Version Genealogy]]; the *régime de preuve* frame defended in this response is grounded in the *ren*-doctrine of [[Mythocritique to PHAROS — The 2010 Master's Thesis as Methodological Keystone|the keystone]]. Part of [[Research and Papers MOC]].

## Context

Three editorial feedback items prompted the revisions:
1. Originality of contribution relative to prior publications from the same fieldwork
2. Clarification of generative AI use in manuscript preparation
3. Theoretical justification for writing choices

The two response documents (v2 and v3) are in French, addressed to the editorial team of *Social Compass*. They demonstrate the revision logic that shifted the paper from the "Wicca 2.0" framing (see [[Virtually Magical Literally — Queer Agency and the Digital Witch]]) toward the *régime de preuve* argument documented in [[Social Compass Paper — Ritual Authority and Régime de Preuve]].

## Details

**Four novelty claims clarified in the revision:**
1. Explicit articulation of Quebec/Canadian ethnographic fieldwork (2013–2016) with *Agatha All Along* as a **narrative interface** — not an empirical substitute for the field
2. Shift of focus from Wiccan/queer identities to the **conditions of credibility**: what makes a practice recognizable, validated, shareable
3. Re-reading of already-used ethnographic materials through a new question about formats, devices, and constraints that make a practice platformable or monetizable
4. Integration of platformization, digital mediation, and popular culture as **analytical infrastructures**, not contextual backdrop

**On AI use:** The manuscript was not generated automatically by an AI system. A limited use of ChatGPT-5 Thinking served only for local reformulation, stylistic smoothing of already-written sentences, checking transitions, and verifying bibliographic formatting. This usage did not touch data production, ethnographic extract selection, central argument construction, theoretical interpretation, or conclusions. All intellectual content remains the author's sole responsibility.

**The v3 revision** additionally incorporated: an explicit 2017 ritual scene, tighter framing of platforms and popular culture as validation infrastructures, and removal of elements that could de-anonymize the manuscript.

## Key Ideas

- The revision shifts the theoretical frame from **digital transformation of identity** (Wicca 2.0) to **authority fabrication under proof regimes** — the more governance-oriented argument that becomes the canonical version
- The AI use declaration is notable as a scholarly accountability document: specific about what AI did and did not do, consistent with the evidentiary discipline in [[PHAROS Recalibration — Unified Governance Architecture|PHAROS]]
- The response-to-reviewers documents how an argument develops through editorial pressure — a meta-level example of the revision logic the PHAROS method formalizes

## Insights

- The explicit distinction between "AI used for local reformulation" and "AI not used for argument construction" anticipates the hybrid authorship questions in [[Refusing Fixity — Aesthetic Governance and Fugitive Authorship]] and the SAKURA project's "ensoulment by proxy" question ([[SAKURA Project — Legitimacy, Mediation, and Ensoulment by Proxy]])
- The v2 → v3 revision itself is an instance of recursive governance: the paper is revised under the pressure of external critique, with explicit tracking of what changed and why — exactly what [[First Method Paper — Recursive AI Governance as Executable Method]] describes
