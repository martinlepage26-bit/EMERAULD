# Mapping the Margins — Crenshaw 1991 (Intersectionality and Violence)

## Summary
Kimberlé Crenshaw’s “Mapping the Margins” is a foundational articulation of **intersectionality** as a structural and political problem, written through the concrete case of violence against women of color. In this vault it is core theoretical infrastructure for [[Queer Theory Foundations]] and for governance claims that explicitly depend on intersectional scope control (see [[Paper 25 — The Pharos Frame - Four Levels Where Ethics Becomes AI Governance]]). Applied directly in [[Social Stratification, Intersectionality, and the Why Be King Manuscript]] (the enriched manuscript adds intersectionality to the queer pagan ethnographic frame) and [[Why be king Im already a queen — Lepage 2017 (PhD thesis, UQAM)]].

## Context
Crenshaw’s framing clarifies why single-axis governance and policy claims routinely fail: the conditions that produce harm are multi-structured, and “fixes” designed for one axis can reproduce domination for people positioned at the intersection. This directly supports the method logic in [[Fluency, Interruption, and Institutional Accountability]] and the citation backbone in [[Qualitative Handbook — Expanded Reference List]].

## Key Ideas (Vault-usable)
- **Structural intersectionality:** intersecting social structures (race/gender/class) shape not only harm but access to remedies, making “standard” interventions miss the actual constraint surface.
- **Political intersectionality:** feminist and antiracist politics can jointly marginalize violence against women of color by treating “woman” and “people of color” as mutually exclusive agendas.
- **Intervention design constraint:** policies built from the experience of people not subject to the same intersections are predictably of limited utility.
