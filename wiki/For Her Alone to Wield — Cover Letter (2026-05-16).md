## Cover Letter

[Date]

Editors, *Slayage: The Whedon Studies Journal*

Dear Editors,

I am submitting "For Her Alone to Wield? Infrastructure, Redistribution, and the Politics of the Scythe in *Buffy the Vampire Slayer*" for consideration in *Slayage*.

**Argument.** The article proposes a methodological reorientation in BtVS power scholarship: reading Slayer power as *infrastructure* — a distributed system with intake conditions, maintenance requirements, distribution architecture, and failure modes — rather than as *property* held or deserved by individuals. Applied to the series' three-phase governance arc (Watchers Council enclosure, "Chosen" redistribution, Season 8 fragmentation), this frame reveals that the series' climactic redistribution spell is a governance failure rather than a liberation: redistribution without institutional infrastructure produces exposure, not emancipation. Drawing on Fraser's redistribution/recognition framework and Berlant's analysis of cruel optimism, the article argues that the "Chosen" spell's consequences — factional fragmentation in Season 8, centuries of institutional dysfunction in the Fray timeline — are not narrative surprises but the structural outcomes of redistribution into a governance vacuum.

**Disciplinary positioning.** The infrastructure frame is feminist political economy before it is political theory in the abstract. The article does not displace the representational questions that have organized BtVS scholarship (Pender 2016; Jowett 2005; Wilcox 2005); it works at an institutional level that the representational frame does not reach — the level at which what the "Chosen" spell actually does to the Slayer power system becomes analytically visible. The Scythe is reread not primarily as a weapon but as a legitimacy device: the artifact that authorizes Slayer power, whose governance function the redistribution suspends rather than resolves.

**Prior publication in *Slayage*.** "Every Hair a Battle Scar: Buffy Summers' Hair as Narrative Engine, Cultural Icon, and Postfeminist Practice" appeared in *Slayage* 23.2 [61] (2025). The two articles are substantively distinct: "Every Hair" reads surface as governance mechanism — hair as charge-carrier and visual text — while "For Her Alone to Wield" reads institutional architecture and redistribution politics through the Slayer power system as infrastructure. They are companion papers in the Slayer cluster, not variants of the same argument.

**Companion paper disclosure.** A companion paper, "Still Running: Willow, Anya, and Queer Ritual Infrastructure," is in development and is not being submitted to *Slayage* simultaneously.

**Corpus.** The article's corpus comprises the television run (Seasons 1–7), Season 8 comics through the Twilight arc, *Fray* (2001–2003), and the "Time of Your Life" crossover. The post-Season 8 reconstruction of the Slayer line (Seasons 9–12) represents a distinct governance phase outside this paper's scope.
