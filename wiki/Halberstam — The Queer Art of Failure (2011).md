# Halberstam — The Queer Art of Failure (2011)

## Summary

Jack Halberstam's *The Queer Art of Failure* (Duke University Press, 2011) argues that failure is a productive political and epistemological position: the queer art of failure **refuses the normative terms of success** and finds in loss, forgetfulness, unbecoming, and undoing an alternative set of possibilities. Key theoretical resource in [[Martin Lepage — Professional Profile|Martin's]] corpus for the **failure-as-method doctrine** that runs through the [[Martin Walks the Witches' Road — Corpus as Charge-Persistence|canonical corpus arc]].

**Citation:** Halberstam, J. (2011). *The Queer Art of Failure*. Durham: Duke University Press.
**Corpus bibliography line:** 816 (MASTER BIBLIOGRAPHY working file).

## Context

In the [[LOTUS Model — Agency and Social Positioning|LOTUS model]], Halberstam is mapped to the **Sh dimension** (shadow remainder) alongside Latour's irreducibility claim. Together they provide the theoretical basis for why the Sh cannot be fully captured: the shadow remainder is not noise — it is the structural residue that, by resisting administration, protects the system. See [[Mythocritique to PHAROS — The 2010 Master's Thesis as Methodological Keystone|MA thesis]] p. 48: *shout* (Egyptian *Khaibit*) as "métaphore de protection."

Halberstam's argument complements the Egyptian-substrate account: where the Khaibit operates as structural mechanism (the shadow refuses classification by design), *Queer Art of Failure* operates as political epistemology (failure refuses the gatekeeper's terms). Together they explain the canonical doctrine's claim that **none of the surfaces is the origin; all are phase transitions**.
