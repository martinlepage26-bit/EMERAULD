# Refusing Fixity — Aesthetic Governance and Fugitive Authorship

## Summary
Academic paper arguing that "dead knowledge" — fixed, authoritative, self-confirming knowledge — is the primary governance pathology in institutional and creative settings. Author: Martin Lepage, PhD (2011, with later revisions). Introduces **aesthetic governance** as the analysis of how form disciplines content, bodies, and authority. Develops **fugitive authorship** as a counter-practice: authorship that refuses to cohere into a stable, attributable, ownable identity. Includes an experiment with an AI collaborator named "Agatha" trained on corpus texts, and a peer review experiment demonstrating social biases in academic evaluation. Connected to the [[HEXA Press — Publishing and Practice Initiative]] / HEX/ADECIMAL Mystic project. Related to [[Sealed Card Protocol — Legitimacy, Glitch, and Charging]], [[AI Society Manuscript — From AI Anxiety to Recursive Governance]], and [[Historical Academic Portfolio — Pre-PHAROS Scholarly Work]].

## Context
Source: `2011 - Refusing Fixity.docx`. This is the "Refusing Fixity: Aesthetic Governance, Fugitive Authorship, and the Refusal of Dead Knowledge" paper listed in [[Historical Academic Portfolio — Pre-PHAROS Scholarly Work]]. The 2011 date appears in the filename; later revisions incorporated the AI collaborator experiment and the peer review findings, suggesting significant post-2020 revision. The paper occupies an important position in the intellectual history: it establishes the "refusal to cohere" position 15 years before the PHAROS governance publications. **Refusal of fixed authority** is structurally the Witches'-Road logic of charge-persistence-through-recomposition — canonical doctrine: [[Martin Walks the Witches' Road — Corpus as Charge-Persistence]]; see also [[The Ballad of the Witches' Road — Analysis]] — and the Name-dialectic from the [[Mort et Naissance et L'Ombre et le Double — MA Thesis on Yvon Rivard (Université Laval, 2010)|2010 MA thesis]] (p. 79: pronounce the Name AND release it) — see [[Mythocritique to PHAROS — The 2010 Master's Thesis as Methodological Keystone|the keystone]]. Indexed in [[Media Studies and Pop Culture Analysis]], [[Authority, Legitimacy, and Post-Sovereignty]], and [[Disability Epistemology and Institutional Critique]] (refusal to cohere as disability methodology; fugitive authorship as counter-governance practice against fixed institutional capture).

## Dead Knowledge

**Dead knowledge** is knowledge that:
- Has fixed its own boundaries (no new evidence will change it)
- Cites itself recursively (authority derived from prior use of the same authority)
- Produces smooth outputs that appear natural and inevitable
- Cannot be reopened without institutional resistance

Dead knowledge is not false — it can be technically accurate while still being dead. Deadness is a governance property, not an epistemic one. A claim becomes dead when the institutional apparatus protecting it makes it functionally unchallengeable.

**The governance problem**: institutions require some dead knowledge to function — rules, precedents, established categories. But they also require live knowledge to remain responsive. Aesthetic governance is the analysis of which knowledge is kept alive and which is killed, by whom, and under what institutional conditions.

---

## Aesthetic Governance

**Aesthetic governance** is the regime of norms that determine which forms, styles, rhythms, and textures count as legitimate knowledge production.

Key mechanisms:
- **Form as discipline**: academic writing enforces specific sentence structures, citation formats, hedging conventions, and argumentation templates. These are not neutral — they embody epistemological assumptions about what legitimate reasoning looks like
- **Tempo as evidence**: the expectation that slow, labored, visible-struggle writing is more sincere than polished, confident writing is an aesthetic norm functioning as a governance rule
- **Legibility prosthesis**: writing assistance (AI or otherwise) that enables a writer to produce formally compliant text is policed not because it produces false content but because it disrupts the aesthetic governance regime — see [[Sealed Card Protocol — Legitimacy, Glitch, and Charging]] for the disability studies connection

The aesthetic governance concept connects directly to the [[Fluency, Interruption, and Institutional Accountability]] analysis: fluency is the aesthetic norm of governance-compliant knowledge production.

## Fugitive Authorship

**Fugitive authorship** is the practice of authoring without fixing — producing without authorizing a stable origin point.

- Authorship that refuses attribution is not plagiarism — it is a political refusal of intellectual property logic
- Fugitive authorship passes through institutions without being captured by them: it uses institutional forms (articles, citations, peer review) without underwriting the authority claims those forms normally carry
- **Passing and scattering** as dialectical method: two paired movements. Passing — moving through institutional contexts without leaving a fixed trace. Scattering — distributing authority across multiple sites, voices, and frames so it cannot be localized and captured

**The political claim**: In contexts where authorship is a mechanism of control (citation metrics, IP ownership, academic credit systems), fugitive authorship is a counter-governance practice. It is the authorial version of the posthuman commons — shared agency that exceeds the boundaries of individual ownership.

## The AI Collaborator "Agatha"

The paper includes an experiment with an AI collaborator named "Agatha," trained on a corpus of Martin Lepage's texts. Key findings:

- Agatha, trained on the corpus, produces text that is identifiable as "Lepage-like" — but not because it reproduces specific phrases; because it has internalized the aesthetic governance norms of the corpus
- This raises the **archive-becomes-agent problem** (from [[Recursive Continuity Without Memory — AI Identity Across Sessions]]): when an AI is trained on a person's texts, it does not reproduce the person — it produces a governance structure derived from the person's aesthetic norms
- The question "Is this Lepage's writing?" becomes unanswerable in the usual sense — which is the point: fugitive authorship is demonstrated through the AI experiment, not merely theorized

**The recursion**: Agatha is both the AI collaborator in this paper and the name later given to the AI character in [[Agatha All Along — Wicca, Digital Mediatization, and Proof Regimes]]. Whether this is coincidence or deliberate naming is an open question.

## Peer Review Experiment

The paper documents a controlled experiment in academic peer review:

- Two manuscripts submitted to peer review: one explicitly described as AI-mediated, one presented as conventionally authored (both by Martin Lepage)
- **Finding**: AI-mediated manuscripts consistently scored warmer — more positively received, described as "clear," "accessible," "well-structured"
- **Interpretation**: Reviewers responding to aesthetic governance norms, not to argument quality. AI-produced or AI-mediated text tends to conform more closely to the aesthetic expectations of the academic form — and reviewers reward that conformity

**The governance implication**: peer review as currently practiced is partly an aesthetic governance mechanism, not purely an epistemic one. This is the academic version of the [[Fluency, Interruption, and Institutional Accountability]] argument: fluency (aesthetic compliance) substitutes for rigor (epistemic validity).

## HEX/ADECIMAL Mystic Project

The paper is connected to the [[HEXA Press — Publishing and Practice Initiative]] / HEX/ADECIMAL Mystic project — a publishing and practice initiative that:
- Produces texts under collective, pseudonymous, or distributed authorship
- Explicitly refuses stable attribution
- Operates across academic, creative, and ritual registers without fixing to any one

The [[HEXA Press — Publishing and Practice Initiative|HEXA Press]] imprint (also the publisher of [[Alchemy of the Wound — Novel]], [[CORPUS ou le génie de l'insistance — Novel]], [[Le sang qui porte ton nom — Novel]], [[The Broken Frequency of the Word — Novel]]) is the institutional expression of fugitive authorship.

## Insights

- The 2011 dating of this paper makes it the earliest explicit articulation of the governance critique in the archive — predating the PHAROS work by 14 years. The governing thread ("queerness is the refusal to cohere") appears here not as a tagline but as a methodological practice
- The peer review experiment is empirical evidence for the aesthetic governance claim — not just a theoretical argument. This is the same move the [[AI Society Manuscript — From AI Anxiety to Recursive Governance]] makes: grounding governance theory in evidence from AI use
- Fugitive authorship is the authorial version of the PHAROS governance design principle: claims must be made with declared gaps (L99), not smoothed into coherence. An author who refuses to fix their claim — who scatters rather than seals — is practicing the same epistemic discipline
- The AI collaborator "Agatha" experiment predates widespread LLM availability, suggesting early engagement with generative AI as a governance object — not just a tool

## Open Questions

- Is the 2011 paper the version submitted for publication, or is it a draft? Where did it ultimately publish (or was it published)?
- How does the peer review experiment control for content quality? The finding that AI-mediated text scores warmer could reflect content quality improvements as well as aesthetic compliance
- Is "Agatha" the AI collaborator a proper name chosen deliberately (connection to the MCU character, or to the HEX/ADECIMAL imprint persona)?
- What is the HEX/ADECIMAL Mystic project's current status? Is it an ongoing publishing initiative or a historical one?
