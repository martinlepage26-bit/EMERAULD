# Consent and Boundary Frameworks

Consent is not permission; it is the continuous answerability required before any authority can act. Boundaries are the mechanisms that enforce consent and protect against institutional intrusion. This TOPIC organizes papers on consent as a governance principle, boundary-setting as refusal, and the contestation of consent-denying systems.

This is the consent/boundary branch of the PHAROS governance-values graph. It sits between [[Care, Ethics, and Governance]] and [[Authority, Legitimacy, and Post-Sovereignty]], making the boundary condition explicit for the wider governance method.

**Core principle:** Authority without answerability to consent is domination. Governance systems must include explicit provisions for boundary-setting and refusal.

**Audit status:** Phase 3B relinking verified by [[Argus Audit — Phase 3A-3B-3C-3D Relinking Campaign (2026-05-06)]] — seven-layer governance audit. Six Consent papers successfully linked with cross-TOPIC coherence confirmed (4/6 authority-consent bridged). **Result: deployment-ready.**
