# Pup Play, BDSM, and Gay Masculinities — Annotated Bibliography (2026)

## Summary

Full annotated bibliography and literature review across three interlocking clusters: pup play / puppy play, BDSM / kink / leather culture, and the evolution of gay masculinities. Produced as research scaffolding for work adjacent to the [[Queering Neo-Pagan Magic — 2026 Paper]] and the broader [[Queer Sociology of Magic and Ritual]] domain. The central hinge text connecting all three threads is Peter Hennen's *Faeries, Bears, and Leathermen* (2008), which directly overlaps with the Radical Faeries work in [[Masculinités et ritualités — La magie chez les Radical Faeries]]. Two source errors in the original brief are corrected here: **Mair Underwood** has not published on pup play (the relevant anthropologist is **Jamie Lawson**, University of Bristol); **Andreas-Holger Maehle** did not author an SM-specific history (the relevant *Medical History* piece is by **Harry Oosterhuis**).

## Context

Sits within the [[Pagan and Queer Ritual Studies MOC]] as the sexuality/kink-subculture branch of the queer pagan research program. The pup-play literature traces pup play's lineage directly to the gay leatherman subculture, making this bibliography a natural extension of the leather/BDSM scholarship that underlies readings of queer ritual masculinity. The theoretical apparatus — Butler's performativity, Foucault's ascesis, Connell's hegemonic masculinity, Bourdieu's symbolic power — is continuous with the [[Queer Sociology of Magic and Ritual]] framework and the genealogy documented in [[MASTER BIBLIOGRAPHY — Theoretical Genealogy of LOTUS]].

## TL;DR

- The pup-play literature is small but internally coherent, anchored by Liam Wignall and Mark McCormack (*Archives of Sexual Behavior*, 2017; OUP monograph, 2022) and Jamie Lawson & Darren Langdridge (*Sexualities*, 2020); this literature itself traces pup play's lineage to the gay leatherman subculture, making it a natural bridge to BDSM and gay-masculinities scholarship.
- The BDSM/leather and gay-masculinities clusters are mature and canonical — Gayle Rubin, Margot Weiss, Staci Newmahr, and Robin Bauer on kink; R. W. Connell, Martin Levine, David Halperin, and Peter Hennen on masculinity — with Hennen's *Faeries, Bears, and Leathermen* (2008) as the single most important hinge text connecting all three threads.
- Two figures named in the original brief require correction: anthropologist **Mair Underwood has NOT published on pup play** (the relevant anthropologist is **Jamie Lawson**, University of Bristol), and **Andreas-Holger Maehle did not author an SM-specific history** (the relevant *Medical History* piece is by **Harry Oosterhuis**).

## Key Findings

- The empirical pup-play corpus is almost entirely 2017–2024 and dominated by UK and North American sociology/psychology of sexuality; the field has progressed from small qualitative interview studies to larger surveys but still lacks a non-Wignall book-length ethnography.
- Foundational kink/leather scholarship clusters around San Francisco ethnography (Rubin, Weiss) and the feminist "sex wars" (Rubin, Califia).
- Gay-masculinities scholarship runs from Levine's 1970s "clone," through Connell's hegemonic-masculinity framework, to Anderson's inclusive masculinity and Bridges/Pascoe's hybrid masculinities.
- Real gaps remain: no book-length pup-play ethnography outside Wignall; almost no work on sexual racism *within* pup play specifically; sparse Francophone scholarship apart from recent Québécois work (Ferlatte and colleagues).

## CLUSTER 1 — PUPPY PLAY / PUP PLAY

**Wignall, Liam, and Mark McCormack. "An Exploratory Study of a New Kink Activity: 'Pup Play.'" *Archives of Sexual Behavior* 46, no. 3 (2017): 801–811. DOI: 10.1007/s10508-015-0636-8.** *(Empirical; foundational.)* The first peer-reviewed empirical study of pup play. Based on interviews with 30 gay and bisexual men in the UK, modified grounded-theory approach. Argues pup play primarily involves mimicking the role of a dog, serves both sexual satisfaction and relaxation (via a "headspace"), and classifies pup play as a kink with no evidence for the framing of it as zoophilia. Speaks to threads 1 and 2.

**Wignall, Liam. *Kinky in the Digital Age: Gay Men's Subcultures and Social Identities.* New York: Oxford University Press, 2022. DOI: 10.1093/oso/9780197503195.001.0001.** *(Empirical monograph; foundational.)* Draws on 74 interviews with gay men to analyze how the internet transforms kink subcultures; includes dedicated chapters on "The Phenomenon of Pup Play" and "Pup Play as a Sexual Subculture." The single most sustained scholarly treatment of pup play to date, locating pups within their communities and cultural contexts. Speaks to threads 1, 2, and 3.

**Wignall, Liam. "The Sexual Use of a Social Networking Site: The Case of Pup Twitter." *Sociological Research Online* 22, no. 3 (2017): 21–37. DOI: 10.1177/1360780417724066.** *(Empirical.)* Based on interviews with 26 UK gay/bi "pups," examines how a pre-existing kink subculture adapts Twitter into a distinct community ("Pup Twitter") for communication, engagement, and visibility. Threads 1 and 3.

**Wignall, Liam, Mark McCormack, Taylor Cook, and Rusi Jaspal. "Findings From a Community Survey of Individuals Who Engage in Pup Play." *Archives of Sexual Behavior* 51, no. 7 (2022): 3637–3646. DOI: 10.1007/s10508-021-02225-z.** *(Empirical; recent.)* The first large-scale quantitative study (733 participants, primarily US but international), reporting demographics, gear ownership/use, social and sexual dimensions, social identity (unique pup names, breed identification), and mental health — confirming and extending the conceptualization developed in earlier qualitative work. Thread 1.

**Wignall, Liam, Rachel Moseley, and Mark McCormack. "Autistic Traits of People Who Engage in Pup Play: Occurrence, Characteristics and Social Connections." *The Journal of Sex Research* 62, no. 3 (2025): 330–340. DOI: 10.1080/00224499.2023.2239225.** *(Empirical; recent; online-first 2023, print volume 2025.)* Survey of 413 practitioners using the Autism-Spectrum Quotient–Short Form (AQ–S); 1 in 2 participants reported a score indicative of an autism diagnosis, substantially higher than general-population prevalence (1 in 44). Argues for attention to intersections of neurodiversity and sexual subcultures. Thread 1.

**Lawson, Jamie, and Darren Langdridge. "History, Culture and Practice of Puppy Play." *Sexualities* 23, no. 4 (2020): 574–590. DOI: 10.1177/1363460719839914.** *(Theoretical/historical; foundational for subculture genealogy.)* Argues pup play is best understood sociologically as a "postmodern subculture" and applies Irwin's (1973) model of scene evolution; crucially traces pup play's historical roots to the highly sexual gay leatherman subculture and analyzes the social/sexual split among practitioners through technologies-of-the-self, sexual-citizenship, and BDSM theory. The key bridging text between threads 1 and 2. **Note:** Lawson (University of Bristol) is the anthropologist the original brief likely intended when it named "Mair Underwood" — Underwood's work is on bodybuilding, not pup play.

**Langdridge, Darren, and Jamie Lawson. "The Psychology of Puppy Play: A Phenomenological Investigation." *Archives of Sexual Behavior* 48, no. 7 (2019): 2201–2215. DOI: 10.1007/s10508-019-01476-1.** *(Empirical; phenomenological.)* A phenomenological study (anthropological fieldwork at fetish fairs and pup events, plus online observation) of the lived experience of pup play, emphasizing well-being, mindfulness, and community alongside the dominance/submission frame. Threads 1 and 2.

**Hammack, Phillip L., and Liam Wignall. "'Be Dog Have Fun': Narratives of Discovery, Meaning, and Motivation among Members of the Pup Subculture." *Sexuality & Culture* 28 (2024): 2537–2556. DOI: 10.1007/s12119-024-10242-y.** *(Empirical; recent. Correction published at DOI 10.1007/s12119-024-10253-9.)* Analyzes open-ended survey responses from 568 pup-identified people (mean age 31.2), identifying four themes: social-technology-driven discovery; constructionist identity development through relationships and other kink communities; individual psychological benefits (stress relief, relaxation, pleasure); and social-psychological benefits in identity/community building. Thread 1.

**Joy, Phillip, Kinda Wassef, Olivia Bonardi, Megan Aston, and Olivier Ferlatte. "Beyond Barks and Bodies: Pup Play and Body Image in Gay, Bi, Trans, and Queer Men through a Deleuzian Lens." *The Canadian Journal of Human Sexuality* 33, no. 2 (2024): 277–291. DOI: 10.3138/cjhs-2023-0042.** *(Empirical; recent; intersectional.)* Uses cellphilming and discourse analysis with 17 GBTQ pups to explore how pup identity reshapes body image — finding that pup gear, hoods, and spaces foster self-acceptance, playfulness, and freedom while also reinforcing hegemonic body standards. The strongest current bridge between pup play and gay embodiment/masculinity (threads 1 and 3), and the principal node of Francophone-adjacent (Montréal-based) scholarship.

## CLUSTER 2 — BDSM / KINK / LEATHER

**Rubin, Gayle. "Thinking Sex: Notes for a Radical Theory of the Politics of Sexuality." In *Pleasure and Danger: Exploring Female Sexuality*, edited by Carole S. Vance, 267–319. Boston: Routledge & Kegan Paul, 1984.** *(Theoretical; canonical.)* The most cited single text in sexuality studies; proposes the "charmed circle" / hierarchy of sexual value and argues for a radical theory of sexual politics partially autonomous from feminism. The conceptual foundation for the depathologization and sex-positive turns. Threads 2 and 4.

**Rubin, Gayle. "The Catacombs: A Temple of the Butthole." In *Leatherfolk: Radical Sex, People, Politics, and Practice*, edited by Mark Thompson, 119–141. Boston: Alyson Publications, 1991.** *(Ethnographic/historical; canonical.)* Ethnographic and historical account of the San Francisco fisting/SM club the Catacombs (1975–1981), central to documenting gay male leather culture at its pre-AIDS height. Threads 2 and 3.

**Rubin, Gayle. *Deviations: A Gayle Rubin Reader.* Durham, NC: Duke University Press, 2011.** *(Collected works; canonical.)* Gathers "Thinking Sex," "The Leather Menace," "The Catacombs," "Of Catamites and Kings," and reflective afterwords; the central single-volume Rubin source. Note: Rubin's PhD dissertation, *The Valley of the Kings: Leathermen in San Francisco, 1960–1990* (University of Michigan, 1994), remains an unpublished but frequently cited ethnographic/historical study. Threads 2, 3, 4.

**Weiss, Margot. *Techniques of Pleasure: BDSM and the Circuits of Sexuality.* Durham, NC: Duke University Press, 2011.** *(Ethnographic; foundational/contemporary. Winner, 2012 Ruth Benedict Prize.)* Ethnography at dungeon play parties, workshops, and Society of Janus business meetings, plus interviews with more than sixty SM practitioners. Argues SM is not inherently transgressive but a technique- and consumption-oriented "circuit" linking self-mastery, expertise, and community — one that can re-instantiate gendered, racialized, and national inequalities. Threads 2 and 4.

**Newmahr, Staci. *Playing on the Edge: Sadomasochism, Risk, and Intimacy.* Bloomington: Indiana University Press, 2011.** *(Ethnographic; foundational.)* Four years of in-depth participant observation in a pansexual SM community; frames SM as serious leisure and a recreational risk-taking endeavor (edgework), in which pain is reframed as something given/received rather than as harm. Threads 2 and 4.

**Bauer, Robin. *Queer BDSM Intimacies: Critical Consent and Pushing Boundaries.* New York: Palgrave Macmillan, 2014. ISBN 978-1-137-43501-9.** *(Empirical/theoretical; contemporary.)* Interview study of the dyke+/queer/trans* BDSM community; theorizes "critical consent," boundary-pushing, and the capacity of BDSM to queer gender and create new intimacies and embodiments. The major queer/trans counterpoint to Weiss's and Newmahr's largely heterosexual scenes. Threads 2 and 4.

**Langdridge, Darren, and Meg-John Barker, eds. *Safe, Sane and Consensual: Contemporary Perspectives on Sadomasochism.* Basingstoke: Palgrave Macmillan, 2007 (2nd ed., 2013, ISBN 9781137338372).** *(Edited collection; foundational.)* Landmark multidisciplinary volume spanning history/culture, medical and legal issues, theory, empirical research, therapy, and the academic/activist divide. Contains Sisson on the cultural formation of S/M, Moser & Kleinplatz on pathologization, and Bauer on BDSM's potential to queer genders; anchor text for the SSC/RACK consent-framework debate. Threads 2 and 4.

**Moser, Charles, and Peggy J. Kleinplatz. "DSM-IV-TR and the Paraphilias: An Argument for Removal." *Journal of Psychology & Human Sexuality* 17, no. 3/4 (2005): 91–109.** *(Conceptual/clinical; foundational for depathologization.)* Argues that the paraphilia category fails the DSM's own criteria for a mental disorder; pivotal in the depathologization debate, explicitly analogized by the authors to the 1970s removal of homosexuality. See also Moser & Kleinplatz, "Conceptualization, History, and Future of the Paraphilias," *Annual Review of Clinical Psychology* 16 (2020): 379–399, DOI 10.1146/annurev-clinpsy-050718-095548. Threads 2 and 4.

**Califia, Pat (Patrick). *Public Sex: The Culture of Radical Sex.* Pittsburgh: Cleis Press, 1994 (2nd ed. 2000).** *(Essays/activist-theoretical; canonical primary source.)* Collected essays defending lesbian S/M, leather culture, and radical sex; a key primary document of the feminist sex wars and the leatherdyke/gay-male-leather interface. Califia co-founded Samois on June 13, 1978, with Gayle Rubin and others. **Caveat:** some of Califia's positions on age of consent are highly contested and should be cited with explicit critical framing. Threads 2 and 4.

**Mains, Geoff. *Urban Aboriginals: A Celebration of Leathersexuality.* San Francisco: Gay Sunshine Press, 1984 (reprint, Los Angeles: Daedalus Publishing, 2002, ISBN 9781881943181).** *(Participant-observation / popular-theoretical; canonical primary source.)* Early hybrid of anthropology, biochemistry, and participant observation theorizing gay male leather/SM as a quasi-tribal subculture and introducing endorphins as a physiological dimension of SM experience. Threads 2 and 3.

**Oosterhuis, Harry. "Sexual Modernity in the Works of Richard von Krafft-Ebing and Albert Moll." *Medical History* 56, no. 2 (2012): 133–155. DOI: 10.1017/mdh.2011.30.** *(History of medicine; foundational for the pathologization narrative.)* Argues that Krafft-Ebing (who coined "sadism" and "masochism") and Moll reframed the perversions from discrete pathologies into graded variations of a continuous sexual instinct. **Correction:** Andreas-Holger Maehle co-edited *Medical History* 56, no. 2 and works on Moll generally, but did not author an SM-specific article; this Oosterhuis piece is the correct source for the history of sadomasochism's pathologization. Threads 2 and 4.

## CLUSTER 3 — GAY MASCULINITIES AND THEIR EVOLUTION

**Connell, R. W. (Raewyn). *Masculinities.* Cambridge: Polity Press / Stanford: Stanford University Press, 1995 (2nd ed., 2005).** *(Theoretical; canonical.)* Introduces hegemonic, subordinate, complicit, and marginalized masculinities; positions gay masculinity as "subordinate" within the gender order. The indispensable theoretical anchor for the masculinities thread. Thread 3.

**Connell, R. W., and James W. Messerschmidt. "Hegemonic Masculinity: Rethinking the Concept." *Gender & Society* 19, no. 6 (2005): 829–859. DOI: 10.1177/0891243205278639.** *(Theoretical; canonical revision.)* Reformulates hegemonic masculinity in response to a decade of critique, emphasizing geographies of masculinity, embodiment, and the capacity of subordinated masculinities to influence dominant forms. Thread 3.

**Levine, Martin P. *Gay Macho: The Life and Death of the Homosexual Clone.* Edited by Michael Kimmel. New York: NYU Press, 1998.** *(Ethnographic; canonical/foundational.)* Posthumously published ethnography of the 1970s gay "clone" — the muscular, hypermasculine, sexually free urban gay man — documenting the radical shift from the "invert" model to a masculine ethos, and the clone's collision with AIDS. Essential text on the masculine turn in gay culture. Threads 2 and 3.

**Halperin, David M. *How to Be Gay.* Cambridge, MA: Belknap Press of Harvard University Press, 2012.** *(Theoretical/cultural; canonical.)* Argues that gayness is a learned cultural practice and subjectivity — style, camp, melodrama, diva worship, "counteracculturation" — not reducible to sexual orientation; theorizes gay male femininity as a distinctive gender formation. Key counterweight to the masc/assimilationist trajectory. Threads 3 and 4.

**Anderson, Eric. *Inclusive Masculinity: The Changing Nature of Masculinities.* New York: Routledge, 2009.** *(Empirical/theoretical; contemporary.)* Proposes Inclusive Masculinity Theory (IMT): as cultural homophobia ("homohysteria") declines, younger men construct softer, less homophobic masculinities. See also Anderson & McCormack, "Inclusive Masculinity Theory: Overview, Reflection and Refinement," *Journal of Gender Studies* 27, no. 5 (2018): 547–561, DOI 10.1080/09589236.2016.1245605. **Note:** IMT is contested — see Bridges & Pascoe. Thread 3.

**Bridges, Tristan, and C. J. Pascoe. "Hybrid Masculinities: New Directions in the Sociology of Men and Masculinities." *Sociology Compass* 8, no. 3 (2014): 246–258. DOI: 10.1111/soc4.12134.** *(Theoretical/review; contemporary.)* Theorizes "hybrid masculinities" — privileged men's selective incorporation of elements associated with marginalized and subordinated masculinities (including gay masculinities) in ways that can obscure and ultimately reproduce inequality. Key corrective to inclusive-masculinity optimism. Threads 3 and 4.

**Hennen, Peter. *Faeries, Bears, and Leathermen: Men in Community Queering the Masculine.* Chicago: University of Chicago Press, 2008. DOI: 10.7208/9780226327297.** *(Ethnographic; THE hinge text; foundational/canonical.)* Comparative ethnography of Radical Faeries, Bears, and Leathermen, examining how each community negotiates the stigma of effeminacy attached to male homosexuality. Hennen argues Faeries deploy a "gender pastiche," Bears claim an "authentic," working-class masculinity, and Leathermen build a BDSM sexual pedagogy that both pays homage to and subverts hypermasculinity. The crucial bridge across all four threads — and directly relevant to [[Masculinités et ritualités — La magie chez les Radical Faeries]]. Critics note its under-theorization of race in these predominantly white communities.

**Ward, Jane. *Not Gay: Sex Between Straight White Men.* Sexual Cultures series. New York: NYU Press, 2015. DOI: 10.18574/nyu/9781479860685.001.0001.** *(Cultural analysis; contemporary.)* Analyzes hazing rituals, personal ads, and tearoom encounters to argue that straight white men's same-sex contact reaffirms heterosexual, white, masculine identity, and that heterosexuality is itself a cultural construction. Threads 3 and 4.

**Butler, Judith. *Gender Trouble: Feminism and the Subversion of Identity.* New York: Routledge, 1990.** *(Theoretical; canonical.)* The theory of gender performativity that underpins queer readings of masculinity, drag, and BDSM role-play throughout this literature — explicitly mobilized by Hennen and Bauer. Threads 3 and 4.

## CLUSTER 4 — INTERSECTIONAL / BRIDGING SCHOLARSHIP

**Foucault, Michel. "Sex, Power, and the Politics of Identity" (1982 interview) and related late interviews, in *Ethics: Subjectivity and Truth* (Essential Works of Foucault 1954–1984, Vol. 1), edited by Paul Rabinow. New York: New Press, 1997. See also *The History of Sexuality, Vol. 1: An Introduction* (1976; trans. 1978).** *(Theoretical; canonical.)* Foucault frames S/M as "the real creation of new possibilities of pleasure" and as "the desexualization of pleasure" — an ascetic, creative practice of the self. Key primary source for queer-theoretical readings of BDSM as *ascesis*. Lawson & Langdridge (2020) explicitly deploy technologies-of-the-self to explain the pup-play social/sexual split, making this the clearest line connecting Foucault, BDSM, and pup play. Threads 1, 2, 4.

**Han, C. Winter (Chong-suk). *Geisha of a Different Kind: Race and Sexuality in Gaysian America.* New York: NYU Press, 2015. DOI: 10.18574/nyu/9781479831951.001.0001.** *(Ethnographic; contemporary; intersectional.)* Argues that gay Asian American men are doubly marginalized — feminized within a white-centric gay community and sidelined within a heteronormative Asian American community. Central text on sexual racism and gay masculinity. See also Han, *Racial Erotics* (University of Washington Press, 2021). Threads 3 and 4.

**Thompson, Mark, ed. *Leatherfolk: Radical Sex, People, Politics, and Practice.* Boston: Alyson Publications, 1991.** *(Edited collection / primary source; canonical.)* Foundational community-scholarly anthology on leather culture, containing Rubin's "Catacombs" essay; key bridge between leather identity, gay masculinity, and SM practice. Threads 2 and 3.

## Recommendations

- **Core cross-cutting reading list (five texts):** Hennen (2008), Rubin's *Deviations* (2011), Weiss (2011), Levine (1998), Wignall (2022). These five cover leather history, gay-masculine embodiment, kink ethnography, and pup play.
- **Frame pup play as a sub-branch, not a silo.** Treat pup play as an offshoot of leather/BDSM scholarship; Lawson & Langdridge (2020) supply the genealogical connective tissue tying pup play to the leatherman subculture and to Foucauldian technologies of the self.
- **Target the thinnest seam.** The masculinity-and-animality/dehumanization-play angle is genuinely under-theorized: Joy et al. (2024) and Hennen's leatherman chapter are the closest anchors. A graduate-level intervention reading pup "headspace" and animality against Butlerian performativity, Foucauldian ascesis, and Connell/Bridges-style masculinity theory would fill a real gap.
- **Handle contested sources explicitly.** Cite Califia with critical contextualization; present Anderson's IMT alongside Bridges & Pascoe rather than as settled.

## Caveats

- **Mair Underwood does not belong in the pup-play literature.** Underwood (University of Queensland) works on bodybuilding and image/performance-enhancing drugs. The anthropologist central to this literature is **Jamie Lawson** (University of Bristol).
- **Maehle did not write an SM-specific history.** Use Oosterhuis (2012, *Medical History*) for the Krafft-Ebing/Moll pathologization narrative.
- **Verification limits.** Several works were confirmed via publisher/library metadata rather than full-text reading. Specific page ranges for chapters within edited volumes should be checked before final citation.
- **Date convention.** Wignall, Moseley & McCormack appears as both 2023 (online-first) and 2025 (print volume 62[3]) under the same DOI; cite per your style guide.
- **Francophone scholarship is sparse.** Beyond Joy et al. (2024) from Montréal, little dedicated Francophone work surfaced; a targeted search of Cairn.info and *Genre, sexualité & société* would be the logical next step.
