# The ethnographer and the algorithm — Christin 2020 (Beyond the black box)

## Summary
Angèle Christin argues that “black box” opacity should not only be bypassed through input/output audits or transparency demands: researchers can **enroll algorithms directly in ethnographic research**. She proposes a practical toolkit of three meso-level strategies—**algorithmic refraction, comparison, triangulation**—to study algorithms as sociotechnical assemblages while also learning from how opacity itself is produced.

## Key Ideas (vault-usable)
- Opacity is not just a property of code; it is reconfigured at the seams between software, people, and institutions.
- Three strategies:
  - **Algorithmic refraction:** study what changes when algorithms meet institutional workflows and human practice.
  - **Algorithmic comparison:** similarity/difference designs to surface what is specific about an algorithmic system.
  - **Algorithmic triangulation:** use algorithms as instruments to gather richer qualitative data (without conceding epistemic authority to them).
- Situates algorithmic audits and technical documentation (e.g., model cards) as useful but limited; warns against re-enacting “black box” ontology by treating internals as unknowable.

## Why it matters here
- Direct method bridge between governance claims (accountability, transparency) and research practice: how to generate evidence about algorithmic systems that is not reducible to vendor narratives or single metrics.
- Pairs tightly with [[Privacy as Contextual Integrity — Nissenbaum 2004 (Public Surveillance)]] and with PHAROS’s insistence that operational truth lives in process, not claims.
