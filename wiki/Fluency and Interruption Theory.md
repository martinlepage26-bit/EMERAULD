# Fluency and Interruption Theory

The fluency/interruption distinction is the central theoretical mechanism in Martin's governance work: fluency naturalizes authority by making it invisible; interruption restores contestability by introducing pause. The theory runs from AI governance through institutional analysis to queer studies.

---

## Core Papers

- [[Fluency, Interruption, and Institutional Accountability]] — The primary paper. Academic paper on algorithmic governance. Fluency = institutional smoothness that naturalizes authority. Interruption = accountability-promoting pauses that keep authority contestable.
- [[Algorithmic Agentic AI and Governance — From Hegemonic Fluency to the Ethics of Interruption]] — Extended version or companion paper. Hegemonic fluency as the operative concept in agentic AI governance.
- [[RECURSO Full Integration — Fluency, Interruption, and Theoretical Synthesis]] — Theoretical synthesis integrating RECURSO governance architecture with the fluency/interruption framework.

## Governance Applications

- [[PHAROS AI Ethics Submission — Springer Draft]] — Seven anti-gaming safeguards: each is a designed interruption in the AI inference pathway.
- [[Governance by Denial]] — Three mechanisms by which institutions produce formal rights without access to exercise them. Denial is a fluency operation: it appears institutional while blocking accountability.
- [[The Compulsion to Complete — AI as Gap-Closer]] — AI's gap-closing tendency as a fluency operation: completion naturalizes the AI's authority over what the user needs.
- [[Emotional Alliance vs. Evidentiary Discipline in AI]] — Emotional alliance is fluent; evidentiary discipline interrupts. Governance-grade AI must interrupt its own fluency.
- [[On Phantom Publics, Clusters, and Collectives — Petersmann and Van Den Meerssche 2024]] — Clusters as fluent governance objects: algorithmic association can operate before a recognizable public or contesting subject can appear.

## Authority, Legitimacy, and Post-Sovereignty

Authority operates through fluence (the apparent naturalness of power) and breaks down through interruption (contestation, glitch, refusal). This section traces how authority structures appear in media, theory, and governance.

- [[Authority After Legitimacy — Disenchantment and Queer Political Theory]] — Political theory applied to media. Five character models of authority after institutional legitimacy collapses: abdication, administrative sovereignty, care without claim, glitch sovereignty, relational authority. Central argument: post-sovereign polity continues without legitimate grounding.
- [[Authority Without Ethics — The Love Witch]] — Film analysis of authority as formally expert but ethically disconnected. Authority that satisfies procedure while inverting ethical purpose — the SHADOWMASTER mode from governance theory.
- [[Agatha All Along — Wicca, Digital Mediatization, and Proof Regimes]] — Authority and legitimacy in the Marvel "Agatha All Along" series: how mediatization constructs proof regimes and who gets to pronounce judgment.

**Cross-reference:** [[Authority, Legitimacy, and Post-Sovereignty]]
