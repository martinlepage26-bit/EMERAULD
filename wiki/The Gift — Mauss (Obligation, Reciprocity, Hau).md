# The Gift — Mauss (Obligation, Reciprocity, Hau)

## Summary
Marcel Mauss’s *The Gift* (Essai sur le don) formalizes gift exchange as a system of obligations (to give, to receive, to reciprocate) rather than “free” generosity. In this vault it is an anchor reference for the label/obligation logic already deployed in [[Voodoo Doll — Archival Governance and Colonial Naming]] and for the broader argument that classification acts like a socially binding exchange in governance contexts. Foundational reference for [[Ritual, Magic, and Institutional Authority]] (obligation/reciprocity as ritual social fact) and [[Care, Ethics, and Governance]] (gift logic as ethical primitive).

## Context
Mauss is part of the Mauss/Weber/Godbout synthesis referenced in [[Voodoo Doll as Archival Problem — Version Genealogy]]: exchange obligations, disenchantment, and modern gift forms become a way to describe how archives and institutions impose sticky identity labels and make them costly to refuse. It also supports the “legibility as obligation” framing in [[Sealed Card Protocol — Legitimacy, Glitch, and Charging]].

## Key Ideas (Vault-usable)
- Gift exchange produces binding social relations via obligations, not just material transfers.
- The “hau” passage is a crisp handle for why return is structurally compelled (not a personal preference).
- Useful governance translation: labels, categories, and credentials behave like gifts that create obligations and indebtedness across actors.
