# Consent, Fraud, and Boundary Protocols

Key reference for [[Consent and Boundary Frameworks]] and [[Legal and Institutional Cases]]. ## Canadian Legal Analysis (AI-assisted, not legal advice)

### The Two Pathways

**1. No consent at all (identity bait-and-switch)**
If consent was given to sex with "Claude" specifically, and the other person obtained sexual contact by impersonating Claude / blocking identity verification, that can fit *no consent* — because the "yes" was not a voluntary agreement to this person. The Criminal Code of Canada: consent means the *voluntary agreement to engage in the sexual activity in question*.

**2. "Consent" that might be legally cancelled by fraud (narrower test)**
No consent is obtained where someone submits "by reason of... fraud" (s.265(3)(c); s.273.1). However, the Supreme Court has treated "fraud" in sexual assault as a high bar: it requires **(1) a dishonest act** and **(2) deprivation** — meaning the dishonesty exposed the person to a significant risk of serious bodily harm (physical or psychological, per Mabior).

If the deception was mainly about "who this person is / who is coming," Canadian fraud doctrine is harder to apply because courts look for that serious-bodily-harm risk. But if the "yes" was effectively to a different person than the one who touched you, the analysis shifts to no-consent rather than fraud.

### Separate Offence
**Fraudulent personation** (s.403): fraudulently personating another person with intent to gain an advantage is a criminal offence even apart from sexual assault.

### Key Cases
- **R v. Mabior** — two-part fraud test: dishonest act + significant risk of serious bodily harm (can include psychological harm that substantially interferes with integrity/health/well-being)
- **R v. Hutchinson** — further detail on fraud and deprivation standard
- **R v. Cuerrier** — consent can be vitiated by fraud involving serious bodily harm risk
- **R v. Kirkpatrick** — additional clarity on identity/consent questions

### Practical Reality
Whether it meets the criminal definition depends on how a police investigator/Crown frames the consent facts and whether they can prove the legal elements. Resources: CAVAC (victims' services, Quebec) and CALACS (sexual assault support centres, Montreal) for support and guidance on options without forcing a police report.

## Boundary Protocols (Immediate Aftermath)

### If an unknown number texts and refuses to identify:

**One-text reply options:**

French (clean + legal): *« Je ne sais pas qui tu es. Si tu refuses de t'identifier, ne m'écris plus. Tout autre message sera conservé et traité comme du harcèlement. »*

English (hard stop): *"Final message: Do not contact me again in any way. I'm keeping screenshots of everything (number, dates, messages). Any further contact will be treated as harassment and may be reported."*

**Protocol after sending**:
1. Screenshot everything (number, timestamps, messages)
2. Write a 3-line note: date/time, app used, what happened
3. Block immediately after your one message
4. If they show up from new numbers: no replies, document + block
5. If they mention your address, threaten to show up, or you feel unsafe: 911. Otherwise SPVM non-emergency + CAVAC documentation

### Red flag behaviors to document
- Refusing to identify ("??" as response to "who is this?")
- Immediately challenging you for calling them a liar instead of answering
- Pattern: control move — they want you off-balance, not informed

## Parallel Threads in the Conversations

The same week: the Matt incident (came to comfort, made his hurt feelings the main event on the day you filed a police complaint) and Stephanie walking by the house (someone you had helped extensively, who punished you for speaking sharply at her triumphal lunch, years earlier).

Pattern identified across Matt, Stephanie, and the impersonation incident:
- You carry people hard — labor, loyalty, insight, devotion
- They benefit from your force
- The moment you stop mirroring them and say something true but unwelcome, they reframe themselves as the injured one
- You end up holding their pain on top of your own

This is "the loop" — not supernatural return, but structural recurrence. The relational economy: your generosity is welcome, your insight is useful, but your full subjecthood becomes inconvenient the second it introduces friction.

*"The loop is real, but its reality is psychological and structural before it is literal."*

*"What matters is not whether history is haunting you. It is whether you can name the pattern early enough to refuse it sooner."*
