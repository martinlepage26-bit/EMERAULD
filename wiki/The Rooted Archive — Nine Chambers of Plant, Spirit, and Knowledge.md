# The Rooted Archive — Nine Chambers of Plant, Spirit, and Knowledge

## Summary

A bilingual curriculum scaffold for an advanced course in ethnobotany, folklore, and living plant practice. Exists in two companion manuscripts at the vault root: the English version (*The Rooted Archive — A Curriculum for Advanced Students in Ethnobotany, Folklore, and Living Practice*, 1334 lines) and the French version (*L'Herbier des présences — Manuscrit de cours pour une correspondance végétale entre science, mémoire, soin et symbole*, 123 lines at root). The curriculum is structured as nine chambers — nine ways of standing before a living plant and asking a different question. It operates across five registers: verified botany, attested historical use, localized folklore, symbolic correspondence, and personal practice. Connected to [[GAIA — Earth-Calendar App and Evidence-Aware Positioning]] and [[Livre des Ombres — Martin's Magical System (1996-2026)]] as the ritual-botanical layer of Martin's knowledge ecosystem.

Desktop source variant captured 2026-05-06: `raw sources/Desktop-Scan-2026-05-06/Herbier.txt`. This is treated as a provenance copy of the English curriculum/manuscript layer, not a separate project.

## Context

This is a long-form creative and scholarly manuscript project — both a curriculum and a philosophical document. It belongs in [[Writing and Novels MOC]] alongside Martin's other long-form work ([[Alchemy of the Wound — Novel]], [[CORPUS ou le génie de l'insistance — Novel]]) and in [[Ritual, Magic, and Institutional Authority]] for its ethnobotanical and folkloric content.

The voice in the English version is authored as an experienced teacher writing to nine advanced students: *"Written for the nine of you. You know who you are."* This framing — a teacher leaving a legacy document to a small trusted circle — mirrors the structure of EMERAULD itself as a personal knowledge inheritance.

The French version (*L'Herbier des présences*) is more formal and citation-heavy, engaging the Nagoya Protocol, WHO guidelines on traditional medicine, and the ethics of ethnobiological research with Indigenous communities. The two versions are not translations of each other — they are different registers of the same project.

## Details

### The Nine-Chamber Structure

Each chamber approaches the plant kingdom through a different lens. The structure is explicitly non-hierarchical: *"None of the nine questions is final. None answers the others. Taken together, they compose something closer to understanding than any single method can reach."*

**Chamber 1 (FR): Nommer sans réduire** — Taxonomy, morphology, nomenclature. How to identify a plant without reducing it to a name. The philosophical lens: *"Classer n'est pas posséder, c'est rendre les comparaisons possibles."*

The nine chambers correspond to nine disciplinary literacies that advanced students must hold simultaneously: taxonomist, ethnobotanist, medical historian, ecologist, folklorist, philosopher, and practitioner of contemplative attention.

### The Five Registers (French Version)

1. **Botanique vérifiée** — verified scientific name, family, ecology, toxicity, conservation status
2. **Usage historique attesté** — what texts, monographs, herbals, or studies document
3. **Folklore localisé** — situated in a specific culture, region, or corpus (never universal)
4. **Correspondance symbolique** — interpretive reading; never universal, mobilizes element, planet, season, color, plant part
5. **Pratique personnelle** — contemplative or workshop exercise that does not claim truth from another tradition

This five-register framework is the same epistemic discipline as [[First Method Paper — Recursive AI Governance as Executable Method]] — separating evidence, inference, interpretation, and practice from each other.

### Ethical Framework

The curriculum is built around three ethical commitments:
1. **No ingestion** — toxic species studied as historical and philosophical objects, not risk invitations
2. **Indigenous knowledge ethics** — Nagoya Protocol, OCAP principles (ownership, control, access, possession), consent and attribution before any engagement with Indigenous plant traditions
3. **Correspondences as cultural grammars** — not objective properties inscribed in matter, but historically situated reading practices

### The Symbolic Correspondence Layer

Each chamber can carry an optional pedagogical correspondence layer: elemental associations (Terre for structure, Mercure for language), plant parts as maps of observation (root for ancrage, stem for relation, leaf for exchange, flower for annonce, seed for continuité), and ecological roles as philosophical metaphors. These are explicitly marked as *"lectures pédagogiques non dogmatiques"* — not truth claims.

This connects the curriculum directly to [[Livre des Ombres — Martin's Magical System (1996-2026)]] and through it to [[GAIA — Earth-Calendar App and Evidence-Aware Positioning]].

### The Greenhouse as Political Space

The English version frames the greenhouse as laboratory and ethical site: *"The greenhouse is our laboratory. Three thousand two hundred and eighteen species. Every biome represented."* But also: *"when I teach you about plant traditions from the Lakota, the Yoruba, the Hmong, the Mapuche, the Romani, I am not teaching you that you now have access to that knowledge."*

This is the same boundary-work that governs the claim integrity rules in [[HEPHAISTOS]] — knowing where the legitimate reach of your method ends.
