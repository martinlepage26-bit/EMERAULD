# Poiesis Poietics Poetics Praxis — Making and Action Distinctions

## Summary

Poiesis, poietics, poetics, and praxis name adjacent but distinct layers of making and action. The distinction matters for [[AI Governance Manifesto — Upstream Institutional Practice]] because AI governance is not only a language problem; it is also a poietic problem of how technical systems, institutions, and conditions are brought into being.

## Core Distinctions

| Term | Clean gloss | Governing question |
|---|---|---|
| Ethics | What ought to be done | What should be done, refused, permitted, or repaired? |
| Poiesis | Making or bringing-into-being | What is being brought into existence? |
| Poietics | Theory or practice of the making process | What is being made, and by what process, conditions, operations, or generative logic? |
| Poetics | Forms, principles, and effects of expressive making | How does form carry meaning, attention, style, rhetoric, or symbolic force? |
| Praxis | Enacted practice in the world | How does thought become lived, applied, coordinated, or accountable action? |

## Poietics vs Poetics

Poietics leans toward generation, production, emergence, and the conditions of making. Poetics leans toward form, expression, structure, style, and effect.

For AI governance, **poietics** is the stronger word when the claim is that governance makes worlds, institutions, permissions, incentives, and operating conditions. **Poetics** is stronger when the claim concerns governance language, framing, rhetoric, symbolic form, or how a truth becomes perceptible.

## Relation to the Braided Discipline

[[Ethics Poetics and Praxis — Braided Discipline]] uses poetics for the form-and-meaning layer of responsible making. This note adds a missing precision: when the emphasis is on how a system, institution, artifact, or world is brought into being, the more exact word is poietics.

The expanded sequence is:

- Ethics asks what should be done.
- Poietics asks what is being made and by what process.
- Poetics asks how form carries meaning.
- Praxis asks how this gets lived, applied, or enacted.
