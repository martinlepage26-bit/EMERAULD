# Supply Chain Enforcement — Secondary Pressure on AI System Vendors

## Summary

EU enterprises face two enforcement mechanisms for AI Act compliance: (1) **primary pressure** — regulatory enforcement by national authorities (fines, orders, audits); (2) **secondary pressure** — contractual/procurement requirements from downstream customers who must conduct vendor due diligence on AI systems. For Canadian AI vendors and software companies serving EU customers, secondary pressure often arrives *before* regulatory enforcement and carries immediate commercial risk (lost deals, contract non-renewal, vendor exclusion).

[[Trust Advantage Analysis — Sales and AI Governance]] identified this mechanism; PHAROS outreach positioning can surface it explicitly, creating urgency without relying solely on regulatory deadlines. Reference for [[PHAROS Commercial Strategy]] (procurement/secondary-pressure as commercial wedge) and [[Governance Controls and Mechanisms]] (downstream contractual due diligence as enforcement vector).

## The Dual-Pressure Model

### Primary Pressure: Regulatory Enforcement (Aug 2, 2026)

EU AI Act high-risk enforcement begins August 2, 2026. National authorities (EU27 + EFTA) have audit and order-making authority. Violations carry penalties up to €35M or 7% of global turnover.

**Timeline:** Aug 2, 2026 onward.

**Who faces it:** EU-based AI providers placing high-risk systems on the EU market; non-EU companies with EU customers.

**Materiality:** Real, but enforcement ramp is gradual (national authorities will prioritize highest-risk systems first).

### Secondary Pressure: Vendor Due Diligence (Now – Ongoing)

EU enterprises with AI systems in their operations must:
1. Map their AI systems (procurement records, contracts, system descriptions)
2. Assess which systems are high-risk per Annex III
3. Conduct vendor due diligence on their AI system suppliers
4. Document evidence of supplier compliance with EU AI Act

This is not regulatory enforcement; it is **contractual obligation**. EU enterprises that cannot prove their suppliers are compliant face their own regulatory liability.

**Timeline:** Already happening (enterprises began this mapping in 2024, acceleration in Q2 2026 as Aug 2 approaches).

**Who faces it:** Canadian vendors selling AI systems or software with embedded AI to EU enterprises. Also applies to: consulting firms, managed service providers, integration partners, resellers.

**Materiality:** Very high. A procurement team can exclude a vendor immediately if the vendor cannot provide compliance evidence. No regulatory process required.

## Why Secondary Pressure Arrives First

### Procurement Cycle Timing

**From Trust Advantage:** 76% of B2B buying cycles are 3+ weeks.

**Implication:** Procurement committees are already sending vendor questionnaires to AI system suppliers. These questionnaires include:
- "Are you EU AI Act compliant?"
- "Describe your approach to high-risk AI governance"
- "Provide evidence of your compliance program"
- "Do you have a signed attestation from a third party?"

**Canadian vendor experience (Q1/Q2 2026):** A vendor receives a procurement questionnaire from a prospective EU customer. The questionnaire requires EU AI Act compliance evidence by a given date. Failure to provide evidence → vendor exclusion → deal lost.

This happens *before* Aug 2 enforcement. It happens *before* regulatory audits.

### Contractual Leverage

When a vendor cannot provide compliance evidence, the customer's procurement team can:
1. **Exclude the vendor outright** — "Your response is incomplete; we cannot move forward."
2. **Require remediation as a contract term** — "We will work with you if you can provide [evidence] by [date]."
3. **Impose contract penalties** — "If you are found non-compliant post-signature, we have contractual grounds to terminate."

All of this is *contractual*, not regulatory. No government involvement needed.

## Open Questions

**1. Vendor Questionnaire Standardization**
Different EU enterprises are sending different vendor questionnaires. Is there an emerging standard (e.g., UNECE, DAMA, Euroclear template)? If PHAROS can position as "aligned with the EU Vendor AI Assessment Standard," it gains credibility.

**Current answer:** Not standardized yet. But the questions are converging around Articles 9–17 (risk management, data governance, oversight, documentation). PHAROS can lead with: "Your vendor questionnaire will ask about these Articles; we map your systems to them."

**2. Contractual Risk Transfer**
If an EU customer's vendor questionnaire is answered by PHAROS on behalf of a vendor, who is liable if the evidence is incomplete or incorrect? This is a legal question that PHAROS outreach may need to address upfront.

**Current answer:** PHAROS deliverables are diagnostic and advisory, not legal opinions or compliance attestations. The vendor's C-suite signs off on the final response to the customer. Liability stays with the vendor, supported by evidence PHAROS helped gather.

**3. Timing: Vendor Questionnaire Response SLA**
How long do EU customers give vendors to respond? If the SLA is 3 weeks and PHAROS sprint is 21 days, does this fit?

**Current answer:** Yes. Average procurement cycle is 3+ weeks. PHAROS can message: "Your customer gives you 3 weeks to respond. We deliver your response in 21 days."
