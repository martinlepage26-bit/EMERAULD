# On Phantom Publics, Clusters, and Collectives — Petersmann and Van Den Meerssche 2024

## Summary
Marie Petersmann and Dimitri Van Den Meerssche's article in *AI & Society* analyzes how algorithmic governmentality remakes legal and social subjectivity. Using the virtual border as its empirical anchor, it argues that data governance increasingly acts on **clusters**: fluid, emergent, inferential associations distilled from distributed data points. These clusters weaken ordinary routes of recognition, representation, and collective action.

The article then refuses a simple return to liberal transparency and inclusion. It asks what legal imagination might look like if it stayed with opacity, fugitivity, and experimental collectivities instead of trying only to reinstall the autonomous liberal subject.

## Key Ideas
- **Cluster as fluid relational subject:** the governed object is not a stable person or demographic group but a temporary association inside a data/vector space.
- **Cluster as emergent composite subject:** categories are generated inductively through risk infrastructures rather than merely applied from pre-existing legal classifications.
- **Cluster as inferential subject:** governance projects future behavior through patterns, correlations, and propensities rather than past actions or causal knowledge.
- **Phantom publics:** algorithmic systems can produce populations affected by infrastructures without giving them the conditions for mutual recognition or political appearance.
- **Opacity as resistance:** the article treats calls for visibility, transparency, and inclusion as necessary but incomplete, because more visibility can also mean more extraction and capture.

## Why It Matters Here
This is a strong anchor for PHAROS because it describes a failure mode upstream of many ordinary AI governance debates: the problem is not only biased outputs, but the creation of governable subjects that cannot recognize themselves as a public.
