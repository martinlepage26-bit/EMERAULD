# Ritual, Magic, and Institutional Authority

Ritual is a governance technology. Magic is the deliberate adoption of illegitimate practices as a form of political and epistemological agency. Together, they reveal how authority is assembled, made legible, and contested — or denied contestation. This TOPIC organizes 95 papers exploring ritual and magic across ethnographic, media, and governance registers.

This is the ritual/magic branch of the PHAROS-adjacent governance graph. It sits beside [[Queer Sociology of Magic and Ritual]] as the scholarly fieldwork branch, and it links upward to [[Authority, Legitimacy, and Post-Sovereignty]], [[Care, Ethics, and Governance]], and [[Consent and Boundary Frameworks]] through the question of how ritual authority becomes answerable.

**Core claim:** Mediatization does not simply broadcast ritual practice; it transforms the conditions of proof itself. Digital platforms, TV narratives, and shared media objects become sites where authority mechanisms are made visible and contestable.

---

## Ritual and Proof Regimes

How ritual establishes legitimacy through proof — and how different proof regimes (oppositional, sensory, narrative) create different possibilities for authority.

- [[Social Compass Paper — Ritual Authority and Régime de Preuve]] — Academic paper on ritual authority in contemporary witchcraft. Introduces **régime de preuve** (proof regime): three types of proof operating in ritual contexts. Based on Montreal fieldwork and *Agatha All Along* analysis.
- [[Agatha All Along — Wicca, Digital Mediatization, and Proof Regimes]] — Digital mediatization transforms the conditions of proof in ritual authority. Analysis of three interlocutors (Jay, Helena, Manon) showing three distinct proof regimes operating simultaneously in the *Agatha All Along* narrative.
- [[The Ballad of the Witches' Road — Lyrics]] — Full lyrics from *Agatha All Along* (2024, Anderson-Lopez & Lopez). Ritual incantation corpus; text for analyzing proof-regime and communitas.

## Magic as Epistemology and Resistance

Magic as a methodology of self and world-making; the deliberate use of illegitimate practices against dominant epistemological regimes.

- [[Queering Neo-Pagan Magic — 2006 Paper]] — Ethnographic study of queer Pagan practitioners in Montreal. Magic as a queer methodology, not merely symbolic or religious practice.
- [[Queering Neo-Pagan Magic — 2026 Paper]] — Most theoretically mature version. Introduces **nomadic epistemology** and **tactical illegitimacy** — the deliberate adoption of illegitimate practices as political agency. Five interlocutors, Montreal-based fieldwork 2013–2024.
- [[Queering Neo-Pagan Magic — Identity Power and the Uses of Magic]] — Placing magic at the center as a queer vector of self and world-making. Engages epistemological refusal.

## Ritual in Media and Popular Culture

How television, film, and digital media represent and operationalize ritual as a governance technology. These papers argue media objects are not illustrations of existing theory but independent theoretical productions.

- [[Ritual Machine Lab — Legitimacy Machines Introduction]] — Book introduction and Chapter 1. Defines the **ritual machine** concept: a narrative system that produces authority through repetition and becomes invisible as it succeeds. Opens into Buffy analysis.
- [[For Her Alone to Wield — Buffy Power as Infrastructure]] — Slayer power as infrastructure — distributed, contestable, governable — rather than individual property. Enclosure → redistribution → fragmentation.
- [[Still Running — Willow, Anya, and Queer Ritual Infrastructure]] — Willow and Anya as posthuman ritual technologies. Characters as reusable procedural components grounded in Montreal queer/trans fieldwork (2020–2024).
- [[The Scythe Already in Motion — Buffy, Queer Ritual, and the Politics of Glitch]] — Post-canonical Buffy fragments in queer and trans magical practice. **Glitch** as media fragments becoming operational ritual technologies. Method: recursive ethnography in Montreal.
- [[Every Hair a Battle Scar — Buffy's Hair as Narrative Engine]] — Hair as a sustained visual governance text. Appearance as mechanism of legitimacy production.
- [[Prologue I Saw Willow Rise — Legitimacy Machines Opening]] — Autoethnographic prologue entering *Legitimacy Machines* through Willow Rosenberg as a ritual saturation figure.
- [[Magic After Legitimacy — Charmed and the Governance of Female Power]] — 2007 paper on **jester femininity**. Earliest operational use of legitimacy vocabulary in the corpus. Chapter II of *Legitimacy Machines*.
- [[Authority Without Ethics — The Love Witch]] — Ritual power and cultural life of witchcraft in *The Love Witch* (2016). What happens when ritual operates without care-as-answerability.
- [[The Familiars of Hollywood — Queerness, Whiteness, and Representation]] — *What We Do in the Shadows* as allegory for Hollywood's vampiric economy of visibility. Colin Robinson as whiteness; Guillermo as queer Latinx servitude.
- [[Reboot — Performance, Gender, and Computing of Identity]] — *ReBoot* (1994) through performativity, dispositif, and cyborg manifesto. Shows are theoretical productions, not illustrations.

## Authority After Institutional Legitimacy

When the institutions that grounded authority are no longer believable — what forms of authority become possible?

- [[Authority After Legitimacy — Disenchantment and Queer Political Theory]] — Five models of authority after legitimacy collapses: abdication (Oona), administrative sovereignty (Archdruidess), care without claim (Ursula), glitch sovereignty (Bean), relational authority without resolution (Mora). Disenchantment as a queer political text.
- [[Legitimacy Machines — Book Skeleton and Architecture]] — Monograph in progress: *Legitimacy Machines, Altars of Control: Governing Wonder and Training Power Across Screen Worlds*. Seven screen objects analyzed as ritual machines. Three movements: enclosure and its failures, redistribution without infrastructure, post-sovereign polity.

## Ritual Language and Incantation

How language functions as ritual technology; the structure of incantation and its power.

- [[Inductive Literary Discourse Analysis — Witchcraft in Song Lyrics]] — Analysis of 20+ songs (1942–2025, 8 genres, 6 languages). Four emergent categories: enchantment as desire/submission, witch as agency/fear, ritual language/incantation, sacred-profane ambivalence.
- [[Sociology of Modern Magic — Song Corpus and Queer Ritual Synthesis]] — Integrative essay synthesizing song analysis with ethnography. Applies Durkheim, Bourdieu, Turner, Foucault. Magic as moving boundary object between entertainment and ontology, norm and resistance.
- [[Recursive Entry Structure — Song Corpus to Governance Prompt]] — Cross-song structural analysis extracting base architecture; maps onto **régime de preuve**, HELIX, and communitas.

## Ritual and Institutional Power

How institutions use ritual to consolidate power; how ritual can be used to expose or contest institutional authority.

- [[Transmutation and the Body — Queer Ethics in Fullmetal Alchemist]] — *Fullmetal Alchemist* as state theology. Chapter V of *Legitimacy Machines*.
- [[Avatar — Lecture queer du pouvoir incarné]] — French and English versions (2023) of queer reading of *Avatar*. Body as curriculum. Chapter III of *Legitimacy Machines*.
- [[Unnatural Selection — Jurassic Park and the Posthuman Commons]] — Queer posthumanism applied to Jurassic Park franchise. **Posthuman commons**: the law cannot protect what it cannot name. Governance systems must include provisions for entities resisting classification.

## Reference and Theory Anchors

Foundational texts supporting ritual and magic analysis across disciplines.

- [[The Meanings of Magic — Bailey 2006 (Magic as Unstable Category)]] — Category hygiene anchor for magic/religion/witchcraft claims.
- [[The Golden Bough — Frazer (Magic, Religion, Regicide)]] — Upstream comparative ritual/magic genealogy.
- [[Queer Religiosities — Wilcox (Queer and Transgender Studies in Religion)]] — Comparative bridge for queer/trans studies-in-religion framing.
- [[In the Study of the Witch — Zwissler 2018 (Magic-Religion categories, gender)]] — Historiography of magic/religion categories through gender.
- [[The Gift — Mauss (Obligation, Reciprocity, Hau)]] — Foundational text on gift exchange as a system of obligation; ritual as binding institutional fact.
- [[L’esprit du don — Godbout & Caillé (1992)]] — Modern complement to Mauss; gift logic as a third regime beyond market and state.
- [[The Protestant Ethic and the Spirit of Capitalism — Weber (Calling, Asceticism)]] — Disenchantment thesis; rationalization as the displacement of ritual authority.
- [[Voodoo — Alfred Métraux (1959) — Haitian Voodoo ethnography]] — Haitian Vodou ethnography; ritual authority and possession.
- [[Anthropologie rituelle autour de la maladie et de la mort — Tourreil 2019 (Abomey, Bénin)]] — Beninese Vodun illness-and-death rituals; comparative anchor for Haitian cluster.
- [[Magic between Europe and India — Fárek & Horák 2021 (Mantras, spells, prayers)]] — Theological residue inside scholarly classification of magic across cultures.
