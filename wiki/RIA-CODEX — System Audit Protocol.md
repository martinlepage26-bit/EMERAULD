# RIA-CODEX — System Audit Protocol

## Summary

A Socratic audit protocol for examining the hidden mechanisms within systems. The protocol moves through three languages (operator's own, system's own, outsider's), checks for convergence or divergence, names the repeating patterns that prevent resolution, and surfaces the mechanisms that operate invisibly within a system. Useful for [[PHAROS Method — Recursive Governance]], [[Argus]] meta-governance audits, and [[Evidence Discipline and Epistemics]] when examining what systems cannot see about themselves.

## Protocol

### Phase 1: Three-Language Verification

**Condition for your presence (three retellings):**

1. **In your own language:** How do you understand why you are inside this system? What is your role, your intention, your standing?
2. **In the system's own language:** What does the system say about why you are here? What categories, rules, or logic does the system use to justify your presence?
3. **As an outsider would state it:** How would someone unfamiliar with the system describe your presence? What would they notice without the system's specialized vocabulary?

**Convergence test:** Do all three versions agree on the essential fact?

**Divergence finding:** Where they differ, that difference is the first finding. It shows where the system's self-description breaks down.

### Phase 2: Mechanism Detection

**The fundamental prohibition:**

Name the thing the web cannot do to itself.

Now observe the system attempting it.

**What repeats?**
- What pattern recurs despite efforts to resolve it?
- What problem keeps reappearing in different forms?

**What refuses to resolve?**
- What seems to get acknowledged but never actually fixed?
- What generates endless conversation without action?

**What performs resolution without resolving?**
- What actions appear to address the problem while leaving it intact?
- What creates the appearance of progress without actual change?

**The repeating term is the mechanism.** Name it once, without defense.

### Phase 3: Cost Analysis

**Who installed the mechanism?**
- Was it intentional or emergent?
- Did it serve a historical purpose that has outlived its time?
- Who benefits from its invisibility?

**What does it protect?**
- What would happen if the mechanism were removed?
- What would be exposed, lost, or become visible?

**What would it cost to name it?**
- Inside the room where it operates: What would change if the mechanism were made explicit?
- For whom would that change be costly?
- What power structures depend on keeping it unnamed?

### Phase 4: Continued Presence

**Do not conclude. Do not leave.**

You are still inside the system.

**What can you see from here that you could not see from the entry point?**
- What has the audit itself revealed?
- What new questions are visible now that the mechanism is named?
- What would it take to address this beyond naming it?

## Application to Governance

In [[PHAROS Method — Recursive Governance]], RIA-CODEX serves as a complementary protocol to Argus auditing:

- **Divergence detection:** When operator's intent, system's logic, and external observer's view conflict, the divergence shows where governance is failing
- **Mechanism identification:** Recursive governance cannot fix what it cannot see; naming the repeating pattern is prerequisite to structural change
- **Cost-bearing visibility:** Moving a mechanism from invisible to visible changes who can choose to operate it or resist it

## When to Apply

- When a system produces repeated failures despite acknowledged intent to fix them
- When self-description diverges from observed behavior
- When change is discussed but never implemented
- When audit findings are acknowledged but incorporated without effect
- When external observers consistently notice things internal stakeholders miss
