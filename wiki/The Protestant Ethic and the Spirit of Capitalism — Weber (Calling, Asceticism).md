# The Protestant Ethic and the Spirit of Capitalism — Weber (Calling, Asceticism)

## Summary
Max Weber’s *The Protestant Ethic and the Spirit of Capitalism* links religious ethics (especially ascetic Protestantism) to the development of a distinctive “spirit” of capitalism (discipline, calling, rationalization). In this vault it serves as a durable reference point for “disenchantment / rationalization” discussions and for explaining how moral frameworks become institutionalized as everyday practice. Foundational reference for [[Authority, Legitimacy, and Post-Sovereignty]] (the disenchantment thesis grounds post-sovereign analyses; see [[Authority After Legitimacy — Disenchantment and Queer Political Theory]]) and [[Ritual, Magic, and Institutional Authority]] (rationalization as the displacement of ritual authority).

## Why it matters here
- **Authority & institutions:** Weber is part of the Mauss/Weber/Godbout synthesis already referenced in [[Voodoo Doll as Archival Problem — Version Genealogy]].
- **Rationalization:** useful for describing how governance becomes *proceduralized* and why “legitimacy” can shift from substance to process.
- **Method:** a template for doing social theory by connecting ideas → practices → institutions (without collapsing into determinism).

## Key Ideas (Vault-usable)
- “Calling” as a moral engine that normalizes disciplined work as ethical duty.
- Asceticism as a driver for rationalized life conduct (and eventually institutional routines).
- Capitalism framed as an ethos (a “spirit”), not only an economic structure.
