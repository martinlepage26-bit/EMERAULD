# Every Hair a Battle Scar — Buffy's Hair as Narrative Engine

## Summary

Academic paper (**published 2025**, *Slayage: The Journal of the Whedon Studies Association* 23.2 [61]) by [[Martin Lepage — Professional Profile|Martin Lepage]] reading Buffy Summers' hair across all seven TV seasons and the canonical Dark Horse Comics continuation as a **visual text** inscribing the heroine's psychological and situational transformations. **Hair as governance text:** appearance as authority legitimation mechanism; micro-governance of female surface as control infrastructure. Part of [[Research and Papers MOC]], [[Historical Academic Portfolio — Pre-PHAROS Scholarly Work]], and [[Authority, Legitimacy, and Post-Sovereignty]].

**Note on source file date:** The source filename prefix `2007` is a Dr. Sort false extraction — likely pulled from Season 8 references (Season 8 launched March 2007) in the paper's text. The `notes_or_journal` suffix is a Dr. Sort format classification, not a publication-status descriptor. The confirmed publication date is 2025 (open PDF reported; name variant "Martin LePage" noted in the bibliography record).

## Context

This paper occupies a unique position in [[Martin Lepage — Professional Profile|Martin Lepage]]'s corpus: it applies the granular attention to visual surface — texture, cut, color — as a governance mechanism, anticipating the [[Sealed Card Protocol — Legitimacy, Glitch, and Charging]] concern with legibility and the [[LOTUS Model — Agency and Social Positioning]] analysis of visible vs. invisible social positioning. Buffy's hair is a charge-carrier across regimes (Witches' Road logic — canonical doctrine: [[Martin Walks the Witches' Road — Corpus as Charge-Persistence]]; see also [[The Ballad of the Witches' Road — Analysis]]); per operator authorial attestation cited in [[Mythocritique to PHAROS — The 2010 Master's Thesis as Methodological Keystone|the keystone]], the encounter ("I saw charge watching Buffy's hair, that's true") precedes the formalization of this paper — encounter-time predates composition-time. The paper connects to [[For Her Alone to Wield — Buffy Power as Infrastructure]], [[Still Running — Willow, Anya, and Queer Ritual Infrastructure]], [[Magic After Legitimacy — Charmed and the Governance of Female Power]] (Charmed parallel), [[Agatha All Along — Wicca, Digital Mediatization, and Proof Regimes]] (Agatha parallel), and [[Legitimacy Machines — Book Skeleton and Architecture]].

## Details

**Subject:** Buffy Summers' hair in *Buffy the Vampire Slayer* (1997–2003) and the canonical comics continuation (Season 8 onwards; full transmedia scope to be confirmed against the published 2025 text).

**Central thesis (twofold):**
1. Hair functions as a **narrative device within the diegesis** — shifting in tandem with the protagonist's psychological and situational states (Season 6 dark roots, Season 7 cropped practicality, etc.)
2. Hair functions as a **cultural artifact** negotiated between performer (Sarah Michelle Gellar), production team, network branding, and audience interpretation

**Theoretical framing:** Negra and Tasker's postfeminist media culture (1990s teen heroine under regulatory discipline); Bruzzi and Church Gibson on style as both character evidence and extradiegetic address to the audience. Hair as simultaneously evidence (in-universe truth about Buffy) and address (how the series wants viewers to see her).

**Under-theorized territory:** The paper notes that costume and set design have received substantial scholarly attention, but hair remains under-theorized except at moments of spectacular disruption (the "Felicity haircut" example). This paper stakes out hair as a sustained visual text, not a crisis moment.

**Scope:** Television run (Seasons 1–7) and the Dark Horse Comics continuation. As a 2025 publication, the scope of comics coverage is not constrained by the false 2007 Dr. Sort date — the paper may engage Season 8 and beyond. Actual transmedia scope should be verified against the published text. This is one of the few papers in the corpus to engage transmedia extensions at all, unlike [[For Her Alone to Wield — Buffy Power as Infrastructure]] which explicitly reads Fray and the full Season Eight arc.

## Key Ideas

- Hair as a form of visual epigraphy — inscribing the heroine's journey in texture, cut, and color
- Postfeminist surface style as simultaneously self-expression and regulatory discipline
- The "Felicity haircut" as example of hair's network-level power — a styling decision blamed for a ratings collapse, revealing that audience investment in female surface is governance-grade
- The extradiegetic dimension: hair tells us about Buffy in-universe AND tells us how the series positions its own address to viewers
