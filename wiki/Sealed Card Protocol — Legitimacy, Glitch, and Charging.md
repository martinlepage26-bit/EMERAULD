# Sealed Card Protocol — Legitimacy, Glitch, and Charging

## Summary
Academic journal manuscript analyzing AI authenticity controversies as a recurring governance problem in legitimacy and mediation. Central concepts: **glitch** (the moment mediation becomes inferable), **seam** (the adjudicative interface where judgment flips), **charging** (how credibility adheres or dissolves in circulation), **legibility** (regime-specific fit between artifact and proof expectations), and **legitimacy** (institutional conversion of recognition into enforceable credit). Three methodological arms: artistic ([[Wheels of Will — Ritual Bottle Art and Research-Creation Practice]] bottle photographs), academic (procedural autoethnography), and ritual (witch bottles across interpretive frames). Author: Martin Lepage, PhD (explicit in the appended publication record). Contains Martin Lepage's complete publication record as of March 2026. Governance artifact for [[Consent and Boundary Frameworks]] (charging as boundary enforcement). Related to [[Social Compass Paper — Ritual Authority and Régime de Preuve]] and [[Fluency, Interruption, and Institutional Accountability]].

## Context
Source: `00_Sealed_Card_Protocol_2004.docx`. The "2004" in the filename is a file index number, not the year of composition — the content references the EU AI Act status and belongs to 2025-2026. The paper positions itself at the intersection of ritual studies, media studies, and AI governance. The "Sealed Card" is a narrative device: a child becomes custodian of forces she neither authored nor fully controls, and the seal is a governance artifact that localizes blame onto a visible bearer regardless of where causation actually lies.

## Five Core Concepts

### 1. Glitch
The moment a viewer infers mediation. Not evidence of error — a **threshold event** that reorients judgment. Automation becomes plausible even when absent. Transforms perception into narrative and narrative into accountability.

The glitch is the tell — but not evidence in the narrow sense. It is the perceptual interruption from which all subsequent governance follows. Once it fires, evaluation migrates from surface to pathway.

### 2. Seam
The adjudicative interface where uncertainty takes form. Frontstage/backstage collapse (Goffman). The moment evaluative attention abandons the surface and settles on the pathway of production.

The seam is where proof regimes collide. It is not a judgment but a site — the point where the question shifts from "is this good?" to "how was this made, by whom, and with what authority?"

### 3. Charging
How credibility adheres in circulation. Anchors: residues, marks of time, procedural traces, relational endorsements, genre-disciplined structures. Charging is not a single act — it is an accumulation of material evidence of sustained engagement.

Charging resists metaphysical definitions (Magliocco on Pagan credibility): it is not about what an artifact *is* but about what it has *accumulated* through circulation. A charged object carries the traces of the hands that held it, the decisions that shaped it, the time it has been in the world.

### 4. Legibility
Regime-specific normative fit between artifact and proof expectations. Not clarity — **compatibility with the reading habits of a particular institution**. An artifact can be perfectly clear and illegible within a specific proof regime (e.g., sensory proof in a scientific evidence regime).

Legibility is produced by historically situated interpretive frameworks, not universal intuitions (Bell). The same artifact can be legible in one context and illegible in another — not because it has changed but because the proof regime has.

### 5. Legitimacy
Institutional conversion of recognition into enforceable credit under consequence pressure. Legitimacy is not the same as credibility or trust — it requires institutional infrastructure to convert recognition into something that produces consequences (resources, permissions, protections).

The mechanism: once mediation becomes inferable → evaluation turns from artifact to pathway → activates regime-specific legibility/legitimacy expectations → determines whether charging stabilizes or collapses.

## The Sealed Card Logic

In [[Sakura [[Cardcaptor]], ]]a child becomes custodian of forces she neither authored nor fully controls. Each capture amplifies potential harm while concentrating accountability onto a single visible bearer.

The seal is a **governance artifact, not a moral one** — a liability device by which institutions localize blame even when production, causation, and mediation stretch across opaque infrastructures.

**The political problem**: Amplification is systemic; accountability is individual. Generative infrastructures reproduce this pattern with particular intensity — sanctions land on the most visible human node (student, artist, author, practitioner) rather than on the infrastructure that made the artifact possible.

This is the AI governance version of the hidden labor problem from [[Fluency, Interruption, and Institutional Accountability]]: the infrastructure is invisible; the individual is accountable.

## Three Methodological Arms

### Arm 1 — Artistic: [[Wheels of Will — Ritual Bottle Art and Research-Creation Practice|Wheels of Will]] Bottle Photographs
Ritual bottle photographs analyzed as objects that carry stable material grammars (sealed mouths, bindings, residues, contents) while aesthetic optimization varies. How optimization triggers seam events by making mediation narratable.

Finding: The moment a photograph looks "too polished," the glitch fires — mediation becomes inferable, and the charging that accumulated through material residues is threatened by the optimization that made the residues legible.

### Arm 2 — Academic: Procedural Autoethnography
The workflow documented as a chain-of-production object. Traceability defined operationally as reconstruction capacity. Controlled variation: implicit pathway vs. explicit trace summary.

Finding: Traceability is double-edged. Under responsibility-oriented norms, it fortifies trust. Under purity-driven norms, it becomes incrimination — dependency rather than discipline. Institutions confuse normative bodily performance with epistemic virtue.

### Arm 3 — Ritual: Witch Bottles Across Interpretive Frames
The same object traverses: archaeological description, contemporary ritual practice, museum display, platform circulation. Incompatible evidentiary expectations across frames produce suspicion even when mediation is entirely traditional.

Finding: Demanding empirical proof of cosmological causation is a category error (Tambiah). The seam fires not because the object changed but because the proof regime changed around it.

## The Sharp Distinction the Paper Insists Upon

- **Epistemic harms** (fabrication, shallow synthesis, misrepresentation): require verification, source-checking, proportionate sanction
- **Legitimacy harms**: emerge when institutions conflate polish with deceit, assistance with negligence, unfamiliar tempos with lack of rigor

First category: risk management. Second category: governance mythology.

"If governance wants trust, it must govern for responsible practice rather than purity performance."

---

## Implications for AI Governance

The disability studies connection (Namaste, Kafer): institutions demand a particular tempo, bodily capacity, and narrative of struggle as evidence of sincerity. Generative assistance as a "legibility prosthesis" — helps reach standardized coherence required by audit cultures while intensifying pathway policing because clarity becomes narratable as shortcut.

The governance contradiction: institutions demand transparency and invisibility at once — transparency of mediation AND invisibility of optimization; transparency of process AND invisibility of struggle. This contradictory demand produces the purity narrative that the paper argues against.

## Insights

- The five-concept mechanism (glitch → seam → charging/legibility → legitimacy) is the most complete theoretical framework in the creative-governance archive. It provides a vocabulary for what happens at the institutional level when mediation becomes visible — not just in AI contexts but in any context where proof regimes and authority claims collide
- "Legibility as regime-specific compatibility" is more useful than "legibility as clarity." An artifact that is perfectly clear can still be illegible within a specific proof regime — this is why sensory proof (from [[Social Compass Paper — Ritual Authority and Régime de Preuve]]) cannot satisfy empirical proof demands: it is legible within the ritual community's proof regime but not within the scientific one
- The disability studies connection (legibility prosthesis) is the most important governance implication: if generative assistance is a prosthetic that enables participation in audit culture, then policing that prosthesis is policing access — not preventing deception
- The publication record reveals the governing thread: queerness as the refusal to cohere, applied as epistemology and politics. This thread runs through the ethnographic work, the creative work, and the governance methodology. The PHAROS method's bounded claims and declared gaps are a form of refusing to produce false coherence
