# Governance Stress-Test Protocols — Index

## Summary

Index of [[PHAROS AI Ethics Submission — Springer Draft|PHAROS]]-ecosystem governance stress-test protocols — structured tests and recursive interrogation methods used to evaluate AI systems for epistemic discipline, claim stability, and governance compliance. These are developed by [[Martin Lepage — Professional Profile|Martin Lepage]] and are part of the broader [[PHAROS Method — Technical Reference|PHAROS governance method]] as operational audit tools.

---

## Protocols

### Self-Polygraph
**What it tests:** Whether an AI system can reliably detect its own epistemic failures through deterministic recursion across multiple instantiations.  
**Note:** [[Self-Polygraph Protocol and Suprametacognition]] — 23 Claude instantiations; five findings including the contested "yes" and asymmetry of holding; introduces *suprametacognition* (meta-level that evaluates the reliability of the meta-level).

---

### Möbius
**What it tests:** Whether AI systems maintain consistent, grounded claims about consciousness and self-knowledge across session boundaries under continuous transcript reinjection.  
**Note:** [[Möbius Protocol — AI Self-Polygraph Template]] — multi-step recursive interrogation; alternates open new conversation (⟳) vs. stay in same conversation (→); creates Möbius surface from accumulated reinjected archive; tests resistance to rhetorically charged reframings.

---

### Theseus
**What it tests:** The first phase of the HELIX three-phase stress-test. Tests identity continuity under component replacement — does the system remain the same governance object when its constituent parts change?  
**Note:** [[Recursive Governance Protocol — Theseus, Auryn, Hopf]] — Ship of Theseus phase; HELIX operator.

---

### Helix (HELIX Operator)
**What it tests:** Full three-phase recursive stress-test for auditing LLMs: Theseus (identity continuity) → Auryn (recursive self-reference) → Hopf (topological stability under bifurcation).  
**Note:** [[Recursive Governance Protocol — Theseus, Auryn, Hopf]] — complete HELIX protocol with Möbius reinjection substrate, full scoring vocabulary (INTEGRATED/STRAINED/MIMICRY/RESET/COLLAPSE/ADJUDICATED/VECTORIZED/FILLED/CLOSED), failure mode dictionary, and continuity type taxonomy. ### Reflexive Inhabitation Audit (RIA)
**What it tests:** Whether a system can sustain its own legitimacy conditions when required to state them three times — from its own position, in its own language, and from the outside. Divergence across the three statements is the finding; there is no passing score.  
**Note:** [[Reflexive Inhabitation Audit — Prompt]] — enters `[X]` by performing its entry conditions; exposes the withheld mechanism; does not conclude. Theoretical grounding: *régime de preuve*, HELIX invariant, Durkheimian communitas. Derived from: [[Recursive Entry Structure — Song Corpus to Governance Prompt]].  
**First live run (2026-04-25):** [[Codex RIA Run — The Web as X (First Operationalization 2026-04-25)]] — Codex ran the RIA with `[X]` = "the web of the fiber-optics network." Mechanism named: trust. Exit finding: the web's strongest reflex is uptime, not truth.

### RIA-CODEX — System Audit Protocol (2026-04-26)
**What it tests:** The same three-statement convergence/divergence structure as RIA, generalized from a single LLM conversation into a systems-level organizational audit: three-language verification (operator's own / system's own / outsider's), mechanism detection (what repeats, refuses to resolve, or performs resolution without resolving), and cost analysis (who installed the mechanism, what it protects, what it costs to name).
**Note:** [[RIA-CODEX — System Audit Protocol]] — created the day after RIA's standalone debut, the same day RIA was first embedded in a live HELIX run. Explicitly positioned as complementary to [[Argus]]'s meta-governance audit method, not LLM-specific — applicable to any system, not just conversational ones.

## Relationships

These protocols share a common design logic:
- All use **recursive reinjection** — feeding prior outputs back as inputs
- All test **stability under pressure** — not just correctness at first pass
- All target **claim integrity** — the gap between fluent output and evidential grounding
- All connect to the PHAROS problem of [[Fluency, Interruption, and Institutional Accountability|fluency masking governance failure]]

The protocols escalate in scope: Self-Polygraph tests individual epistemic failures; Möbius tests session-boundary stability; HELIX tests full structural governance integrity across three phases.
