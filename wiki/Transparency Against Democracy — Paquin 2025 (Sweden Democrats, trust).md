# Transparency Against Democracy — Paquin 2025 (Sweden Democrats, trust)

## Summary
Stéphane Paquin (2025) examines a paradox: democratic accountability tools (strong public transparency + press freedom) can be strategically repurposed by radical-right, anti-establishment movements to amplify scandals, erode trust in mainstream institutions, and harden polarization — using Sweden (exceptionally strong transparency mechanisms) as the focal case. This is the cleanest contemporary instance the vault holds of the [[Governance by Denial|governance-by-denial]] mechanics applied in reverse: transparency itself becoming the channel through which institutional trust is degraded.

## Key Ideas (vault-usable)
- Populist radical-right rise is often explained by economic insecurity + cultural backlash, but Paquin foregrounds **political distrust** and how it is actively manufactured — the kind of motive landscape mapped by [[The Psychology of Conspiracy Theories — Douglas, Sutton, Cichocka 2017|Douglas, Sutton & Cichocka’s]] epistemic / existential / social motive families.
- Transparency mechanisms can be **weaponized**: scandals become raw material for anti-elite narratives, amplified by alternative media ecosystems and disinformation operations (e.g., troll factories); a [[Power in International Politics — Barnett & Duvall 2005 (Taxonomy)|productive-power]] case where accountability artifacts produce the very subject (the “corrupt elite”) that anti-elite politics requires.
- The core governance question becomes: do transparency and media freedom reliably strengthen democracy, or can they also create a predictable pathway for **institutional trust degradation**? Sits adjacent to the [[Strategic Silence — Maor 2016 (Reputation, Regulatory Talk)|strategic-silence]] argument: silence and transparency are paired governance moves, not opposites.

## Why it matters here
- A concrete case of “accountability tools becoming power tools,” aligned with [[Governance by Denial — Legibility, Capacity, Classification (Draft)|governance-by-denial]] mechanics (narrative control + impunity signaling + weak loops).
- Useful for PHAROS governance writing — see [[Paper 25 — The Pharos Frame - Four Levels Where Ethics Becomes AI Governance|Paper 25]] §10 — that needs to distinguish “more information” from “more enforceable accountability,” and reinforces the [[AI Ethics Literature — Contestable Governance Artifacts|contestable-governance-artifacts]] thesis.
