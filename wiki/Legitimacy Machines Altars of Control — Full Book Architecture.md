# Legitimacy Machines Altars of Control — Full Book Architecture

## Summary

Full book architecture and table of contents for *Legitimacy Machines, Altars of Control: Governing Wonder and Training Power Across Screen Worlds* by [[Martin Lepage — Professional Profile|Martin Lepage]], PhD (2025). Copyright registered. Extends and supersedes the skeletal version documented in [[Legitimacy Machines — Book Skeleton and Architecture]]. Part of [[Writing and Novels MOC]] and [[Research and Papers MOC]].

## Key Ideas

- The book is a unified argument across seven screen worlds that popular narrative trains power — not just represents it
- Each chapter is titled with a governing thesis embedded in the chapter title: "Discipline of Recognition," "Domestic Governance," "Body as Curriculum," "Administrative Farce," "State Theology," "Bioethical Altar," "Corporate Womb and Politics of Survival"
- The Coda is a "Research Spell Refusing Closure" — methodological refusal of the clean ending, consistent with [[The Circle Remains Open — Toward a Queer Sociology of the Sacred|the open circle figure]]
- The Afterword's ironic title ("If This Was A Bit Too Much") performs the book's anti-charm principle — [[Authority Without Power-Over|HEPHAISTOS binding principle #7]]: form buys no undue credibility
