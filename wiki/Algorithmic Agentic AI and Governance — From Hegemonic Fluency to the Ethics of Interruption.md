# Algorithmic Agentic AI and Governance — From Hegemonic Fluency to the Ethics of Interruption

## Summary

Research manuscript (2013/expanded) by [[Martin Lepage — Professional Profile|Martin Lepage]] analyzing algorithmic agentic AI as a **double-sided governance mechanism**: simultaneously intensifying surveillance and behavioral normalization while destabilizing inherited assumptions about authorship, legitimacy, and control. Approximately 9,000 words. Part of [[Research and Papers MOC]] and directly connected to [[Fluency, Interruption, and Institutional Accountability]]. Central to [[Consent and Boundary Frameworks]] (ethics of interruption as consent restoration) and [[Governance Controls and Mechanisms]] (interruption as control mechanism).

## Context

This paper bridges [[Martin Lepage — Professional Profile|Martin Lepage]]'s queer theory work and the emerging AI governance framework. It draws on the [[Governance by Denial]] framework, the "Stuttering Machines" vocabulary (connected to [[Beyond Fluency — Stuttering, Autoethnography, and Unstable Epistemology]]), and OECD data on algorithmic management. It anticipates the [[PHAROS Recalibration — Unified Governance Architecture|PHAROS]] method's concern with **upstream classification** — how AI systems reorganize who becomes audible, what counts as evidence, and how institutional sanction is delayed or displaced. **Algorithmic authority as fluency:** systems that present as smooth, neutral, and efficient hide the labor and authority structures they embody. The ethics of interruption proposes contestation as governance mechanism. The contemporary workplace is the paper's central laboratory. Indexed in [[Fluency and Interruption Theory]], [[Recursive Governance Theory]], and [[Authority, Legitimacy, and Post-Sovereignty]]; methodological substrate documented in [[Mythocritique to PHAROS — The 2010 Master's Thesis as Methodological Keystone]].

## Details

**Double-sided thesis:**
1. AI systems intensify surveillance, behavioral normalization, and the upstream organization of credibility by presenting control as smooth, neutral, and efficient
2. The same systems destabilize inherited assumptions about authorship, legitimacy, and control — AI is neither neutral instrument nor human substitute, but a **material arrangement** that acquires force through loops of training, adjustment, classification, and infrastructural repetition

**Central concept — algorithmic dysfluency:** Once the loops of AI training and classification are made visible, fluency ceases to look like a natural property of intelligent systems and instead appears as a **managed aesthetic** that conceals labor, latency, and uncertainty. This is where algorithmic dysfluency becomes analytically and politically important. Directly extends the [[Beyond Fluency — Stuttering, Autoethnography, and Unstable Epistemology|stutter/fluency]] framework into machine systems.

**Method:** Governed writing protocol, interpretive synthesis of cited sources, narrative interfaces drawn from cultural texts. Includes visual protocol schematic and two graphs derived from OECD figures on workplace adoption and invasive monitoring.

**Domains analyzed:**
- Recruitment and workplace evaluation
- Platformed surveillance
- Privacy dilemmas in support-group settings
- Hidden labor in AI supply chains

**Final claim:** Equitable AI governance will not be achieved by perfecting seamless automation. It will depend on institutions willing to make uncertainty audible, preserve contestation, and build **an ethics of interruption** into the conditions under which computational systems are allowed to act.

**Source materials:** OECD study of algorithmic management; [[Governance by Denial]] governance framework; "Stuttering Machines" vocabulary.

## Key Ideas

- Fluency as managed aesthetic: fluency in AI is not natural but constructed to conceal labor and uncertainty
- The ethics of interruption: governance requires building contestation into systems, not perfecting their smoothness
- Upstream classification: AI reorganizes who becomes audible and what counts as evidence before individual decisions are made
- The workplace as governance laboratory: algorithmic management makes visible the governance functions that were always present in institutional employment
