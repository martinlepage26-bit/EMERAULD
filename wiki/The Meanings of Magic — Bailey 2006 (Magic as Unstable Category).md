# The Meanings of Magic — Bailey 2006 (Magic as Unstable Category)

## Summary
Michael D. Bailey’s 2006 article (“The Meanings of Magic,” *Magic, Ritual, and Witchcraft*) argues that **magic is a profoundly unstable category**: it is often defined by authorities opposed to the practices it names, and the definition shifts across time and societies. The piece is the “category hygiene” anchor for vault work that touches [[Queerness and Transgender Identity — Lepage 2017 (Pagan Montreal, Wicca vs Reclaiming)|Wicca]], [[Authority After Legitimacy — Disenchantment and Queer Political Theory|ritual authority]], and popular discourse about magic — see [[Queer Sociology of Magic and Ritual]] for the cluster it sits inside.

## Why it matters here
- Provides a clean scholarly handle for why “magic vs religion” distinctions are contested (and why we should not treat them as timeless); aligns with the [[Agatha All Along — Wicca, Digital Mediatization, and Proof Regimes|proof-regime]] framing where category-stability itself is the governance question.
- Directly interfaces with the [[The Golden Bough — Frazer (Magic, Religion, Regicide)|Frazer]] / Mauss / Durkheim genealogy while leaning toward context-specific analysis; pairs with [[Is Sacred Nature Gendered or Queer — Becci & Grandjean 2022 (Eco-Spiritual Activism Switzerland)]] for a contemporary Swiss eco-spiritual case where the same instability appears in “sacred nature” framings.
- Useful when writing about contemporary Wicca/queer ritual practice without importing a condescending “magic” frame — supports the analytic posture in [[Social Compass Paper — Ritual Authority and Régime de Preuve]] and in [[Still Running — Willow, Anya, and Queer Ritual Infrastructure]].

## Key Ideas (Vault-usable)
- Classic typologies (Frazer/Durkheim/Malinowski/Mauss) still echo, but no single definition cleanly fits all contexts; cf. [[Inductive Literary Discourse Analysis — Witchcraft in Song Lyrics]] for a discourse-analytic move on the same instability.
- Productive shift: study how a society *draws the distinctions* at a given moment, rather than trying to fix a universal definition — a methodological cousin to [[Process-Tracing Methods — Beach & Pedersen 2019 (Mechanisms and Evidence)|process-tracing]] applied to category formation.
- Communal Wiccan rites are a modern counterexample to “magic is private; religion is communal,” reinforcing the Montreal-fieldwork point in [[Les fées dansent sur le Mont-Royal — Lepage & Gauthier (Anthropologica)]] and [[Queering Neo-Pagan Magic — 2006 Paper]].
