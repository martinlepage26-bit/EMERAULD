# The Keyport Domain of Crystal — Novel

## Summary

Finished novel, 76,699 words — 15 Parts, 73 chapters, plus **The Second Record**, a parallel-worlds fork between Parts III and IV. Court fiction in which governance is the physics: a jewelry theft traced back to a record altered not to document an event but to control how it could later be read. Completed 2026-07-31 as *governed fiction* — the full editorial audit trail is part of the work. Listed in the [[Novel Corpus Map]] and annotated in the [[Writing and Novels MOC]]; supersedes the "in development" entry formerly held under the Palace Under Root title.

## Context

The book is the fictional substrate of Martin's three-agent governance architecture ([[HEPHAISTOS|HEPHAISTOS Agent System]]). The kingdom is named Keyport; the novel demonstrates, over 73 chapters, why the governance tier of that architecture carries the name **Queen Keyport** — see Details. Its most protected character, Mira "Diamond Eyes," is the in-book embodiment of the [[Diamond-Eyes — Aesthetic Refinement Skill]]: *"She removed falseness from surfaces that could not afford it."* The novel's drift catalogue (adjacency, portability, false relation, derivative claim) is the narrative form of the drift vocabulary in [[Skill Domain — Governance and Method]], and its central question belongs beside [[Authority, Legitimacy, and Post-Sovereignty]]: what happens to legitimacy when the function of governance exists without its office.

**The structure.** Between Parts III and IV the narrative forks into The Second Record: the same days in a second Keyport, different people in the room, breaking off mid-sentence at the instant a room grants authority that no law allows — *"Granted," she said. Not by law. Not by title.* The coupling mechanism is governance itself: two worlds stay legible to each other for exactly as long as the decisions taken in them stay lawful. No character in either world knows the other exists; the reader is the only crossing. Three things look like errors and are load-bearing (README's "Do not fix these"): the mid-sentence break, the duplicate chapters 13–15 in the contents, and Castor's portability speech verbatim-echoed across both records with Clara's reply identical in each.

**Why Queen Keyport is different** (the reading recorded 2026-07-31): there is no queen in Keyport — two kings, one dead queen's wardrobe surviving as gossip. The governance function is real and distributed entirely among uncrowned women (Nadia, Aisha, Clara, Elena, Rhea, Mira); the kings sign, the women govern. Every attempt to give governance a body — the Witness raised above the child, the narrowing paper, the burden headings, the exchange menus — is the book's catalogue of disasters, and the Second Record dies at the precise moment the queen-function acts without the queen-office. Hephaistos and Hermes are named for gods (capabilities, embodiable); Queen Keyport is named for a **jurisdiction** — her identity is the domain she answers for, and she is the reason there is one version of the work instead of two records that no longer agree. KILLCRITIC's drift trigger #2 (routing before governance) is, in the novel's physics, the world-severing event.

**Mira / Diamond Eyes.** Never once wrong on the page, read by no one, and the book makes wanting more access to her the reader's own enactment of the drift it criticizes — every antagonist is someone trying to convert her nearness into availability. The author's verification function walking around inside the novel; the plot is what happens when people try to turn that conscience into a service.

**Provenance (governed fiction).** Assembled 2026-07-30 from the raw generation transcript (kept verbatim in `source/`); the abandoned first-draft Part IV was cut, then reinstated as the Second Record on parallel-worlds terms; a thirteen-iteration line-by-line continuity read of all 73 chapters completed 2026-07-31 (five inherited generation corruptions repaired with source-line evidence, six assembly-damaged lists restored, two name collisions resolved, three Word-markup defect classes fixed). `BUILD-NOTES.md` records every pass **including where each pass reasoned wrongly** — the audit trail is the version genealogy. The Palace Under Root texts (craft brief + short allegory casting the HEPHAISTOS architecture as a palace court) are preserved in the repo's `source/` and are not part of the novel.

**Submission package** (complete, in `submission/`): query letter declaring the double record in sentence one, with verified comps — primary comp *Trust* (Hernan Diaz, 2022, Pulitzer 2023: four competing accounts of one story, reader as adjudicator), register comp *Piranesi*, lineage *The City & The City*; full-spoiler synopsis; positioning as speculative literary fiction.
