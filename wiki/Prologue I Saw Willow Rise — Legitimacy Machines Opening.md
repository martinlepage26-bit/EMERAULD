# Prologue I Saw Willow Rise — Legitimacy Machines Opening

## Summary

Prologue or opening piece by [[Martin Lepage — Professional Profile|Martin Lepage]] (2025) entering the *Legitimacy Machines* project through a dreamlike fieldwork encounter with Willow Rosenberg — Buffy's witch — as a figure for queer ritual saturation, grief, and the cost of power. Part of [[Writing and Novels MOC]] and the [[Legitimacy Machines — Book Skeleton and Architecture]] project.

## Context

The file `2025 - Martin Lepage - Prologue I Saw Willow Rise.docx` appears to be a prologue for [[Legitimacy Machines — Book Skeleton and Architecture]] or possibly a standalone essay piece framing the book's methodology. It is written in a hybrid register — autoethnographic, literary, fieldwork — and deploys the same governing method as [[Ritual Machine Lab — Legitimacy Machines Introduction]]: art as epistemology rather than ornament. The piece is connected to [[Still Running — Willow, Anya, and Queer Ritual Infrastructure]] (Willow as posthuman ritual technology) and to [[The Scythe Already in Motion — Buffy, Queer Ritual, and the Politics of Glitch]] (queer Pagan fieldwork in Montreal).

## Details

**Form:** First-person autoethnographic prologue, written in the hybrid register of the book's method. Not a table of contents or theoretical introduction — this is lived fieldwork entering through dream and ritual.

**Opening image:** Walking barefoot in a field that smells like "the underside of wet stone, like lilac branches snapped in the heat of July." A wind moves through the grass and into the body before the skin registers anything.

**Central figure — Willow suspended:** "Something that used to be Willow. Something more." Suspended — held by the air, by grief, by the collective hunger of an audience who came to consume. Hair spilling like wet ink, eyes filled with "a darkness that is not void but density. A saturation. It's what happens when too much memory coagulates in one body."

**What the dream reveals:** This is not television. This is not magic as metaphor. "This is the spell as system crash." Willow's speech is language as atmosphere — the words not audible but the intent completely clear: "thick, resinous anger wrapped around the core of her grief, lacquered with a power she never asked for but now refuses to relinquish."

**Fieldwork context invoked:** Queer apartments with drying bundles of mugwort and hacked-together altars; Zoom circles where someone's voice shakes calling the corners. The prologue connects the dream Willow to lived ritual space — the cost of being seen in that moment is real in both locations.

**The writing act:** The author writes in the dream — with pen, stylus, or bone — with the urgency felt "the night we buried M. after the overdose." The prologue marks grief, ritual, and writing as co-constitutive.

## Key Ideas

- Willow as the site where too much power coagulates into saturation — the figure for the cost of queer ritual empowerment
- Dream as fieldwork method: the dream remembers what waking life cannot afford to say
- The spell as system crash: ritual failure and narrative glitch as epistemologically generative
- The prologue as methodological declaration: this book enters through body, grief, and dream before it enters through argument

## Insights

- The prologue establishes that [[Legitimacy Machines — Book Skeleton and Architecture]] is not a book about popular culture texts from a distance — it is a book written from inside the ritual machine, by someone who has been trained by it and who continues to carry it
- "Too much memory coagulates in one body" is a governing figure for the entire [[Research and Papers MOC|research program]]: the scholar whose body carries more than institutional structures are designed to hold
