# Global Publication Search — PHAROS Method and Variants

See also [[PHAROS SURFACE]].
See also [[PHAROS AI governance service business]].
## Summary

Research report documenting a global publication search for the phrase "PHAROS method" and its variants across public web, scholarly databases, and patent records. Key finding: the acronym PHAROS is already in use across multiple unrelated fields, and the specific governance framing used by [[Martin Lepage — Professional Profile|Martin Lepage]] has no prior public publication. Part of [[Governance and PHAROS MOC]] and directly relevant to the [[PHAROS Invention Disclosure]].

## Context

This document provides evidentiary grounding for the originality and naming-risk assessment of the PHAROS AI governance method. It sits alongside [[PHAROS Invention Disclosure]] and [[ANTHRO PHAROS — Anthropic vs PHAROS Governance Comparison]] in the intellectual property and positioning layer of [[Governance and PHAROS MOC]]. The search was conducted against the user-provided draft "Invention Disclosure (Updated) — PHAROS: Recursive AI Governance Protocol … v5.0 on 24 March 2026" as the reference point.

## Details

**Key finding 1 — Patent collision:** The most unambiguous published "PhAROS method" is a patent family (US/WO/CA/EP/KR/TW) describing **phytomedicine analytics for research optimization at scale (PhAROS)** for discovering and optimizing polypharmaceutical compositions. Patent abstracts repeatedly use the exact phrase "the PhAROS method comprising …" with verifiable dates, inventors, and assignee information. This is the highest-risk collision for prior-art confusion.

**Key finding 2 — Naming collisions (no single method):** Multiple independent publications use "PHAROS," "Pharos," or variants as the name of a framework, approach, architecture, system, or solver in: optical networking, indoor localization, robotics/ambient assisted living, remote sensing, and computational holography. These are real peer-reviewed publications but they are independent naming collisions — not a single recognized "PHAROS method."

**Key finding 3 — No prior governance publication:** For the specific assumption — PHAROS as a governance method (Recursive AI Governance Protocol) — no public publication matching that name/definition was found in public web + scholarly/patent surfaces during this search run. The only instance of that governance framing in evidence is the user-provided unpublished draft.

**Risk assessment:** Because "PHAROS" is already used in published patents and peer-reviewed research, the governance method name carries **high risk of discoverability confusion, brand collision, and false prior-art hits** in future searches.

**Governance comparison note:** The PHAROS governance protocol most closely resembles formal AI risk/governance frameworks (NIST AI RMF, ISO/IEC 42001, ISO/IEC 23894, OECD AI Principles) in intent — controls, accountability, documentation, continuous improvement — but is more operational and test-harness oriented than those frameworks.

## Key Ideas

- The PhAROS pharmaceutical patent family is the primary prior-art collision risk — same acronym, different domain, long publication history
- Naming multiplicity: PHAROS is not a field-specific term; it drifts across unrelated technical domains without accumulating as a single recognized method
- Governance originality: the recursive AI governance framing is original to Lepage's work at the time of the search
- Strategic implication: brand clarity and disambiguation from pharmaceutical/technical PHAROS usages is a pre-publication task

## Insights

- This search report functions as a due-diligence artifact for the [[PHAROS Invention Disclosure]] — it documents what the prior-art landscape looked like before publication claims were made
- The collision risk is less about legal conflict and more about discoverability: a reader searching "PHAROS method AI governance" will encounter pharmaceutical patents before finding the governance framework
- The governance analogy to NIST AI RMF / ISO 42001 positions PHAROS as more operational (test-harness, executable) than those frameworks, which is the competitive differentiator to document
