# How French Canadians became White Folks — Scott 2016 (Race in Quebec)

## Summary
Corrie Scott argues that 19th-century Anglo-Saxon racial discourse often positioned French Canadians as **“not quite white”**, and that historians have tended to elide this racial dimension of French-Canadian subjugation. The article traces how French Québécois identities became solidly lodged in **whiteness**, and why those shifts still structure contemporary sense-making about Canadian politics, Quebec’s dual majority/minority position, and racial discourse in the province. The Quebec-specific anchor underneath [[Martin Lepage — Professional Profile|Martin’s]] bilingual EN/FR governance practice and a counterpart to [[Mapping the Margins — Crenshaw 1991 (Intersectionality and Violence)|Crenshaw’s intersectionality]] in a Canadian register. Reference for [[Queer Theory Foundations]] (whiteness as analytic axis intersecting queer/race) and [[Legal and Institutional Cases]] (Quebec dual majority/minority position structuring institutional discourse).

## Key Ideas (vault-usable)
- Whiteness is treated as unstable/relational/performative rather than fixed biological fact (Critical Whiteness Studies frame); a [[Power in International Politics — Barnett & Duvall 2005 (Taxonomy)|productive-power]] case where the unmarked / universal category is itself an ongoing political achievement.
- “Becoming white” is a political and historical process: who counts as white is contested and strategically granted/denied — pairs with [[The Meanings of Magic — Bailey 2006 (Magic as Unstable Category)]] for category-instability as a general analytic move.
- Quebec’s racial assignment shifts still influence political identity narratives and how “minority” is framed; cf. [[Beyond Fluency — Stuttering, Autoethnography, and Unstable Epistemology]] for the autoethnographic move that lets the operator’s own position become a governance variable.

## Why it matters here
- Gives a Quebec-specific, peer-reviewed anchor for “whiteness as governance” claims (what becomes unmarked, neutral, universal) — the same operator-position-as-variable point declared in [[Paper 25 — The Pharos Frame - Four Levels Where Ethics Becomes AI Governance|Paper 25 §10]] (“the operator’s position is a variable”).
- Pairs with intersectionality foundations without collapsing race into a single-axis story; complements [[CDPDJ Complaint — Lepage v Calian and Novartis|the CDPDJ complaint]] as a worked Quebec institutional-accountability case.
