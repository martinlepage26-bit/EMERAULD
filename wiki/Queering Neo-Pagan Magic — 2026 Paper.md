# Queering Neo-Pagan Magic — 2026 Paper

Submission status: revision-complete MRW submission documented at [[Queering Neo-Pagan Magic — MRW Submission (2026)]].

See also [[Virtually Magical Literally — Queer Agency and the Digital Witch]].
See also [[Crossing Under Watch — Troll's Toll and Digital Ritual Passage]].
## Summary
Academic paper applying **nomadic epistemology** to the analysis of Neo-Pagan magical practice in Montreal. Full working title: "Queering Neo-Pagan Magic: Tactical Illegitimacy, Nomadic Epistemology, and the Politics of Efficacy." Author: Martin Lepage, PhD (2026). Five interlocutors (Jay, Maria, Manon, Helena, Simon) each represent a distinct epistemological position on magic's efficacy and authority. Central concept: **tactical illegitimacy** — the deliberate adoption of illegitimate, non-institutionalized practices as a form of agency against dominant epistemological regimes. Draws on Lamoureux's nomadic epistemology framework. Six-section structure. Related to [[Social Compass Paper — Ritual Authority and Régime de Preuve]], [[Agatha All Along — Wicca, Digital Mediatization, and Proof Regimes]], and [[Historical Academic Portfolio — Pre-PHAROS Scholarly Work]].

## Nomadic Epistemology (Lamoureux)

Lamoureux's nomadic epistemology: knowledge is not located in a stable foundation (an origin, an institution, a tradition) but in **movement between positions**. Nomadic knowers:
- Traverse multiple epistemological registers without settling permanently in any
- Derive authority from the capacity to move rather than from the stability of a fixed position
- Use epistemological mobility as a form of resistance against regimes that demand coherence and stability

**Application to Neo-Pagan magic**: Practitioners who are nomadically epistemological do not commit to a single framework for understanding magical efficacy (scientific, religious, phenomenological, relational). They hold multiple frameworks simultaneously, deploying whichever is most useful in a given context — not as inconsistency but as **tactical epistemological flexibility**.

This connects to the governing thread in [[Sealed Card Protocol — Legitimacy, Glitch, and Charging]]: "Queerness is the refusal to cohere" — the nomadic epistemological position is the enacted version of this refusal.

## Tactical Illegitimacy

**Tactical illegitimacy**: the deliberate adoption of practices that are marked as illegitimate, non-scientific, or non-institutional as a form of political and epistemological agency.

The term "tactical" draws on de Certeau's distinction between strategy (operations by powerful actors from stable positions) and tactics (operations by marginalized actors within fields they do not control). Tactical illegitimacy is:
- Not a deficiency (being unable to access legitimate practices) but a deliberate choice
- A form of refusal: refusing to measure oneself against dominant epistemological standards
- A source of community formation: shared illegitimacy produces solidarity among those who refuse dominant knowledge regimes

**The governance implication**: Institutions that treat all illegitimacy as deficiency — as a gap to be closed by training, certification, or compliance — miss the political function of tactical illegitimacy. Some illegitimacy is strategic resistance, not ignorance.

## Five Interlocutors

### Jay — Secular Scientific Frame (returning from earlier papers)
Positions his practice within a secular, scientific-adjacent vocabulary (quantum physics, neuroscience). Tactical illegitimacy operates at the meta-level: he uses the language of legitimacy (science) to make magic legible to himself, while the content (magic) is illegitimate to scientific institutions.

### Maria — New interlocutor
Maria engages magical practice primarily through aesthetic and creative registers. Magic for Maria is an art practice — it produces meaning and experience rather than efficacy in a causal sense. Her tactical illegitimacy is aesthetic: she refuses the demand that practices prove efficacy in order to count.

### Manon — Relational and Obligational (returning)
Magic as a network of obligations to spirits, deities, and community. Tactical illegitimacy: refuses both scientific and institutional religious frameworks, positioning the network of obligations as its own authority.

### Helena — Perceptual and Phenomenological (returning)
Magic as extension of sensory awareness. Tactical illegitimacy: refuses demands for propositional evidence, insisting that sensory experience is a sufficient and self-validating proof regime.

### Simon — New interlocutor
Simon practices magic in explicitly political terms — as a form of resistance to normative epistemologies. His tactical illegitimacy is the most explicit in the group: he chooses illegitimacy deliberately as a political stance, not as a practical compromise.

## Six-Section Structure

1. **Introduction**: Nomadic epistemology and tactical illegitimacy as theoretical framework
2. **Methodological Note**: Collaborative autoethnography; interlocutor introduction; research relationship description
3. **Figures of Efficacy**: Analysis of each interlocutor's implicit theory of what makes magic work
4. **Tactical Illegitimacy in Practice**: Cross-case analysis of how interlocutors navigate institutional illegitimacy
5. **The Politics of Nomadic Knowledge**: Broader theoretical claim about knowledge, mobility, and power
6. **Conclusion**: Implications for understanding spirituality in an age of algorithmic epistemology

## Algorithmic Epistemology Connection

Section 6 (Conclusion) connects nomadic epistemological practice to algorithmic epistemology — the way that AI systems (recommendation engines, content moderation, search) impose epistemological standards on users by rewarding coherence and punishing nomadism:
- Profiles that shift between epistemological positions are harder to classify and therefore receive less targeted content
- Communities built on tactical illegitimacy are harder to monetize and therefore receive less platform support
- The nomadic practitioner who holds multiple frameworks simultaneously is legible to algorithms only when one of those frameworks is foregrounded

**The governance implication**: Algorithmic epistemology produces pressure toward coherence — toward stable, classifiable identity positions — that directly conflicts with nomadic epistemological practice. This is the platform version of the proof regime conflict analyzed in [[Agatha All Along — Wicca, Digital Mediatization, and Proof Regimes]].

## Insights

- Tactical illegitimacy is the most politically specific concept in the Neo-Pagan work: it reframes illegitimacy as agency rather than deficiency, and provides a vocabulary for understanding why marginalized communities sometimes *choose* illegitimacy rather than seeking institutional recognition
- The addition of Maria and Simon to the interlocutor group (relative to the Agatha paper's Jay/Helena/Manon triad) reflects the ethnographic richness of the Montreal fieldwork — more interlocutors, more epistemological positions, a more complex picture of how magic functions as a knowledge system
- The algorithmic epistemology section in the conclusion is the most direct policy claim in the Neo-Pagan work: platform algorithms are epistemological governance systems that systematically disadvantage nomadic and illegitimate knowledge practices
