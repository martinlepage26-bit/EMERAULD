# Strategic Silence — Maor 2016 (Reputation, Regulatory Talk)

## Summary
Moshe Maor’s encyclopedia entry defines **strategic silence** as a calculated organizational decision to withhold internal/external communication to advance long-term goals—often to avoid association with reputational threats or to preserve room for maneuver around policy decisions.

## Key Ideas (vault-usable)
- Strategic silence is not “no communication” but a **governance strategy** about:
  - *what* information is withheld (content)
  - *when* it is withheld (timing)
- Silence/talk choices vary by domain reputation: organizations tend to keep silent where reputation is strong or outside jurisdiction and respond where reputation is weak/evolving (as summarized via Maor/Gilad/Ben-Nun Bloom).
- Maintenance of silence requires internal governance: centralized comms, blackout guidelines, incentives, and enforcement.
- Measurement approach: media content analysis comparing responses/nonresponses while controlling for intensity of coverage and source clout.

## Why it matters here
- Provides a precise handle for the “strategic silence” mechanism referenced in governance/hollowing discussions (without collapsing it into generic secrecy).
- Directly supports [[Governance by Denial — Legibility, Capacity, Classification (Draft)]]’s vocabulary and the broader accountability analysis lane.
