# Voodoo — Alfred Métraux (1959) — Haitian Voodoo ethnography

## Summary
Alfred Métraux’s *Voodoo* is a detailed ethnographic account of Haitian Vodou (history, social framework, pantheon/loa, possession, ritual, and magic). It strengthens the reference backbone for the “voodoo doll” archival/governance line already present in [[Voodoo Doll — Archival Governance and Colonial Naming]] and supports the ritual authority frame in [[Queer Sociology of Magic and Ritual]] and [[Ritual, Magic, and Institutional Authority]].

## Context
Where the “Voodoo Doll” papers use “voodoo” as a contested label and an archival problem, Métraux provides an empirical/ritual account that can prevent category drift (i.e., “voodoo” as an undifferentiated Western imaginary). This is particularly useful for tightening claims in writing that touches ritual authenticity, possession, or the social organization of cult groups.

## Key Ideas (Indexable)
- Clear structure: history → social framework → supernatural world → ritual → magic/sorcery → Christianity interface.
- Possession and pantheon organization are treated as social/ritual systems, not “belief curiosities”.
