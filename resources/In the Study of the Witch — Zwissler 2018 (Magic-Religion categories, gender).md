# In the Study of the Witch — Zwissler 2018 (Magic-Religion categories, gender)

## Summary
Laurel Zwissler (2018) examines the historically competing categories of **magic** and **religion** and argues that their genealogy carries **gendered traces** through the history of religious studies. The article connects early modern witch panics (magic as imagined heresy personified as a woman) to later academic origin-theorizing (Tylor/Frazer/Durkheim/Freud) and to contemporary communities reclaiming “Witch” identity as a legitimating reversal of a Christian-dominant gaze.

## Key Ideas (vault-usable)
- “Magic vs religion” is not a neutral analytic distinction; it is an inheritance shaped by Christian-dominant power histories and gendered personifications.
- Reading the field’s founding origin theories through **gender** surfaces implicit assumptions about what counts as “worth studying” in religion.
- Bridges historiography of religious studies with feminist/new religious movement dynamics (Witch identification as politicized legitimacy-making).

## Why it matters here
- Strengthens the vault’s category-hygiene lane alongside [[The Meanings of Magic — Bailey 2006 (Magic as Unstable Category)]] and the Frazer genealogy note.
- Supports the queer ritual/magic corpus by providing a disciplined account of how “witch” is produced as a category of difference under power.
