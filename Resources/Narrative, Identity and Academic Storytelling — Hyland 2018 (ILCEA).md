# Narrative, Identity and Academic Storytelling — Hyland 2018 (ILCEA)

## Summary
Ken Hyland argues that identity work in academia is better studied through **everyday public narrative genres** (thesis acknowledgements, bios, personal webpages) than through research interviews. His core move is methodological: identity is not only a private self-account but a **public representation** that accumulates reputation and consequence inside professional communities. Sits adjacent to [[Beyond Fluency — Stuttering, Autoethnography, and Unstable Epistemology]] for the autoethnographic counterpart and to [[Martin Lepage — Professional Profile|Martin’s own professional profile]] as a worked instance of the genre. Reference for [[Martin Lepage Professional Identity]] (genre theory underwrites the professional-bio register) and [[Academic Paper Pipeline]] (acknowledgements/bios/profiles as accumulating reputation artifacts).

## Key Ideas (vault-usable)
- Interviews can produce contrived “identity narratives” with low real-world consequence; public genres are where identities become legible and contestable — the same legibility argument running through [[Fluency, Interruption, and Institutional Accountability]] and [[Governance by Denial — Legibility, Capacity, Classification (Draft)|legibility/capacity/classification]].
- Identity narratives are embedded in genre constraints: what can be said, where, to whom, with what expected stance — a discourse-level expression of [[Power in International Politics — Barnett & Duvall 2005 (Taxonomy)|productive power]].
- Three high-signal genres for corpus-based analysis:
  - acknowledgements (theses/dissertations)
  - bio statements
  - personal academic webpages

## Why it matters here
- Useful for Martin’s work where **voice, authority, and legibility** are central: it gives a concrete method for reading identity as an institutional artifact (not just autobiography); applied operationally in [[Inductive Literary Discourse Analysis — Witchcraft in Song Lyrics|the inductive literary discourse analysis]].
- Pairs with mechanism + evidence discipline notes — see [[Causal Mechanisms in the Social Sciences — Hedström & Ylikoski (Mechanistic Explanation)|Hedström & Ylikoski]] and [[Process-Tracing Methods — Beach & Pedersen 2019 (Mechanisms and Evidence)|Beach & Pedersen]] — when translating qualitative method into governance claims.
