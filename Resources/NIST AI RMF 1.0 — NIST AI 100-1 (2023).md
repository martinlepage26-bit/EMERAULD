# NIST AI RMF 1.0 — NIST AI 100-1 (2023)

## Summary
NIST’s Artificial Intelligence Risk Management Framework (AI RMF 1.0) is a voluntary, rights-preserving, use-case-agnostic risk framework intended to help organizations **govern AI risks** across design, development, deployment, and use. Its core is structured as four functions — **Govern, Map, Measure, Manage** — and it defines “trustworthy AI” characteristics (valid/reliable, safe, secure/resilient, accountable/transparent, explainable/interpretable, privacy-enhanced, fair with harmful bias managed). Cited directly as the regulatory anchor in [[CONTROL 1 — Tool Layer Audit Protocol (Regulatory Grounding)|Control 1]] (CA-7 continuous monitoring) and [[CONTROL 3 — Architecture Deprecation Protocol (Regulatory Grounding)|Control 3]] (CM-3 change control), and as the canonical external standard the [[ANTHRO PHAROS — Anthropic vs PHAROS Governance Comparison|PHAROS-vs-Anthropic comparison]] writes against.

## Key Ideas (vault-usable)
- Risk framing is organizational (tolerance, prioritization, integration), not just technical — aligns with the [[PHAROS Method — Technical Reference|PHAROS pipeline]]’s consequence-binding map and with the level-of-abstraction argument in [[ANTHRO PHAROS — Anthropic vs PHAROS Governance Comparison]].
- The “Core” structure (Govern/Map/Measure/Manage) is directly usable as a checklist spine for governance artifacts and audits — the [[Learning Together for Responsible AI — ISED Public Awareness WG 2022|Canadian ISED report]] supplies the public-awareness lane that maps onto *Govern* and *Map*.
- Explicit update schedule language: AI RMF is a living document; formal review expected no later than 2028; versioning uses major/minor (`1.0`, `1.1`, …) — the same supersession discipline written into [[CONTROL 3 — Architecture Deprecation Protocol (Regulatory Grounding)|Control 3]].
