# Framing Processes and Social Movements — Benford & Snow 2000

## Summary
Benford & Snow’s 2000 *Annual Review of Sociology* article is a canonical review of **collective action frames** and **framing processes** as core dynamics of social movements, alongside resource mobilization and political opportunity. In this vault it is useful both for media/governance analysis and for writing that needs disciplined language about “frames” rather than metaphorical usage (see [[Media Studies and Pop Culture Analysis]] and [[Governance by Denial]]). Reference also for [[Authority, Legitimacy, and Post-Sovereignty]] — frames are the discursive infrastructure through which authority claims are made legible or contestable.

## Context
The article’s utility here is methodological: it clarifies what “frame” means, how framing operates, what constrains/frustrates it, and what its consequences are for movement outcomes. This can be imported as conceptual infrastructure for any paper or note that claims a “proof regime” or legitimacy dynamic without collapsing it into vibes.
