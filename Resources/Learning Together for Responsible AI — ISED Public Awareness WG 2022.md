# Learning Together for Responsible AI — ISED Public Awareness WG 2022

## Summary
Report of Canada’s Public Awareness Working Group (ISED) summarizing research findings and recommendations on **AI literacy** and public perceptions of AI (opportunities, concerns, barriers to engagement), based on the “Open Dialogue: Artificial Intelligence in Canada” activities (2021–2022). It includes plain-language AI definitions used for survey/workshop participants and an explicit framing of AI literacy as competencies for critical evaluation, collaboration, and use. The Canadian counterpart to [[NIST AI RMF 1.0 — NIST AI 100-1 (2023)|NIST AI RMF]] for the public-awareness / literacy lane, and a direct input into Level 4 (public legitimacy) of [[Paper 25 — The Pharos Frame - Four Levels Where Ethics Becomes AI Governance|the Pharos Frame]].

## Key Ideas (vault-usable)
- AI literacy framed as competencies to evaluate, communicate/collaborate with AI, and use AI across life contexts (holistic, not only technical) — adjacent to [[Emotional Alliance vs. Evidentiary Discipline in AI|evidentiary discipline]] as the user-side competency required for that discipline to land.
- Notes barriers to engagement and who was/was not reached (participation bias as governance problem); cf. [[Governance by Denial|governance-by-denial]] for the institutional-side argument that what isn’t recognized cannot be governed.
- Useful for governance packaging: public legitimacy depends on literacy + participation infrastructure, not only technical assurance — operationalized in the [[AI Governance Reference Stack — Annotated Library (Operational 2026-03-11)|AI governance reference stack]] and in [[PHAROS Procurement-Unblock Sprint|PHAROS procurement-unblock]] outreach.
