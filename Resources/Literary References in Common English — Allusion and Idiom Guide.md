# Literary References in Common English — Allusion and Idiom Guide

## Summary

A categorized reference list of literary allusions, classical idioms, and pop-culture shorthand that have entered common English usage. Organized by source domain: misfortune/struggle, fate/choice, life paradoxes, knowledge/curiosity, Greek mythology, Aesop's fables, nautical origins, sporting metaphors, and secularized religious idioms. Functional resource for [[Literary References — Craft Guide]] and writing work.

## Context

This reference list complements [[Literary References — Craft Guide]] (craft mechanics for deploying allusions in prose) and [[Publisher Assessment — Literary Idioms and Phrase-Origin Book]] (acquisitions memo for a popular etymology book). The raw source was stored in the vault root without wiki integration — promoted here to close that gap.

## Details

**Coverage by domain:**

- **Classic literature and drama (Shakespeare, Dickens, Burns, Cervantes, Kafka, Heller, Orwell, Huxley):** ~30 phrases including "sea of troubles," "catch-22," "kafkaesque," "brave new world," "green-eyed monster."
- **Greek mythology:** Achilles' heel, Midas touch, Pandora's box, Trojan Horse, Scylla and Charybdis, Herculean task.
- **Aesop's fables:** Cry wolf, lion's share, dog in the manger, slow and steady wins the race.
- **Pop culture and modern media (film, TV, social media):** "Jump the shark," "gaslighting," "bucket list," "catfish," "May the Force be with you," "YOLO," "red pill/blue pill," "main character energy," "core memory."
- **Nautical origins:** Loose cannon, show your true colours, taken aback, feeling blue, smooth sailing.
- **Sporting metaphors:** Ballpark figure, hail Mary, drop the ball, level playing field, home stretch.
- **Combat/weaponry:** Bite the bullet, flash in the pan.
- **Secularized religious idioms:** Writing on the wall, Good Samaritan, forbidden fruit, wash your hands of it.
