# Causal Mechanisms in the Social Sciences — Hedström & Ylikoski (Mechanistic Explanation)

## Summary
Peter Hedström and Petri Ylikoski present a mechanism-based view of explanation for the social sciences: good explanations should “open the black box” by specifying the **entities, activities, and organization** that generate an effect (the “cogs and wheels”), rather than resting on covering-law generalizations or intervening-variable placeholders. This is the methodological spine the [[PHAROS Method — Technical Reference|PHAROS method]]’s mechanism-talk leans on, and a direct cousin to [[Process-Tracing Methods — Beach & Pedersen 2019 (Mechanisms and Evidence)|Beach & Pedersen]]’s process-tracing apparatus.

## Key Ideas (vault-usable)
- A mechanism is always **a mechanism for something** (effect-defined), and is irreducibly causal — directly underwrites the [[Paper 25 — The Pharos Frame - Four Levels Where Ethics Becomes AI Governance|Pharos Frame’s §5]] insistence that ethics is socially implemented through mechanisms, not declared through principles.
- Mechanisms have **structure** (temporal/spatial organization), not just “X causes Y”; the same structural-evidence requirement governs [[Governance by Denial — Legibility, Capacity, Classification (Draft)|legibility/capacity/classification]] analyses.
- Mechanisms form **hierarchies**: higher-level entities presuppose lower-level mechanisms without implying infinite regress; cf. the [[InfraFabric Architecture|InfraFabric]] hierarchy where module-level mechanisms presuppose the lower-level governance harness.
- Bridges philosophy-of-science mechanism talk to social-science uses — analytical sociology, theory of action, middle-range theory, agent-based simulation — and to the [[LOTUS Model — Agency and Social Positioning|LOTUS]] vector model of agency under constraint.

## Why it matters here
- Fits PHAROS-style governance writing: demands that “accountability” claims specify the **actual causal process**, not merely a metric proxy or an institutional story — the same evidentiary discipline written into [[AI Ethics Literature — Contestable Governance Artifacts]] and [[Emotional Alliance vs. Evidentiary Discipline in AI]].
- Useful as method spine for mechanism-language already present in governance notes (what exactly changes what, through what steps, with what blockages); pairs with [[Power in International Politics — Barnett & Duvall 2005 (Taxonomy)]] to distinguish *which kind of power* the mechanism instantiates.
