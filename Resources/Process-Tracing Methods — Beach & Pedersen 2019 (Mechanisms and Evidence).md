# Process-Tracing Methods — Beach & Pedersen 2019 (Mechanisms and Evidence)

## Summary
Derek Beach and Rasmus Brun Pedersen’s book is a detailed guide to process-tracing in social science. It differentiates **four variants** of process-tracing, develops the underlying logic of mechanistic explanation, and gives practical guidance on theorizing mechanisms, case selection, and evaluating evidence (including Bayesian reasoning in an informal, nontechnical way). Pairs directly with [[Causal Mechanisms in the Social Sciences — Hedström & Ylikoski (Mechanistic Explanation)|Hedström & Ylikoski]] (philosophical grounding) and provides the practical evidence apparatus the [[PHAROS Method — Technical Reference|PHAROS method]] borrows when adjudicating bounded-claim cases.

## Key Ideas (vault-usable)
- Process-tracing as: what we trace, what counts as traces, and how traces become evidence for (or against) mechanistic propositions — the same epistemic posture written into [[AI Self-Report — Epistemic Status Recursion and Perturbation|AI self-report as evidence]] and into the [[Agent Session Phenomenology|session phenomenology]] notes.
- Mechanism theory can be minimalist or system-level; context and mechanistic heterogeneity matter for generalization, which is why [[Paper 25 — The Pharos Frame - Four Levels Where Ethics Becomes AI Governance|Paper 25]] §10 specifies its own bounded scope rather than claiming universality.
- Explicit attention to translating empirical material into mechanistic evidence (probative value), not just narrative coherence — directly resists the [[Narrative Capture Failure Taxonomy — Substituting Theory for Contact|narrative-capture]] failure mode.
