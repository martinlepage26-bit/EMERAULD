# Power in International Politics — Barnett & Duvall 2005 (Taxonomy)

## Summary
Barnett & Duvall (2005) argue that IR’s default “A gets B to do what B otherwise wouldn’t” conception of power is too narrow. They propose a broader definition — power as effects produced **in and through social relations** — and a taxonomy that distinguishes forms of power by (1) whether relations are **interactional** vs **constitutive**, and (2) whether effects are **direct/specific** vs **diffuse/indirect**. The taxonomy is the cleanest scholarly handle in the vault for the “authority without power-over” thread that runs through [[Authority After Legitimacy — Disenchantment and Queer Political Theory]], [[Governance by Denial]], and the [[PHAROS — Origin and Doctrine|PHAROS doctrine]].

## Taxonomy (vault-usable)
- **Compulsory power** — direct control of one actor over another (interactional + direct); maps onto naïve “command-and-control” governance reductions critiqued in [[ANTHRO PHAROS — Anthropic vs PHAROS Governance Comparison]].
- **Institutional power** — indirect control through institutions/rules that shape bargaining advantages and constrain action (interactional + diffuse); aligns with [[Governance by Denial — Legibility, Capacity, Classification (Draft)|legibility/capacity/classification]].
- **Structural power** — constitutive relations that position actors (e.g., roles) and generate asymmetries (constitutive + direct); the kind of power that [[LOTUS Model — Agency and Social Positioning|the LOTUS Model]] vectorizes.
- **Productive power** — diffuse production of subjects, categories, and meaning through discourses and social practices (constitutive + diffuse); operative in [[Fluency, Interruption, and Institutional Accountability|fluency-as-governance]] and [[The Data Gaze — Beer (Capitalism, Power, Perception)|data-gaze]] analyses.

## Why it matters here
- Gives crisp vocabulary for “power without command,” which is essential for governance analysis of institutions, classification, and proof regimes — see [[Social Compass Paper — Ritual Authority and Régime de Preuve]] for the proof-regime application.
- Directly supports the “legibility/capacity/classification” frames developed in [[Governance by Denial]] and instantiated in case studies like [[Harrowfield Clinic — AI Governance Failure Case Study]] (where productive power produces the diagnostic categories that compulsory power then enforces).
