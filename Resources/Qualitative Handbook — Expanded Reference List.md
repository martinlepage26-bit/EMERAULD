# Qualitative Handbook — Expanded Reference List

## Summary

A synthesized methods handbook expanded from the [[Master Bibliography — Références bibliographiques 2025|Master Reference List]], shaped by the specific bibliographic strengths of [[Martin Lepage — Professional Profile|Martin Lepage]]'s research archive. Covers five domains: inductive qualitative analysis, life-story and narrative methods, ethnography and field presence, discourse and performativity, and intersectional and queer frameworks — with a final section on ritual, embodiment, and contemporary spirituality. Directly relevant to [[Agatha All Along — Wicca, Digital Mediatization, and Proof Regimes]] and to the qualitative foundations of [[Queering Neo-Pagan Magic — Identity Power and the Uses of Magic]].

## Context

This handbook is a material-first reference for research situated at the intersection of queer religiosity, governance, and qualitative method. It extends an earlier handbook (sections 11–18) and is designed to help match method to material rather than impose a generic framework. The dominant francophone methodological tradition (Paillé, Mucchielli, Bertaux) sits alongside Butler, Foucault, Crenshaw, and Bell, making it particularly suited to [[Martin Lepage — Professional Profile|Martin]]'s bilingual research practice. It is the methodological backbone for work on [[Social Compass Paper — Ritual Authority and Régime de Preuve|ritual authority]], [[Social Stratification, Intersectionality, and the Why Be King Manuscript|social stratification]], and [[Inductive Literary Discourse Analysis — Witchcraft in Song Lyrics|discourse in magical texts]].

## Details

**Section 11 — Inductive Analysis (Paillé, Mucchielli, Blais & Martineau):**
Software aids retrieval and sorting but cannot replace analytic judgment. Analytic rigor comes from transparency of process: track when a code became a concept, when a pattern became a theme, when a contradiction forced a category to split. Writing is an analytic tool, not a polishing stage. This principle applies directly to AI-assisted analysis in the [[First Method Paper — Recursive AI Governance as Executable Method]].

**Section 12 — Life-Story and Narrative (Bertaux, Galligani, Barrick):**
Distinguish the narrated life (self-understanding, identity, sequence) from the reconstructed life-history (archives, institutional traces, third-party accounts). Narrative is form, ordering, justification, omission, and positioning — not just content. Sociological saturation is reached when new interviews yield no new structural features of the milieu, not when a case count is hit. Applied in [[Neo-Shamanism and Gender — Montreal Pagan Community]].

**Section 13 — Ethnography and Field Presence (Beaud & Weber, Bastien, Favret-Saada):**
Participation is a methodological stance, not a switch. Ethnographic material requires a discipline of description: preserve situational density — sequence, atmosphere, objects, bodies, what was withheld. Distance and involvement are not opposites; they are a tension to be managed (Favret-Saada's witchcraft fieldwork remains the canonical demonstration of this). See also [[Queer Neo-Pagan Spiritualities and the Question of Authenticity in Wicca]].

**Section 14 — Discourse, Performativity, and Social Order (Austin, Bourdieu, Foucault, Butler):**
Speech acts do things. Discourse organizes regimes of truth. Identity is constituted through repeated norms and performances, not simply expressed. The right question is not only what participants mean, but what discursive conditions make their meanings possible. Goffman's micro-sociology of interaction (impression management, face, staging) complements this macro analysis. Directly applied in [[Inductive Literary Discourse Analysis — Witchcraft in Song Lyrics]] and [[Beyond Fluency — Stuttering, Autoethnography, and Unstable Epistemology]].

**Section 15 — Intersectionality and Queer Theory (Crenshaw, Bilge, Dorlin, De Lauretis):**
Single-axis frameworks fail to capture how race and gender interact in lived experience and institutional harm. Intersectionality is not a checklist; it is an analytic structuring principle. Queer theory (Butler, De Lauretis, Eribon, Baril) offers tools for examining how categories are made, how bodies are regulated, how recognition operates, and how deviance is assigned. See [[Social Stratification, Intersectionality, and the Why Be King Manuscript]] and [[Authority After Legitimacy — Disenchantment and Queer Political Theory]].
Primary anchor text added: [[Mapping the Margins — Crenshaw 1991 (Intersectionality and Violence)]].

**Section 16 — Ritual, Embodiment, and Contemporary Spirituality (Bell, Houseman, Harvey, Greenwood, Pike):**
Ritual is not reducible to symbol; it is a strategic way of ordering bodies, space, time, and legitimacy. Contemporary spirituality sites — including queer neo-pagan communities in Montreal (Lepage & Gauthier, 2015–2019) — require simultaneous attention to discourse, embodiment, aesthetics, kinship, gender, and experience. Browne, Munt & Yip on Queer Spiritual Spaces: sacred space is contested, sexualized, and politically organized. Directly grounds [[Agatha All Along — Wicca, Digital Mediatization, and Proof Regimes]], [[Social Compass Paper — Ritual Authority and Régime de Preuve]], and [[Queering Neo-Pagan Magic — 2026 Paper]].

**Section 17 — Material-First Workflow:**
- Interview material → thematic analysis / narrative / life-story
- Field material → ethnographic description first; preserve scenes before coding
- Textual material → discourse analysis informed by Austin, Bourdieu, Foucault, Butler
- Ritual/spiritual material → combine ethnographic, symbolic, performative, and interactional analysis
- Identity/marginalization material → intersectional and queer frameworks as primary analytic structure, not add-on

**Selected reading path from source:**
- Paillé & Mucchielli (2003); Blais & Martineau (2006) — inductive foundations
- Bertaux (2010); Galligani (2000) — life-story
- Beaud & Weber (2003); Favret-Saada (1977) — ethnography
- Austin (1962); Bourdieu (1982); Foucault (1975); Butler (1993) — discourse and performativity
- Crenshaw (2005); Bilge (2009); Dorlin (2009); De Lauretis (2007) — intersectionality and queer theory
- Bell (1992); Houseman (2012); Harvey (1997); Greenwood (2000); Pike (2001) — ritual and spirituality

**Core lesson:** Qualitative rigor comes from fitting method to material, then fitting interpretation to power.

## Key Ideas

- Method should emerge from the kind of material in hand; interpretation should never stop at description
- Sociological saturation is structural, not numerical
- Ritual, spirituality, and embodied practice are ideal sites for mixed qualitative design — they require multiple methods simultaneously
- Intersectionality applied correctly changes what questions are even askable, not just what answers emerge
- The francophone tradition (Paillé, Mucchielli, Bertaux) provides a distinct vocabulary for analytic rigor that complements anglophone equivalents
