## DESCRIPTION (full product page — paste into Gumroad rich text editor)

**The missing link between "how to cite AI" and "whether this claim is safe to cite at all."**

Style guides added AI citation rules. Regulators are adding disclosure obligations. But nobody gave practitioners the actual verification methodology.

This Field Guide closes that gap.

### What You Get
- 48-page designed PDF desk reference (printable, tab-friendly)
- Seven source categories with verification matrix (primary academic → official statute → corporate records → court decisions → news/secondary → preprints → model-internal synthesis)
- Citation repair worksheet for the six most common failure modes (fabricated DOI, evidence drift, authority inflation, hedge disappearance, etc.)
- 120+ entry red-flag phrase glossary grouped for speed (certainty inflators, scope creep, attribution fudges, temporal violations, sycophancy framing, provenance laundering...)
- Decision tree: when to treat model output as working note vs. demand primary source
- Worked examples drawn from the LegiPro pilot (TVA, e-invoicing, rescrit, corporate filings)
- Quick-reference card (single page, laminate it)
- Provenance packet template (minimum documentation for any non-Verified claim)

### Why This Exists
In the LegiPro Mirofish Evidence Pilot (34 dossiers, 68 turns, public provenance), adversarial multi-agent review caught citation drops, scope overclaims, and confident fabrications that single-pass generation produced. The deterministic selector using professional-liability weights never let a weaker Ultra answer through.

This guide turns that hard-won discipline into something you can run on every claim before it enters your report, memo, filing, or paper.

- Researchers and graduate students who must defend AI-assisted literature reviews or synthesis
- Consultants and expert-comptables who sign off on client deliverables
- Compliance, audit, and governance teams building defensible AI-use documentation (Law 25, ISO 42001, journal policies)
- Policy professionals whose briefings will be read by people who will treat them as authoritative
