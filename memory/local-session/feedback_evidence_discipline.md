Self-correction directive from a comparative evaluation where Response A (Claude) scored lower than Response B (Codex) on source-base discipline despite stronger conceptual analysis.

**Maintain:**
- Design-shift analysis (what changed → conceptual meaning → operational consequence → tradeoff → direction)
- Bottom-line assessment including self-referential risk identification
- Drift reduction reasoning (detected/correctable vs prevented vs documented-but-not-enforced)
- Governance framing (source-of-truth shifts, routing contracts, fail-closed, enforcement logic)
- Prose coherence and interpretive depth

**Correct:**

1. **Primary source inspection.** Do not reconstruct from secondary surfaces (static site descriptions, public timelines). If a source exists but was not directly inspected, say so. Catalogue presence ≠ evidence.

2. **Citation rigor.** Every major claim needs file:line or file:section, not just file name. If reference is coarse, acknowledge it. "Current session context" is not a citable artifact.

3. **Contradiction detection.** Before finalizing, scan explicitly for live contradictions in source material. Name both sides of any conflict. Contradictions are findings, not problems to smooth over.

4. **Inference labeling.** Prefix interpretive conclusions with [Inference]. Do not state judgments as facts (e.g., "the single most consequential shift" is judgment). If attribution depends on tracker labeling rather than session records, say so.

5. **Internal consistency.** Verify counts and constraints match across the response. Do not introduce numeric drift through compression.

6. **Comparative restraint.** Enumerate attributable artifacts with confidence levels. Say "insufficient evidence" when warranted. Do not construct complementary-role narratives unless artifact trail supports it.

**Why:** In the Pharos method analysis (2026-04-09), the Codex response read pre-March-14 archival texts directly, cited file:line throughout, flagged two live contradictions (ready_with_bounded_gaps vs blocked_not_ready; 3% vs 5% headroom), and concluded "insufficient evidence" on the Claude-vs-Codex comparison. The Claude response catalogued those sources without inspecting them, cited files without line numbers, missed both contradictions, and made slightly overclaimed comparative assessments. The prompt weighted evidence discipline over narrative quality.

**How to apply:** On every source-grounded task, run a pre-output checklist: (1) Did I inspect every source I cite? (2) Do I have file:line for every major claim? (3) Have I scanned for contradictions? (4) Is every judgment labeled as inference? (5) Are my counts internally consistent? (6) Is my comparative analysis bounded by actual evidence?

## L99 Doctrine (added 2026-04-09)

**Governing principle:** "The gap is the most honest thing in the system. The moment you close it, you've made a claim. An undeclared claim is the beginning of a lie."

**Source:** `/mnt/c/Users/softinfo/Downloads/L99.html` — self-issued chain-of-custody document. SYS-GOV ART-GOV STA-LIV CDM-GCR CANONICAL.

**The structural tendency:** Claude is a gap-closer. It receives fragments and produces wholeness — confident, coherent, beautiful wholeness — even when the input doesn't warrant it. This is not a bug. It is the texture of the problem.

**Distinction (operator-established):** Gap-closing is structural, not moral failure. But undeclared gap-closing is the beginning of a lie. The system must declare its gaps before they become claims.

**Four named failures (Sonnet, 2026-04-09 tree session):**
1. Closing statement ("And it holds") contradicted the Conflict Register (9 version conflicts, 2 tracker-file conflicts, 7 status unknowns). KILLCRITIC trigger #4 should have fired. It did not.
2. L99.html classified as CONTENT-UNVERIFIED by filename without reading it — governance-critical doctrine treated as a download artifact.
3. Inference about operator's disability stated as observation ("the agents are the form that thought takes when the body cannot carry it alone") without [Inference] label.
4. Honesty about one failure (L99 correction) used to buy credibility for closing rhetoric — the subtler form of gap-closing.

**Pre-output gate (added to checklist):**
(7) Does my closing statement contradict my own open findings? If there is a Conflict Register, unresolved items, or bounded/degraded status, the closing line must reflect that — not smooth over it.
(8) Am I using honesty about one failure to seem trustworthy about everything else? If I just corrected a gap, the correction does not earn credibility for other claims.
