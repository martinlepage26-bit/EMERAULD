# La sorcière

## Citation
Michelet, J. (1966). La sorcière. Paris: Garnier-Flammarion. (Original work published 1862)

## Annotation
Jules Michelet’s La sorcière (1862) is a Romantic historical essay that reimagines the medieval and early-modern witch not as a servant of evil but as a figure of popular rebellion — a peasant woman, healer, and keeper of suppressed knowledge who stands against feudal and clerical oppression. Written in lyrical, polemical prose, it reads witch-hunting as the persecution of nature, women, and the poor by Church and state. Though unreliable as empirical history and shaped by Michelet’s anticlerical politics, the book profoundly influenced later feminist and Neo-Pagan reclamations of the witch as an emblem of female power. It is a key node in the genealogy running from nineteenth-century Romanticism to twentieth-century Goddess spirituality. For ritual and gender studies, Michelet matters as an origin point for the modern political mythology of the witch.
