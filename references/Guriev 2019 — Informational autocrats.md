# Informational autocrats

## Citation
Guriev, S. and Treisman, D. (2019) ‘Informational autocrats’, Journal of Economic Perspectives, 33(4), pp. 100–127. doi: 10.1257/jep.33.4.100.

## Annotation
Guriev and Treisman's “Informational Autocrats” is a widely cited analysis arguing that many modern dictatorships have shifted from mass repression to the manipulation of information — boosting leaders' apparent competence through propaganda while censoring or co-opting informed elites. Drawing on a new dataset, they document declining violence, concealed repression, and the mimicry of democracy. Published in the Journal of Economic Perspectives, it reframes contemporary authoritarianism. For this corpus it is highly relevant to information control, propaganda, and AI-enabled manipulation.
