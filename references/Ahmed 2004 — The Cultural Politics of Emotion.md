# The Cultural Politics of Emotion

## Citation
Ahmed, S. (2004) The Cultural Politics of Emotion. Edinburgh: Edinburgh University Press.

## Annotation
Sara Ahmed's The Cultural Politics of Emotion is a foundational text in affect theory, arguing that emotions are not private psychological states but social and cultural practices that circulate between bodies and signs. Through the notion of “affective economies,” Ahmed shows how feelings such as fear, hate, and love “stick” to particular bodies and objects, doing political work like producing boundaries between self and other. The book analyzes emotion in relation to nationalism, racism, queerness, and pain. It has been widely influential across queer theory, feminist theory, and cultural studies. For this corpus it grounds an account of how affect structures social and political belonging.
