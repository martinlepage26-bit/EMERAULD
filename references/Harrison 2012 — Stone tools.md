# Stone tools

## Citation
Harrison, R. (2012). Stone tools. In D. Hicks & M. C. Beaudry (Eds.), The Oxford handbook of material culture studies. Oxford: Oxford University Press. https://doi.org/10.1093/oxfordhb/9780199218714.013.0023

## Annotation
Harrison argues that stone tools do not stop acting when they are discarded: their 'afterlives' in heritage management and museum collections are where much of their social efficacy actually happens, and he draws on Ingold to reject the mental-template model of making in favour of form that grows through practice. The transferable claim is that an artefact's meaning is not fixed at production — which is exactly the move needed to think about documents, protocols, and models after release. Peer-reviewed handbook chapter.
