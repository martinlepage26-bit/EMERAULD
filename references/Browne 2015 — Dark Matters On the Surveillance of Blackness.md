# Dark Matters: On the Surveillance of Blackness

## Citation
Browne, S. (2015). Dark Matters: On the Surveillance of Blackness. Duke University Press.

## Annotation
Simone Browne's Dark Matters is a foundational work of surveillance studies that centers Blackness, arguing that contemporary surveillance technologies and practices have deep roots in the transatlantic slave trade and anti-Black control. Browne develops concepts such as “racializing surveillance” and “dark sousveillance,” reading branding, biometrics, and airports through the history of racial subjection. The book is highly influential across surveillance, race, and technology studies. For this corpus it grounds the analysis of how surveillance and data systems are racialized — directly relevant to AI and monitoring.
