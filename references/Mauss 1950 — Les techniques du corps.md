# Les techniques du corps

## Citation
Mauss, M. (1950). Les techniques du corps. In Sociologie et anthropologie. Paris: Presses Universitaires de France. (Original work published 1936)

## Annotation
Marcel Mauss’s 1934 lecture “Les techniques du corps,” collected in the 1950 volume Sociologie et anthropologie, argues that even seemingly natural bodily acts — walking, swimming, sleeping, giving birth — are learned, culturally transmitted techniques that vary across societies. Mauss introduces the notion of habitus (later developed by Bourdieu) to name these socially acquired dispositions of the body. The essay is a founding text for the anthropology and sociology of the body, showing that embodiment is a site of social transmission rather than pure physiology. Its attention to micro-practices anticipates later work on discipline, performativity, and embodied ritual. For research on ritual and gendered embodiment, Mauss provides the classic warrant for treating the trained body as a carrier of culture.
