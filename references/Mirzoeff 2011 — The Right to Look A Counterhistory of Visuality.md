# The Right to Look: A Counterhistory of Visuality

## Citation
Mirzoeff, N. (2011). The Right to Look: A Counterhistory of Visuality. Duke University Press.

## Annotation
Nicholas Mirzoeff's The Right to Look develops a “counterhistory of visuality,” arguing that “visuality” has been a technology of authority — organizing perception to sustain power (from slavery to imperialism to the military-industrial complex) — and that against it stands a “right to look,” a claim to autonomy and mutual recognition. It is a major work in visual-culture studies. For this corpus it grounds a critical politics of vision and surveillance — relevant to how seeing and being seen are governed.
