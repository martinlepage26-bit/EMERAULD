# Mythologies

## Citation
Barthes, Roland. Mythologies. Translated by Annette Lavers, Hill and Wang, 1972.

## Annotation
Roland Barthes's Mythologies (originally 1957) is a foundational work of semiology that reads the “myths” of everyday bourgeois culture — wrestling, soap powders, wine, the face of Garbo — as ideological sign systems that naturalize the historical as eternal. In the concluding essay, “Myth Today,” Barthes theorizes myth as a second-order semiological system that empties signs of history and refills them with ideology. The book is a cornerstone of cultural studies and semiotics. For this corpus it models the critical decoding of popular culture and everyday signification.
