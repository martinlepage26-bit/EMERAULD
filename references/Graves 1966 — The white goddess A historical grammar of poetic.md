# The white goddess: A historical grammar of poetic myth (Amended and enlarged ed.)

## Citation
Graves, R. (1966). The white goddess: A historical grammar of poetic myth (Amended and enlarged ed.). New York: Farrar, Straus and Giroux.

## Annotation
Robert Graves’s idiosyncratic study, first published in 1948 and revised in 1966, advances the thesis that authentic European poetry derives from an ancient cult of a triple “White Goddess” of birth, love, and death. Blending comparative mythology, Celtic and Welsh sources, and Graves’s own poetic intuition, it proposes that true poetry is a form of invocation of this lunar muse. Though widely criticized by classicists and folklorists as speculative and evidentially loose, it became enormously influential on twentieth-century Neo-Paganism, Wicca, and the Goddess movement. Its notion of the triple goddess in particular was absorbed into modern ritual practice. For studies of contemporary Paganism and the poetics of ritual, Graves functions less as a reliable historical source than as a foundational myth-making text whose very influence must be analyzed.
