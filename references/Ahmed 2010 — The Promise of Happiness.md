# The Promise of Happiness

## Citation
Ahmed, Sara. The Promise of Happiness. Duke UP, 2010.

## Annotation
Sara Ahmed's The Promise of Happiness critically interrogates happiness as a social and political demand rather than a self-evident good. Through figures such as the “feminist killjoy,” the “unhappy queer,” and the “melancholic migrant,” Ahmed shows how happiness is often made conditional on conforming to normative scripts, and how refusing those scripts is read as causing unhappiness. The book draws on philosophy, cultural texts, and affect theory to unsettle the moral authority of happiness. It is widely cited in feminist and queer scholarship. For this corpus it extends Ahmed's affect theory into a critique of normativity and the politics of feeling.
