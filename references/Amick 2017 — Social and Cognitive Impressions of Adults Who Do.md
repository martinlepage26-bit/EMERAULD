# Social and Cognitive Impressions of Adults Who Do and Do Not Stutter Based on Listeners' Perceptions of Read-Speech Samples

## Citation
Amick, L., Chang, S., Wade, J., McAuley, J. (2017). Social and Cognitive Impressions of Adults Who Do and Do Not Stutter Based on Listeners' Perceptions of Read-Speech Samples. Frontiers in Psychology, 8. https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2017.01148

## Annotation
This experimental psychology study examines how listeners form social and cognitive impressions of adults who do and do not stutter, based on perceptions of read-speech samples. The authors report that stuttering can shape listeners' judgments of a speaker's traits, contributing to evidence on stigma and stereotyping in communication disorders. Published in Frontiers in Psychology, it belongs to the speech-language-pathology and social-perception literatures. Its place in this corpus is peripheral to the core themes; it is best treated as a specialized empirical source on listener perception and stuttering.
