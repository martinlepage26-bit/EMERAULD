# Before and after gender: Sexual mythologies of everyday life

## Citation
Strathern, M. (2016). Before and after gender: Sexual mythologies of everyday life. Chicago: HAU Books.

## Annotation
Written by Marilyn Strathern in the early 1970s but left unpublished for four decades, this manuscript was edited by Sarah Franklin and released by HAU Books in 2016. It offers an anthropological critique of the sex/gender distinction as it was crystallizing in Anglo-American feminism, arguing that “gender” is not a stable cross-cultural category but a culturally specific way of organizing difference. Strathern draws on Melanesian ethnography to show that the Western opposition between nature and culture — which underwrites the sex/gender split — is far from universal. The book is valuable both as an intellectual-historical artifact, capturing feminist anthropology at a formative moment, and as a still-pointed challenge to naturalized accounts of gender. For work on queer theory and ritual, it supplies a comparative, denaturalizing frame that resists treating gender categories as given.
