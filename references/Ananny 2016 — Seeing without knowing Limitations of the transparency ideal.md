# Seeing without knowing: Limitations of the transparency ideal and its application to algorithmic accountability

## Citation
Ananny, M., Crawford, K. (2016). Seeing without knowing: Limitations of the transparency ideal and its application to algorithmic accountability. New Media &amp; Society, 20(3), 973-989. https://doi.org/10.1177/1461444816676645

## Annotation
Ananny and Crawford critically interrogate the ideal of transparency as a means of understanding and governing algorithmic systems. They argue that “looking inside” a system — seeing its code or data — does not guarantee knowing how it works or being able to hold it accountable, and they enumerate ten limitations of the transparency ideal. Instead they propose shifting from transparency toward accountability grounded in the relationships between systems and the people they affect. The article is a foundational critique in critical-algorithm and AI-governance studies. For this corpus it grounds the argument that visibility is not the same as governability.
