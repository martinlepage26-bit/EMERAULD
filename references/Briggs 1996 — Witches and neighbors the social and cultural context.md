## Annotation
Robin Briggs's Witches and Neighbours is a major social history of European witchcraft that shifts attention from elite demonology to the local, communal dynamics of witchcraft accusations — the tensions, quarrels, and reciprocal obligations among neighbours that generated suspicion. Briggs emphasizes the everyday social context over grand theories of persecution. The book is a standard reference in witchcraft historiography. For this corpus it grounds witchcraft in the micro-politics of community life.
