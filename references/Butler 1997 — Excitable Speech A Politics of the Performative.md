# Excitable Speech: A Politics of the Performative

## Citation
Butler, Judith. 1997. Excitable Speech: A Politics of the Performative. New York: Routledge.

## Annotation
Judith Butler's Excitable Speech develops a politics of the performative applied to hate speech, censorship, and injurious language, arguing that words wound because they are citational and iterable, not because meaning is fixed by intent. Butler cautions against relying on state regulation of speech and emphasizes the possibility of resignification. The book is a key text in speech-act theory, law, and queer theory. For this corpus it links performativity to language, injury, and governance of speech.
