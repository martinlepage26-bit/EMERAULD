# Queer Phenomenology: Orientations, Objects, Others

## Citation
Ahmed, Sara. 2006. Queer Phenomenology: Orientations, Objects, Others. Durham, NC: Duke University Press.

## Annotation
Sara Ahmed's Queer Phenomenology brings phenomenology into contact with queer theory by asking how bodies are “oriented” in space and toward objects. Reworking Husserl and Merleau-Ponty, Ahmed argues that orientations — sexual, racial, and spatial — shape what becomes reachable, familiar, or possible for a given body. “Queer” here names moments of disorientation and the taking of oblique lines away from the straight and the normative. The book is influential across queer theory, feminist philosophy, and studies of space and race. For this corpus it supplies a theoretical vocabulary for embodiment, orientation, and normativity.
