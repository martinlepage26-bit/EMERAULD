# Development,

## Citation
Ferguson, James. The Anti-Politics Machine: "Development," Depoliticization, and Bureaucratic Power in Lesotho. Cambridge: Cambridge University Press, 1990.

## Annotation
James Ferguson's The Anti-Politics Machine is a landmark anthropological study of a development project in Lesotho, arguing that development discourse systematically depoliticizes poverty — recasting political problems as technical ones — while expanding bureaucratic state power as a side effect. Ferguson's analysis reshaped critical development studies. For this corpus it is highly relevant: a canonical account of how technical framings depoliticize problems and entrench institutional power, with clear parallels to techno-solutionism in AI.
