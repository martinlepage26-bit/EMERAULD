# Epistemic Injustice: Power and the Ethics of Knowing

## Citation
Fricker, Miranda. Epistemic Injustice: Power and the Ethics of Knowing. Oxford: Oxford University Press, 2007.

## Annotation
Miranda Fricker's Epistemic Injustice is a foundational work of social epistemology that identifies two forms of specifically epistemic wrong: testimonial injustice, where prejudice causes a speaker's word to be given deflated credibility, and hermeneutical injustice, where gaps in shared interpretive resources leave some experiences unintelligible. Fricker links knowledge to power and ethics. The book has been enormously influential across philosophy and beyond. For this corpus it grounds analysis of whose knowledge counts — directly relevant to evidence, testimony, and bias in governance and AI.
