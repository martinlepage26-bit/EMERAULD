# The Intellectual Challenge of CSCW: The Gap Between Social Requirements and Technical Feasibility

## Citation
Ackerman, M. (2000). The Intellectual Challenge of CSCW: The Gap Between Social Requirements and Technical Feasibility. Human–Computer Interaction, 15(2-3), 179-203. https://doi.org/10.1207/S15327051HCI1523_5

## Annotation
Mark Ackerman's landmark CSCW essay articulates the “socio-technical gap” — the persistent divergence between what social and organizational life requires and what computational systems can actually support. He argues that this gap is not a temporary engineering shortfall but a structural feature of building technology for nuanced human activity. The paper reframes the aim of computer-supported cooperative work as managing, rather than eliminating, this mismatch. It has become a foundational reference in human–computer interaction and the sociology of technology. For AI-governance work, it supplies an early, rigorous statement of why technical systems routinely underserve social requirements.
