# Crime et Châtiment

## Citation
Dostoïevski, Fiodor. 1866. Crime et Châtiment. Paris: Gallimard (Folio classique), [édition consultée].

## Annotation
Dostoevsky's Crime and Punishment (1866) follows the impoverished student Raskolnikov, who murders a pawnbroker to test his theory that extraordinary individuals stand above conventional morality, and then descends into guilt, paranoia, and eventual confession and moral awakening. The novel is a landmark of psychological realism and moral philosophy, probing conscience, suffering, and redemption. For this corpus it is a primary literary source on moral responsibility, the limits of self-appointed exception, and the psychology of guilt.
