# sex

## Citation
Butler, J. (1993). Bodies that matter: On the discursive limits of "sex". New York: Routledge.

## Annotation
Judith Butler’s Bodies That Matter (1993) responds to critics of Gender Trouble by clarifying that performativity is not voluntarist theatrics but the reiterative, citational process through which discursive norms materialize sexed bodies. Butler argues that “sex” is not a bodily given onto which gender is layered but is itself produced as a naturalized effect of regulatory power. Central chapters develop the concepts of citationality, abjection, and the “constitutive outside” — those excluded bodies that do not “matter” yet delimit the domain of intelligible subjects. The book engages Lacan, Foucault, and drag as a site where the contingency of gender norms becomes legible. It remains a foundational text of queer theory and is essential for any account of how norms enact rather than merely describe the bodies they govern.
