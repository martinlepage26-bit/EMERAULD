# Street-Level Bureaucracy: Dilemmas of the Individual in Public Services

## Citation
Lipsky, Michael. Street-Level Bureaucracy: Dilemmas of the Individual in Public Services. New York: Russell Sage Foundation, 1980/2010.

## Annotation
Michael Lipsky's Street-Level Bureaucracy is a foundational work of public administration arguing that frontline public workers — teachers, caseworkers, police — effectively make policy through the discretion they exercise under conditions of scarce resources and high demand. Their coping routines become the policy citizens actually experience. It is essential context for understanding governance in practice. For this corpus it is highly relevant to how discretion, rules, and (increasingly) automation shape service delivery — a key antecedent to debates on algorithmic decision-making.
