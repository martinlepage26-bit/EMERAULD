# The Morality of Law

## Citation
Fuller, Lon L. The Morality of Law. New Haven: Yale University Press, 1964/1969.

## Annotation
Lon Fuller's The Morality of Law is a classic of jurisprudence arguing that law possesses an “internal morality” — eight principles of legality (generality, publicity, clarity, non-contradiction, constancy, possibility of compliance, prospectivity, and congruence between rules and administration) — without which a system fails to be law at all. Fuller's debate with H. L. A. Hart is central to legal philosophy. For this corpus it grounds the idea that legitimate governance requires procedural-moral conditions, not just enforceable commands.
