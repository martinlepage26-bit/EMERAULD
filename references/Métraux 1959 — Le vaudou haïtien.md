# Le vaudou haïtien

## Citation
Métraux, A. (1959). Le vaudou haïtien. Paris: Gallimard.

## Annotation
Alfred Métraux’s Le vaudou haïtien (1959), based on fieldwork in Haiti in the late 1940s, remains a classic ethnography of Vodou as a coherent religious system rather than the sensationalized “voodoo” of popular imagination. Métraux documents the pantheon of loa, possession trance, ritual specialists, sacrifice, and the social organization of cult, situating Vodou within Haiti’s history of slavery, revolution, and African diasporic continuity. The work is notable for its sober, systematic treatment at a time when Haitian religion was widely exoticized. While later scholarship has revised aspects of his framing, the book endures as a foundational reference. For studies of ritual, possession, and Afro-Caribbean religion — including the genealogy of the “voodoo doll” trope and the politics of naming — Métraux is a primary anchor.
