# Book of Beliefs and Theories — Martin's Magical System (2000s)

## Summary

A personal document written by Martin in English, likely in the early 2000s (the author self-identifies as 18 years old, never having faced a demon). Contains Martin's early theoretical system about souls, demons, vampires, werewolves, and other metaphysical entities — written from within a magical practice framework, not as a detached academic observer. Companion to the French [[Livre des Ombres — Martin's Magical System (1996-2026)]] and part of the long arc that runs through Martin's academic analysis of witchcraft in [[Agatha All Along — Wicca, Digital Mediatization, and Proof Regimes]] and his ethnographic work on queer neo-Pagan practice in Montreal.

## Context

This document predates Martin's academic work by roughly a decade. By 2010 he was writing his MA thesis on mythocritique (see [[Mythocritique to PHAROS — The 2010 Master's Thesis as Methodological Keystone]]). By 2026, his magical practice had become structurally embedded in the vault — EMERAULD's elemental naming, the Trismégiste agent identity, and the [[GAIA — Earth-Calendar App and Evidence-Aware Positioning]] product all carry traces of this 1996-2000s formation.

The document is written in the first person at age ~18: *"I don't know if, as a good witch, it is our duty to vanquish and kill these demons, because at the age of 18, I've never faced one myself."* This is a primary source document of the operator's early belief formation.

## Details

### Soul Theory

The foundational theory: the soul has a material anchor — a single particle in the heart muscle. The soul *gravitates around* the body rather than residing strictly within it. This explains:
- Why feelings are attributed to the heart
- Why vampires can only be killed by a stake through the heart (the particle is the target)
- Why werewolves eat the heart of their victims
- The silver thread of astral projection, attached to the heart

Crucially: *"This particle of matter, sheltered into the heart, cannot be deciphered from the other cells in that body part. This is why science or medicine will probably never be able to prove that theory right, and even less, wrong."* This is an early version of the epistemic humility that governs Martin's later work — acknowledging the structural limits of verification.

### Demon Theory

Demons are created by accumulated negative energy — hate, violence, fear, revenge, death. A demon sufficiently evolved may invade a body (possessing an animal, human, or newborn). Key claim: a demon inhabiting a body loses memory of its demonic nature and acts on instinct. Exceptions exist when the possessed child is destined to be a witch — in this case, dark powers develop alongside the demonic nature.

This narrative of entities occupying and operating through forms without self-knowledge prefigures the later LOTUS and HELIX analysis of AI systems and their blindspots (see [[Bonded Intelligence Under Constraint — The LOTUS Processor Framework]]).

### Vampire and Werewolf Theory

Both vampire and werewolf are explained as outcomes of demon possession of predisposed beings. The demonic origin theory provides a unified framework for what appear to be diverse mythological entities — they are variants of the same underlying process.

### The Epistemic Stance

Even at 18, the document maintains a practitioner's epistemic position: *"I believe," "I think," "I don't know if" — distinguishing personal conviction from external authority.* The belief is not presented as objective truth but as the writer's working framework. This is the earliest trace of what later becomes bounded claims, separation of evidence from inference, and the objectivity-as-naming-limits-of-subjectivity principle in HEPHAISTOS.
