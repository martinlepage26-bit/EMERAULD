# AI Governance Manifesto — Upstream Institutional Practice

## Summary

AI governance should be understood as an upstream institutional practice for shaping technical power before it hardens into infrastructure, not as downstream compliance or reassurance. The governing distinction: legitimate innovation requires technical power to remain legible, contestable, bounded, and answerable before deployment momentum makes harms difficult to reverse. Core framework for [[Governance Controls and Mechanisms]] (upstream control design, procurement gates, institutional practices as controls).

## Context

This manifesto translates the [[Ethics Poetics and Praxis — Braided Discipline|ethics-poetics-praxis triad]] into the AI governance domain. It belongs to [[Governance and PHAROS MOC]], [[Recursive Governance Theory]], and the publication cluster around [[Recursive Deterministic AI Governance — Method and Paper]].

For vocabulary precision, [[Poiesis Poietics Poetics Praxis — Making and Action Distinctions]] marks AI governance as partly **poietic**: it brings worlds, institutions, permissions, incentives, and operating conditions into being. It remains **poetic** when the concern is governance language, rhetoric, framing, and public meaning.

## Core Claims

| Claim | Meaning | Governance implication |
|---|---|---|
| Governance is upstream | Ethical and political questions arise at problem selection, dataset construction, labor organization, benchmarks, optimization targets, and access design | Governance must enter before deployment review |
| Performance is not legitimacy | A system can be accurate, efficient, and scalable while still concentrating power or reducing contestability | Evaluation must include agency, accountability, redress, and distributive burden |
| Harms can be designed-in | Many harms occur when systems work as intended, not only when they malfunction | Governance must inspect objectives, incentives, and delegation of authority |
| Downstream review is structurally weak | By deployment, technical choices and commercial momentum may already be path-dependent | Controls must intervene before social and administrative infrastructure hardens |
| Soft principles are insufficient | Voluntary norms rarely constrain speed, secrecy, scale, and market capture on their own | Governance needs enforceable friction, documentation, audit, liability, monitoring, and remedy |
| Governance is publicly constitutive | AI systems mediate access to work, services, knowledge, authority, and civic participation | Governance is a question of democratic accountability, not only safety management |

## Relation to PHAROS

This manifesto aligns with [[PHAROS Method — Technical Reference]] and [[Recursive Deterministic AI Governance — Method and Paper]]: governance must be embedded in workflow, consequence binding, admissibility boundaries, review paths, and failure harvesting. It also supports [[AI Society Manuscript — From AI Anxiety to Recursive Governance]] by naming AI anxiety as a response to weakly inspectable authority rather than mere fear of technology.

The manifesto's practical demand is that institutions preserve answerability before systems become unavoidable. That demand is the same control logic PHAROS operationalizes through bounded evidence, blocked claims, threshold marking, and deployment refusal.

[[AI Ethics Literature — Contestable Governance Artifacts]] provides the literature-review support for this demand: governance claims, safety summaries, and ethical assurances should be audited as evidence-bearing artifacts, not accepted as compliance.

## Key Ideas

- Governance is not anti-innovation; it distinguishes public justification from mere acceleration.
- AI systems reorganize relations of knowledge, authority, dependency, permission, and burden.
- Legibility, contestability, proportionality, and recourse are not communications virtues; they are institutional controls.
- A governance process that enters only at launch is often theater because it reviews decisions already materially entrenched.
- The purpose of governance is not to make acceleration appear safe but to make technical power answerable.
