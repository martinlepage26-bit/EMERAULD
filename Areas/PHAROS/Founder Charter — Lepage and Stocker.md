# Founder Charter — Lepage and Stocker

## Summary
The founding agreement between Martin Lepage (President & CGTO) and Danny Stocker (CEO) for the PHAROS / InfraFabric venture. Establishes equity split (55-60% D / 40-45% ML), decision rights matrix, 10 reserved matters, and the core principle: D owns the engine, ML owns the conditions under which the engine may be trusted, sold, and expanded into regulated markets. Related to [[PHAROS Invention Disclosure]] and [[InfraFabric Architecture]].

## Context
Filed as `founder_charter.docx`. Governs the relationship between the two co-inventors named in the [[PHAROS Invention Disclosure]]. The charter is described as a self-governance document — it applies the same governance logic to the founding relationship that PHAROS applies to AI outputs. The "self-governance claim" is that the charter is itself governed by the method it describes — structurally the Osirian recursive method named in [[Mythocritique to PHAROS — The 2010 Master's Thesis as Methodological Keystone|the keystone]] applied to founder relations.

---

## Equity and Roles

| Person | Role | Equity |
|---|---|---|
| Danny Stocker | CEO | 55–60% |
| Martin Lepage | President & Chief Governance and Trust Officer (CGTO) | 40–45% |

**The core principle**: D owns the engine. ML owns the conditions under which the engine may be trusted, sold, and expanded into regulated markets.

This is not a hierarchy — it is a division of scope. Stocker cannot deploy the engine without Lepage's trust clearance. Lepage cannot define trust conditions for an engine he does not fully control. The asymmetry is functional, not a power differential.

## Decision Rights Matrix

| Class of Decision | Authority |
|---|---|
| Technical architecture and InfraFabric module design | D (CEO) |
| Governance methodology and trust framework | ML (CGTO) |
| Regulated market entry (healthcare, finance, government) | Joint — ML must clear |
| Pricing, product, and commercial strategy | D leads, ML advises |
| IP filings and patent decisions | Joint |
| Partner and investor selection | Joint |
| Reserved matters (see below) | Both — requires consensus |

---

## Ten Reserved Matters

Reserved matters require consensus (both founders); neither can act unilaterally:

1. Material change to the PHAROS governance method
2. Modification of the InfraFabric consequence binding architecture
3. Equity issuance or dilution
4. Change to the decision rights matrix itself
5. External licensing of the core method
6. Entry into regulated domains (healthcare, finance, national security)
7. Merger, acquisition, or sale of the company
8. Departure of either founder
9. Change to the self-governance claim or charter structure
10. Any filing that names a third inventor on the PHAROS method without prior consent

---

## The Self-Governance Claim

The charter asserts that it is itself governed by PHAROS logic:

- The founding relationship is a *corpus* with an *admissibility boundary* (what counts as a legitimate decision vs. unilateral action)
- Reserved matters are *method locks* — once triggered, neither party can break them without going through the adjudication protocol
- Disputes are harvested as *governance evidence*, not suppressed
- The charter is revisable (see revisability principle in [[Recursive Deterministic AI Governance — Method and Paper]]) but only through the same recursive correction protocol the method itself uses

This is not a metaphor. The charter is designed to be auditable: any dispute can be traced to a specific decision-rights breach or reserved matter trigger.

---

## CGTO Role — What ML Owns

The CGTO role is not advisory. Per the charter, ML's trust clearance is a *gate*:

- No regulated-market deployment without CGTO clearance
- No partner agreement with regulated entities without CGTO review
- CGTO has a veto on any claim that misrepresents the method's governance properties (e.g., marketing that overstates determinism or understates bounded gaps)
- CGTO is the named governance authority in the [[PHAROS Invention Disclosure]] consequence binding map — the `if.gov` module answers to the CGTO framework

**The practical consequence**: D can build faster than ML can clear, but D cannot ship to regulated clients without clearance. The tension between velocity and governance is structural and deliberate.

## Insights

- "D owns the engine; ML owns the conditions" — this formulation resolves the classic co-founder governance problem without creating a traditional CEO/advisor hierarchy. Both roles are operational and load-bearing
- The self-governance claim is either the charter's strongest feature or its most dangerous one: if the founders stop following it, there is no external enforcement mechanism
- The CGTO veto on misrepresentation is unusual and significant — most corporate structures do not give a non-CEO founder a veto over marketing claims
- The 10 reserved matters are notably symmetric: neither founder can move on any of them alone, which means a deadlock in reserved matters would require external arbitration
