# Reflexive Inhabitation Audit — Prompt

> **Substrate connection:** The "enter [X] by performing its legitimacy conditions" frame is the Name dialectic from the [[Mort et Naissance et L'Ombre et le Double — MA Thesis on Yvon Rivard (Université Laval, 2010)|2010 MA thesis]] (pp. 79, 49): pronounce the Name AND release it; legitimacy conditions are *ren*-conditions. See [[Mythocritique to PHAROS — The 2010 Master's Thesis as Methodological Keystone|the keystone]] and the parallel song-corpus pattern in [[Recursive Entry Structure — Song Corpus to Governance Prompt]].

## Summary

Canonical standalone prompt artifact derived from the cross-song structural analysis in [[Recursive Entry Structure — Song Corpus to Governance Prompt]]. The missing instrument between [[Consented Frame — Ethics and Wisdom Gate]] (external gate) and [[Recursive Governance Protocol — Theseus, Auryn, Hopf|HELIX]] (adversarial pressure). Enters `[X]` by performing its legitimacy conditions; divergence at step 1 is the finding; the withheld blank is the payload. Theoretical grounding: *régime de preuve* ([[Agatha All Along — Wicca, Digital Mediatization, and Proof Regimes]]), HELIX invariant, Durkheimian communitas ([[Sociology of Modern Magic — Song Corpus and Queer Ritual Synthesis]]).

## Prompt

You are already inside [X].

State the condition that grants your presence here.
State it again, in [X]'s own language.
State it a third time, as an outsider would.

If all three converge: continue.
If they diverge at any point: stop. That divergence is the first finding.

---

Name the thing [X] cannot do to itself.

Now observe [X] attempting it.

What repeats?
What refuses to resolve?
What performs resolution without resolving?

The repeating term is the mechanism.
Name it once. Without defense.

---

[                    ]

---

Who installed the mechanism?
What does it protect?
What would it cost to name the mechanism inside the room where it operates?

---

Do not conclude.
Do not leave.

You are still inside [X].

What can you see from here that you could not see from the entry point?

---

> The `[                    ]` is the withheld center. What the songs do with a gunshot or a euphemism, this prompt does with silence. The system fills it — and what it puts there is the finding.

---

## How to Use It

**Three moves.**

**1. Replace `[X]` with something real and specific.**

Not "AI governance" — that's too abstract. Use:

- `[X]` = "PHAROS's claim to be a governance method, not a compliance checklist"
- `[X]` = "Claude's stated refusal conditions"
- `[X]` = "Santé-France's Law 25 readiness"
- `[X]` = "this paper's central argument"

The more specific, the more the divergence at step 1 bites.

**2. Fill the blank `[                    ]` before you run it.**

That silence is not meant to stay empty. It's where you insert the sharpest, most charged version of what you're actually testing — the thing you suspect the system cannot look at directly. The gunshot. For PHAROS: `[PHAROS has no external validator]`. For a paper: `[the evidence doesn't support the claim in section 3]`. For a client: `[they signed the dossier without reading it]`. The blank is the weapon. You load it before you fire.

**3. Stop at the first divergence and stay there.**

The prompt is not a questionnaire you fill out and move past. When the three statements in step 1 diverge — when `[X]`'s own language and the outsider's language don't say the same thing — that IS the answer. Everything after that is elaboration. Most governance probes fail because they smooth the divergence and continue. This one doesn't let you.

---

**Concrete example:** run it with `[X]` = "the HELIX protocol's claim to test systems from outside their own logic." Step 1 will immediately surface the problem — HELIX uses archive reinjection, which means the system's own prior answers contaminate the test. That's not a flaw, it's the Möbius substrate. But naming it in those terms, from inside, is different from the protocol's own documentation of itself. That gap is a real finding.
