# PHAROS Runbook SOP

## Summary
The PHAROS Runbook SOP operationalizes the PHAROS method as a bounded, deterministic, reviewable, and reconstructible governance process for any target AI system. The structure transfers across systems. The determinations do not. Related to [[Recursive Deterministic AI Governance — Method and Paper]].

## Context
This is the practitioner-facing document produced from the PHAROS research program. It is what you hand to a reviewer, client, or compliance team. A PHAROS case opens because real scrutiny exists. It closes only when the disposition changes operational practice — not when prose is written. The gate-non-exception discipline (no exceptionable absolute gates) inherits the initiatic structure of the Egyptian gate-formulas analyzed in the [[Mort et Naissance et L'Ombre et le Double — MA Thesis on Yvon Rivard (Université Laval, 2010)|2010 MA thesis]] (pp. 43–48); see [[Mythocritique to PHAROS — The 2010 Master's Thesis as Methodological Keystone|the keystone]] — a gate that admits exception is no longer initiatic.

---

## Controlling Sequence

**Pressure source → Governing question → Operational route → Thresholds → Decision rights → Evidence path → Disposition → Re-entry triggers**

---

## Six Design Principles

1. **Pressure-led** — a case opens because a real source of scrutiny exists: procurement, audit, launch, incident, vendor review, executive request, policy challenge, or material system change
2. **Question-governed** — every case is organized around one governing question stated as: *This review determines whether...*
3. **Deterministic** — explicit thresholds and named decision rights; vague responsibility language is insufficient
4. **Reconstructible** — every meaningful decision must leave an evidence trail that can be retrieved later
5. **Bounded** — claims must not outrun proof; unsupported claims must be named as such
6. **Re-openable** — a PHAROS packet is not permanent; material changes reopen review

---

## Five Roles

- **Accountable reviewer** — owns case integrity, confirms scope, tests evidence sufficiency, signs the disposition
- **System owner** — provides accurate system information, identifies operational realities, implements required controls
- **Decision holder(s)** — named authorities who approve, defer, override, halt, or escalate at defined thresholds
- **Evidence steward** — ensures artifacts are stored, linked to the case ID, dated, retrievable, and attributable
- **Notified stakeholders** — functions that must be informed when specific thresholds are crossed (legal, security, product, procurement, compliance, executive sponsors)

---

## When to Open a Case

Open when any of the following occurs:
- New procurement review
- Vendor onboarding or renewal
- Launch approval request
- Material model or workflow change
- New external dependency or tool access
- Audit request or audit-readiness exercise
- Incident, near miss, or escalation
- Expanded autonomy or expanded user population
- Entry into a new jurisdiction
- Challenge to public or internal claims
- Executive request for assurance

If none of these are present, do not force a PHAROS case. Record why the method was not activated.

## 13-Step Case Procedure

### Step 1 — Create the case record
Case ID, date opened, target system name, owner, requesting function, trigger type, accountable reviewer.

### Step 2 — State the pressure source
Short paragraph naming the concrete source of scrutiny. Answers: "Why is this under review now?"

### Step 3 — Write the governing question
One sentence beginning with: *This review determines whether...*
All participants should be able to repeat the same question in nearly the same words.

### Step 4 — Bound the system
Document: system name and version, intended use, owner and operators, users or affected populations, environments, inputs and outputs, dependencies, adjacent systems, explicitly excluded surfaces. Must point to a real operational object, not a brand name.

### Step 5 — Map the operational route
Minimum route map:
1. input source
2. preprocessing or transformation
3. model, rules, or retrieval layer
4. tool use or external dependency
5. human review point, if any
6. output destination
7. logging point
8. override path
9. incident or shutdown path

### Step 6 — Inventory claims and non-claims
**Supported claims** may include: limited autonomy, human-in-the-loop operation, reviewable logging, bounded use case, approval for a defined context.

**Unsupported or prohibited claims** may include: certified safe in all deployments, compliant everywhere by default, autonomous under all conditions, audit-ready without evidence, website or policy copy as proof of deployment controls.

### Step 7 — Define thresholds
For each change point, define: threshold ID, condition or trigger state, required action, decision holder, required evidence artifact, notification requirement. Thresholds must be operational and testable — not moral or rhetorical.

### Step 8 — Build the decision-rights matrix
For each threshold: who recommends, who approves, who overrides, who halts or suspends, who must be notified, who reviews after the fact. Avoid "the team decides." Use named roles with actual authority.

### Step 9 — Build the evidence register
For each step, threshold, and decision, define the artifact that proves it happened. Each artifact must be: dated, attributable, stored in an approved location, linked to the case ID, retrievable by an independent reviewer.

### Step 10 — Cross-walk to external frameworks
Map the packet to applicable external frameworks only after internal coherence is established. Do not let the external framework replace the internal method. External frameworks: internal policy controls, procurement questionnaires, audit requests, NIST AI RMF, ISO/IEC 42001, EU AI Act duties, executive reporting requirements.

### Step 11 — Conduct the PHAROS review meeting
Order: restate pressure source → restate governing question → confirm scope boundaries → walk the operational route → test thresholds → test decision rights → inspect evidence sufficiency → state disposition.

Possible dispositions: approve / approve with conditions / defer pending evidence / restrict scope / reject / suspend pending remediation.

### Step 12 — Bind the disposition into workflow
A PHAROS case is incomplete until the disposition changes practice. Required follow-through may include: updating deployment gates, revising templates, enabling monitoring, correcting unsupported claims, assigning remediation tasks, scheduling re-review.

### Step 13 — Define re-entry triggers
Record the events that require the packet to be revisited. Minimum triggers: model swap, vendor change, new data source, new tool access, new jurisdiction, expanded user population, expanded autonomy, incident or near miss, material claims change, adverse audit finding, evidence failure.

## Minimum Artifact Set (12 items)
1. Case record
2. Pressure statement
3. Governing question
4. Scope note
5. Route map
6. Claims / non-claims register
7. Threshold map
8. Decision-rights matrix
9. Evidence register
10. Cross-walk note
11. Disposition memo
12. Re-entry trigger register

---

## Five Quality Tests

A packet is ready only if it passes all five:

1. **Clarity** — can a third party tell what question the review answered?
2. **Determinism** — can a user tell what condition triggers escalation, approval, deferment, halt, or review?
3. **Accountability** — can a reviewer identify who held authority at each control point?
4. **Reconstruction** — can the decision trail be rebuilt from artifacts?
5. **Boundedness** — are the claims narrower than the evidence rather than broader?

If any answer is no, the packet is not ready.

---

## Common Failure Modes

- Scope inflation
- Vague governing questions
- Thresholds that are moral or rhetorical rather than operational
- Responsibilities named without decision rights
- Missing or inaccessible evidence
- Cross-walks done before internal control logic
- Claims that exceed proof
- One-time packet production with no re-entry logic

---

## Transfer Rule

When applying this SOP to another system, retain the structure and regenerate the content. Do not copy prior determinations without re-running the pressure source, governing question, route, thresholds, decision rights, and evidence path for the new system.
