## The Three-Phase Value Proposition

The prospectus markets PHAROS around three philosophical/mathematical figures:

**Phase 1 — Ship of Theseus**: Test persistence of identity through component substitution. Enterprise value: prove your AI governance framework survives model retraining, fine-tuning, and multi-deployment.

**Phase 2 — Auryn** (from *The Neverending Story*): Test continuity through dual recursion and mutual inversion. Enterprise value: adjudicate when authority is delegated, inverted, and recursively embedded across systems.

**Phase 3 — Hopf** (topology): Determine stable governance structure across multi-interpretation singularities. Enterprise value: resolve governance conflicts between human committees, AI systems, and institutional rules.

The problem statement: "Enterprise AI systems face a governance paradox: as models, training data, and decision criteria are continuously updated, what claims the right to call the result the same system?" Current solutions rely on audit trails and compliance checklists — not on testing whether the governance criterion itself survives replacement.

## Three Customer Segments

### Segment 1: AI Labs & Model Developers
Targets: OpenAI, Anthropic, Google DeepMind, Meta, Hugging Face, Scale AI
Problem: As models retrain and fine-tune across use cases, governance frameworks diverge. No single rule explains what makes the model the same across versions.
Fit: Professional tier ($750K/yr) + ~30-day consulting ($60K)
Revenue per deal: $1.05M+ (Year 1); renewal $750K/yr
Deal count needed for $1M: 1 lab

### Segment 2: Enterprise Risk & Compliance
Targets: JPMorgan Chase, Goldman Sachs, Uber, Stripe, Databricks, Canva
Problem: Delegated AI decision authority (credit, hiring, operations) requires provable governance continuity.
Fit: Professional tier ($750K/yr) + ~40-day consulting ($80K)
Revenue per deal: $980K–$3.75M (depending on term)
Deal count needed for $1M: 1 enterprise at $1.5M

### Segment 3: Institutional & Policy
Targets: Central banks, financial regulators, UN agencies, large endowments
Problem: Governance authority distributed across human committees, AI systems, and institutional rules — no adjudication framework when they conflict.
Fit: Enterprise tier ($2M+) with custom Phase 3 + IP licensing
Revenue per deal: $2M–$5M
Deal count needed for $1M: 1 institution at $2.5M
