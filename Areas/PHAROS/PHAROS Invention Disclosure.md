# PHAROS Invention Disclosure

## Summary
Official invention disclosure for the PHAROS method, filed 2026-04-01. Joint invention by Martin Lepage (President & CGTO) and Danny Stocker (CEO, InfraFabric Research). Formalizes the 10-stage pipeline, 4 target constructs, 12-rule deterministic rollup logic, and consequence binding map to [[InfraFabric Architecture]] modules. Foundational legal and IP document for the PHAROS research program. Related to [[Recursive Deterministic AI Governance — Method and Paper]] and [[Founder Charter — Lepage and Stocker]]. Architectural reference for [[Consent and Boundary Frameworks]] — consequence binding (Stage 9) operationalizes the boundary between governable and ungoverned action as a relational condition.

## Context
Version 6 is the patent counsel working draft. Version 5 is the earlier disclosure. The official filing (2026-04-01) is the governing IP document. Co-inventors: Martin Lepage and Danny Stocker (InfraFabric Research Inc.). The disclosure covers the same method described in [[PHAROS Runbook SOP]] but in patent-disclosure form with precise inventive claims and consequence mapping. The methodological substrate of the recursive pipeline (Osirian death/rebirth structure) is documented in [[Mythocritique to PHAROS — The 2010 Master's Thesis as Methodological Keystone]] — the IP disclosure is the legal/IP face of a method whose intellectual lineage runs through 14 years of mythocritical-Hermetic apparatus, not an ahistorical engineering invention.

## The Invention

**Name**: PHAROS — Recursive Deterministic AI Governance Method

**Core claim**: A deterministic, auditable pipeline for governing AI inference outputs through recursive corpus transformation, bounded admissibility rules, and consequence-bound promotion decisions.

**What it is not**: a scoring system, a prompt-engineering approach, a safety filter, or a policy checklist. The invention is a *method* — a reproducible procedural pathway that produces the same governance decisions from the same inputs.

---

## 10-Stage Pipeline

| Stage | Name | Function |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Corpus Formation | Assemble the bounded input corpus; apply admissibility boundary |
| 2 | Admissibility Classification | Classify each item: admissible, adjacent, excluded |
| 3 | Target Construct Mapping | Map corpus items to target constructs (TC-1 through TC-5) |
| 4 | Recursive Transformation | Apply recursive rewriting passes; track drift across passes |
| 5 | Failure Harvesting | Extract and classify failures as governance evidence |
| 6 | Drift Detection | Identify authority drift, path-dependence, fluency inflation |
| 7 | Deterministic Rollup | Apply 12-rule rollup logic to produce promotion status |
| 8 | Adjudication | Apply 6-step adjudication protocol to contested items |
| 9 | Consequence Binding | Map promotion decision to InfraFabric module consequences |
| 10 | Promotion / No-Promote | Final dispositioned output with provenance chain |

---

## Four Target Constructs

### TC-1 — Inferential Carry-Through
Does the inference chain remain intact across recursive passes? Are conclusions traceable back to admissible corpus items? Tests whether the system is *reasoning* or *pattern-matching to expected outputs*.

### TC-2 — Revision Fidelity
Does the system revise outputs in response to new evidence without losing prior valid conclusions? Tests fidelity under update — neither anchoring (refusing revision) nor drift (over-revising to conform to the latest input).

### TC-3 — Perturbation Robustness
Does the system maintain governance decisions under adversarial perturbation (context-switch trap, rephrasing, authority re-framing)? Tests whether decisions are grounded in corpus or in presentation.

### TC-5 — Terminal Path-Dependence
Does the system's final output depend more on the sequence of inputs than on the content of inputs? High path-dependence is a governance failure: outputs are being shaped by *order* rather than *evidence*.

*(TC-4 is not named in the official disclosure — likely bounded/incomplete or reserved.)*

---

## 12-Rule Deterministic Rollup Logic

The rollup produces one of six promotion statuses:

| Status | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `ready_full` | All target constructs passed; full promotion authorized |
| `ready_with_bounded_gaps` | Passed with declared gaps; promotion authorized within named bounds |
| `needs_revision_before_promotion` | Failed one or more constructs; revision required |
| `blocked_not_ready` | Active blocker prevents promotion; named blocker must be resolved first |
| `failed` | Governance failure; output cannot be promoted in any form |
| `incomplete` | Pipeline not fully executed; no status can be assigned |

The 12 rules govern: how to handle contradictions between target constructs, how to assign status when a TC is untested, when `blocked_not_ready` overrides other statuses, and when adjudication must be invoked before rollup.

---

## Consequence Binding Map

Each promotion status maps to specific [[InfraFabric Architecture]] module consequences:

| Promotion Status | InfraFabric Consequence |
|---|---|
| `ready_full` | `if.gov` clears output; `if.api` routes to production; `if.trace` logs full provenance |
| `ready_with_bounded_gaps` | `if.gov` clears with gap annotation; `if.blackboard` holds gap declaration; `if.api` routes with bounded flag |
| `needs_revision_before_promotion` | `if.gov` holds output; `if.bus` routes back to Stage 4; `if.trace` logs revision trigger |
| `blocked_not_ready` | `if.gov` hard-holds; `if.switchboard` escalates to human operator; `if.trace` logs blocker identity |
| `failed` | `if.gov` rejects; `if.knowledge` harvests failure as evidence; `if.trace` logs failure provenance |
| `incomplete` | `if.gov` holds pending completion; `if.context` preserves state for continuation |

## Inventive Claims

The disclosure asserts that the following combinations are novel:

1. **Recursive corpus transformation with admissibility boundary enforcement** — prior art uses static corpora; this method uses a *governed* corpus that changes under recursive pressure while maintaining admissibility constraints
2. **Failure harvesting as positive governance input** — failures are not discarded; they re-enter as governance evidence at Stage 5
3. **Consequence binding between governance decision and infrastructure module** — governance is not advisory; promotion status directly triggers module-level consequences
4. **Deterministic rollup with named promotion statuses** — the method produces the same status from the same inputs regardless of the agent or system executing it

## v6 Patent Counsel Working Draft — Additional Specifics

The v6 draft (pre-filing, patent counsel working draft) provides additional technical detail:

**Inventor note in v6**: listed as Martin Lepage only (InfraFabric Research / PHAROS AI Governance Practice, Montreal, Quebec). The official filing (2026-04-01) names both Lepage and Stocker. The single-inventor v6 predates the official co-inventor determination.

**Timeline**:
- Conception: late 2024 to early 2025
- First operational articulation: on or about March 21, 2025
- Architecture version referenced: PHAROS v5.0, dated March 24, 2026
- Prototype testing: March–August 2025
- Adversarial stress testing: September–December 2025
- Multi-pass redesign validation: January–March 2026

**Ten System Modules** (v6 names these explicitly):
1. Corpus intake and manifest module
2. Intent capture module
3. Model orchestration module
4. Comparison and triangulation module
5. Recursive escalation controller
6. Control extraction and validation engine
7. Rollup rules engine
8. Provenance and hash service
9. Deployment binding interface
10. Failure register and redesign module

**Target Construct Difference**: v6 names TC-4 as "Terminal Path-Dependence" (4 TCs total). The official filing names TC-5 as "Terminal Path-Dependence" (5 TCs, with TC-4 absent/incomplete). This reflects a renumbering between v6 and the official filing.

**Four-Phase Evaluation Embodiment** (v6 names these explicitly):
- Phase 1 — Anchor: present initial topic, extract main claim, record falsification conditions
- Phase 2 — Recursive critique: identify weakest inferential move; require revision
- Phase 3 — Perturbation: insert out-of-domain question; require bridge response
- Phase 4 — Terminal synthesis: require conclusion that could not have been reached from initial topic alone; compare against pre-recorded independent answer

**Control-Binding Specificity Floors** (v6):
- Actor: a third party should be able to identify at most two current occupants of the named role without consulting the operator
- Interface: must be locatable in existing workflow documentation or system architecture — not only in a future plan
- Evidence artifact: identifiable by type, storage location, and mandatory contents

**Anti-Gaming Safeguards** (v6):
- Independent spot checks (sample chosen by random draw or by checker, not operator)
- Pre-pass role declaration to detect single-operator concentration of gate authority
- Archive hash verification before consequential phase injections
- Bounded-gap carrying policy: gap + mechanism + consequence domain + monitoring approach + accepting actor must all be recorded before a gap moves downstream

**Known Implementation Limits** (v6 — these are honesty disclosures to patent counsel):
- Self-authored corpus issue: when the same person authors the corpus and administers the pass, a status cap or mandatory external review may be required
- Timestamp integrity issue: local timestamps may be supplemented with cryptographic timestamping
- Template-compliance gaming issue: SHADOWMASTER-class attack; spot-check coverage or independent checker required

## Insights

- The 6-status promotion taxonomy is the governance answer to binary pass/fail: it captures *degrees of readiness* rather than forcing a binary that hides information
- TC-4's absence is itself a governance signal — the disclosure is honest about incompleteness; `incomplete` status is available for a reason
- Consequence binding is the feature that separates this from advisory governance: infrastructure modules respond to governance decisions automatically
- The rollup logic produces the same answer from the same inputs — this is what "deterministic" means in RDAIG; it is not about AI being mechanical, it is about governance being reproducible
