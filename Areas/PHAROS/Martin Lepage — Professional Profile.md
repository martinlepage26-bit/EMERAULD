## The Profile as a Whole

The CV represents an unusual combination: PhD in humanities → academic scholar in ritual/media studies → AI governance consultant → co-founder of an AI infrastructure company → CGTO with IP ownership. The trajectory is coherent: the scholarly interest in how authority is produced, contested, and legitimated (régime de preuve, ritual authority, governance by denial) becomes the foundation for a practical method for governing AI authority claims.

The clinical trial coordinator role is the anomalous element — a four-month contract that interrupts the consulting trajectory. The CDPDJ complaint against Calian/Novartis suggests the role ended under adverse conditions, which may itself be relevant to the healthcare governance work (direct experience of governance failure in a regulated research environment).

## Insights

- The humanities PhD is not a liability for AI governance work — it is the credential for the specific contribution: governance methodology that handles contested authority, partial evidence, and narrative control, which are precisely the problems that technical AI safety research tends to undertheorize
- The January 2026 certification cluster suggests a deliberate repositioning moment: acquiring formal AI credentials to credentialize a practice that was already substantively developed
- The creative work volume (multiple novels + monographs in 2025 alone) suggests a practice where creative and scholarly production are parallel, not sequential — the novels are not sabbatical projects, they are concurrent with the governance work
- The CGTO role is a title that ML appears to have defined: it does not exist in standard corporate governance frameworks. The definition — "the person who owns the conditions under which the engine may be trusted" — is specific to the PHAROS architecture
