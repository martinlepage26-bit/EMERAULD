# Historical Academic Portfolio — Pre-PHAROS Scholarly Work

## Summary
Index of Martin Lepage's pre-PHAROS scholarly work found in the raw sources archive. The archive contains 100+ DOCX files spanning 2002-2024 (and some earlier material). Most are drafts, revisions, or versions of papers from the publication record documented in [[Sealed Card Protocol — Legitimacy, Glitch, and Charging]]. Core themes: queer ritual practice, neo-Paganism, gender, authority, popular culture (Buffy, Avatar, Charmed, Agatha All Along, Jurassic Park). All attributed to Martin Lepage, PhD per the authorship instruction for undated/untitled files in this archive.

## Context
The archive contains Martin Lepage's complete scholarly history from PhD work onward. Files with dates in filenames (1980 = likely a file index, not year; 1993-2026 appear to span the actual scholarly career). The filenames use various date formats — some represent publication years, others are likely version/draft sequence numbers.

For externally validated publication metadata, homonym screening, paywalled/open-access status, and acquisition gaps, see [[Martin Lepage Publications — Annotated Bibliography and Verification Leads]]. This portfolio note remains the internal raw-source and draft-history map.

## Key Works by Period

### Early Scholarship (Pre-PhD)
- **Pourquoi rêver encore** — Allegorical and metaphysical reading of Yvon Rivard's initial work (CV/resume format)
- **Reboot** — Multiple versions (1994, 2001) — appears to be an early essay or governance-adjacent piece; filename "policy_or_guidance" for one version

### Neo-Paganism and Gender (2002-2017)
Core ethnographic and theoretical work that forms the PhD foundation:

- **Queer Neo-Pagan Spiritualities and the Question of Authenticity in Wicca** (2002, 2017 versions) — the direct precursor to the Social Compass paper; traces the authenticity question across 15 years
- **(Neo-)Shamanism(s) and Gender: Narratives from the Montreal Pagan Community** (2003, 2010, 2013 versions) — the paper published in *Lithuanian Journal of Anthropology* (2019)
- **THE INNER GODDESS: Gender Normativity and Gay Identity in Wiccan Ritualities** (2003)
- **Pagan Magic: Identity, Religion, and the Power of Norms** (2005, 2013 versions)
- **A Lokian Family** (2013) — published in *Pomegranate* (2013)
- **Why be king? I'm already a queen!** (2017) — title of the PhD dissertation
- **Social Stratification and Social Inequality: Models of Intersectionality and Identity** (2017) — likely a course material or book chapter

### Popular Culture and Governance (2007-2024)
The trajectory from cultural analysis toward governance analysis:

- **Magic After Legitimacy: Witchcraft, Sisterhood, and the Work of Power in Charmed** (2007) — applies the legitimacy framework to the TV series Charmed
- **Every Hair a Battle Scar: Buffy Summers' Hair as Narrative Engine, Cultural Icon, and Post-Feminist Battleground** (2007) — early Buffy paper; notes/journal format
- **Avatar: Une lecture queer du pouvoir incarné** (2012) — French-language thesis/dissertation piece; queer reading of power in Avatar: The Last Airbender
- **Chapter** (2012) — unspecified; likely a book chapter
- **Virtually Magical, Literally** (2013) — early version of the digital Wicca theme
- **Method (Collaborative Autoethnography)** (2013) — methodological piece
- **Authority Without Ethics: Ritual Power and the Cultural Life of Witchcraft in *The Love Witch*** (2016) — film analysis

- **Authority After Legitimacy: Fairy Tales, Governance, and Political Failure in Disenchantment** (2023) — connects to the governance work; listed as forthcoming in the publication record
- **Unnatural Selection: Queer Method and Posthuman Commons in Jurassic Park** (1993, 2022 versions) — older paper revised; queer posthumanism through Jurassic Park
- **Introduction: The Ritual Machine Lab, or, How Stories Train the Body Into Obedience While Claiming to Set It Free** (2025) — a Special Issue or edited volume introduction

## Key Thematic Threads Across the Portfolio

### Thread 1 — Authority Without Origin
From the early Wicca work through *Agatha All Along*: authority that claims no institutional grounding, only performative credibility. The governance methodology is the culmination: an authority that earns credibility through method, not position.

### Thread 2 — Popular Culture as Governance
Buffy, Avatar, Charmed, Jurassic Park, Disenchantment — each analyzed as a site where authority, legitimacy, gender, and institutional power are staged and contested. The 2007 Charmed paper ("Magic After Legitimacy") already uses the legitimacy vocabulary that appears in the 2025-2026 governance work.

### Thread 3 — Queerness as Method
"Refusing Fixity" (2011) and "Unnatural Selection" (2022) — queerness applied as an epistemological and methodological position, not just a cultural content area. This is the thread that becomes "queerness is the refusal to cohere" in the 2026 publication record statement.

### Thread 4 — Posthumanism and Non-Human Agency
Avatar, Jurassic Park — non-human actors (dinosaurs, elemental powers, animal familiars) as sites for theorizing agency, control, and posthuman kinship. The InfraFabric modules in [[InfraFabric Architecture]] inherit this: AI infrastructure as non-human agents with consequential authority.

## Insights

- The 2007 Charmed paper ("Magic After Legitimacy") suggests the legitimacy vocabulary was already operative in the popular culture work 18 years before the PHAROS governance method. The intellectual history is not a sharp turn from cultural studies to AI governance — it is a gradual intensification of the same governance analysis
- "Refusing Fixity" (2011) and "Authority After Legitimacy" (2023) form bookends around the PhD work: the first establishes the theoretical refusal of fixed authority; the second applies it to political failure in a contemporary media context
- The Jurassic Park paper's longevity (1993 → 2022 revision) suggests a persistent interest in the posthuman commons — shared ecological, technological, and bodily conditions that exceed human governance. This connects to the posthumanism framework in [[The Returning Light — Monograph]]
