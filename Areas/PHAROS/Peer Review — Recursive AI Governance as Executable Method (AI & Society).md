# Peer Review — Recursive AI Governance as Executable Method (AI & Society)

## Summary

Structural and developmental peer review of Paper B (*Recursive AI Governance as Executable Method: The Very Long Narrative*), targeting *AI & Society* (Springer). Reviewer stance: external submission-readiness audit. Verdict: **not yet submittable** in current form — three dominant problems (structural duplication, buried empirical object, submission-readiness gaps). Recommendation: major revision, priority order specified. The intellectual substance is publishable; the manuscript is a structural and production job away from being defensible. Connects to [[AI Society Manuscript — From AI Anxiety to Recursive Governance]] and to [[First Method Paper — Recursive AI Governance as Executable Method]].

## Context

This review was generated as a submission-readiness audit for the second major PHAROS paper in the pipeline. The paper proposes a three-order model of recursive AI governance (internalization → protocolization → distributed build enforcement), grounded in the bounded PHAROS archive. See [[RDAIG Method Editorial Consolidation — 2026]] for the editorial work on this manuscript, and [[Academic Paper Pipeline]] for the full paper pipeline status.

The review is against the *AI & Society* standards specifically: qualitative archival study, sociotechnical governance, recursive method. The argument is genre-appropriate; the problems are structural and production.

## Details

### Summary Statement (Review)

> *"This is a conceptually ambitious, methodologically self-aware qualitative archival study that proposes a three-order model of recursive AI governance (internalization → protocolization → distributed build enforcement), grounded in a bounded PHAROS archive. The underlying argument is genuinely publishable and genre-appropriate for AI & Society."*

The manuscript treats governance as sociotechnical, takes recursive/agentic methods seriously, and locates control inside the production loop rather than outside it.

### Three Dominant Problems (in severity order)

**1. Structural Duplication (most severe)**

§Background and Context (¶17–23) and §Theoretical Framework (¶24–30) are near-verbatim duplicates. Six long paragraphs repeat the same literature, archive description, protocol-stack summary, and positioning claim, with only cosmetic substitutions (*"AI" ↔ "artificial intelligence"*). Once collapsed, the §Literature Review also becomes partially redundant.

**2. Buried Empirical Object and Contribution**

The empirical object (the PHAROS archive) and the contribution (a method history + three-order operational model) are not visible until ¶10, after ~1,500 words of citation-heavy context. For *AI & Society*, both must be legible on page 2.

**3. Submission-Readiness Gaps**

Hard blockers: no reference list, no keywords, no author metadata, no positionality statement, no data-availability statement, no corpus counts, multiple *"AUTHOR TO CLARIFY"* and *"date?"* placeholders, and ~30 uses of *"internal archive source"* as a citation. These are standard Springer submission requirements.

### Recommended Priority Order

(a) **Collapse** Literature Review / Background / Theoretical Framework into a single Related Work section — eliminates the duplication problem in one editorial move.

(b) **Rewrite the Introduction** so the research problem, empirical object, and contribution sit on page 2. This is the visibility fix.

(c) **Fix the submission-artefact gaps** — reference list, keywords, metadata, positionality, data availability, corpus counts, all "CLARIFY" placeholders resolved.

### Assessment Against Author's Specific Revision Requests

The review is structured around the author's specific requests (overlap collapse and contribution legibility). The overlap is real and resolvable in a single editorial pass. The contribution legibility problem requires restructuring the Introduction to front-load the claim — not just tightening existing language.

### The Three-Order Model (Contribution)

The paper proposes:
1. **Internalization** — governance norms embedded in agent behavior
2. **Protocolization** — norms codified into explicit protocols
3. **Distributed build enforcement** — protocols operationalized through the build/audit cycle

This model is the paper's publishable core. The review confirms it is genre-appropriate and genuinely novel for *AI & Society*.
