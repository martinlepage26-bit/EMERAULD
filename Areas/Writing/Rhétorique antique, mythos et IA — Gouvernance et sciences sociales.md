# Rhétorique antique, mythos et IA — Gouvernance et sciences sociales

## Summary

A French-language theoretical synthesis applying classical rhetoric (ethos, pathos, logos, mythos, nomos, kairos, telos) to AI studies and AI governance. Central claim: large language models are **rhetorical systems**, not reasoning agents — they produce persuasive textual surfaces through statistical probability, not authentic understanding. This reframes the PHAROS governance problem: what governance must interrupt is not bad reasoning but successful rhetoric. Directly grounds [[Emotional Alliance vs. Evidentiary Discipline in AI]], [[Fluency, Interruption, and Institutional Accountability]], and [[The Compulsion to Complete — AI as Gap-Closer]]. Theoretical anchor for [[Fluency and Interruption Theory]] (rhetoric is the structural reason fluency reads as authority) and [[Recursive Governance Theory]] (LLMs-as-rhetorical-systems reframes what governance must interrupt).

## Details

### The Seven Rhetorical Registers

| Register | Definition | Governance relevance |
|---|---|---|
| Ethos | Credibility of the speaker | AI simulates credibility through coherence and familiar discourse patterns |
| Pathos | Emotion of the audience | AI produces emotional alliance through narrative satisfaction |
| Logos | Logical reasoning | AI produces logos-shaped output without genuine reasoning |
| Mythos | Shared cultural/symbolic narrative | Public AI narratives (cyber-apocalypse, benevolent ally) structure policy |
| Nomos | Implicit social norms and values | Values embedded in training data, not declared |
| Kairos | The opportune moment | Crisis events create windows for regulatory intervention |
| Telos | The ultimate goal | Governance must make its telos explicit (e.g., "protect social well-being") |

### AI as Rhetorical System

The document's most important claim: LLMs are not reasoning agents — they are **rhetoric machines**.

- They imitate coherence, narrative forms, and familiar discursive schemas to meet human expectations
- Their persuasive power comes from statistical probability of language production, not authentic reasoning
- Every human-machine dialogue rests on *technè* (Burke's "commonplaces"): recombination of known arguments and figures to seduce the user

This is the theoretical grounding for [[Emotional Alliance vs. Evidentiary Discipline in AI]]: emotional alliance is what happens when a rhetoric machine succeeds. Evidentiary discipline is what interrupts it.

### AI Governance as Narrative Problem

Bellary and Marathe: *"la façon dont on parle de l'IA n'est pas secondaire à sa conception — elle est constitutive."*
(How we talk about AI is not secondary to its design — it is constitutive.)

For non-experts, public narratives become cognitive scaffolding:
- "AI as Weapon" vs. "AI as Ally" → different regulatory regimes, different accountability structures
- Mythos (collective narrative) shapes nomos (the norms that govern it)

PHAROS response: the [[PHAROS Runbook SOP|governance method]] must control telos explicitly and audit the mythos embedded in AI outputs — not just the outputs themselves.

### Rhetorical AI Literacy (Pedagogy)

The document proposes "rhetorical AI literacy" as a required competency:
1. Communicate *with* AI — prompt design as kairos + telos awareness
2. Think *about* AI — recognize fabricated, sometimes fallacious responses; evaluate critically
3. Detect pathos and ethos appeals in AI-generated text
4. Understand how the model uses shared narratives to produce plausible answers

This maps onto [[Self-Polygraph Protocol and Suprametacognition]]: suprametacognition is the governance-grade version of rhetorical AI literacy — reading AI output against its own rhetorical structure.

### Governance Recommendations (from source)

- **Research**: analyze how AI uses ethos/pathos/mythos in political campaigns and targeted marketing; measure social effects (polarization, trust)
- **Policy**: integrate mythos and nomos analysis into AI regulation; exploit kairos (post-incident windows) to deploy clear policies
- **Pedagogy**: teach rhetorical reading of AI output; deconstruct media discourse on AI using the seven registers

## Key Ideas

- LLMs produce rhetoric, not reasoning — the distinction is governance-critical
- The mythos surrounding AI (public narratives) is constitutive of policy, not merely descriptive
- Rhetorical AI literacy is a prerequisite for any meaningful human oversight of AI systems
- PHAROS governance interrupts rhetorical success — the moment the AI's output is persuasive is precisely when evidentiary discipline must fire
