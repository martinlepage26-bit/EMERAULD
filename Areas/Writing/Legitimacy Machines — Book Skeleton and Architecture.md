# Legitimacy Machines — Book Skeleton and Architecture

## Summary
Table of contents and structural skeleton for the book-length theoretical project *Legitimacy Machines, Altars of Control: Governing Wonder and Training Power Across Screen Worlds*. Author: Martin Lepage, PhD. The skeleton contains the chapter sequence (7 screen objects), the book's introduction (also documented in [[Ritual Machine Lab — Legitimacy Machines Introduction]]), and chapter-level summaries. A theoretical capstone connecting cultural studies, queer theory, ritual studies, and AI governance. Related to [[Ritual Machine Lab — Legitimacy Machines Introduction]], [[Historical Academic Portfolio — Pre-PHAROS Scholarly Work]], and [[Martin Lepage — Professional Profile]].

## Book Architecture

**Full title**: *Legitimacy Machines, Altars of Control: Governing Wonder and Training Power Across Screen Worlds*

**Core argument**: Screen media are not merely representations of social reality — they are **legitimacy machines**: systems that train audiences in the governance norms of authority, power, and permission through repeated, ritualistically structured narratives.

**Unit of analysis**: Technique (not theme, character, or representation) — how narrative mechanisms produce governance effects in audiences through patterned repetition.

## Thematic Architecture

The book is structured around three thematic movements:

**Movement 1 — Enclosure and Its Failures (Chapters 1-3)**
How legitimacy machines enforce enclosure — the concentration of authority, power, and permission in controlled bodies and institutions — and how that enclosure systematically fails.

- Buffy: the Chosen One as enclosure; Season 7's redistribution as enclosure failure
- Avatar: bending as embodied authority that cannot be institutionally enclosed
- Charmed: sisterhood as quasi-institutional power that operates outside both state and religious authority

**Movement 2 — Certification and Its Frauds (Chapters 4-5)**
How legitimacy machines produce certification — processes that verify authority, legitimate power, and authorize permission — and how those certifications can be emptied of ethical content.

- Agatha: the Road certifies; Agatha discovers the certification is self-generated
- The Love Witch: formal expertise without ethical grounding; closed moral systems

**Movement 3 — After Authority (Chapters 6-7)**
What governance looks like when legitimacy has been revealed as fraudulent and the post-sovereign community must continue without the institutional anchors it depended on.

- Disenchantment: five authority models for the post-sovereign polity
- Jurassic Park: the posthuman commons after human governance fails

## Insights

- The 7-chapter sequence maps almost exactly onto the thematic threads in [[Historical Academic Portfolio — Pre-PHAROS Scholarly Work]]: each screen object has a corresponding academic paper already written or in development. The book is the synthesis that shows how all these individual analyses are stages in a single governance argument
- The thematic three-movement structure (enclosure → certification → post-authority) is the same governance arc as the PHAROS methodology: scoping what exists (enclosure), validating it (certification), then determining what to do when validation fails (post-authority governance)
