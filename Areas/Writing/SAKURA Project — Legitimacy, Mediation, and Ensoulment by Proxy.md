# SAKURA Project — Legitimacy, Mediation, and Ensoulment by Proxy

## Summary
SAKURA is [[Martin Lepage — Professional Profile|Martin Lepage's]] three-arm experimental research project examining legitimacy, mediation, and the politics of recognition in knowledge-making, art, and ritual practice. Named after Cardcaptor Sakura — the Clow Cards released into the world as elemental powers — the project treats each paper or artifact as a sealed card released into an adventure. The central question: can AI "charge" an artifact — ensoul it by proxy? Connected to [[Agatha All Along — Wicca, Digital Mediatization, and Proof Regimes]], [[PHAROS AI Ethics Submission — Springer Draft]], [[Alchemy of the Wound — Novel]], [[HEXA Press — Publishing and Practice Initiative]], and [[Wheels of Will — Ritual Bottle Art and Research-Creation Practice]].

## Details

### The Three Arms
**Arm 1 — Academic**: Knowledge-making as institutional practice with genre conventions (article, review, method), evaluative rituals (peer review, editorial gatekeeping), and norms of fluency, pace, and professionalism. AI as legibility prosthesis for a scholar with stuttering, cognitive disability, and institutional trauma.

**Arm 2 — Artistic**: HEXA and [[Wheels of Will — Ritual Bottle Art and Research-Creation Practice|Wheels of Will]] as research-creation laboratory testing contested authenticity in the age of digital production. What does an artifact become when materiality, authorship, and aura are increasingly mediated? (Follows Benjamin 1936; Haraway 1985.) Published work on Amazon includes *Underwatch Rigor* and a three-book communication/knowledge series.

**Arm 3 — Ritual and Spiritual**: Contemporary witchcraft as technology of transformation and survival. Lived experience as a mode of knowing, repair, and agency (Schechner 2002). The ritual arm insists on epistemic status equal to the academic arm — not metaphor, not supplement, but a competing epistemic regime.

### The Core Philosophical Question
Can AI "ensoul" by proxy — instill with soul — what it co-creates? The portrait example: "What you did was definitely me, even though it was not me." Social perception still confers objecthood. The artifact becomes public-facing evidence even when mediation is visible.

### Legitimacy as Infrastructure
Legitimacy is not a property of texts, images, or bodies. It is produced through institutions that decide what counts as evidence, what counts as work, and what counts as a "real" author. SAKURA treats the glitch — the moment a reader detects mediation — as the site where legitimacy conversion either happens or fails.

### Disability and Crip Time
Drawing on Kafer's "crip time" (disability scholarship): academic legitimacy presumes a "normal" tempo and body. AI can function as a legibility prosthesis — compressing editorial labor, stabilizing genre form, accelerating revision cycles. But this makes mediation visible, triggering suspicion precisely because institutions were never only judging ideas but judging who could perform the right kind of labor in the right kind of time.

### The HEXA Pipeline
Martin's academic publishing method with AI:
1. Write outline + core ideas
2. Feed highlighted references to AI
3. Include journal-specific constraints from the start (audience first)
4. AI produces first draft by section (background → lit review → framework → analysis → results → conclusion → abstract/intro)
5. Iterative review passes with AI acting as journal-specific reviewer
6. Final editorial passes for accuracy, references, page compliance
Result: 8 of ~20 submissions published within 8–10 months, including in previously inaccessible journals. Recognized for legibility, clarity, and accessibility.

### The Charging Metaphor
Charging: the act of instilling meaning, loading an artifact with credibility or suspicion as it travels. "Ensoulment by proxy" — not because an object contains an essence, but because social worlds read mediation and decide what feels real enough to trust. The witch bottle (from archaeology) becomes the model: accepted as magical without experimental replication, purely through pattern, record, and persistence.

### What SAKURA Changes
The paper refuses both the "AI as liberation" narrative and the "AI as corruption" panic. Instead: treat mediation as a site of *accountable practice*. Evidence: markdown trace of interaction chain, procedural visibility rather than interior authenticity claims.

## Key Ideas
- Legitimacy is a conversion problem, not a property of texts
- The glitch is where charging either happens or fails — not a bug but a recognition event
- AI is not writing for you; it is redistributing labor to test what institutions actually reward
- Crip time is governance — tempo is a sorting mechanism built into institutional structures
- Witch bottles are compact theories of legitimacy: standing in for protection without institutional verification

## Insights
- SAKURA is the most explicitly autoethnographic of Martin's projects, using his own body, disability, and spiritual practice as data
- The three-arm design parallels the three-arm methodology of [[Recursive Deterministic AI Governance — Method and Paper]] — both treat ambiguity as a probe rather than a problem
