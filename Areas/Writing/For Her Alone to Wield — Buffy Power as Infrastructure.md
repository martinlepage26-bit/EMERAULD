# For Her Alone to Wield — Buffy Power as Infrastructure

## Summary
Academic article analyzing power in *Buffy the Vampire Slayer* as infrastructure — a distributed, contestable, and governable system — rather than as property held by individuals. Full title: "For Her Alone to Wield? Infrastructure, Redistribution, and the Politics of the Scythe in *Buffy the Vampire Slayer*." Author: Martin Lepage, PhD. Submission target: *Slayage: The Whedon Studies Journal*. Central framework: **enclosure → redistribution → fragmentation** as the arc of Slayer power across 7 seasons. Key paper for [[Authority, Legitimacy, and Post-Sovereignty]] — power-as-infrastructure is the post-sovereign reading of Slayer authority. Related to [[The Scythe Already in Motion — Buffy, Queer Ritual, and the Politics of Glitch]], [[Ritual Machine Lab — Legitimacy Machines Introduction]], and [[Historical Academic Portfolio — Pre-PHAROS Scholarly Work]].

## Central Framework: Power as Infrastructure

The article's methodological intervention: reading power in BtVS not as **property** (who has it, who deserves it) but as **infrastructure** (how it flows, who can access it, what institutional conditions shape distribution).

This shifts analysis from:
- "Is Buffy's power feminist?" → to → "What governance conditions produce or foreclose collective access to power?"
- "Is the Chosen One narrative empowering?" → to → "What does it mean that power requires a single body to function?"

**The infrastructure reading**: Power in BtVS is a system with intake conditions (Calling), maintenance requirements (training, the Council), distribution architecture (one Slayer at a time), and failure modes (when infrastructure breaks, power diffuses without governance).

## Three-Phase Arc

### Phase 1 — Enclosure (Seasons 1-6)
Power is centralized through multiple mechanisms:
- **Biological enclosure**: one Slayer called at a time; power passes at death
- **Institutional enclosure**: the Watchers Council controls training, resources, and information
- **Narrative enclosure**: the Chosen One mythology frames singular power as natural, ordained, and necessary

Enclosure produces legibility and manageability at the cost of vulnerability — one death breaks the chain.

### Phase 2 — Redistribution (Season 7 / "Chosen")
Willow's spell activates all potential Slayers globally. Enclosure shatters. Power becomes:
- **Collective**: thousands of Slayers instead of one
- **Uncontrolled**: no institutional apparatus to govern the newly activated
- **Disruptive**: every potential Slayer's life changed without consent or preparation

This is the article's political crux: redistribution is presented as liberation in the narrative, but the article reads it as a governance failure — **power distributed without infrastructure is exposure, not emancipation**.

### Phase 3 — Fragmentation (Season 8 comics and beyond)
Post-redistribution, Slayer power fragments:
- Some Slayers ally with Buffy; others become Twilight's army
- Institutional vacuum produces competing factions
- The lack of governance architecture turns the redistribution into a power war rather than a liberation
- Melaka Fray (future Slayer) inherits a world where redistribution without governance produced centuries of institutional dysfunction

## The Scythe as Governance Artifact

The article's central object: the Scythe is not primarily a weapon but a **legitimacy device** — it determines who can act as authorizing agent of Slayer power.

- **Before Season 7**: the Scythe is found in a tomb, controlled by a "Guardian" outside both Council and demon power structures — a third authority
- **During "Chosen"**: Buffy uses the Scythe to channel Willow's redistribution spell — the Scythe becomes the instrument of institutional redesign
- **After redistribution**: the Scythe's governance function is unresolved — it was designed for one Slayer, not thousands

**The governance reading**: Whoever holds the Scythe holds the authority to determine the conditions of legitimate Slayer power. The Season 7 arc is not primarily about defeating the First Evil — it is about whether the governance architecture of Slayer power can be redesigned mid-crisis. It cannot, without fragmentation.

## Key Theoretical Connections

- **Power as infrastructure** connects directly to the [[Fluency, Interruption, and Institutional Accountability]] analysis: systems that operate as infrastructure (continuous, invisible, taken-for-granted) are governance objects even when they appear natural
- **Enclosure → redistribution → fragmentation** maps onto the governance problem in the [[PHAROS Invention Disclosure]] pipeline: a system that distributes outputs without governance controls produces fragmentation, not liberation
- **Redistribution without infrastructure** is the Buffy version of the [[Governance by Denial]] mechanism: formal power grants without the capacity infrastructure to exercise them are not rights — they are exposure

## Insights

- The "Chosen" spell is the series' most explicit governance failure: a system redesigned in a crisis without adequate time for stakeholder consultation, institutional design, or consequence modeling. The result is not utopia but decades of conflict. This is the strongest argument in the Buffy corpus for why governance redesign requires process — not just good intentions
- The article's contribution to Buffy Studies is methodological: previous scholarship focuses on feminism, representation, and cultural impact. This article shifts to political theory and governance infrastructure — a significant disciplinary expansion
- The Scythe as legitimacy device connects to the [[Sealed Card Protocol — Legitimacy, Glitch, and Charging]] five-concept mechanism: the Scythe is a charging object, a seam-producing artifact (its appearance in Season 7 fires the glitch that rewrites the governance architecture), and the site where legitimacy is institutionally converted
