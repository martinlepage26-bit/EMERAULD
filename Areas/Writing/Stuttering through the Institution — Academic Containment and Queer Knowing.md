# Stuttering through the Institution — Academic Containment and Queer Knowing

## Summary

Autoethnographic essay by [[Martin Lepage — Professional Profile|Martin Lepage]] tracing the layered exclusions faced by a queer scholar navigating academic life — institutional betrayal, failed mentorship, public silencing, and symbolic containment. ~7,129 words. Companion to [[Beyond Fluency — Stuttering, Autoethnography, and Unstable Epistemology]] and part of [[Research and Papers MOC]].

## Context

Where [[Beyond Fluency — Stuttering, Autoethnography, and Unstable Epistemology]] develops the theoretical framework of compulsory fluency as an institutional sorting mechanism, this essay provides the autobiographical account that grounds it. It documents [[Martin Lepage — Professional Profile|Martin Lepage]]'s seven years in a francophone university where queer scholarship on contemporary Paganism was treated as marginal and unserious — and where his stutter made him additionally vulnerable to academic authority's demand for smooth, neutral performance. The essay is directly relevant to [[Fluency, Interruption, and Institutional Accountability]] and [[Refusing Fixity — Aesthetic Governance and Fugitive Authorship]]. As autoethnography, the essay is itself a *texte-rite* — see [[Mythocritique to PHAROS — The 2010 Master's Thesis as Methodological Keystone|the keystone]]; *le silence* of the recent [[Pourquoi rêver encore — Lecture allégorique et métaphysique d'Yvon Rivard|Pourquoi rêver encore]] essay is the L99-Gap-in-prose form this essay enacts. Part of [[Writing and Novels MOC]] and [[Narrative and Method Integration — Creative Articulations of Governance]].

## Details

**Form:** Critical autoethnography.

**Subject:** Symbolic containment — the practice by which institutions **tolerate marginalized scholars just long enough to extract value**, while withholding access to power, authorship, and continuity.

**Narrative structure:** The author traces how his queerness ("too vivid"), his embodied research on Paganism ("too embodied"), and his stutter ("performed smooth, neutral authority that academic culture rewards" — which the stutter prevented) combined to ensure he was allowed to remain but never invited to belong.

**The stutter reclaimed:** Not as a flaw but as a **pedagogical method** — a form of interruption that reveals the compulsory fluency demands of academic institutional life. "To remain, to speak, and to resist, is to refuse erasure on one's own terms."

**Key argument:** The university rewards fluency, conformity, and containment while punishing disruption. Academic authority is performed through smooth speech; disruption of that smooth performance — whether through stutter, queerness, or unconventional research topics — is treated as incompetence.

**The defense as site of power:** The author's doctoral defense is analyzed as a site of both vulnerability and power — passed, boxes checked, but the reality behind the paper trail was more complex.

**Keywords:** Autoethnography; Institutional Betrayal; Queer Methodology; Academic Containment; Epistemic Exclusion.

## Key Ideas

- Symbolic containment: institutions tolerate marginalized scholars without granting access to structural power
- The stutter as pedagogy: disruption reveals the governance functions of institutional fluency
- Epistemic exclusion by form: knowledge produced through unconventional or embodied means is treated as less rigorous, regardless of substance
- The paper trail versus the lived reality: official records of institutional success can coexist with systematic exclusion

## Insights
