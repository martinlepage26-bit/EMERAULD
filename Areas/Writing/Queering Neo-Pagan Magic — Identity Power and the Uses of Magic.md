# Queering Neo-Pagan Magic — Identity Power and the Uses of Magic

## Summary

Academic article (2026) by [[Martin Lepage — Professional Profile|Martin Lepage]] extending the Montreal ethnographic arc to place the category of "magic" at the center of analysis — not as residual belief or marginal ritual form, but as a queer vector where gender, power, and cosmology intersect. Argues that queer magical practices challenge the academic field to confront its own normative logics, and that magic is a queer methodology of self and world-making. Part of [[Research and Papers MOC]] and [[Historical Academic Portfolio — Pre-PHAROS Scholarly Work]].

## Context

**Source overlap note:** Both this note and [[Queering Neo-Pagan Magic — 2026 Paper]] draw from the same raw file (`2026 - Pagan Magic Identity, Power, and the Uses of Magic.docx`). They are not duplicates — [[Queering Neo-Pagan Magic — 2026 Paper]] documents the paper's technical architecture (nomadic epistemology, tactical illegitimacy, six-section structure, five interlocutors) while this note captures the paper's epistemological stance and methodological framing (queering as method, threshold dwelling, refusal of genealogy). Consult both for a complete picture.

This paper is a direct continuation of [[Queering Neo-Pagan Magic — 2006 Paper]], [[Queering Neo-Pagan Magic — 2025 Revised Version]], and the broader ethnographic corpus. It engages explicitly with Michael Bailey's "The Meanings of Magic" (2006) while refusing genealogical stabilization of the term. The 20-year queer Pagan Magic genealogy is the sociocritical extension of the 2010 mythocritique substrate (see [[Mythocritique to PHAROS — The 2010 Master's Thesis as Methodological Keystone]]). Connects to [[The Circle Remains Open — Toward a Queer Sociology of the Sacred]], [[Queer Neo-Pagan Spiritualities and the Question of Authenticity in Wicca]], [[Queer Sociology of Magic and Ritual]], and [[Why Be King Im Already a Queen — Book Project]].

## Details

**Epistemic refusal as opening move:** Rather than tracing the academic genealogy of magic (as Bailey does), the paper begins with an **epistemological refusal** — not from disregard but from strategy. It resists the impulse to stabilize magic through taxonomy and instead attends to how magic is inhabited, felt, and mobilized by queer practitioners.

**Engagement with Bailey (2006):** Bailey contends that magic's analytical utility resides in its capacity to demarcate the limits of religious legitimacy. The paper's interlocutors dwell precisely at those thresholds — not merely testing Bailey's limits but twisting, bending, and reimagining them. Magic becomes a **modality of queer survival and speculative world-making**.

**"To queer" as method:** Queering implies a deliberate, layered movement — simultaneously adapting to and resisting dominant normative frameworks. It is a form of critical negotiation: making space within oppressive systems while refusing full containment. It reorients the gaze, subverts the expected, lingers in contradiction, and destabilizes gender/sexuality/power categories while revealing their regulatory logics.

**Grounding in prior fieldwork:** The paper draws explicitly on earlier ethnographic work on queer Pagan practitioners in Montreal — the same fieldwork base as [[Queering Neo-Pagan Magic — 2006 Paper]] and [[Queer Neo-Pagan Spiritualities and the Question of Authenticity in Wicca]]. Specifically references "non-binary practitioners articulating spiritual legitimacy in spaces still structured by assumptions of gender complementarity."

**Magic as tactical intervention:** In earlier work, rituals operated as tactical interventions — recasting sacred meaning in ways that exposed and unsettled binary scaffolding. This paper extends that finding to magic specifically: magical practice is not just an expression of identity but a **queer methodology** for actively reconfiguring relations to self, world, and other.

**Author positionality:** Uses first-person plural ("our previous ethnographic work," "our interlocutors"), signaling collaborative or longitudinal positioning.

## Key Ideas

- Magic as queer vector: not reducible to superstition or easily assimilable to institutionalized religion — it operates where gender, power, and cosmology intersect in ways that defy disciplinary taxonomy
- Epistemological refusal: refusing to stabilize magic through academic genealogy is itself a methodological/political act
- Queer survival and speculative world-making: magic is the form through which practitioners construct livable relations to a world structured against them
- Threshold dwelling: queer Pagan practitioners do not just test Bailey's limits of religious legitimacy — they inhabit those limits and transform them
- Magic as methodology: not only a religious or symbolic act, but a queer practice of self and world production

## Insights

- This paper is the most explicitly methodological of the Pagan series — it makes magic itself into a theoretical tool, not just an object of study. This move parallels what the [[First Method Paper — Recursive AI Governance as Executable Method]] does for AI governance: making the method visible as a thing that does work.
- The paper's use of "we" raises a question about co-authorship or longitudinal framing — worth clarifying when situating this in the publication record.
- The turn from "authenticity" (2017 paper) to "magic as methodology" (2026) represents the final theoretical move in the ethnographic arc: what began as a question about legitimacy ends as a claim about epistemology.
