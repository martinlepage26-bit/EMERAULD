# Who's the Boob Who's the Trap — AI Governance Through the Booby-Trap Device

## Summary

Book manuscript by [[Martin Lepage — Professional Profile]] arguing that AI governance is a long civilizational problem about defense, hierarchy, labor, secrecy, violence, and legitimacy — not merely a technical risk-control problem. The booby-trap device (a mechanism that prearranges consequence by anticipating movement) becomes a reading practice for how modern AI systems govern through hidden triggers, distributed responsibility, and asymmetric exposure. Connects to [[AI Governance Manifesto — Upstream Institutional Practice]] and runs parallel to the [[Legitimacy Machines — Book Skeleton and Architecture]] project.

## Book Structure (In Progress)

**Full title:** *Who's the Boob, Who's the Trap? AI Governance Through the Booby-Trap Device*

**Central device:** The booby trap as a theory of movement — anticipates behavior, prearranges consequence, hides coercion inside neutral-looking systems.

**Four running questions:**
1. How is the pathway prepared in advance?
2. Who is asked to trust, prove, wait, self-correct, or absorb repair?
3. Where does responsibility fragment (procurement, middleware, thresholds, interfaces, human review)?
4. What would let an affected public interrupt, reconstruct, contest, or stop the system?

**Completed chapters:**
- **Prologue** — The Trap Is Older Than the State. AI governance as civilizational problem; governance as distributed field (data collection governs, labeling governs, metrics govern, procurement governs).
- **Chapter 1** — Cities, Granaries, and Hidden Defenses. Early urbanism as defensive technology; selective reassurance and unequal geography of risk; proto-analytic practices (ditches, chokepoints, archives) as precursors to risk engines and model thresholds.
- **Chapter 2** — Akkad, Ur III, and Bureaucratic Fragility. Centralization and brittleness as co-produced; bureaucratic tempo as governance; the archive as extraction technology; bureaucratic trap = process whose punitive consequence is triggered by conditions the subject cannot see or challenge.

**Key concepts:**
- **Boob** = structural cost position in anti-accountable systems; not foolishness but political arrangement
- **Trap** = prearranged consequence that acts before open confrontation; governs through anticipation
- **Bureaucratic fragility** = systems most legible just before internal simplifications become irreversible
- **Temporal governance** = compliance pace calibrated to an ideal subject; everyone else carries the surplus labor

## Governance Framing

The book refuses dispersal of responsibility as a political inevitability. It must name the setter, the path, the trigger, the target, and the population made available to risk. Directly relevant to [[AI Governance Failure Cases]] (distributed blame as design feature) and [[Recursive Governance Theory]] (governance as upstream, reflexive, contestable).

The torture/bureaucracy pairing (Chapters 1–2) makes explicit what [[AI Governance Manifesto — Upstream Institutional Practice]] implies: governance is not a neutral layer added after technology is built.
