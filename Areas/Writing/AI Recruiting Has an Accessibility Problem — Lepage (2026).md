# AI Recruiting Has an Accessibility Problem — Lepage (2026)

## Summary

First-person testimony and governance critique of AI-mediated hiring processes, written by [[Martin Lepage]] from direct experience as a person who stutters. Source: `00_Inbox/Raw/AI Recruiting Has an Accessibility Problem.md`. Connects to [[Stuttering through the Institution — Academic Containment and Queer Knowing]] and [[Disability Epistemology and Institutional Critique]].

## Core Argument

AI recruiting presents itself as scalable, efficient, and bias-reducing. In practice, it converts speech fluency into a proxy for competence — without accommodation pathways, without transparency, and without meaningful human review.

The piece documents repeated rejection from AI-mediated processes (including Aligner and Mercor) across roles from customer service to specialized positions, despite high domain expertise. The system provided no feedback, no appeal mechanism, no accommodation pathway, and no response to explicit disability-related concerns raised during the interview.

## Key Claims (verified — source is first-person testimony)

- **Fluency ≠ competence**: A system that scores hesitations, repetitions, and restarts as deficits is not evaluating expertise; it is evaluating speech production.
- **Silence as governance failure**: When AI-mediated processes return no actionable feedback, no accommodation pathway, and no human review of disability-related concerns, the silence is a system design choice — not a neutral outcome.
- **The asymmetry of accountability**: Applicants are evaluated at scale; they cannot evaluate the evaluation. The system produces consequences but not evidence.
- **Explicit requests were made and ignored**: The source explicitly notes that accessibility concerns were raised *within the interview process* and produced no adjustment or human escalation.

## Context and Stakes

This connects to three active threads:

1. **[[Stuttering through the Institution — Academic Containment and Queer Knowing]]** — pattern of institutional containment where non-normative speech is read as incapacity. AI recruiting is a new institutional site of the same pattern.
2. **[[Disability Epistemology and Institutional Critique]]** — the argument that disabled people have epistemic access to institutional failure that non-disabled evaluators miss. The source demonstrates this directly.
3. **PHAROS governance work** — AI recruiting systems are high-stakes AI systems in the AI Act / Law 25 sense. This piece is a use case for why governance matters at the deployment layer, not just the model layer.
