# Queerness and Transgender Identity — Lepage 2017 (Pagan Montreal, Wicca vs Reclaiming)

## Summary
[[Martin Lepage — Professional Profile|Martin Lepage]]’s 2017 article (Studies in Religion / *Sciences Religieuses*) asks how **witchcraft/magic** functions as a practical vehicle for **queer and transgender participation** in Montreal Pagan communities. It contrasts Montreal Wicca and Reclaiming Witchcraft’s gender frameworks and shows how “queer negotiations” reshape religious/magical practice and community positioning. The original peer-reviewed grounding for the [[Queer Sociology of Magic and Ritual|queer sociology of magic and ritual]] cluster, and one of the [[Ethnographic Data Loss — Ethics Decision and Publication Disclaimer|publications affected by the 2020–2024 ethnographic data loss]].

## Key Ideas (vault-usable)
- **Queer negotiations**: ongoing adjustments between (1) lived gender/sexual identity and (2) gendered symbolic frameworks in Pagan religiosities (especially binary Wicca) — the same governance-of-recognition pattern read theoretically in [[Authority After Legitimacy — Disenchantment and Queer Political Theory]].
- Wicca and Reclaiming Witchcraft can develop **opposite and/or complementary** views of gender; that difference becomes a governance condition for participation, comparable to the [[Is Sacred Nature Gendered or Queer — Becci & Grandjean 2022 (Eco-Spiritual Activism Switzerland)|Swiss eco-spiritual]] differentiation across milieus.
- Magic is not only “belief expression” but a **practice-space** where binaries can be contested and identity can become actionable; cf. [[The Meanings of Magic — Bailey 2006 (Magic as Unstable Category)]] for the category-stability question that frames the practice-space claim.

## Method (as described in the paper)
- Montreal fieldwork using participant observation + interviews; focus on life trajectories and personal narratives of transgender / queer interlocutors — see [[Les fées dansent sur le Mont-Royal — Lepage & Gauthier (Anthropologica)]] for the earlier Montreal Pagan anchor.
- Uses case-study logic to show how gender/queerness changes relationship to beliefs, practice, and community — a methodological cousin to [[Process-Tracing Methods — Beach & Pedersen 2019 (Mechanisms and Evidence)|process-tracing]] applied to participation conditions.

## Why it matters here
- Directly grounds the vault’s Montreal Pagan lane and supports the ritual authority / proof-regime writing in [[Social Compass Paper — Ritual Authority and Régime de Preuve]] without drifting into abstract “magic” talk.
- Reinforces the continuity between Montreal queer fieldwork and the later governance vocabulary in [[Paper 25 — The Pharos Frame - Four Levels Where Ethics Becomes AI Governance|Paper 25]]: participation depends on what a system can *recognize* and *allow* as legitimate practice — the same legibility/capacity/classification spine in [[Governance by Denial]].
