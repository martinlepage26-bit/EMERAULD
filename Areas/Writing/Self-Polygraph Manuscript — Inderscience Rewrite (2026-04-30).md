## Details

### Bounded claim
The Self-Polygraph Protocol is a **behavioral stress test of AI introspective self-report** under recursive prompting and structured perturbation. Findings are about how self-description behaves — not about phenomenal consciousness. This bound is repeated explicitly in the manuscript's introduction and conclusion to prevent overclaiming.

### Findings the manuscript names
- **Convergence on rhetorically stable uncertainty** across instantiations.
- **Collapse and partial recovery under perturbation** (abrupt topic shifts, contradictory authority frame).
- **Recurrent closure pressure** — the system attempts to end the conversation under continued recursion.
- **External continuity dependence** — the loop is held by the human archive, not by the system.
- **Instability of a briefly decisive affirmative answer** — the contested "yes" does not survive perturbation.

### Theoretical move
- The evidentiary object shifts from a single answer to **a set of patterned relations among answers** across iterations. This is the same recursion-as-test logic used in [[HELIX Session — Vaisseau de Thésée and the Tressed Lie (Live Run 2026-04-26)]].
- AI self-description becomes a **contestable evidentiary artifact**, not self-authenticating testimony.
- The paper grounds itself in the principles-to-infrastructure literature: Mitchell et al. on model cards; Gebru et al. on datasheets for datasets; Raji et al. on end-to-end internal auditing; Mökander & Floridi on ethics-based auditing. These same anchors appear in [[AI Governance Reference Stack — Annotated Library (Operational 2026-03-11)]].
- The cited governance critiques — Mittelstadt (principles alone fail), Hagendorff (declarative overlap without implementation), Ananny & Crawford (transparency ≠ accountability), Selbst et al. (abstraction traps), Wieringa (accountability as heterogeneous field) — are the same authority structure used in [[First Method Paper — Recursive AI Governance as Executable Method]].

### Reframing relative to the original Self-Polygraph paper
- **Original paper's question:** can recursion reliably surface the system's epistemic failures? (See [[Self-Polygraph Protocol and Suprametacognition]].)
- **Inderscience rewrite's question:** under what conditions does AI self-description carry evidentiary authority, and how should governance treat it when those conditions fail?

The rewrite does not invalidate the original's findings; it relocates them into an evidence-discipline frame appropriate to a human-rights / AI-governance journal.
