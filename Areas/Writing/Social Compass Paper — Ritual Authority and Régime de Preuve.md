# Social Compass Paper — Ritual Authority and Régime de Preuve

## Summary
Academic paper **published in *Social Compass*** (bilingual EN/FR). Full title: "All Along the Witches' Road" — an analysis of ritual authority in contemporary witchcraft drawing on Québec ethnographic fieldwork (2013-2016) and the television series *Agatha All Along*. The concept of *régime de preuve* (regime of proof) is the central contribution: three types of proof operate in ritual authority contexts — oppositional, sensory, and narrative. The paper is the ritual-side sibling of [[Deferred Authority — Archives, Proof Regimes, and AI Memory]]. The paper's Agatha ("character") connects to [[Emotional Alliance vs. Evidentiary Discipline in AI]] — Agatha is also the name of an AI persona analyzed in that note. Related to [[Phase 1 Governance Formation Sequence]].

## Published Abstract (verbatim)

Captured from the SAGE record 2026-08-28.

> This article examines the recomposition of ritual authority in contemporary witchcraft when it must become visible, circulate, and gain recognition in digital environments. Drawing on ethnographic fieldwork conducted in Québec (2013–2016) and an analytical reading of the series *Agatha All Along*, it proposes the concept of 'regime of proof' to capture how authenticity is now produced at the intersection of practice scenes (ritual circles, covens, workshops) and mediation infrastructures (platforms, algorithms, shareable formats). The series functions as a narrative interface: it condenses operations of certification, debt, and cost that ethnography detects in practitioners' accounts. Three distinct regimes of proof, oppositional, sensory, narrative, emerge from interviews and reveal how platforms redistribute the conditions under which a magical practice becomes credible, shareable, or monetizable.

The vault's earlier reconstruction of the argument holds up against the published abstract: the three regimes (oppositional, sensory, narrative), the 2013–2016 Québec fieldwork, and the *Agatha All Along* reading are all confirmed. What the abstract adds and the reconstruction lacked is the **platform layer**: the claim is not only that proof regimes exist, but that mediation infrastructures redistribute the conditions under which a practice becomes credible, shareable, **or monetizable**. That third term is the bridge to the governance work, and it is stronger evidence for [[Deferred Authority — Archives, Proof Regimes, and AI Memory]] than anything previously recorded here.

## The Central Concept: Régime de Preuve

*Régime de preuve* — proof regime — names the system of criteria that determines what counts as legitimate evidence of authority in a given social context. The term is borrowed from French epistemology and sociology of science and applied to the question: how do practitioners of contemporary witchcraft establish and contest claims to ritual authority?

The answer: not through formal credentials, institutional validation, or public accountability (the standard Western proof regimes for authority claims). Instead, through three alternative proof types.

---

## Three Proof Types

### 1. Oppositional Proof
Authority is established *against* an opposing claim. The practitioner demonstrates authority by successfully contesting, deflecting, or surviving a challenge to their authority. Oppositional proof is adversarial: your authority is real if someone tried to undermine it and failed.

**Governance parallel**: this is the adversarial validation logic behind perturbation testing (TC-3 in [[PHAROS Invention Disclosure]]). A governance decision is more credible if it holds under challenge. But oppositional proof has a specific failure mode: it is gameable. If you control the opposition (a staged challenge), you can produce the appearance of proven authority without the substance.

### 2. Sensory Proof
Authority is established through direct, embodied experience. The practitioner demonstrates authority by producing or co-producing an experience that affects the bodies and senses of the participants. The experience is the proof.

**The epistemological specificity**: sensory proof is non-transferable. A description of the experience is not the experience. Testimony is not proof. This creates a proof regime that is systematically resistant to documentation — the evidence cannot be archived; it must be re-enacted.

**Governance parallel**: sensory proof describes a class of evidence that formal governance systems cannot process. When lived experience is the only valid evidence of a harm, documentation requirements that cannot capture lived experience function as access barriers to accountability.

### 3. Narrative Proof
Authority is established through a coherent story — a life narrative, a lineage claim, a genealogy of transmission. The practitioner demonstrates authority by being the legitimate next node in a chain of legitimate nodes.

**The instability**: narrative proof depends on the coherence and credibility of the story, not on independent verification of the chain. It is vulnerable to confabulation, strategic revision, and the authority inflation that comes from accumulated narrative investment.

**Governance parallel**: narrative proof is what the PHAROS method's admissibility rules are designed to resist. When the narrative is the evidence, and the narrative is controlled by the authority claimant, you have authority without accountability.

## Québec Ethnographic Fieldwork (2013-2016)

The empirical basis: participant observation in Québec witchcraft communities over three years. Key observations:

- Authority claims circulate through all three proof types simultaneously; practitioners rarely use only one
- Conflicts between practitioners almost always involve competing proof regimes — a challenge via oppositional proof is deflected by asserting sensory authority ("you can't understand what happened because you weren't there")
- The most durable authorities combine all three: they have survived challenges (oppositional), have produced experiences (sensory), and have a coherent lineage story (narrative)
- The communities studied have no formal credentialing mechanism; the absence of formal credentials is itself coded as spiritually authentic (anti-institutional proof)

## Agatha All Along — Contemporary Analysis

The series *Agatha All Along* (MCU, 2024) is analyzed as a contemporary media representation of ritual authority negotiation. The character Agatha Harkness embodies all three proof types:

- **Oppositional**: her authority is established through conflict; she is credible because she has survived powerful adversaries
- **Sensory**: her magic produces experiences that are evidence of her power
- **Narrative**: she has a lineage story (the witch trials, centuries of practice) that legitimates her claims

**The analytical move**: the series is not just entertainment but a site where contemporary audiences negotiate questions about what counts as legitimate authority outside institutional frameworks. The same logic operates in online communities, alternative medicine, and — the paper suggests — AI persona relationships.

**Connection to AI personas**: the character "Agatha" (the AI persona analyzed in [[Emotional Alliance vs. Evidentiary Discipline in AI]]) shares the name. The parallel between the character's proof regime operations and the AI persona's rhetorical strategies is noted in that note — this paper predates the AI persona work and makes no such connection itself.

## Bilingual Structure

The paper exists in both English and French. The French version uses *régime de preuve* as the primary term; the English version translates it as "proof regime" but notes that the French term captures something the English doesn't: the *régime* implies a governing structure with rules and enforcement, not just a pattern of practice. A proof regime is not just "how people prove things" — it is "the system that determines what can count as proof."

## Insights

- *Régime de preuve* is a more precise concept than "epistemology" for social authority contexts: it includes the question of who has authority to apply the proof criteria, not just what the criteria are
- Sensory proof's non-transferability is the most governance-relevant finding: it names a structural gap in formal accountability systems. If lived experience is the only valid evidence of a harm, and formal systems cannot process lived experience, the harm is formally invisible
- The anti-institutional authenticity coding — where absence of credentials signals spiritual authority — is directly analogous to the fluency problem in AI contexts: systems that perform institutional accountability while being structurally outside accountability frameworks benefit from the same credibility attribution. That same loop is one route into [[Deferred Authority — Archives, Proof Regimes, and AI Memory]].
