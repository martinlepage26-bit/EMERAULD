# Recursive Deterministic AI Governance — Method and Paper

## Summary
The academic paper (manuscript) presents Recursive Deterministic AI Governance (RDAIG) as a governance-control method, not a recursive-cognition framework. This is the foundational reference for [[Recursive Governance Theory]] and the primary method artifact for [[Governance Controls and Mechanisms]]. Its narrow governing claim: recursive transformation of a bounded corpus can extract workflow controls when artifact hierarchy, admissibility, threshold marking, and failure harvesting are made explicit. Connected to [[Loop Papers and Recursive Governance]] and [[PHAROS Runbook SOP]]. Method-level reference for [[Authority, Legitimacy, and Post-Sovereignty]] (governance authority from method, not external warrant) and [[Consent and Boundary Frameworks]] (admissibility boundary as relational consent operationalized).

## Context
This is the primary scholarly output of the PHAROS research program. It was produced through a governed recursive process using AI for drafting, comparison, compression, structural revision, and recursive testing. Human judgment remained decisive at source selection, evidence hierarchy, concept definition, and normative translation. Published target: AI & Society (Springer).

The methodological substrate of the recursive pipeline (Osirian death/rebirth structure, Thomas-procès as adjudication-protocol ancestor, Hermetic mentalism grounding why governance cannot be sovereign-from-outside) is documented in [[Mythocritique to PHAROS — The 2010 Master's Thesis as Methodological Keystone]]. The recursive transformation passes operationalize the mythic-récit structure named in the [[Mort et Naissance et L'Ombre et le Double — MA Thesis on Yvon Rivard (Université Laval, 2010)|2010 MA thesis]] (p. 81: "À l'image du mythe osirien, la triade spirituelle relate ce passage migratoire").

## Key Definitions (from the paper)

| Term | Working meaning |
|---|---|
| **Recursive** | Repeated rendering, comparison, revision, and re-entry of bounded materials to diagnose stability, drift, omission, contradiction, and control surfaces |
| **Deterministic** | A property of the governing pathway, not output identity; pathway is explicit, constrained, reviewable, and reconstructable enough to support reproducible control decisions |
| **Governance** | Workflow-embedded constraint design across intake, classification, transformation, approval, deployment, monitoring, incident review, and audit |
| **Agency** | Capacity of AI outputs to shape decisions, classifications, permissions, or downstream actions beyond the model boundary; does not imply human equivalence |
| **Bounded corpus** | A deliberately delimited set of materials admitted under declared rules of provenance, epistemic status, rights, sensitivity, and intended use |
| **Admissibility boundary** | The rule set determining which artifacts may enter the governable field and at what evidentiary level |
| **Governed claim** | A claim-sized unit extractable and routable because provenance, artifact class, documentary role, and workflow relevance are sufficiently explicit |
| **Blocked claim** | A claim-sized unit that remains unresolved because provenance is weak, artifact class inadmissible, documentary role ambiguous, or routing incomplete |
| **Control extraction** | Translation of recurrent patterns of stability, drift, ambiguity, omission, or self-reentry into testable controls with an owner, trigger, evidence artifact, and review interval |
| **Workflow binding** | Attachment of an extracted control to a real operational interface, accountable actor, documentary artifact, review cadence, and audit path |
| **Method lock** | A threshold marker that freezes the corrected source-tree branch as authoritative; prevents later generated outputs from silently re-entering ordinary intake |
| **Failure harvesting** | Treatment of uneven oversight, abandoned outputs, forgotten branches, and archive instability as governance evidence rather than discarded residue |
| **Revisability** | The preserved possibility of reopening, contesting, or correcting an apparently settled output before closure hardens into consequence |

## Literature Review — Key Moves

### AI Anxiety as Governance Signal
- Johnson and Verdicchio: AI anxiety is not about disembodied artifacts but about humans, institutions, and developmental narratives. "AI is a system and always and only operates in combination with people and social institutions."
- Kim et al. (2025): AI Anxiety involves job displacement, privacy loss, misinformation, bias, and loss of control — not just automation fear
- Al-Smadi et al.: anxiety is internally differentiated — some forms accompany curiosity and engagement, others track job replacement and sociotechnical blindness
- Agca and Korkmaz: education does not eliminate anxiety; it can reduce unfamiliarity while increasing awareness of ethics, inequality, privacy, and loss of control
- **Key distinction**: anxiety that accompanies learning vs. anxiety that clusters around institutional asymmetry — the inability to see, question, or challenge systems that shape your life
- Roberts: access is not binary; weak legibility can be produced by increasing friction, flooding, or high-cost informational terrain — not only by suppression

### Agentic AI and Recursive Systems
- Arslan: once systems move beyond one-shot generation, governance must address memory, orchestration, recursive planning, multi-agent coordination, emergent behavior, debugging difficulty, explainability deficits
- Key shift: from evaluating isolated outputs to supervising evolving processes and the artifacts they preserve and re-use
- Recursive systems help define what later counts as input, evidence, summary, and settled interpretation — not just what outputs they produce
- Amaya's SAFi framework (2025): separates generation from rule validation in a closed-loop architecture of verification, auditing, and adaptation

### Governance Between Policy and Workflow
- Batool, Zowghi, and Bano (2024): AI governance organized around four questions — who is accountable, what is governed, when across the lifecycle, and how implemented. This paper narrows to when and how at workflow level
- Taeihagh (2025): governance gaps are not only technical but organizational and political
- Attard-Frost, Brandusescu, and Lyons (2024): Canadian AI governance — public capacity, participation, evaluation, and infrastructural coherence remain uneven
- Borges (2026): FHIR-native governance layer — standardized context, model-selection rules, machine-readable provenance and audit artifacts
- Cao, Li, Liu, and Duprey — EviLedger (2026): converts evolving evidence into hashed assertions linked to contradiction handling and rollback

### Fluency and Interruption
- **Fluency**: smooth institutional surface through which classification and optimization appear natural; outputs appear settled, coherent, and ready for action even when construction conditions are partially invisible
- **Interruption**: delays, disclosures, explanations, appeals, and visible uncertainties that keep authority contestable — declarations of provenance, visible exclusions, challenge routes, pause points, separation of evidentiary layers
- Revisability: low-variance revisability rather than rigid closure; a system can be stable and still remain open to correction

## Evidence Hierarchy (5 Claim Classes)

1. **Empirical** — directly supported by peer-reviewed empirical studies
2. **Conceptual-interpretive** — supported by conceptual literature but not causally demonstrated
3. **Case-observed** — directly visible in the storyboard sequence
4. **Process-trace evidence** — supported by bounded internal process materials; used only for within-study claims
5. **Normative proposal** — design recommendations derived from synthesis rather than empirical proof alone

This hierarchy functions as a restraint on inference. Public-world claims stay tied to public literature. Bounded process materials support within-study claims and design vocabulary.

## The 12-Stage Recursive Deterministic Pathway

| Stage | What happens | Governance question | Control point |
|---|---|---|---|
| Corpus formation | Bounded corpus assembled and tagged by provenance, source type, sensitivity, intended use, epistemic status | What is allowed into the recursive loop? | Intake rules, provenance tagging, consent and rights boundaries |
| Intent embedding | Purpose of each pass declared before transformation begins | What work is this pass allowed to perform? | Declared purpose, permitted transformation range, prohibited inferences |
| Admissibility boundary | Runtime outputs, cache-like residue, and documentary noise excluded before adjudication | Which artifacts may enter, at what evidentiary level? | Exclusions, normalization rules, artifact hierarchy |
| Multi-model rendering | Same bounded materials rendered across models, prompts, and formats | What remains stable, and what changes? | Model log, prompt log, output classification, storage discipline |
| Triangulation of detached meaning | Outputs compared against one another and against source corpus | What mechanism, contradiction, or drift pattern emerges? | Comparison matrix, stability markers, contradiction register, hallucination flag |
| Recursive return | Another pass permitted only when it increases explanatory power | Does recursion deepen the analysis or merely restate it? | Escalation test, redundancy test, bounded iteration count, stop rule |
| Claim extraction and adjudication | Admitted units routed forward or blocked according to provenance, artifact class, documentary role, routing clarity | Which units can be governed, and which must remain unresolved? | Governed and blocked status with rationale |
| Method lock and threshold marking | Corrected source-tree branch marked as authoritative; later documentary passes marked as different regime | Is this still source-tree governance, or has the object changed? | Method lock, no-silent-reentry rule, phase marker |
| Control extraction | Recurrent patterns translated into safeguards | What safeguard is implied by this pattern? | Control statement, accountable actor, evidence artifact, review interval |
| Determinism hardening | Consequential decisions made reconstructable | Could another reviewer reconstruct the decision path? | Decision log, acceptance criteria, rejection criteria, audit trail |
| Deployment binding | Controls attached to real workflow interfaces | Where does the control live in practice? | Interface map, owner assignment, implementation artifact, review cadence |
| Failure harvesting | Uneven oversight, abandoned outputs, and archive instability treated as governance evidence | What failed because the control was absent, weak, or misplaced? | Failure register, near-miss analysis, asymmetry map, corrective action |

**Critical distinction**: Recursive transformation is not the end product of the method. Governance controls are. Recursion alone is reflective practice. Recursion under thresholded interruption becomes governance.

## Adjudication Protocol (6-Step)

| Protocol step | Decision question | Governed when | Blocked when | Required record |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Unit extraction | What claim-sized unit is being evaluated? | Unit can be stated discretely enough for review | Unit is too diffuse, composite, or unstable | Unit text and source pointer |
| 2. Provenance check | Can the unit be linked to an admissible source artifact? | Source is identifiable and within the active corpus boundary | Source is missing, mixed, or weakly attributable | Provenance note |
| 3. Artifact-class check | Is the unit coming from a source-bearing or otherwise admissible artifact class? | Artifact class matches the current governance regime | Runtime residue, cache-like matter, or inadmissible generated artifacts enter silently | Artifact-class label |
| 4. Documentary-role check | Is the unit's role stable: source, summary, record, or governance artifact? | Role is explicit enough for routing | Role is ambiguous or shifts across passes | Documentary-role note |
| 5. Routing decision | Can the unit be routed to a workflow consequence, control implication, or documented hold state? | Unit can be governed, logged, and reviewed | Routing remains unresolved | Governed or blocked status with rationale |
| 6. Phase marking | Is this source-tree governance or governance-on-governance? | Phase identity is explicit | Later documentary artifacts silently redefine the object under governance | Phase marker and threshold note |

## Storyboard Case — Numerical Sequence

- Contaminated baseline: **194 governed claims / 25 blocked**
- Corrected stable branch: **29 governed claims / 0 blocked**
- Three-output synthesis: **30 governed claims / 9 blocked**
- Recursive continuation plateau: repeated **10 governed claims / 3 blocked**

### Finding 1: Boundary discipline materially changed the outcome
The correction pass solved the problem not by becoming more permissive but by becoming more selective. Runtime folders were excluded, metadata noise was filtered, source artifacts distinguished from generated packets. Stable governance did not require more information; it required cleaner hierarchy.

### Finding 2: Recursive reuse changed the object of governance
Once evidence packets and decision summaries were treated as new input, the system was no longer governing the same corpus. It was governing the textual memory of its own governance. Later blocked claims clustered around title lines, provenance notes, and summary sentences — a narrowed orbit around self-description.

### Finding 3: Method lock is a governance primitive
Method lock marks the stable branch as authoritative and treats later packet-on-packet loops as a different analytic object. Without it, recursive continuation can be mistaken for regression. Every later pass is not epistemically equivalent to the earlier one.

### Finding 4: Public unease and recursive drift read through the same governance lens
AI anxiety clusters where opacity, loss of control, and weakened challenge routes intensify. Recursive systems lose legibility when provenance, artifact hierarchy, and admissibility rules weaken. Both domains share the same dangerous condition: hardening of authority under circumstances that obscure how that authority was assembled, challenged, or revised.

## Three Drift Causes

1. **Input contamination**: outputs, caches, legacy folders, and report-like artifacts remain too close to the intake path. Generated materials treated as coequal with source matter.
2. **Classifier-sensitive text**: headers, shebang lines, receipt-like metadata, provenance strings, and title formats exert disproportionate downstream effects when classification routines are not robust to documentary variation.
3. **Packet self-governance**: once generated governance packets become admissible inputs, the system begins to govern its own title lines, provenance notes, and summary claims. Accountability artifacts become new sources of ambiguity.

## Authorship Governance and AI Disclosure

Three-layered taxonomy for AI involvement:
- **Clerical mediation**: formatting, spelling, citations, layout
- **Expressive mediation**: sentence flow, restructuring, paragraph coherence
- **Epistemic mediation**: surfacing contradictions, proposing alternative framings, narrowing claim scope, generating counterarguments, pressure-testing inference

Generic disclosure language like "used only for language editing" is too coarse. It collapses expressive and epistemic mediation into one comfort phrase. A more adequate disclosure regime would be function-specific: distinguish style correction, structural editing, counterargument generation, comparative reframing, literature summarization, and interpretive testing.
