# Still Running — Willow, Anya, and Queer Ritual Infrastructure

## Summary
Two closely related academic papers analyzing Willow Rosenberg and Anya Jenkins from *Buffy the Vampire Slayer* as **posthuman ritual technologies** — modular components used in queer and trans mourning, transition, and spellwork practices in Montreal and online ritual networks. Author: Martin Lepage, PhD. File 1 ("Still Running: Glitch, Refusal, and the Ritual Afterlife of Willow Rosenberg and Anya Jenkins") is a phenomenologically immersive draft. File 2 ('"Bored Now": Glitching Grief and Recasting the Unresolved in Queer Ritual Media') is the journal-formatted version. Ethnographic fieldwork: Montreal and online queer/trans ritual networks, 2020–2024. Related to [[The Scythe Already in Motion — Buffy, Queer Ritual, and the Politics of Glitch]], [[For Her Alone to Wield — Buffy Power as Infrastructure]], and [[Ritual Machine Lab — Legitimacy Machines Introduction]].

## Context

## Central Argument

Willow Rosenberg and Anya Jenkins are not studied as representation objects (what do they mean symbolically?) but as **ritual infrastructure**: reusable procedural components that support mourning, transition, and spellwork practices in queer and trans communities.

They are valuable **precisely because they are unresolved, unfinished, and broken**:
- Willow's Season 6 arc (grief, addiction, near-apocalypse) does not resolve cleanly — it remains as executable, loopable code
- Anya's death (offscreen, uncommented, narratively sidelined) is a model for ungrieved loss
- Neither figure heals or provides consolation — their usefulness is procedural, not therapeutic

**The pivot word is "usable"** — these characters are not worshipped, they are handled.

---

## Character-as-Infrastructure

**Character-as-infrastructure** is the paper's core theoretical category. A media figure becomes infrastructure when it is:
- Reused across multiple practice sites without losing recognizability
- Dropped into ritual sequences as a component
- Handled in job-language: "run," "loop," "use," "cast"
- Circulating as material artifacts: audio loops, screenshot-altars, gif threads

This is distinct from fandom devotion. The paper explicitly refuses the fandom studies framework: this is not religious devotion dressed as entertainment. The characters are tools, not saints.

## Two Infrastructural Logics

### Willow — Recursive Magic (Loop)
Willow's resurrection chant and grief arc function as **loop infrastructure**:
- The loop creates **sustained ritual time** — duration that is elastic, not narrative
- The chant is run, not remembered: "She is not remembered — she is run"
- Negative case: loops that run too long become entrapment — "we're cooked"
- Field example: 2022 digital Beltane rite with glitch-looped resurrection chant used to hold grief open rather than close it

### Anya — Ethical Glitch (Cut)
Anya's misalignment — the "The Body" monologue, her offscreen death, her bluntness — functions as **rupture infrastructure**:
- The cut interrupts the ritual demand for legible, performatively correct feeling
- Anya is useful when grief is not sanctioned: by family, by genre, by narrative convention
- Her offscreen death is a template for mourning the officially ungrieved
- "Bored now": operates as a tiny cut-line — refusal of compulsory legibility, exit from the demand to perform the correct emotion

**Together, loop and cut form the grammar of the unresolved** — the two moves required for queer ritual practice that refuses consolatory closure.

## Glitch as Method

The paper draws on Russell's *Glitch Feminism* (2020): glitch is not error but **refusal** — a site where the system's demand for coherence is interrupted.

The glitch here is not a metaphor imported onto Buffy studies — it is derived from the ethnographic data. Participants use fragments that are structurally broken (corrupted video, degraded audio, narrative loose ends) precisely because their brokenness is generative. "A ritual opening: a crack in a scene that becomes a re-entry point into practice elsewhere."

Connects to the **glitch** concept in [[Sealed Card Protocol — Legitimacy, Glitch, and Charging]]: the moment mediation becomes inferable, the threshold event that reorients judgment. In the ritual context, the glitch of Anya's death (the moment the show's governance of death fails to manage the loss properly) becomes the opening through which queer grief can enter.

## Contrast with Agatha Harkness (WandaVision)

File 2 explicitly situates itself against a prior paper on Agatha Harkness in WandaVision:
- Agatha is **sealed, contained, narratively punished** for excess — her power is controlled, absorbed, institutionalized
- Willow and Anya are **open, glitching forward** — their power is not contained, their stories are not resolved
- The contrast is structural: who is allowed to persist and fail visibly?

This is the paper's political claim: queer ritual infrastructure requires figures who are **not redeemed** — whose failure, excess, and death remain unmanaged.

## Methodology: Recursive Media Ethnography

**Recursive media ethnography** treats media fragments, ritual performance, and circulation artifacts as one analytic field. Three material types: fieldnotes, interviews/conversations, digital artifacts.

Five specific field examples:
- 2022 digital Beltane rite with glitch-looped resurrection chant
- 2021 online ritual for ungrieved kin
- 2022 Mile End loft rite with Season 6 imagery and looping degraded video
- 2023 Samhain gathering
- 2021 séance for estranged kin

Ethics protocol: consent-first, paraphrase over quotation for memorial content, de-identification, no platform metadata, anonymized typologies.

**Important note**: Recognition is not required for ritual function. Fragments work even when the room has never watched Buffy — affective and procedural, not dependent on shared fandom literacy.

## Theoretical Framework

- **Braidotti** (posthuman) — Willow and Anya as posthuman subjects: continuous with biological, technological, and cultural flows
- **Haraway** (cyborg) — the technologized, non-innocent subject; boundary-crossing as method
- **Ahmed** (queer phenomenology) — affective misalignment with scenes that should be joyful; structural exile
- **Berlant** (cruel optimism) — attachment to "staying open" can become coercive inside the ritual group; compulsory endurance masquerading as authentic refusal
- **Muñoz** (queer futurity) — the non-arrival as productive; the unfinished as the condition of possibility
- **Russell** (glitch feminism) — glitch as refusal, not error
- **Taylor** (archive/repertoire) — Buffy canon is archival; fragments that circulate in ritual life are repertorial

## Insights

- Character-as-infrastructure is the most methodologically precise contribution: it reframes media studies analysis from "what does this mean?" to "what can this do?" — and "can" is answered empirically through fieldwork, not through interpretation
- The loop/cut distinction maps onto the fluency/interruption framework in [[Fluency, Interruption, and Institutional Accountability]]: the loop is duration infrastructure (sustained engagement without resolution); the cut is interruption infrastructure (accountability-producing pause in compulsory emotional performance)
- "Bored now" as shared grammar is the paper's most ethnographically specific contribution — a phrase that circulates across ritual sites as a refusal tool, requiring no explanation in the communities studied
