# Beyond Fluency — Stuttering, Autoethnography, and Unstable Epistemology

## Summary
Autoethnographic academic paper connecting stuttering as a lived disability experience to epistemological and political arguments about **compulsory fluency** — the institutional demand for smooth, uninterrupted speech and writing as evidence of intelligence, discipline, and worth. Author: Martin Lepage, PhD (2012, with later revisions). Four autoethnographic vignettes, each staging a different encounter between a stuttering subject and an institutional fluency requirement. Central argument: stuttering is not a deficit to be overcome but an **epistemological position** — a site from which the governance functions of fluency become visible. Stuttering as the refusal to consent to fluency. Connects to the [[Fluency, Interruption, and Institutional Accountability]] framework, [[Consent and Boundary Frameworks]], and to the [[CDPDJ Complaint — Lepage v Calian and Novartis]] disability experience. Related to [[Historical Academic Portfolio — Pre-PHAROS Scholarly Work]] and [[Refusing Fixity — Aesthetic Governance and Fugitive Authorship]].

## Context
Source: `2012 - Beyond Fluency Stuttering.docx`. This is the "Beyond Fluency" paper — the direct empirical and autobiographical foundation for the theoretical arguments about fluency, interruption, and institutional accountability developed in the later governance work. The 2012 date appears in the filename. The paper predates the PHAROS governance methodology by over a decade but provides the lived-experience grounding for key concepts including compulsory fluency, the disability-AI connection, and the politics of interruption. Maps directly onto *le silence* of the recent [[Pourquoi rêver encore — Lecture allégorique et métaphysique d'Yvon Rivard|Pourquoi rêver encore]] essay — silence and stuttering as generative pre-linguistic threshold rather than deficit. Per [[Mythocritique to PHAROS — The 2010 Master's Thesis as Methodological Keystone|the keystone]], *le silence* is the empirical-autobiographical floor of the L99 Gap Declaration: the unnamed that is also unnameable (MA p. 20: *"l'innommé est aussi l'innommable"*).

## Compulsory Fluency

**Compulsory fluency** is the institutional system that:
- Establishes smooth, rapid, uninterrupted speech and writing as the unmarked norm for intelligence and competence
- Pathologizes deviation from that norm (stuttering, hesitation, rewriting, delay) as evidence of disorder, insufficient preparation, or lesser capacity
- Distributes institutional rewards (grades, jobs, social recognition) differentially based on fluency compliance
- Presents its own standards as natural or neutral rather than as governance mechanisms

**The political analysis**: Compulsory fluency is not merely aesthetic — it is a sorting mechanism. It sorts people into competent/incompetent, prepared/unprepared, credible/discreditable along lines that correlate with disability, class, language background, and neurodiversity. The person who speaks slowly, who revises in real time, who pauses to find words — this person is penalized for the same cognitive processes that a fluent speaker performs invisibly.

This is the autobiographical and disability-studies foundation for the [[Fluency, Interruption, and Institutional Accountability]] argument that fluency is a mode of authority exercise, not a neutral operational condition.

## Four Autoethnographic Vignettes

### Vignette 1 — The Classroom
A scene of being called on to read aloud in school. The institutional demand: demonstrate comprehension through fluent oral reading. The stuttering disrupts the demonstration. Institutional response: sympathy that functions as disqualification — the teacher moves on, the moment is lost.

**Analysis**: The classroom vignette shows compulsory fluency as an **assessment technology** — one that conflates communication capacity with speech fluency and thereby produces systematic misclassification of intelligence.

### Vignette 2 — The Job Interview
A professional context in which stuttering triggers a legibility failure: the interviewer shifts from evaluating qualifications to managing discomfort. The interview becomes about the speech event rather than about the work.

**Analysis**: The job interview vignette stages the legibility problem from [[Sealed Card Protocol — Legitimacy, Glitch, and Charging]]: the seam fires (the stutter is the tell), evaluation migrates from surface (qualifications) to pathway (how does this person communicate?), and the legitimacy question replaces the competence question.

### Vignette 3 — The Academic Conference
A paper presentation where preparation and command of material are undermined by performance anxiety amplifying stuttering. The Q&A becomes a different kind of performance — managing others' discomfort with the stutter rather than engaging the argument.

**Analysis**: The conference vignette shows that compulsory fluency is a **social contract** — the audience has expectations of the speaker's form, and when those expectations are violated, the social contract is perceived as broken regardless of content quality.

### Vignette 4 — The Writing-with-AI Encounter
A later vignette (revision-era) staging the encounter between stuttering experience and AI writing assistance. AI generates fluent text from input that is itself halting, interrupted, and associative. The result is formally compliant text that carries no trace of the production conditions.

**Analysis**: The AI vignette asks: if the stutter is an epistemological position, what happens when AI renders it invisible? Is the fluent output a liberation or an erasure? This is the governance contradiction identified in [[Sealed Card Protocol — Legitimacy, Glitch, and Charging]] — the legibility prosthesis that enables participation while intensifying pathway policing.

## Stuttering as Epistemological Position

The paper's central theoretical move: reframing stuttering not as a speech disorder to be managed but as a site from which specific things can be seen:

- The **governance functions of fluency** become visible to someone who cannot perform fluency automatically — in the same way that the governance functions of whiteness or masculinity are more visible to those who are not white or male
- **Hesitation as thinking**: the stutter-pause is often the moment of most active cognitive processing — compulsory fluency pathologizes the visible sign of thought
- **Repair as method**: stuttering involves continuous self-monitoring, repair, and rerouting — cognitive practices that are devalued in fluency-centric assessment but are precisely the skills required for governance work (error detection, course correction, revision)

## Masculinity and Disability Pride

The paper includes sections on stuttering and masculinity:
- Stuttering violates normative masculine speech codes (confident, direct, authoritative)
- Male stutterers are subject to double compulsion: the general compulsory fluency regime and the gender-specific version
- Disability pride politics applied to stuttering: the stutter is not a deficit to overcome but a difference to inhabit with dignity

And on disability pride vs. cure narratives:
- Medical/therapeutic model: stuttering is a deficit; treatment is toward cure or management; success is measured by fluency approximation
- Social model: stuttering is a difference; the problem is the compulsory fluency regime, not the speech pattern; success is measured by access and accommodation
- **The governance implication**: the [[CDPDJ Complaint — Lepage v Calian and Novartis]] occupational context is directly shaped by this analysis — the disability claim is that failure to accommodate a stuttering employee is a governance failure, not a personal limitation

## Insights

- This paper is the autobiographical foundation of the governance methodology: the technical arguments in PHAROS about interruption, legibility, and the politics of fluency have their origin in lived experience. This is not incidental — it explains the persistence and precision of the fluency/interruption framework across 12+ years of research
- The AI vignette in the revision is one of the most honest articulations of the AI governance problem: the question is not whether AI helps (it does) but whether the help erases the epistemological position that the assistance was supposed to support. Liberation that erases its own conditions is not liberation — it is substitution
- The future directions section is notable as a research retrospective from 2012 that accurately predicts the PHAROS research program — suggesting the governance methodology was already conceptually present in the disability and autoethnographic work, waiting for technical instantiation
