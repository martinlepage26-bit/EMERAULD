# Ethnographic Data Loss — Ethics Decision and Publication Disclaimer

## Summary

Operator decision (2026-04-26): go forward with all publications drawing on the Montreal and online queer/trans ritual network fieldwork (2020–2024). Original fieldwork materials — recordings and supporting files — were lost due to theft and file corruption. Real names were never recorded at any point in the research process. Contemporaneous writing produced during the fieldwork period survives and supports the field vignettes. Ethics review: no participant rights breach. Canonical disclaimer text below. Applies to [[Still Running — Willow, Anya, and Queer Ritual Infrastructure]], [[The Scythe Already in Motion — Buffy, Queer Ritual, and the Politics of Glitch]], and [[Glitching the Sacred]] (if drawing on same fieldwork corpus).

## Context

The [[Still Running — Willow, Anya, and Queer Ritual Infrastructure]] article and the [[The Scythe Already in Motion — Buffy, Queer Ritual, and the Politics of Glitch]] book prospectus both rest on Montreal queer Pagan and online queer/trans ritual network fieldwork (2020–2024). During a Hermes routing pass (2026-04-26), the operator confirmed that original recordings and supporting materials were lost to theft and file corruption. Two facts close the ethics concern: (1) contemporaneous writing produced during the fieldwork exists, providing evidentiary grounding for the field vignettes; (2) real names were never recorded — the consent-by-design protocol excluded identifiable information at the point of collection, not after.

This connects to the broader [[Research and Papers MOC]] publication ethics track and the [[Governance and PHAROS MOC]] bounded-claims discipline: the theoretical framework derived from this fieldwork stands independently and is not undermined by the data loss.

## Ethics Analysis

### Why there is no participant rights breach

Under the [[Consent, Fraud, and Boundary Protocols]] framework and standard research ethics (REB/IRB criteria):

**Data stewardship:** Theft and file corruption constitute an involuntary loss, not a deliberate breach. The committee concern would normally be whether participants can be harmed by the loss. Here they cannot.

**Names never recorded:** This is consent-by-design — identifiable information was structurally excluded from the data collection protocol from the start. There was no identifiable dataset to lose, steal, or breach. What was lost was already anonymous at the point of creation.

**Contemporaneous writing:** Field vignettes are drawn from writing produced during the fieldwork period, not reconstructed from memory after the fact. This satisfies the evidentiary basis question a committee would press.

**De-identification timing:** The protocol was consent-first and built-in, not applied retroactively. This is the strongest possible position under any ethics framework.

### What an ethics committee would require

- Disclaimer in the methodology section of each affected publication — **done (see below)**
- Data availability statement in journal submission forms — **template below**
- Confirmation that de-identification was built into collection, not applied after — **confirmed**
- Confirmation that contemporaneous writing exists to ground the vignettes — **confirmed**

**Verdict:** No breach. Go forward with disclaimer.

## Canonical Disclaimer (Methodology Section)

> The fieldwork underlying this analysis was conducted in Montreal's queer Pagan and online queer/trans ritual networks from 2020 to 2024 under a consent-first protocol. Real names and identifying information were not recorded at any point in the research process. Field vignettes and interpretive synthesis are drawn from contemporaneous writing produced during the fieldwork period. Original recordings and supporting materials were subsequently lost due to theft and file corruption. Raw materials cannot be made available; no identifying data existed within them.

## Data Availability Statement (Journal Submission Forms)

> Original fieldwork data (recordings and supporting materials) were lost due to theft and file corruption and cannot be made available. No identifying participant data was recorded at any point in the research process.
