## Source 1 — Alchimie et Histoire des Sciences (Gérardin)

**Source:** [[raw/Clippings/Alchimie et Histoire des Sciences.md]] — Lucien Gérardin, captured from a ChatGPT conversation with an alchemical text

**Key argument (verified from source):**

The history of science is systematically deformed by what Gérardin calls "selection bias toward the legible." Modern historians include what current science recognizes as valid and discard the rest as superstition or mysticism. Gérardin's central evidence: Newton's unpublished manuscripts show half theology, one quarter alchemy, one quarter physics — yet Newton is canonized as a physicist. The alchemy was erased from the official record not because it was absent but because the historiographers couldn't or wouldn't account for it.

**The "lab-oratoire" distinction (source-verified):**

The title plays on laboratory vs. *lab-oratoire* — the laboratory as a space of prayer, meditation, and inner work, not just chemical experiment. This collapses the work/prayer distinction and positions alchemical practice as simultaneously material and contemplative.

**The "sauveteurs destructeurs" problem (source-verified):**

Byzantine compilers saved texts by destroying them — summarizing, editing, embedding personal ideas, creating the only surviving record but a falsified one. The mechanism: archives that exist only as mediated, compressed, often manipulated traces. No original.

**Routing into the ritual-authority framework:**

This connects to [[Queer Media and Ritual Map]] and the proof-regime argument at the center of Martin's governance work. The Gérardin argument is structurally identical to the Diamond-Eyes problem: a governance evaluation system is only as good as what it decides to count as evidence. What's excluded as "mysticism" in one historiographic tradition becomes the operative mechanism in another. The "lab-oratoire" frames contemplative synthesis (including vault work, governance synthesis) as methodologically serious — not soft.
