# A Lokian Family — Queer and Pagan Agency in Montreal (Pomegranate 2014)

## Summary

Peer-reviewed article by [[Martin Lepage — Professional Profile|Martin Lepage]] studying queer pagan kinship in Montreal. Central argument: ritual identity, belonging, and agency are made **collectively rather than inherited**. Uses the figure of the Lokian family (chosen ritual kinship structured around Loki-as-trickster) to show how queer pagan actors construct agentive belonging outside normative kinship structures. One of the earliest operational appearances of the agency-under-constraint charge in [[Martin Walks the Witches' Road — Corpus as Charge-Persistence|the canonical corpus arc]].

**Citation:** Lepage, Martin. "A Lokian Family: Queer and Pagan Agency in Montreal." *The Pomegranate: The International Journal of Pagan Studies* 15, no. 1-2 (2014): 79–101.

The canonical date is 2014. PDF on D:: `D:/CODEX/Papers/Old/A_Lokian_Family_Queer_and_Pagan_Agency_i.pdf`.

## Context

This paper belongs to the ethnographic fieldwork phase (Montreal Pagan Community, 2003–2016). It operates in the same territory as [[Neo-Shamanism and Gender — Montreal Pagan Community]] and [[Queering Neo-Pagan Magic — 2026 Paper]], but with a distinct focus on kinship structure rather than ritual practice or gender performance. The Lokian figure (Loki as trickster, shape-shifter, boundary-crosser) is the mythological analog for the queer pagan community's refusal of inherited identity — agency as collective construction, not fixed property. This is the trickster-logic variant of the Witches' Road charge: the form changes (from mythology to lived kinship to governance theory) while the claim about agency-under-constraint persists.

## Corpus Position

In the [[Martin Walks the Witches' Road — Corpus as Charge-Persistence|corpus table]], this sits between the 2010 MA thesis (Egyptian/mythocritical substrate) and the 2017 PhD (full sociocritical formalization).
