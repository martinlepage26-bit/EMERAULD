# The Inner Goddess — Gender Normativity and Gay Identity in Wiccan Ritualities

## Summary

Academic article (English journal submission, unpublished) by [[Martin Lepage — Professional Profile|Martin Lepage]] examining how Wicca's gendered ritual framework — while claiming feminist inclusivity and LGBTQ openness — reproduces normative constructions of masculine and feminine identity, with particular focus on the experience of a gay male Wiccan high priest. The `LEPAGE-SHE-THE INNER GODDESS.docx` filename indicates this was prepared for submission to a journal abbreviated *SHE* — publication not confirmed.

**Distinct from the published chapter:** This English submission is separate from "La Déesse intérieure : normativité du genre et identité gaie dans les ritualités wiccanes" — the **published 2018 French book chapter** in *Rites et ritualisations* (Presses de l'Université Laval). That chapter is validated (publisher page + excerpt); this English version's publication status is unconfirmed.

**Note on source file date:** The `2003` prefix is a Dr. Sort false extraction — the actual composition date is unconfirmed. Part of [[Research and Papers MOC]] and [[Historical Academic Portfolio — Pre-PHAROS Scholarly Work]].

## Context

This is one of [[Martin Lepage — Professional Profile|Martin Lepage]]'s earliest academic publications, predating the doctoral dissertation and establishing the core critical tension that runs through the entire Pagan research arc: Wicca claims inclusivity but structurally encodes gender binarism. The paper anchors the [[Inner Goddess Wicca Gender — Version Genealogy]] across two nearly identical files (`2003 - ...THE INNER GODDESS.docx` and `LEPAGE-SHE-THE INNER GODDESS.docx`, differing by one minor formatting line). Part of the queer Pagan corpus that extends the 2010 mythocritique method (see [[Mythocritique to PHAROS — The 2010 Master's Thesis as Methodological Keystone]]); indexed in [[Queer Sociology of Magic and Ritual]]. Connects to [[Queering Neo-Pagan Magic — 2006 Paper]], [[Neo-Shamanism and Gender — Montreal Pagan Community]], and the full arc culminating in [[Why Be King Im Already a Queen — Book Project]] and [[The Circle Remains Open — Toward a Queer Sociology of the Sacred]].

## Details

**Focal case:** A participant who identifies as a gay man and Wiccan high priest — his life trajectory (in the sense of Beaud & Weber, Bertaux, Lemieux) illuminates how gender norms are attributed meaning and how gay identity is received and experienced within a religious context that claims to welcome it.

**Core observation:** In Wicca, feminine identities are highly valued (positioned as primary source of power and authority), but masculine identities are rarely questioned beyond typical figures of husband, father, or hunter. Gay male identities — despite being prevalent in the movement — are often subject to normalization rather than genuine inclusion.

**Ritual mechanism analyzed — the Great Rite:** In this fertility rite, the high priest (representing the God) plunges the athame (ceremonial dagger) into the chalice held by the high priestess (representing the Goddess), symbolizing the erotic and creative union of feminine and masculine. The rite fixes gender to biological sex through symbolic reproductive logic. Gay practitioners must navigate this heteronormative template even when claiming the high priest role.

**Contrasting currents named:** Reclaiming Witchcraft and the Radical Faeries, where critiques of heteronormativity and struggles against homophobia and transphobia are explicitly reflected in ritual performance and belief. Here, the masculine/feminine complementarity is differently configured.

**Theoretical grounding:** Gender performativity; sociology of religion; life trajectory method (Beaud & Weber, Bertaux, Lemieux). Sources include Griffin, Dufresne, Hasbrouck, Pike, Harper, Roberts on Pagan gender; Charbonneau, Oboler, Kraemer on Wicca and sexual duality.

**Word count:** Approximately 4,996–4,998 words across the two versions.

## Key Ideas

- Wicca's inclusive claim is structurally undermined by ritual forms that naturalize sexual duality and fix gender to biological sex
- Gay male practitioners are normalized rather than truly included — their presence is assimilated to the existing gender binary rather than challenging it
- Feminine identities are paradoxically privileged in Wicca (primary source of power) while masculine identities remain unexamined — this asymmetry affects how gay men experience ritual participation
- The Great Rite as a case study in how symbolic performance encodes normative logics that practitioners may not consciously endorse
- Life trajectory as method: meaning attributed to gender norms must be understood through the biographical arc of the practitioner, not just the ritual text

## Insights

- This early paper establishes the methodological and critical DNA for all subsequent Pagan research: ethnography plus queer theory plus attention to ritual form as ideology. Every later paper in the arc extends or complicates one element of this foundation.
- The focus on gay male identity (rather than lesbian, bisexual, or trans) is historically positioned — 2003 queer theory had a different center of gravity. Later papers in the arc expand to broader LGBTQ and non-binary experience.
- The gap between inclusivity claim and structural reproduction is the same gap that drives the [[Social Compass Paper — Ritual Authority and Régime de Preuve|Social Compass]] argument about proof regimes: the question of who counts as legitimate, and by whose standards, is continuous across the entire corpus.
