## Diff Summaries

### v1 (paper) content
The paper argues that Spider-Man's universality is conditioned by dubbed voice as an infrastructure of recognizability. Core scene: the author's failure to perform the dubbed Québec French "Spideurmanne" voice while playing with a nephew. Three conceptual moves: universality claim → vocal mediation → Saguenay scene as embodied proof. The conclusion reads the glitch as a speech act of refusal.

### v1 → v2 (editorial pass)
v2 is not a revision of the paper but a record of editorial diagnosis: "The argument is not underdeveloped. It is over-circulated." v2 proposes a compressed core claim ("Anyone can wear the mask only if they can speak through it in a recognizable voice") and replacement paragraphs for thesis, literature review gap, and method sections.

### v2 → v3 → v4 (working sessions)
Progressive accumulation of editorial material, notes on the paper's argument, and replacement text. v4 contains an extensively expanded conclusion drawing on Butler (1997), Fraser (2000), and Austin (1962). These files document the revision process rather than representing submitted drafts.

### v4 → v5/v6 (conversation logs)
v5 and v6 are large conversation log exports (46,000+ words each). They contain the full AI-assisted working sessions that produced the editorial passes above, including discussions of exclusion, recursion, latency, and the relationship between this paper and other works in the Loop Hinge cluster.

## Canonical Version
**`Spider‑Man .docx`** (= `02_Loop_Hinge_support__Spider-Man.docx`) is the actual academic article. The numbered variants (1)–(5) are editorial working sessions and should not be mistaken for revised drafts.

## Key Insights from Version History
This group illustrates a working practice where AI-assisted editorial sessions are preserved as docx files alongside the actual manuscript. The paper itself never changed between iterations — what changed was the surrounding critical apparatus and editorial scaffolding. The Loop Hinge archive copy indicates this paper was designated as support material for a larger manuscript project.
