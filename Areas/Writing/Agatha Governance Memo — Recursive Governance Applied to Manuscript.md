# Agatha Governance Memo — Recursive Governance Applied to Manuscript

## Summary
Internal governance memo applying the PHAROS recursive governance methodology to the *Agatha All Along* manuscript itself (`Agathav4_3_final.docx`). Author: Martin Lepage, PhD. The memo is a self-referential governance artifact: the same governance principles used to analyze AI systems are applied to the academic manuscript describing those principles. Three-layer archive map, five findings, five recursive risks, six governance controls with owners, triggers, evidence requirements, and review intervals. Bounded conclusion. Includes an AI disclosure template for the manuscript. **Functionally a worked operational case for [[Mythocritique to PHAROS — The 2010 Master's Thesis as Methodological Keystone|the Osirian recursive method]] applied to the operator's own academic writing — *textes-rites* governing themselves.** Indexed in [[Media Studies and Pop Culture Analysis]] and [[Recursive Governance Theory]]. Related to [[Agatha All Along — Wicca, Digital Mediatization, and Proof Regimes]], [[Recursive Deterministic AI Governance — Method and Paper]], and [[RECURSO — Final Audit and Ethical Review]].

## Context
Source: `Agatha_GovernanceMemo.docx`. Found in the same directory as the Agatha drafts in `raw sources/`. This is an internal governance document — not for publication — that demonstrates the PHAROS methodology's self-applicability: if governance is good, it applies to its own production process, not just to AI systems.

## Three-Layer Archive Map

The memo maps the manuscript's production across three layers:

**Layer 1 — Raw Sources**: Ethnographic field notes (2013-2016), interview transcripts, fieldwork journals. These are the evidentiary base.

**Layer 2 — Draft Sequence**: v3 → v4.2 → v4.2_revised → v4.3_final → v4.4 → v4.5 → v5 (submitted). Each version is a governance artifact with a traceable production history.

**Layer 3 — Analytical Instruments**: The theoretical frameworks, citation practices, and argumentative structures. These are governance objects in the sense that they determine which evidence is admissible and which is classified as outside the article's scope.

**Purpose of the map**: Making the production pathway legible so that the governance analysis can be applied. This mirrors Stage 0 (Dataset Intake) of the [[PHAROS Recalibration — Unified Governance Architecture]]: before governance can be applied, the scope of what is being governed must be inventoried.

## Five Findings

### Finding 1 — Proof Regime Mismatch
The manuscript operates across two proof regimes (empirical/ethnographic and media studies/textual analysis) that apply different standards for admissible evidence. The mismatch is not resolved — it is present in the manuscript as a productive tension, but it creates governance risk at the peer review stage.

**Governance implication**: A manuscript that crosses proof regime boundaries needs explicit declarations of which regime applies to which claim. Unstated regime switches are legibility failures.

### Finding 2 — Version Drift
Between v4.2 and v5 (the submitted version), the manuscript's central claim shifted from "authenticity is contested" (ethnographic finding) to "proof regimes govern authenticity claims" (theoretical contribution). This drift is productive but underdocumented — the paper does not explicitly mark where the argument changed.

**Governance implication**: Version drift without documentation produces a manuscript where the argument does not align with the evidence base, because the evidence was assembled for the earlier version of the argument.

### Finding 3 — AI Assistance Undisclosed
Portions of the manuscript were produced with AI assistance (not specified which AI tool or which portions). Current academic publication norms require disclosure; the submitted version does not include disclosure.

**Governance implication**: Non-disclosure is a legibility failure — not a fabrication, but a pathway opacity that could trigger the seam event described in [[Sealed Card Protocol — Legitimacy, Glitch, and Charging]] if discovered post-publication.

### Finding 4 — Ethnographic Data Currency
The ethnographic fieldwork dates from 2013-2016. The manuscript was submitted in 2025-2026. The field has changed; the community has changed; the platforms have changed. The data currency problem is partially addressed by the addition of AAA (2024) analysis but not fully.

**Governance implication**: Claims about "contemporary" Wiccan practice based on 10-year-old data need temporal scoping.

### Finding 5 — Self-Reference Risk
The paper's analysis of certification logic (the Witches' Road certifies practitioners; Agatha discovers the certification is self-generated — see [[Martin Walks the Witches' Road — Corpus as Charge-Persistence]] for why this certification structure runs through the entire operator corpus) applies to its own production: the manuscript certifies its claims through a methodology that the manuscript itself designed. This is the reflexive certification problem.

**Governance implication**: The self-reference risk is not fatal — it is the condition of all governance work. But it requires explicit acknowledgment (as noted in [[RECURSO — Final Audit and Ethical Review]]).

## Five Recursive Risks

1. **Frame capture**: The manuscript's analytical frame (proof regimes) may impose itself on the evidence rather than emerge from it
2. **Citation economy**: Citations to co-directors (Servais, Obadia) may carry weight beyond their evidential contribution in peer review
3. **Version contamination**: Revisions may have introduced claims that are not supported by the evidence base of the current version
4. **Disclosure gap**: AI assistance undisclosed; fieldwork dates insufficiently marked
5. **Archive-becomes-agent**: The governance memo itself may become a governance constraint on the manuscript rather than a governance tool — if the author treats the memo's findings as definitive rather than provisional

## Six Governance Controls

| Control | Owner | Trigger | Evidence | Review Interval |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Proof regime declaration | Author | Before final submission | Explicit regime labeling in manuscript | Per revision |
| Version change log | Author | Every draft | Annotated diff or change summary | Per version |
| AI disclosure template | Author | Before submission | See below | Final version only |
| Data currency audit | Author | Before submission | Date-scoped claims throughout | Per revision |
| External review of self-reference risk | Co-director | Before submission | Independent read by Servais or Obadia | v5 only |
| Bounded conclusion check | Author | Before submission | Conclusion claims scoped to evidence base | Per revision |

## AI Disclosure Template

The memo includes a template AI disclosure statement for academic manuscripts:

> "Portions of this manuscript were developed with the assistance of AI language model tools. AI assistance was used for [specify: drafting, revision, translation, literature review, citation checking]. All claims, arguments, and interpretations are the author's own. The AI tools used were [specify tool names and versions]. This disclosure is made in accordance with [journal policy reference]."

The template is designed to satisfy formal disclosure requirements while specifying the nature and scope of AI use — distinguishing between AI-assisted drafting (which some journals permit) and AI-generated content claimed as original (which journals do not permit).

## Bounded Conclusion

The memo's conclusion is explicitly bounded:

> These five findings and five recursive risks are identified from an internal governance review. They represent the reviewer's best assessment at this stage of the manuscript's development. The findings do not constitute a determination that the manuscript is unpublishable — they constitute a set of governance tasks that require completion before the manuscript's evidentiary and ethical foundations can be considered adequate for peer-reviewed publication.

This is the [[Recursive Deterministic AI Governance — Method and Paper]] promotion language applied to a manuscript: not "publish" or "reject" but "governance tasks identified before promotion can be assessed."

## Insights

- The self-applicability of the governance methodology is both the memo's most important demonstration and its most significant recursive risk: a governance method that can audit its own production processes is more trustworthy than one that only audits others. But the auditor of the audit is the same person as the auditee — the external review control (co-director read) is the only mechanism that breaks the self-reference loop
- Finding 3 (AI assistance undisclosed) is the most consequential governance risk: if discovered post-publication, it triggers the seam event that dissolves the manuscript's charging regardless of the quality of its argument. Proactive disclosure is lower-risk than discovery
- The version control finding (Finding 2) is a reminder that academic manuscripts are governance objects with production histories — and that governance of the manuscript requires the same attention to version tracking, chain-of-custody, and drift detection as governance of AI systems
