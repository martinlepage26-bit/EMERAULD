# Virtually Magical Literally — Queer Agency and the Digital Witch

See also [[Queer Neo-Pagan Spiritualities and the Question of Authenticity in Wicca]].
See also [[Historical Academic Portfolio — Pre-PHAROS Scholarly Work]].
See also [[Pagan and Queer Ritual Studies MOC]].
## Summary

Academic article by [[Martin Lepage — Professional Profile|Martin Lepage]] submitted to *Social Compass* examining the contemporary reconfiguration of Wiccan identity and witchcraft practices in Canada (especially Québec) within the digital landscape. ~10,225 words. Introduces the concept of **Wicca 2.0**. Closely related to [[Social Compass Paper — Ritual Authority and Régime de Preuve]] and [[Agatha All Along — Wicca, Digital Mediatization, and Proof Regimes]]. Part of [[Research and Papers MOC]].

## Context

The full title is "Virtually Magical, Literally: Queer Agency and the Digital Witch." The submission letter (`LEPAGE-LetterWicca2.0.docx`) names this as submitted to *Social Compass* through SAGE. The main manuscript file is `LEPAGE-Queer Agency and the Digital Witch.docx`. This paper is a distinct document from the v4.5 Social Compass merged manuscript (Agathav4.5) — the latter focuses on the *régime de preuve* argument and is the revised/resubmitted version; this file appears to be an earlier or parallel version with different framing. The key distinction: this version foregrounds "Wicca 2.0" and digital agency explicitly, while the v4.5 shifts the theoretical frame toward proof regimes and authority fabrication.

## Details

**Central concept — Wicca 2.0:** The digital transformation of Wicca that challenges traditional modes of ritual transmission and authenticity, reshaping the sorcerous self as a performative, hyper-visible, and often monetized identity. Includes TikTok and Instagram as platforms mediating spiritual identity.

**Theoretical framework:** Queer theory, ritual studies, sociology of religion. Analysis of the MCU television series *Agatha All Along* as the primary popular culture case study — the witch mobilized as a site of political tension, spiritual empowerment, and commodified identity.

**Key argument:** Rather than undermining authenticity, digital witchcraft enables new forms of **enchantment and agency**, particularly for queer practitioners navigating marginalization, virtual community, and ritual experimentation. "Digital enchantment" as a distinct phenomenon that resists traditional hierarchies and reclaims sacred space as plural, performative, and politically charged.

**Fieldwork base:** Ethnographic research on Wicca in Québec and Canada.

**Submission context (from cover letter):** Approximately 10,225 words, submitted exclusively to *Social Compass*, formatted per SAGE guidelines. ORCID iD: 0009-0006-4320-6254.

**Relationship to the other Social Compass versions:**
- This (`LEPAGE-Queer Agency and the Digital Witch.docx`) = earlier version, "Wicca 2.0" framing
- `Agathav4.5_socialcompass_merged.docx` = later revision, shifted to *régime de preuve* argument
- Reviewer responses (`SocialCompass_response_to_reviewers_v2/v3.docx`) = response to editorial feedback on the later version
- The canonical current state is documented in [[Social Compass Paper — Ritual Authority and Régime de Preuve]]

## Key Ideas

- Wicca 2.0: socially mediated, aestheticized forms of online rituality
- Algorithmic mysticism: how platform algorithms shape what spiritual practice looks like and who practices it visibly
- Virtual covens and media-saturated spirituality: new communities that are not lesser substitutes for in-person practice but distinct formations
- Commodification of identity: the sorcerous self as a monetized performance

## Insights

- The "Wicca 2.0" concept is the earlier theoretical frame for what becomes the *régime de preuve* argument in the v4.5 version. The revision moved from describing digital transformation to analyzing the **proof conditions** that digital mediation imposes on ritual authority — a more governance-oriented framing consistent with [[PHAROS Recalibration — Unified Governance Architecture|PHAROS]]
- The cover letter's explicit statement "I stutter" is absent here; the queer identity disclosure is textual/content-based rather than performatively embedded in the submission itself
