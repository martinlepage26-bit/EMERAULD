# AI Society Manuscript — From AI Anxiety to Recursive Governance

## Summary
Academic journal manuscript targeting *AI & Society*. Full title: "From AI Anxiety to Recursive Governance Under Constraint: Designing an Ethics of Interruption for Agentic AI Systems." The paper reframes AI anxiety not as a psychological phenomenon but as a governance response to weakly inspectable authority. The central concept is **revisability** — whether a person or institution can reopen a claim, inspect its provenance, and alter the path of action before closure hardens into consequence. Revisability is stronger than transparency. Includes an embedded storyboard case study with real governance numbers. Related to [[Recursive Deterministic AI Governance — Method and Paper]] and [[Fluency, Interruption, and Institutional Accountability]].

## Core Claim

AI exerts two simultaneous pressures:
1. It is a socially destabilizing force across work, privacy, education, healthcare, and institutional trust
2. It is a recursive technology whose outputs can re-enter later decision cycles

Both AI anxiety and recursive governance drift can be read through a single governance lens: the acquisition of practical authority under conditions of **weak legibility** and **weakened revisability**.

**Revisability** (the central concept): whether a person or institution can reopen a claim, inspect its provenance, and alter the path of action before closure hardens into consequence. This is stronger than transparency. A transparent system can still be difficult to contest in practice.

## The Storyboard Case (Embedded Internal Case Study)

A recursive governance storyboard with four phases:

| Phase | Governed Claims | Blocked | What Changed |
|---|---|---|---|
| Contaminated Baseline | 194 | 25 | Broad, unfiltered intake; commingled artifact classes |
| Corrected Stable Branch | 29 | 0 | Tightened admissibility; explicit exclusions |
| Three-Output Synthesis | 30 | 9 | Governance artifacts re-admitted as inputs (governance-on-governance) |
| Recursive Continuation Plateau | 10 | 3 | Narrowed orbit around self-description |

**What the numbers reveal**: Not model performance variation — a change in the *object under governance* across phases. The numbers are evidence of what governance discipline does, not evidence about AI capability.

**Phase 3 significance**: When governance artifacts are admitted as inputs, the system governs its own governance (governance-on-governance). Blocked claims cluster around title lines, provenance notes, and summary sentences — the system is governing the textual memory of its own governance.

**Phase 4 significance**: The "plateau" is not random failure. It is a narrowed orbit around self-description — the system has recursed into self-reference.

---

## Three Causes of Drift

1. **Input contamination**: outputs, caches, and reports that are too close to the intake path enter the corpus and distort later governance
2. **Classifier-sensitive text**: peripheral cues (headers, shebang lines, timestamps) exert disproportionate downstream effects on classification
3. **Packet self-governance**: generated governance packets admitted as inputs, destabilizing the governance function they were meant to secure

---

## Four Key Findings

**Finding 1 — Boundary Discipline Materially Changed the Outcome**: Admissibility rules and source boundaries materially altered the governance profile. Excluding runtime folders and normalizing headers redefined the field itself. **More information is not the cure for evidentiary collapse; cleaner hierarchy is.**

**Finding 2 — Recursive Reuse Changed the Object of Governance**: The storyboard shows what happens when governance packets become inputs — the system no longer governs the original corpus; it governs its own prior governance outputs. Recursion limits and distinct documentation rules are required.

**Finding 3 — Method Lock as Governance Primitive**: Method lock marks the stable branch (29/0) as the authoritative source-tree outcome and treats later packet-on-packet loops as a different analytic object. Without such markers, recursive systems invite narrative inflation where every later pass is treated as most authoritative.

**Finding 4 — AI Anxiety and Recursive Drift Converge**: The anxiety literature shows anxiety intensifies when systems become opaque and challenge routes weaken. The storyboard shows legibility degrades when provenance becomes unstable. These converge on the same governance mechanism.

## Authorship Taxonomy (Three Levels)

1. **Clerical mediation**: formatting, spelling, citations
2. **Expressive mediation**: sentence flow, restructuring
3. **Epistemic mediation**: surfacing contradictions, proposing framings, narrowing claims, generating counterarguments

The paper used AI for structural revision, comparative reframing, adversarial testing — but not as evidentiary authority. Human judgment remained decisive at source selection, evidence hierarchy, concept definition, and normative translation.

---

## Four Operational Design Principles

1. **Provenance boundaries**: source material always distinguishable from generated artifacts
2. **Artifact hierarchy**: not all coherent outputs enter institutional reasoning at the same evidentiary level
3. **Contestability**: classifications remain open to challenge before hardening
4. **Bounded recursion**: later-stage synthesis must not silently redefine the object of governance

## Insights

- "Revisability is stronger than transparency" — this is the paper's most important conceptual contribution. Transparency means the information is available; revisability means it can actually be used to reopen and alter a decision. Most governance frameworks target transparency; this paper argues they need to target revisability
- The storyboard numbers (194 → 29 → 30 → 10) are the empirical backbone of the PHAROS method. They are not proof that the method works in general; they are proof that governance discipline *changes* what is counted, classified, and blocked — which is itself the claim
- Method lock as a governance primitive: the system needs explicit markers that separate the governed object from governance artifacts about that object. Without these markers, the governance apparatus colonizes the corpus, and the system ends up governing its own shadow
- Packet self-governance (governance artifacts as inputs) is the most dangerous failure mode: the system starts managing the appearance of its governance process rather than the underlying corpus. This is the RECURSO SHADOWMASTER problem at the epistemological level
