# Masculinités et ritualités — La magie chez les Radical Faeries

## Summary

Academic paper (French-language; ~2018 or later) by [[Martin Lepage — Professional Profile]] examining masculinity, magic, and ritual practice in the Radical Faeries movement. Part of the [[Queer Sociology of Magic and Ritual|queer Pagan studies corpus]]. The Radical Faeries appear in the broader research arc as a contrasting current to mainstream Wicca: a movement where critiques of heteronormativity and struggles against homophobia and transphobia are explicitly integrated into ritual performance and belief, and where the masculine/feminine complementarity is differently configured than in institutional Wicca. This paper analyzes that difference directly. Belongs to [[Historical Academic Portfolio — Pre-PHAROS Scholarly Work]].

## Context

Source file: `2012 - Masculinités et ritualités la magie chez les Radical Faeries.pdf` in the Dr. Sort sorted library (UNCLEAR/2012). The `2012` date is a Dr. Sort false extraction — Martin confirmed the paper is ~2018 or later. No raw source or submission record confirmed at this time.

The Radical Faeries appear as a named contrasting current in [[The Inner Goddess — Gender Normativity and Gay Identity in Wiccan Ritualities]] (2003): a stream of neo-Paganism where gay male identity and non-heteronormative masculinity are not merely tolerated but structurally incorporated into ritual design. This paper extends that observation into a dedicated analysis. It connects to the Montreal fieldwork documented in [[Neo-Shamanism and Gender — Montreal Pagan Community]] and the Pomegranate paper on queer Pagan agency ([[A Lokian Family — Queer and Pagan Agency in Montreal (Pomegranate 2014)|A Lokian Family, 2014]]). The full genealogy of the queer Pagan corpus runs through [[Queering Neo-Pagan Magic — Identity Power and the Uses of Magic]].

**Unverified hypothesis — possible published version:** The 2018 book chapter "La ritualisation des masculinités gays : diversité et émancipation" (co-authored with David Jeffrey, in *Rites et ritualisations*, Presses de l'Université Laval) shares a research site (gay masculinity and ritual in Montreal neo-Paganism). These may be the same work (working title vs. published title; solo draft vs. co-authored final), or two distinct papers. They have different titles and different authorship (solo vs. co-authored). This identification is **unverified** — it must not be treated as confirmed until the source files are cross-checked and Martin confirms the relationship.
