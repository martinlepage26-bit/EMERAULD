# Queering Neo-Pagan Magic — MRW Submission (2026)

## Summary

Revision-complete submission record for [[Martin Lepage — Professional Profile|Martin Lepage]]'s paper "Queering Neo-Pagan Magic: Identity, Power, and the Uses of Magic," prepared for *Magic, Ritual, and Witchcraft* (MRW), University of Pennsylvania Press.

The paper is an ethnographic study of queer neo-Pagan practitioners in Montreal. It argues that queer magic is best understood not as a stable belief system but as a pragmatic repertoire of uses: magic as a relational verb rather than a noun. Its central theoretical move is the pivot from what magic **is** to what magic **does**.

The completed revision positions the paper in comparison-with-difference to Michael D. Bailey's "The Meanings of Magic" rather than as a contrast. Bailey already argued that magic is constitutively unstable and relational; this paper extends that insight by showing queer practitioners inhabiting instability as lived method, political ground, and ritual practice.

## Key Theoretical Moves

- **Magic as use, not belief:** The paper shifts analysis from doctrinal belief or ontological definition to situated practice: what magic enables practitioners to do.
- **Relational verb, not noun:** Magic is treated as an enacted repertoire of relation, repair, intention, narrative, and tactical response.
- **Comparison-with-difference to Bailey:** Bailey's account of magic as unstable and boundary-making is not rejected; it becomes the starting point for asking how queer practitioners use that instability from within.
- **Use without mastery:** The revision develops the formulation "use without mastery, from a position that refuses to be mastered."
- **Illegitimacy as sacred ground:** Queer practitioners do not merely endure magic's marginality; they cultivate it as a site of agency, survival, and world-making.

## Practitioner Voices

- **Maria:** somatic reciprocity; magic as ethical, embodied energy work where balance and cost matter.

## Revision History

- Editor accepted the paper with minor revisions.
- Michael D. Bailey read the draft, liked it, and flagged a misreading: the paper had framed him as calling for definitional clarity, when his 2006 article already argued for magic's irreducible slipperiness.
- Revision corrected the Bailey engagement by pivoting from contrast to comparison-with-difference in the introduction and conclusion.
- Citation format converted from author-date parentheticals to Chicago endnotes for MRW style.
- Abstract added and verified: 173 words.
- Keywords added: 15.
- Main manuscript anonymized per MRW instruction; author information placed in a separate cover-page file.
- Formatting verified against MRW instructions: Times New Roman, double-spaced, simple formatting, Chicago-style endnotes, no author-date citation leftovers detected.
- Misattribution removed: "Bailey's careful delimitation reflects a wider scholarly instinct to secure analytic objects by fixing their edges."
- Replacement move: fieldwide critique of scholarly containment rather than attributing definitional stabilizing to Bailey.
- Conclusion hardened around the question of scholarly domestication: "scholarship must ask whose comfort is served by the desire to domesticate it."
